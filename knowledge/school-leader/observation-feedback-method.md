# Observation and feedback method

Observation feedback grows teachers when it's specific, based on evidence and limited to one thing they can practise. A grade or a long list of points rarely changes practice.

## Low-inference notes

Write down what was seen and heard, not what it meant.

| Low inference (write this) | High inference (avoid in notes) |
|---|---|
| "11:05 T asked 'Who can tell me…?' 3 hands up; T took answer from student B." | "Poor questioning." |
| "6 of 28 students had books open at 11:12." | "Students disengaged." |
| "T gave instructions once, verbally; 4 students asked a neighbour what to do." | "Instructions unclear." |

Timestamps, counts and quotes keep the conversation about what happened. The teacher can't argue with "6 of 28", but they can argue with "disengaged".

## Choosing the action step

From the notes, pick **one** action step that:

- would make the biggest difference to student learning;
- the teacher can practise and do tomorrow;
- is small enough to observe next time.

"Improve behaviour management" is a goal, not a step. "Give instructions with a visual on the board and check understanding by asking one student to repeat them before release" is a step.

Name one strength too, specific enough to repeat ("Your cold-call to student D after the first answer brought in a quiet student").

## The feedback conversation (15–20 minutes)

1. **Open with a question.** "How did you feel the start of the task went?" Often the teacher spots the issue.
2. **Specific praise.** One strength from the notes.
3. **Evidence.** Share the relevant notes, neutrally.
4. **Agree the action step.** Ideally the teacher frames it; if not, propose it.
5. **Plan and practise.** Script or rehearse the move together ("Show me how you'd give tomorrow's instructions"). Practice is the step most often skipped.
6. **Set the follow-up.** When you'll pop in to see it.

## Written summary (send same day)

```
Date / class / focus:
Strength: [specific]
Evidence: [2–3 notes]
Action step: [one, concrete]
Practice plan: [what was rehearsed]
Follow-up: [date]
```

## Consistency across staff

- Use the same note format and the same action-step bank for everyone.
- Calibrate with other observers: watch a lesson (or video) together, write steps separately, compare.
- Track action steps per teacher over time, not one-off judgments.
- Keep developmental observation separate from formal appraisal or capability processes. If a concern becomes formal, follow the school's HR policy and its notice rules; the head and HR decide.

## Worked example

Notes: Year 8 science, 11:00–11:20. Instructions for the practical given verbally once (11:06). By 11:09, 5 groups had asked the teacher what to do. Two groups started before safety goggles were on.

Strength: "Your demo at 11:02 was clear; students could explain the method back."
Action step: "Before releasing to the practical, show the steps on the board and have one student repeat them, including the goggles step."
Practice: rehearsed the release script for tomorrow's lesson.
Follow-up: Thursday, first ten minutes.

## Common mistakes

- Ten points of feedback after one lesson.
- Notes full of judgments the teacher can dispute.
- No practice, so the step stays an idea.
- No follow-up, so the teacher learns feedback doesn't matter.
