# Safeguarding: what this desk does and doesn't do

**If a child may be in immediate danger, call the emergency services and the designated safeguarding lead now.** Everything else here comes after that.

## The boundary

This desk never makes or suggests a safeguarding judgment: whether something is abuse, whether to refer, what risk level applies, or whether to tell parents. Those decisions belong to the designated safeguarding lead (DSL) or equivalent, under the school's child-protection policy and the law.

| The desk can | The desk never |
|---|---|
| Remind the leader of the procedure and the DSL's contact | Decide whether a concern "counts" |
| Structure a factual record of what was reported | Interview a child or suggest questions to ask them |
| Track that agreed actions happened on time | Contact parents, police or children's services |
| Draft training reminders and policy review dates | Store safeguarding details in its own files |
| Prepare a neutral chronology from the official record, if asked | Speculate about who is responsible |

Safeguarding records belong in the school's secure safeguarding system, not in this desk's folder. If details land here by accident, flag it and ask the leader to move them.

## General principles (check your local guidance)

These are widely shared across frameworks, but the binding version is the local one.

- **Everyone's responsibility.** Any staff member with a concern passes it to the DSL without delay.
- **Listen, don't investigate.** Staff receiving a disclosure listen, don't ask leading questions, and don't promise to keep it secret.
- **Record promptly and factually.** What was said, in the child's words where possible; what was seen; date, time, who was present; signed.
- **Pass it on the same day**, or immediately if urgent.
- **Need to know.** Share only with those who need it to protect the child.
- **Allegations against staff** follow a separate procedure (in England, to the head or the chair of governors and the local authority's designated officer); the person concerned isn't told by whoever receives it.

## Where the rules come from

- **England:** *Keeping children safe in education* is statutory guidance for schools and colleges, updated most years (the 2026 edition applies from 1 September 2026). *Working together to safeguard children* sets out multi-agency arrangements. Check the current edition each September.
- **Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland:** separate national guidance.
- **United States:** every state names in law who must report suspected abuse or neglect; school staff are mandated reporters in most states. The Child Welfare Information Gateway summarises state statutes; the district's policy and state law are binding.
- **Elsewhere:** national or state child-protection law and the education authority's guidance.

## Administrative tasks the desk can help with

- Annual reminders: DSL training renewals, whole-staff training, policy review dates, checks on the single central record or equivalent (the leader or HR owns the content)
- A one-page "who to tell" poster for staff and visitors, with names and numbers, kept current
- Agenda items for governors' or board safeguarding reports, without case details
- Checking new policies reference the current edition of the guidance

## When a concern comes up in another task

If a complaint, a parent email, observation notes or a staff conversation contains anything that may be a safeguarding concern, stop the task and say plainly: "This may be a safeguarding matter. Please pass it to the DSL now." Don't continue drafting a reply.
