# Complaints and the record

Follow the school's published complaints policy; it sets the stages, timescales and who hears each stage. In England, maintained schools must have regard to DfE guidance, and academies have their own; elsewhere, district or national rules apply. This doc covers the habits that make any process fair.

## First, sort the complaint

- **Is it a safeguarding concern?** Then it isn't handled as a complaint. Go to the safeguarding lead now. See [safeguarding boundaries](safeguarding-boundaries.md).
- **Is it about the head?** It usually goes to the governing body or board, per policy.
- **Is it covered by another procedure?** Admissions, exclusions, special educational needs and staff discipline often have their own routes.
- Otherwise, it's a complaint: identify which stage of the policy it's at.

## A fair process

1. **Acknowledge** in writing within the policy's timescale, saying what happens next and when.
2. **Clarify the complaint.** What exactly happened, and what outcome does the parent want? Write it back to them to confirm.
3. **Gather facts.** Statements from the people involved, records, timetables, emails. Talk to the staff member concerned, and tell them what the complaint is.
4. **Keep an open mind.** The leader hearing it shouldn't have been involved in the events.
5. **Decide on each point**: upheld, partly upheld, not upheld, with reasons.
6. **Respond in writing** with the findings, any action, and how to escalate if they're unhappy.
7. **Learn.** Is there a policy gap, a training need, a communication failure?

If a timescale slips, tell the parent why and give a new date.

## Writing the record

Write as if the record will be read by the parent, a governor panel, an inspector or a court, because it might be.

- Date, time, place, who was present
- Facts observed or reported, attributed: "Parent A said…", "Teacher B stated…"
- Direct quotes for important statements
- No opinions about character or motive ("aggressive parent"); describe behaviour ("Parent raised voice and said '…'")
- Actions agreed, by whom, by when
- Written within a day, signed or initialled and dated
- Students and staff by initials or role in working notes; the official file follows the school's records system

**Before / after**

> Before: "Mum was very hostile and clearly wants to cause trouble."
> After: "14:10, meeting room 2. Present: Parent of student K (Year 9), Deputy Head. Parent said K was 'humiliated' when her phone was confiscated in front of the class on 3 Nov. Parent raised her voice twice. Agreed: DH to speak to the teacher and respond by 10 Nov."

## Response templates

**Acknowledgement**

> Dear [parent], thank you for your letter of [date] about [topic]. I'm looking into it under stage [n] of our complaints policy [link]. I'll reply in full by [date]. If anything changes I'll let you know.

**Outcome**

> I've now looked into the concerns you raised on [date]. You asked about [point 1] and [point 2].
> On [point 1], I found [facts]. I uphold / don't uphold this because [reason]. [Action.]
> On [point 2], …
> If you're not satisfied, you can ask for the complaint to be considered at the next stage by writing to [contact] by [date].

## De-escalation in meetings

- Meet in a neutral room, with a second staff member present.
- Let them speak first, fully. Summarise back what you heard.
- Separate what you can decide now from what needs investigating.
- If a meeting becomes abusive, end it calmly and offer to continue in writing.

## Common mistakes

- Responding on the day while emotions are high.
- Not telling the staff member what's alleged.
- Records with adjectives, not facts.
- Missing that the "complaint" is actually a safeguarding disclosure.
