# Space planning reference

Plans fail on movement and use, not on looks. Check every layout with this method before it goes on a board.

## Layout check method

1. **Measure everything,** including door swings, radiator and window positions, socket locations, ceiling height and skirting depth.
2. **Draw the paths.** Mark how people enter, cross and leave the room, and where they stop (sofa, desk, fridge).
3. **Check the paths stay clear.** No furniture in a main route; no door that hits a piece when it opens.
4. **Check each activity zone** has what it needs: seat, surface, light, socket.
5. **Tape it out on the floor** for big pieces. Clients understand tape far better than plans.
6. **Check delivery routes:** stairs, doorways, lifts, tight corners. A sofa that can't get in is the most expensive mistake in the job.

## Kitchen guidelines

The NKBA (National Kitchen & Bath Association, US) publishes widely used planning guidelines. They're recommendations, not building code:

- work aisle: 42 inches for one cook, 48 inches for more than one;
- walkway (where people pass but don't work): 36 inches.

Other countries use different conventions and metric standards. Check local codes and the kitchen fitter's requirements.

## Living rooms

Common rules of thumb (not code; adjust to the room and client):

- **Conversation distance:** seating close enough to talk without raising voices, usually arranged around a focal point.
- **Coffee table:** close enough to reach from the sofa, far enough to walk past knees.
- **Rugs:** large enough that at least the front legs of the main seating sit on it. A rug floating in the middle of the furniture makes a room look smaller.
- **TV:** placed to avoid window glare at the times it's used most.

## Bedrooms

- Clear space to walk on at least one side of the bed (both sides for a couple).
- Room to open wardrobe doors and drawers fully.
- Bedside surface and light on each sleeping side.

## Lighting layers

Plan three layers in every room:

| Layer | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | general light to move around | ceiling lights, wall washers |
| Task | light for a specific job | reading lamp, under-cabinet light, desk lamp |
| Accent | mood and focus | picture lights, uplights, LED strips |

Check: can each zone be lit separately? Are switches by the doors? Is the colour temperature consistent across a room? Any new wiring goes to a qualified electrician, and in many countries is regulated work.

## Art and mirrors

- Hang art relative to the furniture and eye level of the people using the room, not the ceiling.
- Group pieces as one shape; lay them out on the floor or with paper templates first.
- Mirrors: check what they'll reflect from the main seat.

## Common mistakes

- Planning from the client's floor plan without measuring.
- Furniture scaled to the showroom, not the room.
- Rugs too small.
- Lighting planned as one ceiling point per room.
- Ignoring radiators, vents and sockets until install day.

## Worked example

A client wants a 3-seat sofa facing the fireplace in a narrow living room. Taping it out shows the route from the door to the garden doors would squeeze behind the sofa. The fix: float the sofa a little off the wall to keep the route behind it clear, swap the coffee table for a narrower oval, and add a pair of swivel chairs that work for both the fireplace and the garden view. The client sees the tape and agrees in one visit.
