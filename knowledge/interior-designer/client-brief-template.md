# Client brief template

The brief is the scope. Everything later (concepts, budget lines, change orders) refers back to it, so it has to be specific.

## Intake questions

**How they live**
1. Who uses the space, and when? (Adults, children, pets, guests, home working.)
2. Walk me through a normal weekday in this room, then a weekend.
3. What annoys you about it now? (This list becomes the program.)
4. What has to stay? Inherited pieces, the piano, the art collection.
5. What has to survive? Toddler, dog, red wine, sun.
6. Storage: what lives here that has no home?

**Taste**
7. Show me three images you love. What do you like *in* each one: colour, light, tidiness, a piece?
8. Show me something you hate.
9. If two people decide: where do you agree, where don't you?
10. Words for how the room should feel. (Calm, lively, cosy, grand.)

**Practical**
11. Budget: what's the number you'd like to spend, and what's the most you'd stretch to?
12. Timeline: is there a real date (a baby, a party, a sale) or a preference?
13. Who approves decisions, and how fast can they respond?
14. Owned or rented? Any building or landlord rules on changes?
15. Accessibility needs now or likely soon?

## Budget conversation

- Ask for the number directly. Clients who won't say often have one.
- Compare it to the scope with researched ranges for the region, dated: "This scope at this finish level typically runs X-Y here; this is what moves the number."
- Add a contingency line. 10-15% is a common planning allowance; the designer sets the house standard.
- Explain the fee model (flat, hourly, percentage, or a mix) and how purchases and markups work.

```
Project: [client] – [rooms]          Date: [date]       Version: [n]
```

## Common mistakes

- Starting with mood boards before understanding how the room is used.
- A budget given as "flexible". Get a number and a ceiling.
- Not naming the decision-maker in a couple, so decisions get reopened.
- An unsigned brief, so "while you're here" requests have nothing to measure against.

## Worked example

Couple, living room, two young children and a large dog. The complaints list: nowhere to put toys, sofa fabric stained, glare on the TV in the afternoon. Program: closed storage at child height, performance fabric, a layout that moves the TV off the west wall, and a reading chair by the window for one partner. Budget agreed at a figure the designer checked against regional ranges, with 15% contingency. One decision-maker named for final approvals. Signed before any boards were made.
