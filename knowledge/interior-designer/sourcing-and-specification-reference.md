# Sourcing and specification reference

A complete spec makes ordering, substitutions and snag lists fast. A vague one ("grey sofa, Supplier X") causes most procurement mistakes.

## What every spec line needs

- [ ] Item and room.
- [ ] Supplier, product name and code.
- [ ] Dimensions: width, depth, height, plus seat height and arm height for seating.
- [ ] Finish or fabric: name, colour reference, and meterage (COM: customer's own material) if applicable.
- [ ] Quantity.
- [ ] Price (trade and retail as the designer's model requires), with delivery and fees.
- [ ] Lead time as quoted, and the date it was quoted.
- [ ] Budget line it belongs to.
- [ ] Approval: who approved and when.
- [ ] Care and warranty notes, for the handover pack.

## Fabric terms

- **Abrasion (rub) rating.** Fabrics are tested for wear. In the US, the Wyzenbeek test reports "double rubs"; in the UK and Europe, the Martindale test reports "rubs". The two aren't directly comparable. Ask the supplier which test was used and what use it's rated for (domestic, heavy domestic, contract).
- **Pilling and light-fastness.** Ask for these too for sunny rooms and heavy-use seating.
- **Cleaning codes (common in the US):** W (water-based cleaner), S (solvent-based cleaner only), W/S (either), X (vacuum only, no liquids). Put the code in the handover pack.
- **Performance fabrics** resist stains; check what the treatment is and how to clean it.
- **Railroaded vs up-the-bolt:** whether the pattern runs along or across the roll. It affects seams and how much fabric is needed.

## Fire safety rules for upholstery

These are country-specific and change; check before specifying.

- **UK:** the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 set fire resistance requirements for domestic upholstered furniture and require labels. When re-covering in a client's own fabric, the combination must still comply; ask the upholsterer how they'll meet the rules. The regulations were amended in 2025, so check the current text.
- **US:** requirements differ by state and by product. Ask the supplier which standard their product meets.
- **Contract or commercial spaces** (offices, hospitality) often have stricter rules; check with the client's building or fire officer.

## Lead-time management

- Order long-lead items first, as soon as the concept is signed off.
- Chase anything ordered but not confirmed within a few working days.
- When a date slips, decide the same day: re-sequence install, or start the substitution conversation.

## Delivery inspection checklist

Do this on arrival; many suppliers have short windows for damage claims.

- [ ] Count boxes against the delivery note before signing. Note any damaged packaging on the delivery note.
- [ ] Unpack and inspect within the supplier's claim window.
- [ ] Check item against the spec: code, size, colour, finish, fabric.
- [ ] Look for scratches, dents, wrong legs, missing hardware.
- [ ] Photograph any defect with the label visible.
- [ ] Report the same day in writing, with photos.
- [ ] Record storage location in the manifest.

## Substitutions

Same function and dimensions first, look second. Present the alternative with the honest differences in price, lead time and appearance, and log the client's approval before ordering.

## Common mistakes

- No seat height on a dining chair spec, so chairs don't fit the table.
- Missing the fabric meterage, so the upholsterer is short.
- Signing for deliveries without counting.
- Specifying a fabric without checking its fire compliance for the country.
