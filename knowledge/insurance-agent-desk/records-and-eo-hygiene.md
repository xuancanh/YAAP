# Records and E&O hygiene

Errors and omissions (E&O) claims against agents often come down to "what was the client told, and when?" A clean record answers that. This doc sets the standard; the agency's own compliance procedures and the regulator's rules come first where they're stricter.

## What to log, every time

For each client contact (call, meeting, email, text):

- date and time;
- who was involved;
- channel;
- what the client asked or reported, in their words where it matters;
- what the agent said or offered, as the agent reports it;
- what was agreed, with owner and due date.

Write facts, not impressions. "Client said she doesn't want flood cover because of cost" is useful. "Client being difficult" isn't.

## Declined coverage

When the agent offers a coverage or limit change and the client declines:

1. Log what was offered and that it was declined, with the date.
2. If the agency uses written declinations or signed rejection forms (some states require them for certain coverages), file the signed copy.
3. Send a short confirmation if the agency's process calls for one:
   > Thanks for talking today. As discussed, you've decided not to add [coverage] at this time. If anything changes, just let me know.

Wording and requirements vary by state and line. Use the agency's approved templates.

## File completeness checklist

- [ ] Current declarations pages and endorsements for every policy.
- [ ] Applications as submitted, with the client's confirmation of the facts.
- [ ] Certificates issued (commercial clients), with dates and holders.
- [ ] Communication log up to date.
- [ ] Review notes and question answers from the last review.
- [ ] Declinations and their confirmations.
- [ ] Claims history as recorded.

Run a quarterly sweep for stale contact details and missing documents.

## Data care

- Collect what the agency needs and no more. No full ID or account numbers in notes or chat.
- Keep client files in the agency's system, not personal email or messaging apps.
- Follow the agency's privacy policy and the applicable data protection law (for example, state privacy laws in the US, or UK GDPR in the UK).
- If a data breach is suspected, tell the agent immediately; there may be legal notification deadlines.

## Licensing (how it's checked)

Agents must be licensed for the lines they sell, in the places they sell.

- **US:** each state's insurance department licenses agents. The NAIC offers a public agent search that shows whether a person is licensed in a state and for which lines; the state's own lookup is the authority.
- **UK:** the FCA authorises firms. Its Firm Checker and Financial Services Register show whether a firm is authorised and for which activities.

The desk can track licence and continuing-education renewal dates on the radar. Whether an activity needs a licence is a compliance question for the agent.

## Common mistakes

- Logging only the outcome ("renewed") without what was discussed.
- Recording the desk's own interpretation as if the agent said it.
- No record of a declined offer.
- Client documents scattered across inboxes.

## Worked example

A client calls about a new home business. The log entry: date, "client reports starting a bakery business from home kitchen, selling at weekend markets", agent offered to review home policy and discuss business cover, client asked for a call next week, task filed for the agent with a due date. A week later, the follow-up is logged too, whatever the result.
