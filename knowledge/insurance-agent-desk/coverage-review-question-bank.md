# Coverage review question bank

These questions are for the agent to ask. The desk's job is to pick the right ones from the client's file, note the answers and flag them. Whether an answer means a coverage change is the agent's call.

## Life-change triggers

When any of these comes up in conversation or correspondence, log it in the client file and flag it to the agent as a review trigger:

- moved, bought, sold, or started renting out a home;
- renovation, extension, new pool, trampoline, solar panels, home office;
- new driver in the household (especially a teenager), a driver leaving, a new or sold vehicle;
- marriage, divorce, new baby, a dependant moving in or out;
- retirement, new job, started a side business or home business;
- valuable purchases or inheritances (jewellery, art, collections);
- new pet (some breeds affect liability questions);
- travel or living abroad for extended periods;
- for businesses: new premises, employees, vehicles, products, services, contracts requiring certificates, or a big change in revenue.

## Personal lines questions

**Home and contents**
1. Has anything changed about the house: work done, rooms added, systems replaced (roof, wiring, heating)?
2. Is anyone else living there, or is it rented out at any point, including short lets?
3. Is it ever empty for long periods?
4. Have you bought anything valuable since we last spoke?
5. Do you run any business or keep business equipment at home?
6. Do you know roughly what it would cost to rebuild today?

**Vehicles**
1. Who drives each car, and how often?
2. Any new drivers in the household, or anyone who's moved out?
3. Has use changed: commuting, business use, ride-share or delivery work?
4. Any accidents, claims or tickets since the last review?

**Liability and umbrella**
1. Any new exposures: pool, dog, trampoline, rental property, volunteer board roles?
2. Has your overall financial situation changed significantly?

**Life and income** (where the agent is licensed for these lines)
1. Any changes to dependants, mortgage or debts?
2. Any changes to employer benefits?

## Small business questions

1. Has revenue, payroll or headcount changed?
2. New locations, equipment, vehicles or stock levels?
3. New products, services or customer types?
4. Any contracts that require specific insurance certificates or limits?
5. Do you store customer data? Any changes to how?
6. Any incidents, near misses or complaints since the last review?

## Documented-gaps questions

Built from the file, not from opinion. Phrase as "the file shows…":

- "The file shows no flood or earthquake policy. Do you have one elsewhere?"
- "The file shows one vehicle; you mentioned your son's car. Is it insured elsewhere?"

## Recording the answers

For each question asked: the answer as given, the date, and whether the agent decided on a follow-up. If a client declines an offered change, record that too (see [Records and E&O hygiene](records-and-eo-hygiene.md)).

## Common mistakes

- Asking every question to every client. Pick from the file.
- The desk interpreting an answer as "you're covered" or "you're not". That's coverage advice.
- Not capturing "no change" answers. They're part of the record that the review happened.

## Worked example

The client file notes "mentioned kitchen renovation" in a March email. Ninety days before renewal the desk preps four home questions (work done, systems replaced, contents bought, rebuild cost) and one gaps question. The agent asks them; the client confirms a full kitchen refit. The agent decides to requote the dwelling limit. The desk logs the answers, files the requote task and adds the outcome to the renewal tracker.
