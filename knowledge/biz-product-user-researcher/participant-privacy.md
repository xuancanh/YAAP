# Participant privacy and consent

Participants give you their time and their stories. In return they get clear consent, minimal data collection, and quotes that can't be traced back to them.

**Jurisdiction note:** data protection and consent rules differ by country and region and change over time. Some jurisdictions require a specific legal basis for processing personal data, special handling for sensitive categories, or limits on transferring data abroad. This doc gives general good practice. The company's privacy policy and Legal decide what applies; check them before your first study and whenever you recruit in a new country.

## Before the session: consent

Get consent before any recording starts, and keep a record of it. The consent should say, in plain words:

- [ ] Who is running the study and for what purpose.
- [ ] What you will collect (notes, audio, video, screen recording).
- [ ] How it will be used (internal product decisions; not marketing unless separately agreed).
- [ ] Who will see it, and that quotes will be anonymised.
- [ ] Where it is stored and how long it is kept.
- [ ] That taking part is voluntary, they can skip any question, and stop at any time.
- [ ] How to ask for their data to be deleted.
- [ ] The incentive, and that it doesn't depend on what they say.

At the start of the session, restate it briefly and ask for a spoken yes before you press record. If they decline recording, take notes instead.

## Collect less

- Ask only for the personal data you need for recruiting and scheduling.
- Don't ask for sensitive information (health, finances, beliefs) unless the study requires it and Legal has approved.
- On screen shares, ask participants to close other windows; pause recording if personal information appears.
- Don't record what you won't use.

## Anonymise and separate

- Assign a participant code (P1, P2) at recruitment.
- Keep one **key file** linking codes to identities, stored only where the company allows, with restricted access.
- Everything else (notes, synthesis, reports, the repository) uses codes only.
- In quotes, remove or generalise names, company names, colleagues' names, locations and anything distinctive. "At my company, the CFO…" is fine; "At <named company>, <named person>…" is not.
- Video and audio clips are identifying by nature; share them only within the team and only as the consent allows.

## Store and retain

- Store recordings and the key file in the company's approved location, not in personal drives, chat threads or shared notes.
- Set a retention period in the research plan and delete raw recordings when it ends. Keep the anonymised synthesis.
- Honour deletion requests promptly: remove the raw data and the key-file entry; anonymised insights already aggregated can usually stay, but confirm with the company's policy.

## Recruiting and incentives

- Recruiting messages and incentives are drafts for human approval unless you have been granted to send them.
- Describe the study honestly: time needed, topic, format, incentive.
- Don't recruit employees or close contacts of the product team for user research, unless that is the study's point.
- Pay the incentive whatever the participant said. Tax and gift rules for incentives vary by country; follow Finance's guidance.
- Be careful with customers in an active sales or support situation; coordinate with the account owner so the study doesn't interfere.

## Sharing findings

- Share syntheses and anonymised quotes widely; share raw notes and recordings narrowly.
- Before posting a quote in a channel, re-read it as if you were the participant.
- If someone outside the team asks for a participant's identity (for example, to sell to them), the answer is no unless the participant agreed to be contacted.

## Quick pre-study checklist

- [ ] Consent text approved and matches what you'll record.
- [ ] Storage location and retention period in the plan.
- [ ] Key file created with restricted access.
- [ ] Recruiting message and incentive approved.
- [ ] Note template uses participant codes.
