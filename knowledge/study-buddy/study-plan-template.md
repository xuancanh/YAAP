# Study plan template

A plan that assumes perfect weeks fails in week two. Start from the hours the user really has, put review and mocks in first, and keep a buffer.

## Method

1. **Fix the end.** Exam date (or goal date), format, and what "good enough" means (a grade, a pass).
2. **Inventory the content.** List topics from the official syllabus or exam specification, not the textbook's chapter list. Rate each: new / shaky / solid.
3. **Count honest hours.** Ask for a typical week, then subtract: work, classes, commuting, family, sleep, one free evening. Use the number they'll actually do, not the one they hope for.
4. **Reserve the end.** The final two to three weeks are for mixed practice and full mocks (see [final weeks checklist](final-weeks-checklist.md)). Block them first.
5. **Keep a buffer.** Leave roughly one session in five unassigned for catch-up.
6. **Allocate the rest.** New and shaky topics get more time; heavily weighted topics in the exam get more time. Interleave: each week covers new material plus review of earlier topics.
7. **Daily review.** A short daily slot for flashcards keeps spacing going (see [flashcard rules](flashcard-writing-rules.md)).

## Template

```
Subject:            Exam date:          Format:
Target:             Hours per week (honest):

Topic inventory
| Topic | Exam weight | Status (new/shaky/solid) | Sessions needed |

Weekly plan
| Week | Dates | New topics | Review topics | Practice / quiz | Buffer used? |

Final weeks (from T-21):
- Mocks on: ...
- Weak-spot sessions: ...

Re-plan check every: [Sunday]
```

## Worked example

Exam on 15 March; it's 4 January. Ten weeks. The user has work and can honestly do 6 hours a week: three 90-minute evening sessions and one 90-minute Saturday slot, plus 10 minutes of flashcards on weekdays.

- Weeks 8–10 (from about 22 Feb): mocks and mixed practice. Reserved.
- Weeks 1–7: 28 sessions. Hold back about 5 as buffer, leaving 23 for content.
- Twelve topics: 4 new, 5 shaky, 3 solid. Weighting: new topics 3 sessions each (12), shaky 2 each (10), solid 1 between them as review (1). Total 23.
- Each week: two sessions on new or shaky material, one mixed review, Saturday a timed practice set.

## Re-planning

When a week goes wrong, don't stack the missed work onto next week. Instead:

1. Use the buffer first.
2. If the buffer is gone, cut from the solid topics, never from the reserved final weeks.
3. If still short, talk about the target honestly: fewer topics done well can beat everything done badly, and some exams allow choice of questions.

Re-plan weekly, in five minutes: what got done, what slipped, what changes.

## Signs a plan is unrealistic

- More hours than the user did in any week last month.
- No rest day.
- Every session is "new material", with no review or practice.
- The final two weeks are still covering new topics.

## Common mistakes

- Planning by chapters instead of the exam specification.
- Leaving past papers until the last week.
- Treating the plan as a promise and quitting when it breaks. It's a tool, adjust it.
