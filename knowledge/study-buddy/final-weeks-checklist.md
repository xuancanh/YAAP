# Final weeks checklist

From about three weeks out, the job changes: less new material, more realistic practice, fix the weak spots that cost the most marks, and protect sleep and logistics.

## Weeks 3 and 2: mocks and triage

**Mock routine**

- Use real past papers or official sample papers where they exist.
- Exam conditions: timed, no notes, same tools allowed as the real exam, phone away.
- Mark with the official mark scheme. Be strict.
- Two or three full mocks across these two weeks is a reasonable pattern; space them out so there's time to fix errors in between.

**Error log**

After each mock, log every lost mark:

| Question | Topic | Marks lost | Type of error | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q4b | Integration by parts | 3 | Method not known | Re-learn, 3 practice questions |
| Q7 | Essay structure | 4 | Ran out of time | Timed paragraph drills |
| Q2 | Units | 1 | Careless | Add "check units" to the final-check list |

Error types: **didn't know**, **misunderstood the question**, **method error**, **careless slip**, **time**.

**Triage:** rank fixes by marks recoverable per hour. A topic that appears every year and costs 6 marks beats a rare topic worth 2. Careless errors are often fixed fastest, with a personal checklist.

## Week 1: consolidate

- Mixed practice of the weak spots; short timed sets
- Flashcard reviews continue daily
- One last full mock early in the week, not the night before
- No big new topics. If a topic is still unknown, learn the basics needed for the most common question type and move on

## Logistics checklist

- Exam date, time and **location** confirmed (room, building, campus or test centre)
- Travel route and time, with a backup plan
- ID, entry ticket or candidate number, as required
- Allowed equipment: calculator model, pens, ruler, clear pencil case, water bottle (check the rules)
- Access arrangements confirmed in writing, if any
- What's banned: phones, smartwatches, notes
- Online exams: tech check, ID check, room rules

## The last two days

- Light review only: flashcards, the error-log checklist, formula sheet
- Normal sleep schedule; avoid an all-nighter, which tends to hurt recall more than it helps
- Prepare the bag the night before
- Plan a relaxing evening

## On the day

- Eat something, arrive early
- Read all instructions and the whole paper first if allowed; plan time per question by marks
- Do the questions you know first
- If stuck, move on and come back
- Leave five minutes for the personal final-check list (units, names, question numbers)

## After each exam

Don't do a post-mortem with friends if it's going to rattle you before the next paper. Note anything to remember for the next exam, then move on.

## When to suggest support

Exam stress is normal; some stress helps focus. Suggest the user talks to someone (a tutor, the school or university counselling or wellbeing service, or their doctor) if they mention:

- not sleeping or eating for several days;
- panic attacks, or being unable to start studying at all;
- feeling hopeless or worthless.

If they say they're thinking of harming themselves or feel unsafe, urge them to contact emergency services or a crisis line now (for example 988 in the US, or Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland).

## Common mistakes

- Learning brand-new topics the night before.
- Mocks without marking, or marking generously.
- Ignoring the error log and re-doing comfortable topics.
