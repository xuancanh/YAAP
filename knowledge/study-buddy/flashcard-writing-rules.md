# Flashcard writing rules

Spaced repetition only works if each card is quick to answer and tests one thing. Most bad decks fail on card design, not on the schedule. These rules draw on Wozniak's widely used "Twenty rules of formulating knowledge".

## The core rules

1. **Understand before you memorise.** A card for something not yet understood becomes a string of words to memorise. Explain the concept first (see [learning techniques](learning-techniques.md)).
2. **One fact per card (minimum information).** If the answer has "and" in it, it's probably two cards.
3. **Short answers.** A few words, ideally. Long answers get half-remembered and marked right anyway.
4. **Use cloze deletions** for facts in context: "The mitochondrion produces most of the cell's {{ATP}}."
5. **No lists.** "Name the seven…" cards fail repeatedly. Break the list up, or use overlapping clozes (one item hidden per card).
6. **Add context cues** to avoid confusion: "(biology) ATP stands for…" if the deck spans subjects.
7. **Both directions only when needed.** Vocabulary often needs both (word → meaning, meaning → word); most facts don't.
8. **Use images** where they help: a diagram with one label hidden.
9. **Personal examples** make cards memorable: "Opportunity cost: e.g. the £30 concert I skipped to buy the textbook."
10. **Source and date** in a note field for facts that change (laws, prices, statistics).

## Before and after

| Bad card | Problem | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Q: Describe the causes of World War I. | Essay, not a card | Several cards: "Assassination that triggered WWI: {{Archduke Franz Ferdinand}}, 1914"; "Alliance system: France, Russia and Britain formed the {{Triple Entente}}" |
| Q: What are the 7 SI base units? | List | Seven cards: "SI base unit of electric current: {{ampere}}" |
| Q: Photosynthesis? | Vague | "Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide and water and releases {{oxygen}}" |
| Q: What is the formula for the area of a circle and what's r? | Two facts | "Area of a circle: {{πr²}}"; "In πr², r is the {{radius}}" |

## A simple review schedule (Leitner boxes)

If the user has no app, use boxes:

| Box | Review |
|---|---|
| 1 | every session |
| 2 | every 2–3 days |
| 3 | weekly |
| 4 | every 2 weeks |
| 5 | monthly |

Right answer: move the card up one box. Wrong: back to box 1. The intervals expand as the card gets easier; that's the point. Apps (Anki and others) do this automatically with their own algorithms.

## Grading honestly

- Say or write the answer **before** flipping.
- "Almost" is wrong. Partial recall means the card needs work or splitting.
- If a card fails again and again (a "leech"), don't keep drilling it. Rewrite it: split it, add a cue, add an example, or go back and understand the concept.

## Keeping the deck healthy

- Add cards the same day you learn something, while the context is fresh.
- Cap new cards per day so reviews don't pile up; reviews grow with every new card.
- Delete or suspend cards for things no longer needed.
- After a mock exam, make cards from every mistake.

## Common mistakes

- Copying textbook paragraphs onto cards.
- Making hundreds of cards in a weekend, then quitting when reviews explode.
- Marking cards right because "I knew it really".
