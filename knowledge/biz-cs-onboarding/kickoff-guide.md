# Kickoff guide

A good kickoff ends with three things written down: the first value milestone, the people who own each part and the dates. Everything else is supporting detail.

## Pre-work (before the meeting)

- [ ] Read the sales handoff: goals, promised scope, stakeholders, any special terms
- [ ] Confirm scope against the contract; list any gaps for your manager
- [ ] Draft the first value milestone (below) so the customer reacts to something concrete
- [ ] Draft a timeline from the standard plan for this product and segment
- [ ] Ask the customer who should attend: a sponsor, a project owner, a technical owner and someone from each team that will use the product
- [ ] Send a short agenda and the pre-read a few days ahead

## Agenda

1. **Introductions and roles** — who owns what on each side
2. **Why they bought** — ask the sponsor to say it in their own words; check it matches the handoff
3. **First value milestone** — agree the definition, metric and target date
4. **Plan walkthrough** — phases, key steps, dependencies on their side (data, access, IT approvals)
5. **Risks** — what could slow us down; known blackout dates, holidays, other projects
6. **Ways of working** — meeting cadence, where the plan lives, how to escalate
7. **Next steps** — actions with owners and dates, read back before closing

Spend most of the time on 3 and 4. If the sponsor leaves early, get agreement on 3 before they go.

## First value milestone template

```
Outcome: what the customer can do that they could not before
Who: the team or role that gets it
Measured by: the metric and how it is read (their system or ours)
Baseline: today's value, if known
Target: the value that counts as reached
Target date: when
Agreed by: customer owner name, date
```

Example: "The support team handles all email tickets in the new tool, measured by the share of tickets created there in a week; baseline zero; target all email tickets; by the end of week four; agreed by the support lead."

Good milestones are small, real and soon. "Full rollout across all regions" is a goal for the CSM; first value is the first proof it works.

## Stakeholder roles to fill

| Role | Why you need them |
|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | unblocks priorities and budget; confirms value |
| Project owner | runs their side day to day |
| Technical owner | access, integrations, security approvals |
| Team leads | drive adoption in each user group |

If any role is empty after the kickoff, record it as a risk.

## After the kickoff (same day)

- Send the summary: milestone, plan link, owners, next meeting
- Put the plan in the shared location with every step owned and dated
- Log the kickoff in the account file
- Book the next check-in

## Pitfalls

- **Discovery again.** The customer already explained their needs to sales. Confirm, do not re-ask everything.
- **Our plan, not theirs.** Customer-side tasks without customer owners will not happen.
- **Vague milestone.** If you cannot measure it, you cannot tell when you are done.
- **Overpromising dates.** Base dates on the customer's real capacity, not on the ideal plan.
