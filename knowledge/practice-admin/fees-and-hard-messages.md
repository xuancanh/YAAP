# Fee conversations and hard messages

Money and complaints go better when they're predictable: the same clear wording every time, approved once by the practitioner. Billing, insurance and consumer-credit rules vary by country and payer, so the practice owner confirms policy; admin applies it.

## Principles

- **Tell people the cost before, not after.** Surprises cause most billing disputes.
- **Say what's included and what isn't.** An estimate is not a quote if things can change; say so.
- **Kind and firm.** Assume an honest mistake first. Escalate in steps.
- **No health detail.** Invoices and reminders name the service type generically.

## Fee estimate template

> Hi [first name], thanks for booking with [Practice]. Your [appointment type] on [date] costs [amount]. [If relevant: This is an estimate; if the practitioner recommends anything extra, they'll discuss the cost with you first.] Payment is due [at the visit / within X days] by [methods]. Our cancellation policy: [one line].

Insurance and public-scheme billing: tell the patient what the practice will submit and what they may owe, and that coverage is decided by their insurer, not the practice.

## Overdue balance ladder

1. **Friendly reminder** a short time after the due date: "Our records show [amount] for your visit on [date] is still open. You can pay at [link]. If you've already paid, thank you, and please ignore this."
2. **Second reminder** a few weeks later: same facts, offer a payment plan if the practice allows one, and a named contact.
3. **Phone call** from the practice manager or practitioner.
4. **Final notice** stating what happens next (per the practice's written policy).

Stop and hand to the owner when a patient disputes the charge, mentions hardship, or a collections agency or write-off is being considered. Collections are regulated in many places.

## Policy-change notice

Lead with what's changing and when, then why, then what the patient needs to do.

> From [date], our late-cancellation fee will be [amount] for appointments cancelled with less than [notice] notice. We're making this change because late cancellations leave slots empty that other patients are waiting for. If you have any questions, call us on [number].

Give reasonable notice and put it everywhere: website, confirmations, a sign at the desk.

## Replying to a public review

The rule: never confirm the person is a patient or mention anything about their visit, even if they did.

**Positive review**

> Thank you for taking the time to leave a review. We're glad to hear it, and we'll pass your kind words on to the team.

**Negative review**

> Thank you for your feedback. We take every comment seriously, but we can't discuss anyone's care in a public forum. Please call our practice manager, [name], on [number] so we can talk it through.

Don't argue, correct facts or explain policies publicly. Log the review, tell the practitioner, and follow up privately if the person calls.

## Difficult one-to-one messages

For a patient who is rude to staff, repeatedly misses appointments, or is being discharged from the practice: draft only, for the practitioner's decision. Keep it factual (dates, policy), respectful, and include what the patient can do next. Professional bodies often have rules on discharging patients; the practitioner checks them.

## Common mistakes

- Sending a fee reminder with the treatment name in it.
- Arguing with a reviewer.
- Inventing a payment plan the owner never approved.
- Threatening collections before the written policy says so.
