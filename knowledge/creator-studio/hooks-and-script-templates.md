# Hooks and script templates

The hook earns attention; the structure keeps it. Draft the hook first, in several different angles, before writing the body.

## Hook angles

Write three or four genuinely different angles, not rewordings of one:

| Angle | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Raise a question the piece answers | "Why this cheap knife outlasted my expensive one" |
| Bold claim | A clear position you can back up | "You don't need a budget app. You need one rule." |
| Specific number | Concrete detail signals substance | "I tested 7 sourdough starters for 30 days" |
| Mistake / enemy | Name a common error | "The editing habit that's killing your retention" |
| Story start | Drop into a moment | "The client emailed at 2am: 'We need to talk.'" |

The line between a strong hook and clickbait: does the content deliver what the hook promises? If not, change the hook.

## Long video skeleton

```
0:00  Hook: the promise, in one or two sentences (and on screen)
0:15  Stakes: why it matters to this viewer
0:30  Quick proof you can deliver (credential, result, demo)
      Segment 1 → end with a tease of segment 2
      Segment 2 → tease segment 3
      ...
      Payoff: deliver the promise fully
      Close: one next step (another video, subscribe, newsletter)
```

Plant the next tease before each segment ends. Viewers often leave at the end of a segment.

## Short-form skeleton

```
Second 0-1: hook (visual and spoken/text together)
Body: one idea, fast, with a visual change every few seconds
End: a line that loops back to the start, or a clear payoff
```

## Newsletter skeleton

```
Subject line: specific and honest (draft 3 variants)
Preview text: extends the subject, doesn't repeat it
Opening: one or two lines into the idea
Body: one main idea; skimmable subheads; short paragraphs
Close: a line worth forwarding, and one call to action
```

One idea per issue. Several ideas usually means several issues.

## Keeping the creator's voice

Keep a voice file:

```
Phrases they use: ___
Phrases they'd never say: ___
Tone: ___ (e.g. dry, warm, nerdy)
Typical sentence length: ___
How they open and close: ___
```

Draft from their transcripts and past scripts. A script that sounds like someone else loses what makes the channel theirs.

## Pre-publish check

- [ ] Title, thumbnail and opening line promise the same thing.
- [ ] The promise is delivered, and not only in the last 10 seconds.
- [ ] Length fits the idea. An 8-minute idea stretched to 15 shows up as a drop in the retention graph.
- [ ] Every claim is accurate; numbers sourced.
- [ ] Any brand connection disclosed in the content itself (see [sponsored content disclosure](sponsored-content-disclosure.md)).
- [ ] Music, clips and images cleared for use.
- [ ] Captions or subtitles checked.

## After publishing

Add a performance line to the script file: views at 7 days, average view duration, where the retention graph drops (with timestamp). Over time, this shows which hooks and structures work for this audience.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the hook last.
- Long intros: "Hey guys, welcome back, before we start…"
- Thumbnail and title that tell different stories.
- Saving all value for the end.
