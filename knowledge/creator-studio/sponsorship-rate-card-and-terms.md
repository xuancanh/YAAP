# Sponsorship rate card and terms

Creators lose most money in sponsorships not on the base fee, but on the extras given away for free: usage rights, exclusivity, unlimited revisions and rush timelines.

## Rate card

```
Audience (as of [date]):
  Platform | Followers/subscribers | Typical views per piece (median, last 10) | Engagement notes
Deliverables and rates:
  Integration (60-90s in a video): floor ___ · target ___
  Dedicated piece: floor ___ · target ___
  Short-form post: floor ___ · target ___
  Newsletter slot: floor ___ · target ___
  Bundle (e.g. video + 2 shorts + newsletter): ___
Premiums (priced separately):
  Paid usage rights (brand runs it as an ad): per month, per platform
  Exclusivity (no competitors): per month, per category
  Rush (under ___ days): +___
  Extra revision rounds beyond ___: ___ each
Market research: [sources, dated]
```

Base the floor on the creator's own numbers (median views, not best-ever) and on dated market research. Market rates vary widely by niche and platform, so research for the specific niche rather than using generic rules.

## Qualifying a brand

- [ ] Would the creator use or recommend this product honestly?
- [ ] Does it fit the audience? (A bad fit costs trust.)
- [ ] Is the brand reputable? Quick check for complaints or regulatory problems.
- [ ] Clear budget and timeline?
- [ ] Will they agree to proper disclosure?

## Contract red flags

Flag these for a lawyer's review before signing. Don't treat this list as legal advice.

- **Perpetual or unlimited usage rights**, or rights "in all media now known or later developed".
- **Broad exclusivity:** a whole category, long duration, or not paid for.
- **Unlimited revisions** or approval rights with no deadline.
- **Payment on performance** (paid only if views or sales hit a number) presented as a flat fee.
- **Long payment terms** or payment only after the brand's own approval with no deadline.
- **Content ownership transfer:** the brand owns the video.
- **Morality clauses** that are vague and one-sided.
- **No kill fee** if the brand cancels after work starts.
- Anything asking the creator to skip or hide disclosure.

## Negotiation drafts

Counter with evidence:

> "Thanks for the offer. My median views per video over the last ten uploads are [n], and my rate for an integration is [target]. Happy to discuss a bundle if that helps the budget."

Scope boundaries:

> "Rate includes one round of revisions on the script and one on the edit. Usage: organic posting on my channels only. Paid usage is available at [rate] per month per platform."

Exclusivity:

> "I can offer category exclusivity for [n] days around publication at [rate]. Longer periods are priced per month."

Agree the walk-away number with the creator before the call.

## Deal pipeline

```
Inquiry → Qualified → Negotiating → Contracted → Delivering → Invoiced → Paid
```

Per deal:

```
Brand | Contact | Deliverables & dates | Fee | Usage | Exclusivity | Disclosure confirmed | Approvals log | Invoice date | Paid date
```

## After the deal

- Send the brand a short results summary (the numbers they asked for, honestly).
- Note whether the sponsor is a good renewal candidate.
- Quarterly: revenue by sponsor type, which deals were worth it, and whether demand supports a rate rise.

## Common mistakes

- Quoting a single number with no premiums.
- Agreeing to usage rights in a reply email.
- Delivering before the contract is signed.
- No invoice-chasing schedule.
