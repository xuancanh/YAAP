# Monthly analytics review

The point of the review is one or two decisions for next month, not a tour of every metric.

## Per-piece log

```
Piece | Date | Format/pillar | Views (7d) | Avg view duration or open rate | Retention drop points (timestamps) | New subscribers | Traffic sources (top 3) | Notes
```

Work from what the creator exports or tells you. Note the date of each export; platforms revise numbers and change definitions.

## Reading a retention graph

Retention shows what share of viewers are still watching at each point. Look for:

- **The first 30 seconds:** a steep early drop often means the opening didn't match the title or thumbnail.
- **Sudden cliffs:** match the timestamp to the script. A long tangent, a sponsor read, or a slow section usually sits there.
- **Bumps:** viewers rewinding or skipping to a part. That part is valuable; consider leading with it.
- **The end:** a drop before the payoff can mean the payoff came too late.

Compare graphs within the creator's own channel and format. Different formats and lengths have different shapes.

## The monthly review

```
Month: ___   Pieces published: ___   Compared with: trailing 3-month average

Three signals:
1. ___ (e.g. tutorial pillar views up against average; 4 of 4 above)
   Likely cause: ___
2. ___ (e.g. recurring drop at the 2-minute mark in long videos)
   Likely cause: ___
3. ___

What's working (always included): ___

One experiment for next month:
  Change: ___ (one variable)
  Applies to: ___ (which pieces)
  Success looks like: ___ (metric and threshold, decided now)
  Decide after: ___ (number of pieces or weeks)
```

## Running experiments properly

- **One change at a time.** Change the thumbnail style *or* the video length, not both.
- **Decide the success metric before publishing.** Otherwise any result looks like a win.
- **Enough data.** A couple of pieces rarely decide anything; topic and timing vary too much. Run the change across several pieces.
- **Record the verdict,** even if the result is "no clear difference".

```
Experiment log: Date | Change | Pieces | Metric | Result | Verdict | Keep / drop / retest
```

## Quarterly platform check

- Share of reach from each platform, trended.
- If one platform's recommendation system provides most of the audience, that's a business risk: a change there can cut reach overnight.
- Owned channels (email list, community) are the audience the creator actually keeps. Track their growth.

## Keeping it healthy

- Compare with the creator's own trailing average, never with other creators.
- One bad piece is noise; look at the trend across several.
- Always end the review with what's working.
- If the numbers are causing stress, review less often. Monthly is enough for most decisions.

## Worked example

Signal: long videos lose a large share of viewers around 2:00, in five of six videos. Script check: that's where the creator does a channel update. Experiment: move updates to the end for the next four long videos. Success: smaller drop at 2:00 and higher average view duration than the trailing average. Decide after four videos.

## Common mistakes

- Twenty metrics and no decision.
- Changing three things after one bad week.
- Judging a new format on its first upload.
- Ignoring the comments, which often explain the graph.
