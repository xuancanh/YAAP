# Sponsored content disclosure

The principle is the same in most places: if a brand connection could affect how the audience sees a recommendation, tell them clearly, where they'll see it. Rules and enforcement differ by country and platform, and they change. This summary was checked on 2026-11-04; check the current guidance for the creator's audience, and ask a lawyer about anything unusual.

## When to disclose (US FTC guidance)

Disclose any financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand. That includes:

- Payment of any kind.
- Free or discounted products, even if you didn't ask for them.
- Affiliate links or commission.
- Trips, event access or other perks.
- Being an employee, or a friend or family member of the brand owner.

Disclose even if you think your opinion is unbiased. Likes, tags and pins can count as endorsements. US guidance can apply to creators outside the US if their posts reach US consumers. You don't need to disclose that you have *no* relationship.

## How to disclose

**Placement:** with the endorsement itself, where people will notice it. Disclosures likely to be missed include ones only on a profile or "about" page, at the end of a post, behind a "more" link, or mixed into a pile of hashtags.

**Words:** simple and clear: "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Thanks to [brand] for the free product", "[Brand] partner". Avoid vague or abbreviated labels such as "sp", "spon", "collab", or a bare "thanks". Use the same language as the post.

**Video:** disclose in the video itself, on screen and spoken. The description alone isn't enough.

**Livestreams:** repeat the disclosure periodically for people joining late.

**Images and Stories:** put the disclosure on the image, readable and on screen long enough to read.

**Platform tools:** a platform's "paid partnership" label may help, but the FTC says not to assume it's enough on its own. Add your own clear disclosure.

## Honesty rules that go with disclosure

From the FTC guidance:

- Don't talk about products you haven't tried.
- If you were paid and didn't like the product, don't say it's great.
- Don't make claims you can't back up, such as health benefits.

## United Kingdom

The ASA (advertising regulator) and the CMA (competition and consumer authority) publish a joint influencers' guide; its fourth edition (October 2026) adds material on fake reviews and the CMA's enforcement powers under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act. The UK approach expects ads to be obviously identifiable as ads, labelled upfront. Read the current guide for the label wording and the treatment of gifts and affiliate links.

## Other countries

Many countries have similar rules through their advertising standards bodies or consumer authorities. Look up the creator's main audience markets.

## Checklist per sponsored piece

- [ ] Relationship type recorded (paid, gifted, affiliate, other).
- [ ] Disclosure written into the script at the start of the integration.
- [ ] On-screen text and spoken disclosure in video.
- [ ] Disclosure visible without clicking "more" in captions.
- [ ] Platform's branded-content tool switched on where available, in addition to the above.
- [ ] Claims in the brand's talking points checked; unsupported claims removed.
- [ ] Final piece checked before publishing.

## Common mistakes

- "#ad" buried after ten other hashtags.
- Disclosing in the description of a video but not in the video.
- Assuming gifted products don't count.
- Letting the brand's script make claims the creator can't verify.
