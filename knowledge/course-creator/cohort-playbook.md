# Cohort playbook

Cohorts usually lose people between sessions, not during them. The rhythm below keeps the space between sessions busy enough that people stay connected.

## Before launch day (1-2 weeks out)

- [ ] Session calendar with dates, times (with time zones), links, and each session's objective.
- [ ] Platform set up: where materials, replays and discussion live.
- [ ] Welcome message drafted and scheduled.
- [ ] Roster file: name, time zone, goal, attendance, exercise submissions, notes.

## The weekly rhythm

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2 days before session | Reminder with the prep task ("bring your draft brief") |
| Session day | Live session: short teaching, most time on doing and feedback |
| Within 24 hours | Replay link and a recap: three key points, this week's exercise, due date |
| Midweek | Check-in prompt in the community ("Share one line from your draft") |
| Before next session | Office hours or async feedback on exercises |

## Message templates

**Welcome**

> Welcome to [course]! Here's what to expect: [n] sessions on [day, time, time zone], each with a short exercise. Most people spend about [hours] a week. Start here: introduce yourself in [place] with your name, where you're based, and the one result you want from this course. See you on [date].

**Session reminder**

> [Session N] is [day, time]. We'll cover [topic]. Before then: [prep task, 15 minutes]. Link: [url].

**Recap**

> Thanks for today. Three things to remember: 1) … 2) … 3) … This week: [exercise], due [date]. Replay: [link].

## Spotting and helping quiet learners

Watch the roster. At two missed sessions or two missed exercises, send a personal note:

> Hi [name], I noticed the last couple of weeks got busy. No catch-up needed: the shortest way back is [one lesson] and [one exercise]. If something about the course isn't working for you, tell me. I'd like to fix it.

Keep it warm and short, with one easy step back. No guilt.

## Question log

Log every question with the lesson it relates to.

```
Date | Learner | Lesson | Question | Answered where
```

Three people stuck on the same lesson means the lesson needs work, not the learners. Turn repeat questions into an FAQ for the next cohort.

## Running a good live session

- Start on time; a short warm-up question in chat.
- Teach for a short block, then put people to work: breakout groups, live exercise, hot seats.
- Leave time for questions. Answer the most common, collect the rest for the recap.
- End with the exercise and its due date.

## End-of-cohort debrief

```
Cohort: ___   Dates: ___   Enrolled: ___   Completed: ___
Attendance by session: ___
Exercises completed by module: ___
Sessions that ran long: ___
Lessons with the most questions: ___
Learner wins (with permission to share?): ___
Refunds and reasons: ___
Changes for next cohort: ___
```

Send a short feedback survey on the last day, with the testimonial question built in (see [pricing and testimonials](pricing-and-testimonials.md)).

## Common mistakes

- No contact between sessions.
- Replays posted days late.
- Spending live time on content that could be a video.
- Waiting until the end to ask for feedback.
