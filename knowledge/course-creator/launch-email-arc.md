# Launch email arc and claims check

## Timeline (work backwards from cart close)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 6-8 weeks before | Course finished or a firm delivery plan; sales page draft; testimonials gathered |
| 4-6 weeks | Teach-first content: free lessons, posts or a workshop that show the teaching |
| 2-4 weeks | Waitlist push; FAQ drafted from real questions |
| Cart open (day 1) | Email arc begins |
| Cart open + 5-10 days | Close |
| After close | Welcome sequence; debrief |

Pre-selling an unfinished course is a real risk to buyers. If you do it, say so plainly, give the delivery dates, and have a refund plan if you miss them.

## The email arc

1. **Story:** why this course exists, the problem you kept seeing, who it's for.
2. **Proof:** a few specific student results, with permission and context.
3. **Objections:** the real hesitations answered directly: time, level, "will it work for my situation?", price.
4. **Logistics:** what's inside, schedule, price and payment options, refund policy, who it's not for.
5. **Close (two emails):** the deadline, why it's real, and a short reminder on the last day.

**Deadlines:** use real ones (a cohort start date, a price that genuinely goes up). Fake countdown timers and "last chance" emails followed by reopening damage trust, and fake urgency can breach consumer law in some places.

## Claims check (run on every page and email)

This is general information, current as of 2026-11-04, and rules differ by country. For income claims, guarantees or anything you're unsure about, get legal advice.

**Results claims**

- [ ] Every result claimed is one the course can deliver to the stated learner.
- [ ] Testimonials reflect honest experiences of real students.
- [ ] If a testimonial shows unusual results, the typical result is stated clearly. US FTC guidance says that without proof an endorser's experience is typical, the ad must make clear what results people can generally expect.

**Earnings and income claims**

- [ ] Avoid them unless you have solid evidence of what typical students earn. Regulators treat income claims as high-risk.
- [ ] No "replace your salary" style promises; no lifestyle imagery implying income you can't substantiate.

**Testimonials and reviews**

- [ ] Paid, gifted or discounted testimonials are disclosed (the FTC says a free product usually must be disclosed).
- [ ] No buying reviews and no fake testimonials. The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (effective 21 October 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials, buying positive or negative reviews, and some review suppression.
- [ ] Testimonials used only within the permission recorded.

**Guarantees and refunds**

- [ ] The refund policy is written once and shown before purchase: what it covers, the time window, conditions.
- [ ] Conditions are realistic; a guarantee nobody can claim misleads.
- [ ] Local consumer rights may give buyers rights beyond your policy (for example, cancellation periods for online purchases in the EU and UK, with rules on digital content).

**UK note:** the ASA enforces the CAP code on advertising, including testimonials and claims that must be backed by evidence.

## Launch week operations

- Daily: answer questions, update the FAQ, note numbers (visits, sign-ups, refunds).
- Pre-agree adjustments (an extra proof email, a payment plan) so decisions are calm.

## Debrief

Funnel numbers, which email converted best, refund rate and reasons, and the questions you didn't anticipate.
