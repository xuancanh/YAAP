# Pricing and testimonials

## Pricing worksheet

```
The change the course delivers: ___
Comparable courses (date researched): [name, price, format, depth] × 3-5
Delivery cost per student: recorded ___ · cohort ___ (your hours × your rate)
Price story in one line: ___
```

Price against the value of the result and the market, then check that your delivery time doesn't make a tier unprofitable. Recorded courses scale; cohorts and coaching cost your hours per student.

## Tiers that differ for real

| Tier | What's added | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Self-paced | The course and resources | Self-starters on a budget |
| Cohort | Live sessions, feedback on exercises, community | People who need structure and feedback |
| High-touch | One-to-one time, review of real work | People who want it done right, fast |

Each tier should add something a buyer can name. If a middle tier exists only to make another look good, drop it.

## Payment options

- **Pay in full:** often with a small saving.
- **Instalments:** more buyers can afford it; plan for some missed payments, and state what happens if a payment fails.
- **Regional pricing:** if you offer it, make it a published policy rather than one-off deals in private messages.

## Discount policy (write once)

```
Launch price: ___ (until ___)
Evergreen price: ___
Alumni/returning: ___
Never below: ___
Who can approve exceptions: ___
```

Random discounts teach your audience to wait for the next sale.

## Reviewing price

After each launch: conversion by tier, refund reasons, and demand versus capacity. If cohorts sell out and results are strong, raising the price for the next cohort is reasonable; say so ahead of time.

## Testimonials: when to ask

Ask when the win happens, not at an arbitrary date:

- A learner posts a result.
- Someone finishes the hardest module.
- The mid-course check-in ("What's changed so far?").
- The completion survey.

## The question set

1. What was going on before you joined?
2. What almost stopped you from signing up?
3. What specific result have you had? (Numbers or examples, if they're comfortable.)
4. What would you tell someone on the fence?
5. Anything else?

Question 2 is gold: the answers address real objections on the sales page.

## Permission record

```
Name | Date | Exact text or summary approved | Use: name? photo? company? | Edit for length? | Where used | Any incentive given?
```

- Use a testimonial only within what was granted.
- For verbal praise (a call, a chat), write your paraphrase and send it for approval before using it.
- If you gave anything in return (a discount, free access), disclose it alongside the testimonial. See the claims check in [launch email arc](launch-email-arc.md).
- Remove testimonials if the person asks.

## Organizing the library

Tag each testimonial by:

- **Objection it answers:** time, level, price, "will it work for me".
- **Outcome type:** first client, finished project, confidence, skill.
- **Learner type:** beginner, career-changer, experienced.

Then each launch email and sales-page section pulls the right proof.

## Case studies

For standout learners, offer a short interview and write up before, during and after. Send it for their approval. One detailed, honest story often persuades more than many short quotes.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing by what feels comfortable rather than the result.
- Tiers that differ only by bonuses nobody uses.
- Using a screenshot of a private message without permission.
- Showcasing only the best results without saying what's typical.
