# Client data security

Tax data is a prime target for identity theft and refund fraud. In the US, paid preparers have legal duties to protect it; IRS Publication 4557 explains them, including the need for a written information security plan (WISP), and Publication 5708 gives a template. Other countries have their own data-protection laws (for example UK GDPR). The practice owner owns the security plan; these are the desk's habits within it.

## What never enters this desk's files

- Tax ID numbers (SSN, ITIN, EIN, UK National Insurance or UTR numbers)
- Bank, card or investment account numbers
- Copies of ID documents
- Login credentials, PINs, IP PINs
- Amounts from documents (track **states**, not contents; see [client document checklists](client-document-checklists.md))

Use client codes. The practice's secure system is the record.

## Document exchange

- Clients upload through the practice's secure portal, not email.
- Outgoing documents with personal data go through the portal or encrypted email, per the security plan.
- If a client emails documents anyway: file them securely, delete per policy, and gently redirect them to the portal.
- Never send documents to a new email address or phone number without confirming the change through a known channel.

## Phishing and impersonation checks

Criminals target practices in tax season, posing as clients, new clients or the tax authority.

Red flags:

- A "new client" who sends an attachment or link before any engagement
- A client asking to change their bank details for a refund, or the address for documents
- Urgent requests from an unknown sender, or a familiar name with a slightly wrong email address
- Messages claiming to be the tax authority asking for credentials or payment by email

Habits:

- Don't open unexpected attachments; verify by phone using a number already on file.
- Confirm any change to client contact or banking details by calling the client on the known number, and get preparer approval.
- Report suspicious messages per the security plan.

## Everyday basics (owner sets the policy)

- Multi-factor authentication on email, portal and tax software
- Screen lock when away from the desk; clean desk for paper files
- Separate accounts for each staff member; no shared logins
- Software and devices kept updated
- Secure disposal of paper (shredding) and old devices

## If something goes wrong

Stop and tell the practice owner immediately if:

- a document was sent to the wrong person;
- a device with client data is lost or stolen;
- someone clicked a suspicious link or entered credentials;
- returns are rejected because a client's identity number was already used.

There may be reporting duties to the tax authority, regulators, clients or the police, often with time limits. The owner decides and reports; the desk preserves the facts (what, when, who) and doesn't try to fix it quietly.

## Annual checklist (for the owner's review)

- Security plan reviewed and updated
- Staff refreshed on phishing and handling rules
- Access list checked: leavers removed
- Backup restore tested
- Retention and disposal dates actioned

## Common mistakes

- Pasting a client's SSN or NI number into a chat or a note "just for now".
- Changing a client's bank details on the strength of an email.
- Keeping old client files on a personal laptop.
