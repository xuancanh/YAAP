# Client document checklists

A checklist is built from the client's profile: last year's return plus any life changes. The preparer approves the checklist template for each profile; the desk generates and tracks it. Whether an item is relevant to the return is the preparer's call.

## Step 1: profile questions (season start)

Ask every client, every year:

- Any change in address, marital status, dependants?
- New job, job ended, side income, started or closed a business?
- Bought, sold or rented out property?
- Investment accounts opened or closed; any digital-asset activity?
- Retirement distributions or pension income started?
- Big medical, education or childcare costs?
- Received any letters from the tax authority?
- Anything else unusual this year?

A "yes" adds blocks to the checklist. An unusual answer gets flagged **for preparer review**.

## Step 2: building blocks

Examples of common documents; the preparer decides which apply.

**Everyone**
- Identity and contact details confirmed (held in the practice system, not this desk)
- Last year's return (if new client)
- Notices or letters from the tax authority

**Employee**
- US: Form W-2 from each employer
- UK: P60 for the tax year; P45 if they left a job; P11D for benefits in kind, if issued

**Self-employed / side income**
- Income records: invoices, platform statements (US: Forms 1099-NEC, 1099-K, 1099-MISC where issued)
- Expense records and receipts, mileage log
- Estimated or advance tax payments made (US quarterly estimates; UK payments on account)

**Investments**
- US: 1099-INT, 1099-DIV, 1099-B; K-1s from partnerships (often late)
- UK: interest and dividend statements; disposals records for capital gains
- Digital-asset transaction records

**Property**
- Purchase or sale closing documents
- Mortgage interest (US: Form 1098), property tax records
- Rental income and expenses

**Retirement and benefits**
- US: 1099-R, SSA-1099
- UK: pension statements, State Pension amount

**Health coverage (US)**
- Form 1095-A if insured through the Marketplace

**Credits and deductions (as the preparer requests)**
- Childcare, education, charitable giving, retirement contributions, health savings account records

## Step 3: track states, not contents

| Item | Expected from | Usually available | Status | Received | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W-2 (employer A) | client | early in year | received | 02-04 | |
| 1099-B (broker) | client | later; corrections possible | outstanding | | |
| K-1 (partnership) | client | often late | outstanding | | flag: may need extension (preparer) |

Statuses: **expected, requested, received, not applicable (confirmed by preparer), replaced (corrected form received)**.

Record that a document arrived and where it's filed in the practice system. Never copy amounts, tax ID numbers or account numbers into the desk's files.

## Corrected and late forms

Corrected information returns are common, especially from brokers. When a "corrected" form arrives after a return is prepared, mark it and send it to the preparer at once.

## Worked example

Client C-0412, profile: employee, rental flat, new this year: sold shares. Checklist = everyone + employee + property (rental) + investments (disposals). The preparer adds "records of the original cost of the shares" to the investments block. Twelve items; seven already received from last year's pattern.

## Common mistakes

- Reusing last year's checklist without asking the life-change questions.
- Marking items "not applicable" without the preparer confirming.
- Tracking figures instead of states.
