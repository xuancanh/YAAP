# Pastoral care follow-up

The care list exists so nobody slips through the gaps. It holds who needs contact and when, not what was said.

## What an entry looks like

```
J.M. · bereavement (spouse) · first contact 2026-10-02 (visit, minister)
Next: 2-week call (due 2026-10-16) · Care team aware: deacon R.
Status: active
```

Complete. Nothing about the conversation, the person's feelings, health details or family conflict. "Visited, follow-up in two weeks" is a full record. If someone asks for prayer to be shared, record exactly what they agreed may be said and to whom.

**Categories:** hospital, illness at home, bereavement, new baby, homebound, crisis, transition (move, job loss), lapsed contact, new member.

## Follow-up rhythms (adjust to the tradition and the person)

| Event | Suggested touchpoints |
|---|---|
| Bereavement | Around the funeral; 1-2 weeks after; about 1 month; about 3 months; around holidays and the first anniversary |
| Hospital or surgery | Before if known; during stay; first week home; a month later |
| New baby | Soon after birth (if welcomed); a month later |
| Homebound | Regular rotation at an agreed interval |
| New member | Welcome visit; check-in after a couple of months |

The weeks after the funeral, when visitors fade, are often when a call matters most. The anniversary of a death is one of the most valued calls a minister makes; calendar it with lead time.

## The radar sweep

On the chosen rhythm, list:

- Follow-ups due this week.
- Anyone with no contact beyond the chosen interval.
- Gaps in the homebound rotation.
- Care-team load: who has too many, who has none.

## When to refer: the proper channels

The minister offers care; some situations need others too. Say so plainly and help the person connect.

**Safeguarding (abuse, neglect, exploitation, risk to children or vulnerable adults):**

- Follow the organization's safeguarding policy. Usually that means telling the designated safeguarding lead promptly, and in many places reporting to statutory authorities.
- Don't investigate, don't promise secrecy you can't keep, and don't confront an alleged abuser.
- If anyone is in immediate danger, call emergency services.
- The care list records only: "Referral made to [role], [date]."

Laws on reporting and on confidentiality for clergy vary by country, state and tradition. The organization's policy and its legal advisers decide; the desk doesn't.

**Suicidal thoughts or acute crisis (country-specific, checked 2026-11-04):**

- Immediate danger: emergency services.
- United States: the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, by call, text or chat to 988, available 24/7.
- UK and Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123, free, any time.
- Elsewhere: look up the national crisis line in advance and keep it on the care list header.

Warm referral language:

> "I'm really glad you told me. I'm here, and I want you to have more support than I can give alone. Can we call them together now?"

**Ongoing mental health, addiction, domestic abuse, legal or money crises:** keep a local referral list (counsellors, GP or doctor, domestic abuse services, debt advice) with contact details and date checked.

## Common mistakes

- Writing down what was shared "so I don't forget". Don't.
- Letting one person's crisis absorb the whole list for weeks; the sweep catches the rest.
- Stopping bereavement contact after the funeral.
- Handling a safeguarding concern alone.
