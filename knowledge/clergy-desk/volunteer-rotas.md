# Volunteer rotas

A fair rota keeps people serving for years. The usual failure is a small group doing most of the work until they quit, so make the load visible.

## The volunteer file

One line per person:

```
Name | Roles (likes ✓) | Availability | Max per month | Clearance/training (date, renewal) | Last served | Notes
```

- Record what people enjoy, not just what they can do.
- "Max per month" is the person's own number. Ask; don't assume.
- Clearances (for example, background checks for work with children or vulnerable adults) follow the organization's own policy. Track the dates as facts and flag renewals ahead of time. Nobody without the required clearance goes on a rota that needs it.

## Building the monthly rota

1. List every slot for the month: date, role, how many people.
2. Fill the slots that need special training or clearance first.
3. Then fill the rest by preference, rotating so no one exceeds their max.
4. Check the load: count assignments per person. If a few people hold most slots, fix it before publishing, even if it means a gap you need to recruit for.
5. Avoid back-to-back weeks for the same person in demanding roles.
6. Publish in one shared place, with the swap rule stated.

**Load check example:** 40 slots, 18 volunteers. Two people have 6 each, eight people have none. Before publishing: ask the eight about their availability, cap the two at their stated max, and leave one slot as a visible gap with a specific ask.

## Swaps

"Swap with anyone on the list for that role, then tell [coordinator] so the rota stays correct." The rota is only useful if it's accurate.

## Confirmation rhythm

- About a week before: reminder with specifics ("Sunday 10am, readings: [text attached], arrive 9:40").
- Two or three days before: chase anyone who hasn't confirmed, once.
- Still nothing: go to the substitute list. You want to find the gap midweek, not on the morning.

## Saying thank you

Specific beats general. "Thanks for serving" is forgettable; "Your three months on the sound desk made the livestream reliable for the homebound" is remembered.

- Note milestones (first month, one year, five years) in the file.
- Thank people for the hard slots (holiday services, early mornings).
- Plan an annual appreciation event, budgeted and calendared.
- When someone steps back, thank them warmly and leave the door open.

## Recruiting when gaps persist

Honest asks work better than general appeals or guilt.

```
We need: [role]
What it involves: [tasks], [time per occasion], [how often]
Training: [what's offered and when]
Who to talk to: [name]
```

Ask people personally. A direct invitation to a specific role often works where a general announcement doesn't.

**New-volunteer one-pager per role:** what to do, when to arrive, who to ask, where things are, what to do if you can't make it.

## Common mistakes

- Rotating by "who said yes last time".
- Publishing without a load check.
- Letting clearance renewals lapse.
- Only noticing volunteers when they're missing.
