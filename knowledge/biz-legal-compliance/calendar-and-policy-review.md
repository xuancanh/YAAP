# Regulatory calendar and policy review

The calendar is only as good as its list of obligations. Most missed deadlines weren't late; they were never on the list.

## Finding obligations

Go through each category and ask the owning function what applies. The General Counsel decides which obligations the company is subject to; you make sure each has a row, an owner and a date.

| Category | Examples | Usual owner |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate | Annual company filings, registered details updates, beneficial ownership registers | Legal |
| Tax | Returns, payments, sales tax / VAT filings | Finance |
| Employment | Payroll filings, pension or benefit scheme duties, required notices and postings | People |
| Privacy | Registration or fees with a data protection authority (where required), records review, assessment reviews | Legal |
| Security and certifications | Audit and certification renewals, penetration tests, access reviews | IT & Security |
| Contracts | Notice deadlines and reporting duties in customer and vendor contracts | Legal (from the obligations register) |
| Licences and insurance | Business licences, sector licences, insurance renewals | Legal / Finance |
| Policies | Each policy's review date | Policy owner |

Requirements, rates and dates are jurisdiction-specific and change. Record the legal source for each obligation (the law, regulator page or contract clause) so someone can check it changed.

## Calendar row

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Obligation | Annual accounts filing |
| Legal source | Statute / regulator page / contract section |
| Jurisdiction | Where it applies |
| Due date | Next due date |
| Recurrence | Annual, quarterly, event-driven |
| Lead time | When work must start |
| Owner | Role and function |
| Task | Link to the task |
| Status | Not started / in progress / done / at risk |
| Evidence | Filing receipt, signed document, certificate |

**Lead time** is the field that prevents misses. A filing due on the 30th that needs the close, a director's signature and an accountant's review has to start weeks earlier. Ask the owner how long it really takes.

## Event-driven obligations

Some obligations start with an event, not a date. Keep them in a separate section with the trigger and the clock:

- **Personal data breach.** Under GDPR Article 33(1), notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people. Other laws have their own rules. Keep the breach procedure's link here; the incident lead and the General Counsel run it.
- **Data subject requests.** See [data subject requests](data-subject-requests.md).
- **Changes of company details**, new offices, new hires in a new country, new products in a regulated area: each may create new filings.

## Weekly routine

- List items due in the next 60 days. Each needs an owner and a task.
- For items within their lead time, check progress with the owner.
- Raise anything at risk within 14 days the same day, per your playbook.
- Mark completed items with evidence attached, not just "done".
- Add any new obligations found during the week.

## Policy review checklist

Review each policy on its date, and also when the law, a regulator's guidance, the company's practice or its tools change.

- [ ] **Version and owner** noted; last review date.
- [ ] **Practice check.** Ask the owners what actually happens. A policy that describes something nobody does is a liability.
- [ ] **Law check.** Note what has changed since the last review in each applicable jurisdiction (ask the General Counsel for a view when unclear). Cite sources.
- [ ] **Consistency.** Matches the processing records, the privacy notice, contracts and other policies (retention periods are the usual mismatch).
- [ ] **Plain language.** Short sentences; say who must do what. Remove words like "appropriate" where a specific rule exists.
- [ ] **Actionable.** Each rule says who, what, and what to do if it can't be followed.
- [ ] **Redline with reasons.** One line per change.
- [ ] **Next review date** set and added to the calendar.

Publication and sign-off are the General Counsel's decision.

## Pitfalls

- A calendar with due dates but no lead times.
- "Owner: Legal" instead of a specific role.
- Marking a filing done because the owner said so, without the receipt.
- Updating a policy without updating the documents that repeat it.
