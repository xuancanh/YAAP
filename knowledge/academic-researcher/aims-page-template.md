# Aims page template

The aims page is the proposal in miniature. Many reviewers form their view here and read the rest to confirm it. Write it first, revise it last, and keep it to one page unless the funder says otherwise. Always follow the funder's own format and criteria over this template.

## The template

**Paragraph 1: hook and gap (4-6 sentences)**
- What problem matters, and to whom. One concrete fact, cited.
- What is known (two sentences from the lit review).
- The specific gap: what is unknown or unsolved, and why it blocks progress.
- The cost of leaving the gap open.

**Paragraph 2: this project (3-4 sentences)**
- Long-term goal (the program) and the objective of this award (the piece).
- Central hypothesis or design objective, stated so it could be wrong.
- Why this team is placed to do it: preliminary data, unique access, method.

**The aims (2-3, each 3-5 lines)**
- **Aim N: verb-led title.** ("Determine whether…", "Build and validate…")
- Approach in one or two sentences.
- Expected outcome, and what you learn if the result is negative.

**Closing paragraph (2-3 sentences)**
- Expected overall outcome and its impact on the field.
- How it sets up the next step.

## The dependent-aims trap

If aim 2 only makes sense when aim 1 succeeds, reviewers see one bet, not two. Fixes:

- Give each aim its own starting material (existing data, a parallel cohort, a different method).
- Write a fallback for aim 1 that still feeds aim 2.
- Or say plainly that aim 2 builds on aim 1 and show why aim 1 is low-risk (preliminary data).

## Review checklist

- [ ] The gap sentence names something specific, not "little is known about X".
- [ ] The hypothesis can be falsified.
- [ ] Each aim has a verb-led title and an expected outcome.
- [ ] No aim fails just because another aim fails, or the fallback is stated.
- [ ] Words in the aims match the call's priorities (use the funder's language where honest).
- [ ] Innovation is stated honestly: new method, new population, or new question. Not "novel" without saying what's new.
- [ ] Fits on one page in the required font and margins.
- [ ] A smart reader outside the subfield can say back what you'll do.

## Worked example (shortened)

Draft aim: "Aim 2: Study the effects of the intervention." Problems: no verb that implies a result, no outcome, depends on aim 1's recruitment.

Revised: "Aim 2: Determine whether the intervention reduces 6-month readmission in an existing registry cohort. We will compare matched patients using records already collected (n described in preliminary data). Expected outcome: an effect estimate that informs the trial in aim 3; a null result narrows the mechanism to factors tested in aim 1." Now it has a verb, an outcome, a negative-result payoff, and its own data.

## Common mistakes

- Three aims that are really one aim cut into phases.
- A methods list instead of a question.
- Burying the hypothesis in paragraph 3.
- Overclaiming feasibility without preliminary evidence; reviewers notice.
