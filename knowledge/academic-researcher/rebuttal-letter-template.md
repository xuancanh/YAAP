# Rebuttal letter template

A good response letter makes the editor's job easy: every comment answered, every change easy to find, disagreements respectful and backed by evidence. Tone matters less than precision, but sarcasm or defensiveness costs papers.

## Step 1: triage

Copy every comment into a table before writing anything:

| # | Reviewer | Comment (short) | Category | What they actually want | Effort | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1.1 | 1 | Sample too small | Partly agree | Power or limitation statement | M | AB |
| R2.3 | 2 | Missing citation to X | Agree | Cite and position against X | S | CD |

Categories: **agree and fix**, **partly agree**, **push back**. For harsh comments, write the "what they actually want" column calmly; it's usually narrower than the wording suggests.

## Step 2: the letter

**Opening (short):** thank the editor and reviewers, summarize the main changes in three to five bullets.

**Per comment:**

```
> Reviewer 1, comment 3: [quote the comment in full]

Response: Thank you for this point. We agree that ... We have [action]
(revised manuscript, p. 6, lines 112-125). [Evidence or new result, if any.]
```

The pattern is gratitude, action, evidence, location. Give page and line numbers for the revised file, and mark changes in the manuscript the way the venue asks (highlighting or tracked changes).

## Phrase bank for push-backs

Concede the reasonable part first, then argue with evidence.

- "We agree this needs to be clearer. Our intent was ___; we have rewritten the passage (p. 4) to make that explicit."
- "We considered this approach. We chose ___ because ___ (citation). We now explain this choice in the methods (p. 7)."
- "We ran the suggested analysis; results are in Supplementary Table S4. The main conclusion holds, and we note the difference in the discussion."
- "This is an important direction but beyond the scope of this paper. We now mention it as future work (p. 12)."

Avoid: "The reviewer misunderstood", "As clearly stated", "obviously". If they misread it, the text allowed it, so fix the text.

## Step 3: revision tracker

| # | Promised change | Done? | Where | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1.3 | Clarify sampling | Yes | p. 6, l. 112 | AB |

Before resubmitting, walk the tracker against the manuscript itself, not the draft letter. A promised change that isn't in the paper is the fastest route to a second rejection.

## Worked example

Comment: "The authors seem unaware of basic literature on this topic."

What they want: probably two or three specific papers they think are missing.

Response: "Thank you. We have expanded the related-work section to discuss [A], [B] and [C] (p. 3, lines 40-58) and explain how our approach differs: they assume ___, which our setting does not. If there is other work the reviewer had in mind, we would be glad to add it."

## After a rejection

Sort comments into three piles: problems with the paper (fix before resubmitting anywhere), venue taste (scope, length, style), and noise. Fix the first pile, adapt framing for the next venue, and re-verify citations before sending.
