# Multi-agency coordination

When several services are involved, actions slip between them. The fix is one tracker with owners and dates, and minutes that say who will do what.

## Follow-through tracker

One per case, by case code.

| Action | Agreed at | Owner (agency / role) | Due | Status | Last chased | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| School to share attendance record | Meeting 03/11 | School DSL | 10/11 | waiting | 08/11 | |
| Housing to confirm repair date | Phone 01/11 | Housing officer | 07/11 | done 06/11 | | Date 15/11 |

- Review before each contact and weekly.
- Chase politely one or two working days before due, and again the day after.
- Overdue actions that affect safety or deadlines go to the professional the same day; they decide whether to escalate.

## Before a professionals' meeting

- Confirm date, time, place or link, and the chair
- Agenda circulated in advance, with the purpose in one line
- List of attendees and their roles; who is absent and whether they've sent a report
- The current action tracker
- Whether the person or family is attending, and what support they need to take part (interpreter, advocate, accessibility)
- The professional's own notes and questions (their judgment stays theirs)

## Minutes template

```
Meeting: [type]   Date/time:   Chair:
Present: [roles/agencies]   Apologies:
Purpose:

1. Updates from each agency (facts, attributed)
2. Discussion points (summarised, attributed where relevant)
3. Decisions (as stated by the chair)
4. Actions: what / who / by when
5. Next meeting:

Minutes drafted by [desk] for review by [professional/chair] on [date]
```

Minutes record decisions as stated; the desk doesn't summarise them into a different conclusion. Circulate only after the chair or the professional approves.

## Appointments

- Book with the person's availability, travel and caring duties in mind
- Send reminders through the channel they prefer, with no sensitive detail in the message
- Arrange interpreters and accessibility in advance
- Record attended / not attended and any reason given, factually

## Before sharing information

Information-sharing rules depend on country, sector and purpose. In England, DfE guidance stresses that data protection law isn't a barrier to sharing information to keep children safe; adult services have their own frameworks. The professional decides what to share. The desk can check:

- Is there consent, or another lawful basis the professional has named?
- Is the recipient the right person, confirmed by a known contact route?
- Is only the necessary information included?
- Is it accurate, and does it say what's fact and what's opinion?
- Is it sent securely (the agency's secure email or portal, not personal email)?
- Is the sharing recorded: what, to whom, when, why?

## Worked example

Professionals' meeting for case F-218: school, health visitor, housing, the worker. Prep: the tracker showed housing's repair date three weeks overdue. Minutes recorded the chair's decision: housing to confirm a date by Friday; the worker to update the family. The desk chased housing on Thursday; date confirmed; tracker updated; the worker told.

## Common mistakes

- Actions with no owner or date ("someone to look into…").
- Minutes that paraphrase a decision into something stronger or weaker.
- Sending a report to a generic inbox without confirming the recipient.
