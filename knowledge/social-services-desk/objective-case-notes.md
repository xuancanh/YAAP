# Objective case-note writing

Case notes may be read by the person they're about, a supervisor, another agency, an inspector or a court. Write them so that each reader can tell what was observed, what was said, and what the professional concluded, and who concluded it.

Professional codes ask for accurate, timely records that reflect the services provided (for example NASW Code of Ethics standard 3.04 in the US). The professional's agency format and regulator rules come first.

## Three layers, kept separate

1. **Observation:** what was seen or heard. Dates, times, places, actions, direct quotes.
2. **Reported information:** what someone else said, attributed. "Ms. A reported…", "School stated…".
3. **Professional assessment:** interpretation and judgment. **This layer is left for the professional.** Drafts mark it as a placeholder: `[Assessment — for worker]`.

## Word swaps

| Avoid | Why | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| "Client was aggressive" | Interpretation | "Client raised his voice, stood up and said '…'" |
| "Mother is in denial" | Diagnosis-like judgment | "Mother said, 'There's nothing wrong with him.'" |
| "House was filthy" | Loaded | "Kitchen floor had food debris; sink had dishes; strong smell of urine in hallway" |
| "Seemed drunk" | Inference | "Slurred speech; smelled of alcohol; unsteady walking" |
| "Non-compliant" | Label | "Did not attend appointments on 3 and 10 Nov; said transport was unavailable" |
| "Claims that…" | Implies doubt | "Said that…" / "Reported that…" |

## Neutral note structure

Agencies use different formats (narrative, SOAP, DAP, BIRP and others). If the professional's framework is known, follow it. If not, use this neutral one:

```
Date / time / duration:
Type: [home visit / office / phone / video]
Present: [initials or roles]
Purpose of contact:
Observations: [factual, specific]
Reported information: [attributed]
Actions taken: [what the worker did]
[Assessment — for worker]
Plan / next steps: [who, what, by when]
Recorded by / date written:
```

## Good habits

- Write as soon after the contact as possible; note the date written if later.
- Use the person's own words for anything important, in quotation marks.
- Include strengths and what went well, not only problems.
- Record what was offered and the person's response.
- Avoid jargon and abbreviations the person couldn't understand if they read their file.
- Only what's relevant and necessary; leave out gossip and unrelated history.
- Initials or case codes in this desk's drafts; the official system holds identities.

## Before and after

**Before**

> Visited Mrs. T. She was very anxious and clearly not coping. Flat was a mess. Son is out of control. Will need to escalate.

**After (draft for worker)**

> 06/11, 14:00–14:45, home visit. Present: Mrs. T, son (J, 14), worker.
> Observations: Living room had several bin bags of clothing on the floor; kitchen sink had unwashed dishes. Mrs. T spoke quickly and twice said, "I can't keep up with it." J left the room after five minutes and closed the front door hard.
> Reported: Mrs. T said J has not attended school since 30/10. She said she has been sleeping "three or four hours a night".
> Actions: Worker gave the number for the family support line; Mrs. T agreed to a school meeting.
> [Assessment — for worker]
> Plan: Worker to contact school by 08/11.

The draft drops "not coping", "out of control" and "escalate". Those are judgments, and whether to escalate is the worker's call.

## Corrections

Never overwrite an entry in the official record. Corrections follow the agency's procedure, usually a dated addendum. This desk's drafts can be revised freely before the professional files them.
