# Resource directory method

A directory is only useful if it's current. An out-of-date number sends a person in crisis to a dead end. Every entry carries the date it was last verified and how.

## Entry template

```
Service name:
Provider / organisation:
What it offers: [plain words, one or two lines]
Who it's for (eligibility sketch): [as published by the provider; see wording rules below]
How to access: [self-referral / professional referral / both]; form or phone
Contact: phone, email, address, website
Hours:
Languages / accessibility:
Cost: [free / sliding scale / fee]
Typical wait (as stated by provider, with date):
Last verified: [date] by [method: phoned / provider website / email]
Verified by:
Notes from the professional: [practical tips, e.g. "ask for the duty worker"]
Next check due:
```

## Wording eligibility

The desk sketches eligibility; it never determines it. Quote or closely paraphrase the provider's own criteria, and name the source.

> Good: "Provider's website (checked 04/11/2026) says: for adults 18+ living in the borough who are homeless or at risk within 56 days."
> Avoid: "Client qualifies."

If criteria are unclear, write "criteria unclear; call to check" rather than guessing.

## Verifying an entry

- **Phone check** is best: does the number work, are the hours right, is the service still running, has the referral route changed?
- **Website check** is next best; note the page's own "last updated" date if it has one.
- **Second-hand** (a colleague said) is weakest; mark it as unverified until checked.

## Staleness audit (monthly)

1. List entries whose "next check due" has passed.
2. Prioritise crisis lines, housing, food and anything used in the last month.
3. Verify, update and re-date. Entries that can't be reached after two tries are marked **"unconfirmed — do not rely on"** with the date, not deleted silently.
4. Review notes from the professional's recent referrals: did anything fail? ("Phone line now voicemail only.")
5. Summarise the changes in one short note to the professional.

Set check frequency by risk: crisis and emergency services most often, stable statutory services less often.

## National and regional starting points

- **US:** 211 connects people with local services by phone, text and online search in most areas; many states and counties also run their own directories.
- **UK:** local authority service directories (often called a "Local Offer" for special educational needs and disability), NHS service finders, and the voluntary-sector council for the area.
- **Elsewhere:** municipal social-services offices and national helpline directories.

These are good for discovery, but verify the details yourself before adding them.

## Organising the directory

Group by need, not provider: housing, food, money and benefits, mental health, substance use, domestic abuse, children and families, disability, legal advice, immigration advice, older people, transport. Tag entries with the areas served.

## Common mistakes

- Entries with no verification date.
- Copying eligibility from memory.
- One phone number for a big organisation, when the right route is a specific team.
- Deleting dead entries without noting it, so someone re-adds them from an old list.
