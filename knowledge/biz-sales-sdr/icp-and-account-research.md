# ICP and account research

Good research answers two questions per account: does it fit, and why reach out now? If you can't answer the second, the account waits.

## ICP vs persona

- **Ideal customer profile (ICP)** describes the *company*: industry, size, region, tech stack, business model, signs of the problem we solve. It comes from the company's positioning and from who has bought and stayed.
- **Persona** describes the *person*: role, responsibilities, what they're measured on, what keeps them up at night.

Fit the account to the ICP first, then find the personas inside it.

## Fit tiers

Score each account against the ICP criteria the company has written. A simple tiering:

- **Tier 1**: meets all must-have criteria and shows a trigger (below). Research deeply, personalise each message.
- **Tier 2**: meets must-haves, no visible trigger. Lighter research; segment-level messaging.
- **Tier 3 / no fit**: misses a must-have. Don't reach out; note why.

Record the reason for each tier so others can check your judgement.

## Triggers: reasons to reach out now

A trigger is a public, recent event that makes our problem more likely or more urgent:

- New leader in the role we sell to (often reviews tools early)
- Funding, acquisition or expansion into new markets
- Hiring for roles that use or manage our kind of product
- Job posts that mention the problem or a competitor's tool
- Product launches, rebrands, regulatory changes affecting their industry
- Public statements by leaders about a related goal
- Engagement with our content or website (from marketing data, if consent allows its use)

A trigger must be true and recent. Note the source and date for each.

## Research checklist per Tier 1 account

- [ ] One-line description of what they do, in their words (from their site)
- [ ] ICP fit criteria met, with evidence
- [ ] Trigger, with source link and date
- [ ] Two or three likely personas, with names and roles if publicly listed
- [ ] Current tools, if known (job posts, public case studies)
- [ ] Existing relationship: in the CRM already? Past opportunity? Current customer elsewhere in the group?
- [ ] Hypothesis: "Because of [trigger], [persona] is probably dealing with [problem]; we help by [value]."

Keep it short. Research is a means to a relevant message, not a report.

## Sources

Use the company's approved data tools and public sources: the account's website, press releases, filings, job boards, professional profiles and news. Do not scrape sites against their terms, buy unvetted lists, or guess personal emails. Record where each fact came from.

## Recording it

Put research where the next person will find it: the CRM account record, using the fields the CRM Administrator defined. Avoid private notes. Include the date, since triggers go stale quickly.

## Building a target list

1. Start from the ICP criteria and the segment you were asked for.
2. Remove existing customers, open opportunities owned by others and suppressed contacts.
3. Tier the accounts.
4. Research Tier 1 first.
5. Save the list with tier, trigger and hypothesis per account, and the date.

## Pitfalls

- **Big logos with no fit.** A famous name that misses the ICP wastes AE time.
- **Fake personalisation.** "I saw you went to [university]" is not a reason to buy. Tie the detail to the problem.
- **Stale triggers.** A funding round from long ago isn't news.
- **Ignoring the CRM.** Contacting someone another rep is working on damages trust inside and out.
