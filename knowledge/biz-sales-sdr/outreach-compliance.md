# Outreach compliance basics

> **Jurisdiction- and time-sensitive.** This is a general orientation, not legal advice. Rules depend on where the recipient is, whether they are a business or an individual, and the channel. Laws and regulator guidance change. The company's outreach policy and its legal team decide; when in doubt, ask before sending.

## United States: CAN-SPAM (email)

According to the FTC's compliance guide, the CAN-SPAM Act covers commercial email and makes no exception for business-to-business email. Its main requirements:

- **Accurate headers**: "From", "To", "Reply-To" and routing information must identify the sender.
- **Honest subject lines** that reflect the content.
- **Identify the message as an ad**, clearly (the law allows flexibility in how).
- **Include a valid physical postal address.**
- **Explain how to opt out** of future marketing email, with a working mechanism.
- **Honour opt-outs within 10 business days**; the mechanism must work for at least 30 days after sending; you can't charge a fee or ask for more than an email address to opt out.
- **Responsibility can't be contracted away**: both the company promoted and the sender can be liable.

The FTC notes that each non-compliant email can bring a separate civil penalty. Phone and text outreach in the US are governed by other rules (for example on autodialled calls and texts) — check the company's policy before using those channels.

## European Union: GDPR (personal data)

Business contacts at named people (jane.doe@company.com) are personal data under the GDPR. The company needs a lawful basis to process them for outreach, set by its legal team. One rule is absolute: under **Article 21**, a person may object at any time to processing for direct marketing, and once they object, their data must no longer be processed for that purpose.

Electronic marketing in the EU and UK is also governed by national e-privacy rules (in the UK, PECR), which can treat individuals and corporate subscribers differently. The company's policy should say which regions and contact types you may email, and on what basis.

## Other regions

Many other countries have their own anti-spam laws, and some are built around the recipient's prior consent rather than an opt-out. Don't assume the US rules apply everywhere; check the policy for the recipient's country.

## Operating checklist (every list, every send)

- [ ] Every contact checked against the suppression list and the CRM opt-out flag
- [ ] Recipient region known; the policy allows outreach to that region and contact type
- [ ] Data source recorded and approved (no scraped or unvetted bought lists)
- [ ] Message uses the company's template with sender identity, postal address and opt-out
- [ ] Subject line and sender name honest
- [ ] Opt-outs and objections recorded the same day, across all tools and sequences
- [ ] "Don't contact me" in any wording counts as an opt-out
- [ ] Data subject requests (access, deletion) forwarded to the privacy contact immediately

## Practical rules

- **Opt-outs are global** across the company's sequences unless the policy says otherwise; removing a contact from one sequence isn't enough.
- **Bounced or role addresses** (info@, sales@) are handled per policy; they often belong on the suppression list.
- **Re-engaging opted-out contacts** is not allowed, even with a new campaign or a new rep.
- **Forwarded or referred contacts** are still subject to the same rules.
- **Keep records**: when and how a contact was sourced and when they opted out.
