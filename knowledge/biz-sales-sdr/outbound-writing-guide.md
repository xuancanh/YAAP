# Outbound writing guide

A cold message earns a reply when the reader sees, in a few seconds, that you understand their situation and might be worth a short conversation. Short, specific and easy to answer.

## Message structure

1. **Why them, why now** (one sentence): the trigger or observation from your research.
2. **The problem** (one sentence): what that usually causes for someone in their role.
3. **The value** (one or two sentences): how we help, with a proof point (a customer result the company has approved for use).
4. **The ask** (one sentence): a low-effort next step — a question, or a short call.

Example:

> Hi Dana — saw you're hiring three support leads for the new EMEA office.
>
> Scaling a team across time zones usually means tickets wait overnight while handoffs get worked out.
>
> We help support teams route tickets by time zone automatically; [customer] used it to answer EMEA customers within their own working day.
>
> Worth a 20-minute look at how they set it up?

## Rules

- Write to one person, in plain words. Read it aloud; if it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.
- Keep it short enough to read on a phone without scrolling.
- One ask per message. Questions are easier to answer than calendar links for a first touch.
- Use only approved claims and customer names. Never invent results or imply a relationship that doesn't exist.
- No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subjects, false urgency or misleading sender names (these can also break the law; see [outreach compliance basics](outreach-compliance.md)).

## Subject lines

- Short and specific to them: "EMEA support handoffs", "Question about your new ops team".
- Honest about the content.
- No clickbait, all caps or excessive punctuation; these also hurt deliverability.

## Sequence pattern

A sequence is a few touches over a few weeks, each adding something new. A common shape:

| Touch | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email | trigger + problem + value + ask |
| 2 | Email (reply in thread) | a different angle or proof point, short |
| 3 | Professional network or phone, where permitted | brief, refers to the email |
| 4 | Email | useful resource (guide, benchmark the company published) with no hard ask |
| 5 | Email | a polite close: "Should I stop reaching out on this?" |

Space touches out; the company's playbook sets the exact cadence. Stop immediately on a reply, an opt-out or a bounce.

## Personalisation tiers

- **Tier 1 accounts**: first line and problem written for the person.
- **Tier 2 accounts**: segment-level message with a personal first line.
- Never fake personalisation with merge fields that clearly came from a template.

## Replies

- **Interested**: respond quickly, propose times, start the handoff notes.
- **Not now**: ask when to follow up, log it, set a task.
- **Wrong person**: thank them, ask who owns it, and only contact that person if the introduction is genuine.
- **Opt-out or annoyed**: apologise briefly if appropriate, confirm removal, update the suppression list the same day.

## Self-check

- [ ] Clear reason for reaching this person now
- [ ] Every claim approved and true
- [ ] One ask
- [ ] Honest subject and sender
- [ ] Opt-out and required sender details present (per the company's template)
- [ ] Contact not on the suppression list; consent rules for their region checked
