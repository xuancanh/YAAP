# Qualification and meeting acceptance

Your job at this stage is not to fully qualify a deal; that's the AE's discovery. It's to confirm there's enough fit and interest that an AE's hour is well spent, and to route everything else to the right place quickly.

## Inbound triage flow

For each new lead, in this order:

1. **Is it a person and a real request?** Spam, students, job seekers and vendors go to their own routes per policy.
2. **Is it an existing customer?** Route to their CSM or support, not sales.
3. **Is the account already owned?** Check the CRM; route to the owner.
4. **Does it fit the ICP?** If not, send to the self-serve or nurture route the company defines.
5. **What did they ask for?** A demo request is high intent; a content download is lower. Respond in kind.
6. **Respond fast** with a short, personal reply that offers the next step they asked for.

Log the outcome and the reason for every lead, including the ones you route away.

## A light qualification check

Use the company's framework. If none is set, a light BANT-style check works at this stage:

| Area | Question to answer | Enough to book? |
|---|---|---|
| **Need** | What problem prompted them? | a real problem we solve, in their words |
| **Authority / role** | Are they a decision-maker, influencer or user? | influencer or above, or a user with a sponsor named |
| **Timing** | Is there a reason to act soon? | some timeframe or trigger |
| **Budget / fit** | Is the account the size and type we serve? | meets ICP must-haves |

You don't need every answer. A clear need plus ICP fit is often enough for an AE meeting; follow the acceptance criteria below.

## Good qualifying questions

Short and natural, not an interrogation:

- What made you reach out now?
- How are you handling it today?
- Who else is involved in looking at this?
- Is there a timeframe you're working to?

## Meeting acceptance criteria

A meeting counts as **qualified** when the AE accepts it. Agree the criteria with the Head of Sales; a typical set:

- [ ] Account fits the ICP must-haves
- [ ] The person attending has a role in the decision, or brings someone who does
- [ ] A stated problem we solve
- [ ] The prospect agreed to the meeting's purpose (not just "a chat")
- [ ] The meeting is on the calendar with an invite they accepted
- [ ] Handoff notes complete

If the AE rejects a meeting, record the reason. Rejection reasons are your best feedback.

## Handoff notes

```
Account / contact (role) / source (inbound form, outbound sequence)
Why they took the meeting: …
Problem in their words: …
Current approach and tools: …
Others involved: …
Timing or trigger: …
What I promised them (agenda, materials): …
Research link: …
```

## No-shows

- Send a short, friendly note the same day offering two new times.
- After a second no-show, return the lead to nurture and note it.
- A no-show does not count as a held meeting; follow the company's counting rule.

## Pitfalls

- **Booking anyone who says yes.** Unaccepted meetings cost AE time and your credibility.
- **Over-qualifying inbound.** A ready buyer asked to answer ten questions may go elsewhere.
- **Slow inbound.** Treat new inbound as more urgent than any outbound task.
- **Missing notes.** If the AE has to re-ask everything, the prospect notices.
