# Structured screen notes

The standard: a note the candidate could read without being embarrassed or hurt. Every line is evidence against a criterion the client agreed to. If it isn't, it doesn't go in.

## Screen call outline (20-30 minutes)

1. Intro: who you are, the role, what happens with their information.
2. Motivation: why look now, what they want next.
3. Evidence per criterion: "Walk me through the last time you…" Ask for the situation, what they did, and the result.
4. Logistics: notice period, location and work pattern, comp expectation (asked directly).
5. Their questions, answered from the brief honestly.
6. Next steps and when they'll hear from you.

## Note template

```
Candidate: initials + file id   Role: ___   Date: ___   Screener: ___
Consent to submit to [client]: yes/no (date)

Criterion 1: ___
  Evidence: [specific example, their words where useful]
  Rating: strong / some / none / not explored
Criterion 2: ...

Motivation (job-related): ___
Logistics: notice ___ · location/pattern ___ · comp expectation ___
Concerns (job-related, with evidence): ___
Recommendation: submit / hold / decline, and why in one line
```

Use the same criteria for everyone in the role. That's what makes notes comparable and shows the process was fair.

## Never record

- Age or birth year, marital status, pregnancy or family plans, children or caring duties.
- Race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity.
- Health or disability, unless the candidate asks for an interview adjustment (then record only the adjustment needed).
- Appearance, accent, "culture fit" or "energy" with no job-related evidence.

If a candidate volunteers any of this, don't write it down, and don't let it influence your recommendation.

## Legal background (country-specific, general information only)

- **United States:** federal law enforced by the EEOC prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation and gender identity), national origin, age (40 or older), disability and genetic information. This covers job ads and recruitment, and it applies to employment agencies. Many states and cities add more protected grounds.
- **United Kingdom:** the Equality Act 2010 protects nine characteristics: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation.
- **Candidate data:** under UK and EU GDPR, collect only what you need, tell candidates how their data is used, and keep it only as long as your stated retention period. Other countries have their own privacy laws.

This is background, not legal advice. Questions about a specific case go to the agency's compliance lead or an employment lawyer.

## When a client asks for something discriminatory

Refuse the requirement, not the client, and steer to the job-related need.

> "I can't screen on age; it's unlawful and it would narrow the pool. If what you need is someone who can ramp up fast on your stack, let's add that as a criterion and I'll test for it in every screen."

Record that the request was declined, the date, and who you told at the agency.

## Worked example

Weak note: "Great energy, young and hungry, a bit quiet on the call."

Evidence note: "Criterion 2 (owns a release end-to-end): led the March checkout migration, wrote the rollback plan, cut errors after launch (her figure, not verified). Rating: strong. Concern: no people-management evidence yet; brief lists it as a preference, not a must-have."
