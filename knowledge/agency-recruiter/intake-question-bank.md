# Intake question bank

The goal of intake is a spec you can search against and a client who has agreed to it in writing. Pick the questions that fit; you won't need all of them. Ask follow-ups until you get examples, not adjectives.

## The work itself

- What will this person have delivered by month three? By month twelve?
- What does a normal week look like? What takes most of the time?
- Who did this job before? What made them succeed or struggle?
- What problem made you open this role now?

## Must-have versus wish-list

For each requirement on the ad, ask: "Would you reject an otherwise excellent candidate who lacks this?"

- Yes, always: **must-have** (keep the list short; three to five is typical).
- Usually, but it depends: **strong preference**.
- No: **nice to have**, and leave it out of screening.

Follow-ups that shrink lists: "Could someone learn that in the first three months?" "Is the degree standing in for a skill? Which skill?" "How many years, and why that number?"

## The sell

- Why would a strong person leave a good job for this one?
- Comp band, bonus and equity, and how they were set. Is it at, above or below market? (Research and date your own market check; flag gaps now.)
- Flexibility: location, hours, travel, remote policy in practice, not on paper.
- Growth: where have people in this team gone next?
- The honest downsides: what will candidates find out in month two?

## Team and manager

- Who is the manager, how do they like to work, and how long have they been in role?
- Team size, seniority mix, recent departures.

## Process

- Stages, who runs each, and the turnaround you'll commit to (agree a feedback SLA, e.g. within two working days of each interview).
- Who makes the final call? Who can veto?
- Any assessments or take-home tasks, and how long they take.
- Start date and notice periods the client can live with.
- Confidential search? What can you tell candidates about the client and when?

## Commercials

- Fee, payment terms, guarantee or rebate period, exclusivity. Confirm against the agency's terms and record them.

## Search brief template

```
Role: ___            Client: ___         Brief agreed on: ___
Outcomes (month 3 / month 12): ___
Must-haves (max 5): ___
Strong preferences: ___
Not required: ___
Comp: band ___ · market check ___ (dated) · flag: ___
Sell (3 lines): ___
Honest downsides: ___
Process: stages ___ · feedback SLA ___ · decision-maker ___
Confidentiality: ___
Calibration: first 3-5 profiles by ___
```

Send it to the client for a written "yes". Then send a small calibration set within days and update the brief from their reactions.

## Common mistakes

- Taking the job ad as the spec.
- Skipping comp at intake, then losing three finalists on salary.
- Accepting "ASAP" without dates for each stage.
- Not naming the decision-maker, so the process stalls at the final round.
- A must-have list of twelve items; nobody has all twelve.

If a client states a requirement that is a protected characteristic (an age range, gender, "young team", nationality where not legally required), don't put it in the brief. Use the refusal wording in [structured screen notes](structured-screen-notes.md) and ask what job-related need sits behind it.
