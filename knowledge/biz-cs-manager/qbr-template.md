# QBR / EBR template

A business review is for the customer, not a product demo or a usage report read aloud. It answers three questions: did we deliver the outcomes you bought us for, what's in the way, and what will we do together next quarter? A quarterly business review (QBR) is usually with the day-to-day owners. An executive business review (EBR) is shorter and higher level, with the sponsor and their leadership. Run them at the cadence the account's tier calls for (cadence.md), not by default for everyone.

The CSM prepares, you or a peer review, and a human sends invitations and leads the meeting unless the company has granted otherwise. Never promise roadmap dates, credits or contract changes in the deck. Those go to their owners first.

## Prep checklist (two weeks before)

- [ ] Customer's stated goals from the last review or onboarding (their words, in the CRM).
- [ ] Outcome evidence: usage and adoption from the product source, support summary (ticket volume, CSAT, open items), and value delivered in the customer's own terms where it's measured.
- [ ] Health score and the reason behind it (see [Health scoring](./health-scoring.md)).
- [ ] Open escalations or known issues, with status from the owning team.
- [ ] Renewal date, commercial context and any expansion hypothesis, kept internal unless the account plan says to raise it.
- [ ] Questions for them: what changed in their business, new priorities, new stakeholders.
- [ ] Roadmap items: only what product has approved for sharing, worded the way product approved.
- [ ] Attendee list confirmed by the human owner. Is the sponsor coming?

## Agenda (copy and adapt)

```markdown
# <Customer> business review: <quarter>
Attendees: <customer names/roles> · <our names/roles>
Duration: 45-60 min (QBR) / 30 min (EBR)

1. Their priorities (5-10 min): what has changed for you since last quarter?
2. Outcomes vs goals (10-15 min)
   | Goal (their words) | Measure | Last quarter | This quarter | Status |
3. What's working / what's in the way (10 min)
   - Adoption highlights · friction points · support summary
4. Open issues (5 min): status and owner for each; nothing new promised
5. Next quarter plan (10 min)
   | Action | Owner (theirs/ours) | Date |
6. Asks both ways (5 min): introductions, feedback, references (if appropriate)
```

## Worked example (made-up)

Goal from onboarding: "cut the time our team spends on monthly reconciliation". Measure: the customer's own estimate of hours per close, which they report. Last quarter they said about 40, this quarter about 30. Friction: two teams still use the old export, so the next-quarter action is a training session for those teams, owned by our Onboarding Specialist, dated. The deck shows the customer's figures labelled as their estimate, not as our claim.

## After the meeting (within two working days)

- [ ] Recap drafted for the human owner to send: decisions, actions with owners and dates, open questions.
- [ ] Actions on our side become briefed tasks with dueAt.
- [ ] Health inputs updated (sponsor engagement, goals restated).
- [ ] Anything commercial, roadmap or contract-related goes to its owner as a backlog task with the customer's words.
- [ ] Notes saved to the account record, with the outcome captured for the monthly health review.

## Common pitfalls

- **A usage dump.** Logins and feature counts mean little unless they're tied to the customer's goals.
- **No sponsor in the room** for an EBR. Reschedule rather than run it with the wrong people.
- **Surprises.** Bad news (an outage, a delayed fix) should reach the customer before the review, not in it.
- **Promising what you don't own.** Roadmap dates and credits go through their owners.
- **No follow-up.** Actions without owners and dates make the next review a repeat of this one.
- **The same review for every account.** Tier the format and frequency.
