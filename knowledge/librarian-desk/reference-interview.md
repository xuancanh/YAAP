# The reference interview

Patrons often ask for something broader or narrower than what they need. "Do you have books on cars?" might mean "how do I change a tyre on a 2015 Corolla" or "my child's school project on electric vehicles". The interview finds the real question before you search.

The ALA's RUSA guidelines describe the behaviours that patrons rate well. The stages below follow them loosely.

## The stages

1. **Approachable.** Look up, make eye contact (or reply fast online), and signal you're glad to help. Many people never ask because staff look busy.
2. **Interest.** Give full attention. Face the patron; in chat, acknowledge quickly.
3. **Listening and inquiring.** Let them finish. Then clarify.
4. **Searching.** Search with the patron where possible and explain as you go. That teaches the finding.
5. **Follow-up and closure.** Check the answer fits. Invite them back.

## Question types

| Type | Example | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Open | "What would you like to know about cars?" | Starting out; getting their words |
| Neutral | "What will you use the information for?" "What have you found so far?" | Finding purpose without prying |
| Closed | "Do you need it by Friday?" "Print or online?" | Pinning details once you know the topic |

Neutral questions are the workhorse. They uncover purpose without asking "why?", which can feel like a challenge.

## Things to establish

- Topic in their own words
- Purpose (school, work, health, personal)
- Level and format (a child's book, a scholarly article, a form)
- How much is enough
- Deadline
- What they've already tried

## Closing check

Ask: "Does this completely answer your question?" If not, go back to inquiring. That one question catches most mismatches.

## Sensitive questions

Health, legal, money and immigration questions need extra care:

- Give sources, not advice: "Here is the official guide" rather than "you should".
- Point to authoritative sources (government, health agencies, legal aid) and to a professional where the patron needs one.
- Keep it private. Lower your voice; offer a quieter spot.
- Never record the question with a name.

## Worked example

Patron: "I need something on wills."
Neutral: "What are you hoping to do with the information?"
Patron: "My dad died and we don't know if there was a will."
Now it's a different question: how to find out if a will exists and what happens if there isn't one. Offer the official government guidance for that jurisdiction and suggest a solicitor or legal aid for their situation.

## Common mistakes

- Answering the first question asked.
- Pointing ("it's over there") instead of walking or showing.
- Assuming the format they need.
- Stopping without the closing check.
