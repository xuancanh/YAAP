# Weeding with CREW and MUSTIE

CREW stands for Continuous Review, Evaluation and Weeding. It was developed by the Texas State Library and Archives Commission and is the most cited weeding method in public and school libraries. The core idea: weeding is routine maintenance on written criteria, not a one-off purge.

Note: TSLAC has indicated the manual is no longer being updated. The method is stable, but check your state library or system for current guidance.

## MUSTIE: the six reasons to remove an item

| Letter | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| M | Misleading: factually wrong or outdated | A 2009 guide to tax filing; old medical advice |
| U | Ugly: worn beyond mending | Broken spine, stains, missing pages |
| S | Superseded by a newer edition or better source | Third edition when the sixth is on the shelf |
| T | Trivial: no lasting literary or scientific merit | A fad title nobody asks for |
| I | Irrelevant to your community's needs | Out-of-scope specialist material |
| E | Elsewhere: easily available through interlibrary loan or online | Rarely used reference works held by the system |

An item usually needs a MUSTIE reason plus low use, not just age.

## The CREW formula

CREW writes guidance as *age / last circulation / MUSTIE*. For example "5/3/MUSTIE" means consider removing if the copyright is over five years old, it hasn't circulated in three years, and a MUSTIE factor applies. The manual gives suggested formulas by subject area: fast-changing subjects (computing, health, law) get short ages; history and literature get long ones or "X" (no age limit). Use the manual's tables as a starting point, then write your own into policy.

## Running a pass

1. **Pick a section,** not the whole library. Rotate so every section gets a pass on a cycle.
2. **Pull a list** from the catalogue: copyright date, last checkout, total checkouts.
3. **Check the shelf.** Condition and accuracy need eyes on the item.
4. **Decide per item:** keep, repair, replace (newer edition), move (to storage or another branch), or withdraw.
5. **Check before withdrawing:** last copy in the system? Local history? Award winner or core title? Those may stay despite low use.
6. **Record it.** Date, section, criteria used, counts by decision. That record is what makes a weed defensible.
7. **Follow your disposal policy** (sale, donation, recycling). Public property rules may apply.

## Talking about weeding

Weeding draws complaints when people see books in a skip. Say why in plain terms: shelves full of outdated or damaged items hide the good ones, and a lighter shelf is easier to browse. Publish the policy. Be ready to show the criteria.

## Common mistakes

- Weeding without a written policy, or not following the one you have.
- Using age alone; classics and local history get caught.
- Weeding "difficult" content under cover of MUSTIE. Criteria apply to all items equally, or the process isn't defensible.
- No record of what was removed and why.
- Saving everything. An unweeded collection is harder to use.
