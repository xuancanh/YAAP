# Program planning checklist

A program is an event with a budget of almost nothing. Plan it like one, with an extra eye on cost and accessibility.

## Before saying yes

- Who is it for? Name the audience, not "everyone".
- What should they leave with? One sentence.
- Is anyone else doing this nearby? Partner rather than compete.
- Cost: presenter fee, materials, refreshments, staff hours. Staff time is the cost most often left out.
- Does it fit the library's plan or a community need you can point to?

If you can't answer the first two, it's not ready.

## Timeline (adjust to your library)

**6–8 weeks out**
- Book the room and date. Check clashes: school holidays, local events, religious holidays.
- Confirm presenter in writing: date, time, fee, equipment, cancellation terms.
- Decide on registration (needed if numbers are capped or materials are per person).

**4 weeks out**
- Publicity: catalogue or events listing, posters, social media, partners' newsletters, school newsletters for youth programs.
- Plain-language description: what, who for, when, where, cost (usually free), how to register, accessibility info.

**2 weeks out**
- Order materials.
- Plan the room: seating, sightlines, power, hearing loop or mic.
- Brief front-desk staff so they can answer questions.

**Week of**
- Reminder to registrants.
- Print sign-in sheet (counts only; no names if not needed).
- Test any tech in the room.

**Day of**
- Set up early. Welcome people at the door.
- Count attendance at the peak, not the start.
- Have something to hand out: next event, a book list, a library card form.

**After**
- Thank the presenter and partners within a few days.
- Record: attendance, cost, what worked, what didn't, one quote if someone offers one.
- Decide: repeat, change or drop.

## Accessibility checklist

- Step-free access and accessible toilets, stated in publicity
- Seating with space for wheelchairs and pushchairs
- Large-print handouts available
- Microphone and hearing loop if the room has one
- Times that suit the audience (after school, after work, daytime for retirees and parents of babies)
- Sensory-friendly options for children's programs where possible

## Evaluation that's worth the effort

Counts tell you reach. Outcomes need a question. A one-question card ("What will you do differently after today?") or a quick show of hands is usually enough. Don't survey every program; pick a few each season.

## Common mistakes

- Publicity that starts a week before.
- Booking a presenter without written terms.
- Forgetting staff hours in the cost.
- Not recording numbers, then having nothing to show at budget time.
- Running the same program forever because it's always been done.
