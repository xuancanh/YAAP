# Qualification and stage exits

Qualification answers one question: will this buyer buy something, from us, in this period? A framework is a checklist of what you must know to answer it. Use the company's chosen framework; if none is set, use the one below.

## MEDDPICC as a checklist

MEDDPICC is a widely used enterprise qualification checklist. Each letter is something you should know and be able to evidence.

| Letter | Question | Evidence that counts |
|---|---|---|
| **M**etrics | What measurable outcome does the buyer expect? | their number, in their words ("cut month-end close from ten days to five") |
| **E**conomic buyer | Who can approve the spend? | you have met them or have a confirmed meeting |
| **D**ecision criteria | What will they judge options on? | a written or stated list, ideally ranked |
| **D**ecision process | What steps lead to a decision? | named steps, people and dates |
| **P**aper process | What happens from "yes" to signature? | legal, security, procurement steps and lead times |
| **I**dentify pain | What problem forces a change, and what if they do nothing? | a consequence with a cost or a deadline |
| **C**hampion | Who sells for us when we are not there? | they have done something for us (shared info, arranged a meeting) |
| **C**ompetition | What else are they considering, including doing nothing? | named alternatives |

Mark each letter **known**, **partly known** or **unknown**, with the evidence. Unknowns become the agenda for the next meeting.

**BANT** (budget, authority, need, timeline) is a lighter check, fine for small, fast deals or for SDR qualification. Use MEDDPICC when several people decide.

## Stage exit criteria

A stage is a statement about the buyer, not about your effort. Use the company's stage definitions; a typical set looks like this:

| Stage | Exit criteria (all must be true) |
|---|---|
| **Discovery** | pain and metrics identified; buyer agrees to evaluate; next meeting booked |
| **Evaluation** | decision criteria and process known; champion identified; technical evaluation planned with the Sales Engineer |
| **Proposal** | economic buyer engaged; solution and price presented; mutual close plan agreed |
| **Negotiation** | verbal selection; paper process started; open terms listed |
| **Closed-won** | signed order form received |
| **Closed-lost** | buyer chose another option or no decision; reason recorded |

Move a deal back if criteria stop being true (the champion left, the budget was frozen).

## Champion test

Someone who likes you is a coach, not a champion. A champion:

- Has power or influence in the decision
- Personally gains if the deal succeeds
- Has already acted for you, such as introducing you to the economic buyer

If you cannot name an action they took, test them: ask for an introduction or for the decision process in writing.

## Disqualify early

Disqualifying a deal is a good result. It frees time for deals that will close. Disqualify, or move out of the current period, when:

- There is no pain with a consequence ("nice to have")
- You cannot reach anyone with budget authority after repeated attempts
- The decision criteria clearly favour a capability we do not have (check with the Sales Engineer)
- The timeline is outside the period and nothing forces it earlier

Record the reason in closed-lost or in the next step.

## Pitfalls

- **Filling every letter on the first call.** Qualification builds over meetings; mark what you do not know.
- **Assumed economic buyer.** A title is not authority. Ask how the last similar purchase was approved.
- **Ignoring "do nothing".** Most lost deals go to no decision. Treat inertia as a named competitor.
- **Stage by hope.** If you would not explain the evidence to your manager, the deal is not in that stage.
