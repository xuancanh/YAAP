# Forecast categories

A forecast is a prediction you are accountable for. Categories exist so your manager can add your deals to everyone else's and know what each number means. Use the company's definitions if they differ from these.

## Definitions

| Category | Meaning | Evidence test (all should be true) |
|---|---|---|
| **Commit** | You would bet on it closing this period at this amount. | economic buyer has said yes or is in final approval; paper process known and on schedule; close plan confirmed by the buyer; no open blocker |
| **Best case** | Could close this period if things go right. | strong pain and champion; decision process known; one or two open items with a path to resolve them in time |
| **Pipeline** | Real opportunity, not expected this period. | qualified, but timing, budget or decision still open |
| **Omitted / closed** | Not counted. | lost, pushed out of the period, or disqualified |

A deal's category is about this period only. A great deal that will sign next quarter is pipeline now.

## Questions to ask before calling commit

- Has the economic buyer personally confirmed, or are you hearing it from the champion?
- Do you know every remaining step, and does each one fit before period end with time to spare?
- Is the amount final, or still being negotiated?
- What could stop it? If you can name something likely, it's best case.

## Writing a change

Every change in category, amount or close date needs a one-line reason that states a fact:

- Good: "Moved commit → best case: their legal asked for a new data processing addendum; review takes about two weeks per their counsel; signature now unlikely before the 28th."
- Weak: "Slipped", "Customer busy", "Timing".

Your submission per deal:

```
Deal / amount / close date / category (previous → now)
Reason for any change: …
Next step: what, who, date
Risk to this call: …
```

## The weekly rhythm

1. Update every deal's stage, next step, amount and close date in the CRM first.
2. Review the close plans for commit and best-case deals.
3. Set categories using the evidence tests.
4. Submit totals and the per-deal notes before the forecast call.
5. After the period, compare what you called with what closed (see forecast accuracy in [sales metrics](sales-metrics.md)) and note what you misjudged.

## Common forecasting errors

- **Sandbagging**: holding back deals you expect to close. It hides capacity and makes planning harder. Call it as you see it.
- **Happy ears**: hearing a yes in a friendly meeting. Look for actions, not words.
- **End-of-period hope**: piling deals into the last week without a buyer reason.
- **Stale close dates**: dates in the past are not a forecast. Fix them before the call.
- **Amount drift**: committing the list price when the buyer is negotiating. Commit what you expect to sign.

## Using history

Look back at your last few periods:

- What share of your commit closed? Of best case?
- Where did deals slip — security, legal, budget?

If commit regularly misses, tighten your own evidence test. If best case regularly closes, you may be too cautious. Note what you learned in your forecast file so your judgement improves each period.
