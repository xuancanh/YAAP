# Discovery question bank

Discovery is where you learn what the buyer needs and whether you can help. Good discovery is mostly listening. Pick a handful of questions per call from the groups below, based on what your qualification still lacks.

## Before the call

- Write two or three **hypotheses**: "They likely struggle with X because of Y." Questions test hypotheses; they don't fish.
- Check what you already know (account plan, SDR handoff, website, recent news) so you don't ask what they told the SDR.
- Pick the one outcome for this call: e.g. "confirm pain and get a meeting with the operations director".

## Current state

- Walk me through how you handle [process] today.
- Who is involved, and where does it slow down?
- What tools do you use for it, and what do you like about them?
- How do you measure whether it's working?

## Pain and impact

- What prompted you to look at this now?
- What happens if nothing changes this year?
- How does this problem show up for your team? For your boss?
- Have you tried to fix it before? What happened?
- Can you put a number on it — hours, cost, revenue, risk?

## Desired outcome

- If this went perfectly, what would be different in six months?
- How would you know it worked? What number would move?
- Who else would care about that result?

## Decision and process

- How do decisions like this usually get made here?
- Who else needs to be involved before you could move ahead?
- Who signs off on the budget for this?
- What does your team need to see to choose an option?
- Have you bought something similar recently? How did that go from choice to signature?
- Are there security, legal or procurement reviews we should plan for?

## Alternatives

- What other options are you looking at, including keeping things as they are?
- What would make you choose one over another?

## Techniques

- **Ask one question at a time** and let silence work.
- **Follow up on the answer** before moving on: "Tell me more about that", "What do you mean by slow?", "What did that cost you?"
- **Summarise and confirm**: "So the real issue is…, and it matters because… Did I get that right?"
- **Ask about the last time**, not hypotheticals: "When did that last happen?" gets facts; "Would you ever…?" gets guesses.
- **Mind the ratio.** If you are talking more than the buyer, you are presenting, not discovering.

## Write-up template (same day)

```
Account / opportunity / date / attendees (name, role)
Hypotheses: confirmed / rejected / still open
Current state: …
Pain and impact (their words, with numbers if given): …
Desired outcome and metric: …
Decision process and people: …
Alternatives: …
Qualification update: per framework letter — known / partly / unknown
Next step agreed: what, who, date
Open questions for next call: …
```

Update the CRM fields and the account plan from the write-up, then send the buyer a short recap with the next step.

## Pitfalls

- **Pitching too early.** If you demo before you understand the pain, you demo the wrong thing.
- **Leading questions.** "Wouldn't it be great if…" teaches you nothing.
- **Accepting the first answer.** The first answer is usually the symptom; the second or third is the cause.
- **Skipping process questions** because they feel awkward. Unknown process is the main reason close dates slip.
