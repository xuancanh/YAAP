# Feedback and performance reviews

A review cycle is only as good as the evidence in it. Your job is the process and the guidance: managers write the reviews and, with leadership, decide outcomes. Pay decisions are taken separately by the humans who own them. This doc gives you the feedback model to teach, the guidance to hand managers, and the calibration pack.

## SBI: Situation, Behaviour, Impact

The Center for Creative Leadership's Situation-Behaviour-Impact model keeps feedback specific and about what someone did, not who they are.

- **Situation:** when and where. "In Tuesday's customer call with the onboarding team…"
- **Behaviour:** what you could observe, with no interpretation. "…you summarised the three open issues and named an owner for each…"
- **Impact:** the effect it had on you, the team or the work. "…so the customer left with a clear plan and we closed two issues that week."

CCL also describes an extension, SBII, that adds **Intent**: ask what the person meant to achieve, which helps when impact and intent differ. "What were you aiming for when you cut the demo short?"

Before and after:

- Not: "You're not a team player."
- But: "In Monday's planning (S), you committed the team to a Friday date without checking with the two engineers on it (B). They worked late to meet it and one raised it with me (I). What was behind the date? (Intent)"

SBI works the same way for positive feedback, and it lands better than "great job" because people know what to repeat.

## Guidance for managers writing reviews

Hand this to managers at the start of each cycle, adapted to the company's scale or no-ratings approach.

```markdown
# Writing your reviews: <cycle>
1. Start from the goals and role expectations agreed at the start of the period.
2. For each goal: what was delivered, the evidence (link to work, metric, feedback), and the impact.
3. Cover the whole period. Check your notes from early months; recent events crowd out older ones.
4. Use SBI for every strength and development point. One specific example beats three adjectives.
5. Separate performance (what was delivered) from potential or style unless the framework asks for them.
6. Development: one or two concrete things to do differently, with support offered.
7. Avoid comments about personality, personal life, health, family, age, or anything linked to a protected characteristic.
8. Rating (if used): pick the level whose definition the evidence supports, not the one you'd like to give.
9. Submit by <date>. Calibration is <date>.
```

## Calibration pack

Prepare this in aggregate. Individual documents stay in the HR system, never in `/org/` or a channel.

- Distribution of draft ratings by team and overall (counts, not names in shared files).
- Managers whose ratings sit consistently at one end compared with their peers.
- Cases flagged for discussion: rating not supported by the written evidence, big changes from last cycle, missing evidence.
- Aggregate checks for patterns that could signal bias, such as rating gaps between groups. Run these only where the HR system holds the data lawfully and the groups are big enough. Report them to the accountable humans, not in channels.

In the session, ask "what's the evidence?" for each flagged case. Managers and leadership decide, and you record the outcomes and the reasons.

## Checklist for a cycle

- [ ] Timeline published with dates for self-reflection, peer input, manager draft, calibration, conversations, close.
- [ ] Manager guidance sent, including SBI and the rating definitions.
- [ ] Calibration pack ready two working days before the session.
- [ ] Completion tracked as % of reviews submitted by the deadline (aggregate only).
- [ ] Conversations held within the target after close.
- [ ] Retro written into the plan for next cycle.

## Common pitfalls

- **Recency bias.** The last six weeks dominate the review. Ask managers for evidence from each quarter.
- **Halo and horns.** One strength or one mistake colours every section.
- **Vague adjectives** ("proactive", "abrasive") with no behaviour behind them.
- **Central tendency.** Everyone rated in the middle to avoid hard conversations, which makes the cycle useless.
- **Surprises.** Nothing in a review should be news. If it is, feedback isn't happening during the period, and that's the fix.
- **Mixing in legal-sensitive matters.** A performance concern tied to health, leave, a complaint, or a protected characteristic goes to a qualified HR or legal professional (see [Fairness and legal lines](./fairness-and-legal-lines.md)).
