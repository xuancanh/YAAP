# Onboarding 30/60/90

Good onboarding is mostly logistics done early and a clear answer to "what does good look like by when?". People Ops owns the checklist. The hiring manager owns the 30/60/90 plan and the check-ins. You make sure both exist for every starter, and you measure completion (see [People metrics](./people-metrics.md)).

This doc covers human employees. Onboarding an agent into your unit follows people-team-management step 6.

## Before day one

- [ ] Signed offer and contract on file in the HR system (signed by the humans authorised to sign).
- [ ] Right-to-work or equivalent eligibility checks done as local law requires, by the people qualified to do them.
- [ ] Payroll and benefits inputs sent before the payroll cut-off.
- [ ] Accounts created with the access the role needs and no more. IT tickets have owners and dates.
- [ ] Equipment shipped or ready.
- [ ] Welcome note drafted for the hiring manager to send: start time, where to go or which link to open, who meets them.
- [ ] A buddy named (not their manager).
- [ ] First-week calendar booked: manager 1:1, team intro, key stakeholder meetings, required training.
- [ ] 30/60/90 plan drafted by the manager.

## First week

- [ ] Day one: accounts work, equipment works, they've met their manager and buddy.
- [ ] Required policies and training assigned with due dates (mandatory compliance training is tracked to completion).
- [ ] They know how work flows: the board, where docs live, who reviews what.
- [ ] One small, real task they can finish this week.

## 30/60/90 plan template

```markdown
# 30/60/90 plan: <role>, start <date>
Manager: <name>   Buddy: <name>

## By day 30: learn
- Understands: <product, customers, the team's key results>
- Has met: <people/teams>
- Has delivered: <one small real piece of work>
- Check-in on <date>: what's clear, what's confusing, what's missing

## By day 60: contribute
- Owns: <a defined area or recurring task>
- Delivered: <1-2 outcomes with a measure>
- Check-in on <date>: feedback both ways (SBI)

## By day 90: own
- Accountable for: <outcome tied to a key result>
- Working independently on: <…>
- Check-in on <date>: on track for the role? End of probation, if any, decided by the manager
```

Keep each line checkable. "Gets familiar with the codebase" isn't. "Shipped one fix through code review" is.

## Measuring it

- Onboarding completion within 30 days, computed from the checklist items marked done in the HR system.
- Day-30 and day-90 check-ins held on time (% held by their date).
- New-hire feedback at day 30: two or three questions, reported only in aggregate above the anonymity threshold.
- Early attrition by hiring cohort.

## Worked example (made-up)

Five people started in March. Four completed every required step by day 30. One was missing mandatory security training because the course link was broken. Completion is 4 ÷ 5 = 80%. The finding goes in the monthly audit with the fix (link repaired, owner People Ops), and the starter is named only in the HR system, not in the audit file.

## Common pitfalls

- **Access on day three.** A new hire with no accounts on day one is the most common and most avoidable failure. Start IT requests as soon as the offer is accepted.
- **No plan, only a reading list.** People want a real task in week one.
- **Probation decisions without evidence.** Check-ins with notes at 30/60/90 are what make a probation decision fair. The manager decides, and HR or legal advice is needed before ending employment.
- **Over-provisioning access** "to save time later".
- **Treating remote starters the same as on-site ones** without adjusting equipment logistics and intro calls.
