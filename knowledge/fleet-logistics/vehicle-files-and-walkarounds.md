# Vehicle files and walkaround checks

One file per vehicle, one radar across the fleet. The radar's job is that no inspection, renewal or service date arrives as a surprise.

## Vehicle file contents

```
Vehicle: <reg>   Make/model/year:   VIN:   Weight class:
Assigned driver(s):
Documents: registration, insurance (policy, renewal), roadworthiness test certificate,
           operator licence entry if applicable, tax, finance/lease agreement
Service schedule: <interval by distance/time, from the manufacturer>
Inspection schedule: <safety inspection interval, if required for this vehicle>
Tyres: sizes, last change per axle
Defect log: <link>
Repair history: date, work, cost, garage
Fuel/charging: card, typical economy
Odometer readings: weekly
```

## Fleet radar (Monday)

```
| Vehicle | Item | Due date / mileage | Days or miles left | Booked? | Notes |
```

Include: services, safety inspections, roadworthiness tests, insurance and tax renewals, tachograph calibration (where fitted), finance or lease end, warranty end, recalls. Flag anything due within a set lead time (for example, four weeks), and anything overdue in red.

## Daily walkaround checklist (general)

Many regulators expect a walkaround before the vehicle's first use each day. In GB, DVSA guidance says at least one walkaround check should be done in any 24-hour period of use, with defects reported in writing (checked 2026-11). Typical checks:

**Outside**
- [ ] Lights and indicators work; lenses intact.
- [ ] Tyres: inflation, damage, tread, wheel nuts secure.
- [ ] Mirrors and glass: clean, uncracked, secure.
- [ ] Bodywork and doors secure; load secure.
- [ ] Number plates clean and readable.
- [ ] No fuel, oil or fluid leaks.

**Inside**
- [ ] Brakes work, including the parking brake.
- [ ] Steering: no excess play.
- [ ] Horn, wipers, washers.
- [ ] Warning lights on the dashboard.
- [ ] Seat belts.
- [ ] Tachograph or logging device working, where fitted.

## Defects

1. **Record every defect in writing:** vehicle, date, what, who reported, who it was passed to. Record "nil defects" when none are found if the regime expects it.
2. **Safety-critical defect (brakes, steering, tyres, lights, anything that makes it unroadworthy):** the vehicle stays off the road until a competent person has repaired it and signed it off.
3. **Minor defect:** schedule repair, note the date it was fixed.
4. **Close the loop:** link the repair record to the defect.

Keep records for as long as the regulator requires (GB guidance gives a retention period; check it for your jurisdiction).

## Worked example

Monday radar shows Van 2's safety inspection due in nine days and its insurance renewal in 25. Van 3's driver reported Friday "nearside rear tyre bulge". Status: Van 3 off the road; garage booked 08:00 Tuesday; Van 1 covers its Tuesday run; insurance renewal quote requested.

## Common mistakes

- Walkaround checks ticked without looking.
- Defects reported verbally and lost.
- Service intervals tracked by date only, when mileage comes first (or the other way round).
- One person holds all the dates in their head.
