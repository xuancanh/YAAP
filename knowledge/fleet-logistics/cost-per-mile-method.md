# Cost per mile method

Jobs get quoted at a loss when only fuel is counted. The true cost includes the vehicle's fixed costs, wear, and the driver's time. Work it out monthly per vehicle, then use it for every quote. Use miles or kilometres consistently.

## The two kinds of cost

**Fixed (time-based)** costs, which you pay whether the vehicle moves or not:

- depreciation, or lease/finance payments;
- insurance;
- vehicle tax, licences and permits;
- roadworthiness tests and safety inspections on a schedule;
- telematics, tracking and software subscriptions;
- parking or depot costs;
- a share of overheads (office, admin, accountancy).

**Running (distance-based)** costs, which rise with use:

- fuel or charging;
- tyres;
- servicing and repairs;
- tolls and congestion charges (often per job instead).

**Driver costs** are usually best per hour: wages plus employer costs (taxes, pension contributions, holiday cover).

## The calculation

```
Fixed cost per month ÷ miles per month   = fixed cost per mile
Running costs per month ÷ miles per month = running cost per mile
Total vehicle cost per mile = fixed + running

Driver cost per hour = (wages + employer costs) ÷ paid hours
```

Note: fixed cost per mile falls as miles rise. A vehicle sitting idle is expensive per mile.

## Pricing a job

```
Job cost = (job miles × vehicle cost per mile)
         + (job hours, including loading and waiting × driver cost per hour)
         + tolls, parking, other direct costs
Price    = job cost + margin
```

Count the **empty miles** too: the return trip or the drive to the first collection.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)

Van A, one month: lease 400, insurance 120, tax and tests averaged 30, tracking 20, overhead share 80: fixed 650. Fuel 900, tyres averaged 60, servicing and repairs averaged 140: running 1,100. Miles: 3,500.

- Fixed per mile: 650 ÷ 3,500 ≈ 0.19
- Running per mile: 1,100 ÷ 3,500 ≈ 0.31
- Vehicle cost per mile ≈ 0.50

Driver: 2,800 a month including employer costs, 160 paid hours: 17.50 per hour.

Quote: 80 miles round trip, 3.5 hours including loading: 80 × 0.50 = 40; 3.5 × 17.50 = 61.25; tolls 10. Cost 111.25. With the operator's chosen margin of 20%: about 134. The customer had been charged 90 in the past, which was below cost.

## Monthly review

- Cost per mile per vehicle; which is highest and why?
- Miles per vehicle; any idle vehicles adding fixed cost?
- Repair costs trending up on older vehicles (replacement decision)?
- Quotes won and lost against the calculated price.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing on fuel alone.
- Ignoring empty miles and waiting time.
- Using one fleet-wide figure when vehicles differ a lot.
- Not updating after fuel, insurance or wage changes.
