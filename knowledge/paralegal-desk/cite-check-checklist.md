# Cite-check checklist

A cite-check answers four questions about every authority: does it exist, does it say what we claim, is it still good law, and is it cited correctly? **For attorney review.** The attorney decides how to handle anything flagged.

Courts have sanctioned lawyers for filing briefs that cited cases which didn't exist, including ones produced by AI tools. Never treat an authority as verified unless you've seen it in a reliable source.

## The four questions

**1. Does it exist?**
- [ ] Found in an official reporter, official court website, official statute or code, or a reputable legal database
- [ ] Case name, court, year, volume and page match
- [ ] If it can't be found: mark **NOT FOUND**. Don't guess a correction.

**2. Does it say what we claim?**
- [ ] The proposition in the brief matches what the authority holds, not just a phrase in it
- [ ] Holding versus dicta noted where it matters
- [ ] Quotations checked word for word, including emphasis, ellipses and alterations
- [ ] Pin cites point to the right page or paragraph
- [ ] Majority opinion, not a concurrence or dissent (unless the brief says so)

**3. Is it still good law?**
- [ ] Checked with a citator service (where available) for reversal, overruling, or negative treatment
- [ ] Statutes and rules: the version in force at the relevant time; amendments since
- [ ] Flag anything with negative treatment for the attorney. The desk notes it; the attorney judges its effect.

**4. Is it cited correctly?**
- [ ] Format follows the required style (court rules, local rules, or the citation guide the jurisdiction uses)
- [ ] Short forms and "id." used correctly after the full cite
- [ ] Parallel citations if the court requires them
- [ ] Subsequent history included where required

## Results log

```
| # | Page | Citation as written | Exists | Supports | Good law | Format | Notes |
|---|------|---------------------|--------|----------|----------|--------|-------|
| 1 | 4 | Smith v. Jones, … | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | fix pin cite | p. 212 not 221 |
| 2 | 6 | … | NOT FOUND | — | — | — | No match in any source; attorney to review |
| 3 | 7 | … | ✓ | partial | negative treatment | ✓ | Later case limits holding; attorney to assess |
```

Send the log with a short summary: how many citations checked, how many clean, and the items needing the attorney's decision, listed first.

## Red flags

- A case that appears only in one secondary source or a chat transcript
- A quotation you can't find in the opinion
- A citation where the volume and page don't match the case name
- A "leading case" you've never seen cited elsewhere
- Party names that sound generic or invented
- Statutes cited without a year or version, in an area that changes often

## Worked example

Brief says: "Courts routinely hold that X (Smith v. Jones, 123 F.4th 456, 460 (9th Cir. 2025))."

- Exists? Look up 123 F.4th 456. Found; case name matches.
- Supports? Page 460 discusses X, but in a footnote about a different issue; the holding is narrower. Flag: "partial support; holding concerns Y."
- Good law? Citator shows no negative treatment as of today.
- Format? Correct.

(This example is illustrative; the citation is not a real case.)

## Common mistakes

- Checking that a case exists but not what it says.
- Trusting quotations copied from another brief.
- Correcting a citation silently instead of flagging it.
- Forgetting to re-check statutes after an amendment.
