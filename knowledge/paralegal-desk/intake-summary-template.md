# Intake summary template

The intake summary lets the attorney walk into the first meeting already knowing the story, the paperwork and the open questions. It organises; it doesn't assess. **For attorney review.**

## Before the summary

- **Conflict check first.** Run all names (client, opposing parties, related companies, key witnesses) through the firm's conflicts process before substantive information is collected or shared. The result goes to the attorney.
- **Client code.** Assign the matter code. Names appear in the intake file only where needed.
- **Urgent dates.** If the client mentions any date (a court date, a letter with a deadline, an event that happened long ago), flag it at the top. Limitation periods and response deadlines may be running; the attorney determines them.

## Template

```
# Intake summary — <client code> — <date>
FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW

## Urgent
- <any date mentioned, document with a deadline, or "none identified">

## Who
- Client: <code>, <role (individual / company / representative)>
- Other parties: <names and roles>
- Others involved: <witnesses, advisers, insurers>
- Conflict check: <status, run by, date>

## What the client wants
<one or two sentences, in the client's words where possible>

## Facts (chronological, sourced)
| Date | What happened | Source |
|------|---------------|--------|
|      |               | client interview / doc C-#### |

## Documents
- Received: <list with control numbers>
- Mentioned but not yet received: <list>
- To request: <list>

## Issues to explore (as questions, not conclusions)
- <e.g. "Was there a written contract, and what does it say about notice?">
- <e.g. "When did the client first learn of the problem?">

## Gaps and inconsistencies
- <conflicting dates, missing pieces>

## Client practicalities
- Preferred contact method, language, accessibility needs
- Anything the client said about cost or timing expectations
```

## Writing the facts

- Use neutral, factual words: "the client states", "the email of 14 March says".
- Separate what documents show from what the client recalls.
- Keep the client's own key phrases in quotation marks; they often matter later.
- No conclusions about liability, strength of claim or outcome.

## Issues as questions

| Not this | This |
|---|---|
| "The landlord breached the lease." | "Does the lease say who is responsible for repairs to the boiler?" |
| "This is out of time." | "When did the events occur, and what limitation rules apply? (attorney to determine)" |
| "Strong case for unfair dismissal." | "What reason was given for the dismissal, and was it in writing?" |

## Worked example (short)

Client code M-2611. Client says their builder abandoned a kitchen job in August, paid 60% upfront, and has stopped answering. Has a quote and some texts.

- Urgent: none stated; attorney to consider any time limits.
- Documents received: quote (C-0001), screenshot texts (C-0002–0005). Not yet received: bank transfer records, any contract terms.
- Questions: Was the quote accepted in writing? What do the texts say about completion dates? Were any stage payments tied to milestones?

## Common mistakes

- Starting the intake before the conflict check.
- Writing conclusions instead of questions.
- Missing a date the client mentioned in passing.
- Mixing client recollection and documentary evidence in the same column.
