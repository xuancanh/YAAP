# Budget and payment calendar

Weddings rarely go over budget on the big lines. They go over through dozens of small yeses and the lines nobody wrote down. The fix is a complete category list from day one, a contingency you defend, and a payment calendar the weekly sweep can see.

Research the price ranges for the region and date them. Don't use national averages from articles. They hide huge regional differences.

## The easily missed lines

- Service charges and taxes added to venue and catering quotes
- Vendor meals (photographers and bands usually need feeding; check their contracts)
- Overtime charges if the day runs late
- Delivery, setup and collection fees for hired items
- Corkage or cake-cutting fees
- Postage on invitations, especially heavy or unusual sizes
- Marriage notice or licence fees, and certified copies
- Travel and accommodation for vendors at distant venues
- Outfit changes, shoes and underlayers
- Emergency kit, and cash for tips on the day

## Contingency

Set a contingency line from the start, as a percentage of the ceiling that the couple chooses and records in decisions.md. Ten per cent is the planner default. Rules:

- Spending from the contingency is a decision, logged with its reason.
- Before using it on a nice-to-have, the scope guard asks: what does this displace?

## Payment calendar (payments.md)

One row per payment, sorted by date:

| Due | Vendor | Amount | Type | Paid? | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-12-01 | Venue | | Deposit | ✔ 2026-11-02 | INV-221 |
| 2027-03-15 | Photographer | | 2nd instalment | | |
| 2027-05-20 | Caterer | | Final headcount + balance | | Contract cl. 7 |

The weekly sweep flags anything due within 30 days. Final headcount deadlines are payment events too, so they go here.

## The health check (top of every money conversation)

```
Ceiling: <amount>   Committed: <amount> (<%>)
Still open: <categories>
Projected final: <committed + expected for open lines>
Contingency left: <amount>
```

## Worked example: the overspend

Six months out, the committed total plus the expected open lines comes to 6% over the ceiling, mainly because the catering quote went up when the guest list grew from 90 to 104.

Options for the couple:

1. **Cut the "could" tier** of the guest list back towards 90. This saves the per-head cost for each guest removed.
2. **Change the bar**: a limited drinks package instead of an open bar.
3. **Trim the lines guests rarely notice**: favours and extra decor.
4. **Use part of the contingency**, which leaves less cover for surprises.

The couple chose a mix of 2 and 3 and recorded it in decisions.md. The projected final is now just under the ceiling.

## Common mistakes

- Budgeting catering on the hoped-for headcount, not the honest one.
- Forgetting that a deposit is usually non-refundable. Read the terms before paying.
- No record of who pays for what, when families contribute.
- Leaving the contingency until something goes wrong, then finding it's already spent.
