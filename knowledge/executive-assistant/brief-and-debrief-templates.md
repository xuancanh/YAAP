# Brief and debrief templates

A brief is read in the minute before something starts. Lead with what the reader must know or decide, then the background. If it doesn't change what they do, leave it out.

## Five-line morning briefing

```
1. Today's one thing: <the meeting or task that matters most, and why>
2. Calendar: <count of meetings; first at HH:MM; any clash or travel squeeze>
3. Needs you: <decisions or replies waiting on them, oldest first, max 3>
4. Owed to you: <anything due in today from others; chase planned?>
5. Heads-up: <tomorrow's big item, a deadline this week, or nothing>
```

**Example**

1. Today's one thing: the 14:00 budget review with Priya. She'll ask about the Q1 hiring line.
2. Calendar: six meetings, first at 09:00. 12:30 and 13:00 overlap; I suggest moving the 13:00 vendor call.
3. Needs you: approve the offsite venue (deposit due Thursday); reply to Marco's partnership email (5 days old).
4. Owed to you: legal's contract redlines were due yesterday. I'll chase at 10:00 unless you say not to.
5. Heads-up: board deck draft due Friday.

## One-page meeting brief

```
Meeting: <title>    When: <date, time, length>    Where: <room / link>
Why it matters: <one sentence: what the user wants out of it>
Attendees: <name — role — what they care about — last interaction>
Background: <3–5 bullets, newest first>
Open items from last time: <commitments, both directions>
Likely asks of you: <what they will want>
Suggested goal and questions: <2–3>
Materials: <links, pre-reads, page numbers that matter>
```

Keep attendee notes factual and professional. Assume the brief could be forwarded.

## Debrief: from scrappy notes to actions

Work through any notes, however rough, in this order:

1. **Decisions made.** One line each.
2. **Commitments.** Each with owner, what exactly, and due date. If a due date was not said, propose one and mark it "(proposed)".
3. **Open questions.** Who is finding out.
4. **Follow-ups to draft.** Thank-yous, recaps, introductions promised.

```
Meeting: <title, date>
Decided: …
Actions:
| Owner | Action | Due | Direction (we owe / owed to us) |
Open: …
Drafts ready: <list>
```

Then add every action to the commitment tracker (see [commitment tracking](commitment-tracking-method.md)).

## Recap email to attendees

"Thanks all. Recap from today: we agreed X. Next steps: Priya sends the revised numbers by Wed 12th; I'll confirm the venue by Friday. Shout if I've missed anything."

Short, actions with names and dates, and an invitation to correct.

## Common mistakes

- Briefings that list every meeting instead of the one that matters.
- Attendee notes that would embarrass the user if forwarded.
- Debriefs that summarise discussion but miss who promised what.
- Actions with no date, which never close.
