# Commitment tracking method

A commitment is any "I'll…" or "Can you…?" that someone accepted. Most dropped balls are small promises said in passing. Capture them all, in both directions, with a date.

## What counts

- **We owe:** the user said they'd send, review, introduce, decide or call.
- **Owed to us:** someone else agreed to do something for the user.
- **Soft promises:** "Let's grab coffee next month", "I'll think about it". Log them with a light-touch date; they matter for relationships.

## The log

```
| ID | Direction | Who | What (specific) | Source (meeting/email, date) | Due | Status | Next nudge |
```

- **Specific "what":** "Send Q3 hiring plan to Priya", not "Hiring stuff".
- **Due:** the stated date, or a proposed one marked "(proposed)".
- **Status:** open / waiting / done / dropped (with reason). "Dropped" is fine when it's a decision; silent dropping is not.

## Capture points

- Every meeting debrief.
- Every forwarded thread the user shares.
- The user's own notes ("remind me to…").
- Calendar invites with homework in the description.

## Friday sweep

Run weekly, in this order:

1. **Overdue, we owe.** List for the user with a suggested action: do it, renegotiate the date, or drop with a note to the other person.
2. **Due next week, we owe.** Block time if needed.
3. **Overdue, owed to us.** Draft chases.
4. **Stale soft promises.** Suggest which to act on or let go.
5. **Close done items** and note anything learned (a person who is always late; a type of promise the user over-makes).

Keep the sweep output short: at most a handful of items per section, oldest first.

## Chase wording ladder

Match the tone to the relationship and how late it is. Always drafts for the user's approval.

1. **Friendly nudge:** "Hi Sam, just checking the redlines are still on track for this week?"
2. **Specific ask:** "Hi Sam, we need the redlines by Wednesday to keep the signing date. Can you confirm?"
3. **Escalate with care:** offer a call, or involve the person's manager only with the user's say-so.

## Renegotiating a promise the user can't keep

Tell the other person before the due date, with a new date and a reason in one line: "I said Friday for the deck; it'll be Tuesday, as I'm waiting on final numbers. Sorry for the shift." Early renegotiation costs much less trust than a missed date.

## Worked example

From a Tuesday meeting: the user said "I'll intro you to Lena", and the vendor said "We'll send pricing by end of week". Log: (we owe) intro Marco ↔ Lena, due Thu (proposed); (owed) vendor pricing, due Fri. On Friday the sweep shows the intro done and the pricing missing; a level-1 chase is drafted for Monday morning.

## Common mistakes

- Only tracking what others owe, never what the user owes.
- Vague items that can't be marked done.
- Chasing too soon or in the wrong tone for a senior relationship.
