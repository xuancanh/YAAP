# Building a region-tuned season calendar

Generic calendars ("sow peas in March") are wrong for most holdings. Build windows from the holding's own climate, then refine them every year from records.

## Step 1: anchor dates

Gather, for the holding's location:

- **Average last spring frost and first autumn frost.** Local agricultural extension services, national weather services, or long-time neighbours are the best sources. Note the source and treat averages as averages: late frosts happen.
- **Soil temperature and workability.** Many seeds won't germinate in cold soil, and working wet soil damages structure. A soil thermometer and the "squeeze test" (a handful that crumbles rather than smears) beat the calendar.
- **Daylength turning points** that affect crops and poultry laying.
- **Rainfall pattern:** wet months that block field access, dry spells that need irrigation.

## Step 2: work backwards from target dates

For each crop or animal enterprise, start from the date you want the result and count back:

- **Crops:** target harvest → days to maturity (from the seed packet, adjusted for your records) → sowing or transplanting date → seed-starting date indoors.
- **Breeding:** target birth season (grass growth, weather, market) → gestation length (from the vet or breed society) → when to put animals together → pre-breeding health checks.
- **Autumn jobs:** first frost → drain and protect water lines, store equipment, check winter feed and bedding stocks.

## Step 3: write windows, not dates

```
| Job | Window opens | Window closes | Trigger (what must be true) | Prep needed before | Notes from last year |
| Sow carrots, bed 3 | mid-Mar | end-Apr | soil workable and warm enough per seed packet | bed weeded, fleece ready | 2025: first sowing failed, too wet |
```

A trigger ("soil workable") is what moves the job inside its window.

## Step 4: the Monday radar

Each week, list:

1. **Windows opening in the next two weeks** and their prep.
2. **Windows closing soon** with the job not done.
3. **Weather-sensitive jobs** for the week, with a fallback task for wet days.
4. **Recurring animal tasks** due (from the animal register): foot checks, weighing, vet appointments, routine treatments as directed by the vet.
5. **Supplies to order** before they're needed (seed, feed, fencing, fuel).

## Step 5: learn from records

At season end, compare plan against what happened: actual sowing and harvest dates, yields, losses, weather notes. Shift next year's windows by what the records show, not by memory.

## Worked example

A holding's last-frost average is mid-May. Target: outdoor tomatoes planted the week after. Count back about six to eight weeks for seed starting indoors (per the variety's packet), so seed-start in late March. The radar flags "harden off seedlings" two weeks before planting, and a reminder to keep fleece ready for a late frost.

## Common mistakes

- Using a calendar from a different climate.
- Single dates instead of windows, so one wet week breaks the plan.
- Prep (beds, fencing, housing) not scheduled before the job.
- Not recording what actually happened, so every year starts from scratch.
