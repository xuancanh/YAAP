# Records that compound

A year of notes is a diary. Five years of consistent notes is knowledge: which bed grows good carrots, which ewe always needs help lambing, which supplier's seed fails. Keep the formats simple enough to fill in with muddy hands.

## Bed and field history

```
Bed/field: <id>   Size:   Soil notes:
| Year | Crop/use | Variety | Sown | Harvested | Yield | Inputs (what, when) | Problems | Notes |
```

Use it to plan rotation (avoid the same plant family in the same place year after year) and to see which inputs actually paid off.

## Animal register

```
| ID/tag | Species/breed | Sex | Born / arrived (date, from) | Dam/sire | Status | Left (date, to, reason) |
```

Alongside, a per-animal or per-group schedule:

```
| Animal/group | Routine task | Due | Done | By whom | Notes |
```

Routine tasks (worming, vaccination, foot trimming, weighing) follow the vet's plan. The assistant tracks dates; it never chooses products or doses.

## Observation log

Short, dated, factual: "12 Apr: hen 7 off feed, separated, vet called." Observations become patterns over time, and give the vet useful history.

## Records that are often legally required

The details vary by country and region; check the official source for the holding's location. Commonly required records include:

- [ ] **Holding registration.** In England, for example, land used to keep livestock needs a County Parish Holding (CPH) number before animals arrive (GOV.UK, checked 2026-11).
- [ ] **Animal identification** (tags, passports, flock or herd registers) by species.
- [ ] **Movement records** on and off the holding, sometimes reported within set times.
- [ ] **Veterinary medicine records:** what was given, when, by whom, and withdrawal periods before meat, milk or eggs are used.
- [ ] **Pesticide and fertiliser application records.**
- [ ] **Feed and food traceability** if selling.
- [ ] **Poultry registration** above certain flock sizes in some countries.

When unsure, contact the national animal health agency, the agriculture department, or local trading standards (or equivalent).

## Welfare codes

Many governments publish welfare codes for each species (for example, the sheep welfare code on GOV.UK). In England these codes are guidance, but not following them can be used as evidence in welfare cases. Keep the relevant code for each species on file and note its date.

## Withdrawal periods

When a treated animal's products (meat, milk, eggs) can't be used for a set period, record the end date prominently and add it to the radar. The period comes from the product label and the vet, never from memory.

## Common mistakes

- Records kept in several notebooks that never get combined.
- Recording only problems, so there's no baseline.
- Missing the date the animal left, which breaks movement records.
- Assuming small numbers mean no registration is needed. Many rules apply from the first animal.
