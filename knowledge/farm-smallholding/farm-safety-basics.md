# Farm safety basics

Farming is one of the more dangerous lines of work, and small holdings share the same hazards with fewer people to notice when something goes wrong. The UK's Health and Safety Executive (HSE) and its counterparts elsewhere publish free guidance; use the one for the holding's country. These basics are general, not a substitute for training.

## Machinery and vehicles

**Safe Stop.** Before leaving the seat of a tractor or other vehicle, or letting anyone approach it, follow the Safe Stop routine promoted by HSE and the NFU: **handbrake on, controls in neutral, engine off, key out** (checked 2026-11). Where a machine has to keep running, follow its manual's extra precautions.

Other habits:

- [ ] Never get on or off a moving vehicle.
- [ ] Roll-over protection (cab or frame) fitted and seat belt worn where provided.
- [ ] PTO shafts fully guarded; never step over a turning shaft.
- [ ] Quad bikes and ATVs: training, helmets, and no passengers unless the machine is designed for them.
- [ ] Maintenance done with the machine stopped, raised parts supported, and the key held by the person working.
- [ ] Training before using any unfamiliar machine; read the manual.

## Livestock

- Plan handling: good facilities (pens, races, gates) are safer than strength.
- Treat newly calved cows, bulls and rams with extra caution; never be alone in a pen with a bull.
- Know an escape route before entering a pen.
- Zoonoses (diseases passed from animals to people) are a real risk: wash hands after handling animals, and take extra care around lambing and with pregnant people present. Ask a doctor about specific risks.
- Footpaths through livestock fields bring public-safety duties in some countries; check local guidance.

## Children and visitors

Farms are workplaces with risks that children can't judge.

- [ ] Children kept out of working areas, away from machinery, slurry, grain stores and water.
- [ ] No children riding on tractors or in trailers unless local law and the machine allow it with a proper seat.
- [ ] Chemicals, medicines and sharp tools locked away.
- [ ] Visitors briefed, kept to safe routes, and supervised near animals.

## Chemicals and stores

- Follow the label. Wear the protective equipment it lists.
- Store pesticides, veterinary medicines and fuels securely, away from food and feed.
- Many countries require training or certification to apply professional pesticides.

## Working alone

- Tell someone where you'll be and when you'll be back.
- Carry a charged phone; know where signal works on the holding.
- Agree a check-in routine for risky jobs.
- First-aid kit stocked, and the address and grid reference or what3words-style location written by the phone for emergency calls.

## When to call a professional

- Electrical work, gas appliances and tree felling near structures: qualified contractors.
- Any injury beyond minor first aid: emergency services or medical care.
- Animal illness or injury: the vet.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the engine running "for a second".
- Rushing when tired at the end of the day.
- Assuming a quiet animal will stay quiet.
- Kids "helping" in the yard while machines move.
