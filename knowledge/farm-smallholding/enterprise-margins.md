# Enterprise gross margins

A holding often has several small ventures (eggs, lamb, veg boxes, honey). Overall it may break even while one venture loses money and another carries it. A gross margin per enterprise shows which is which.

## Definitions

- **Enterprise:** a venture you could start or stop on its own (laying hens, a market garden, a holiday let).
- **Output:** the value it produces in the period: sales, plus produce used at home at a fair value, plus change in stock (more animals or stored crops than at the start).
- **Variable costs:** costs that rise and fall with that enterprise: feed, seed, bedding, vet fees, packaging, market fees.
- **Gross margin = output − variable costs.**
- **Fixed (overhead) costs:** costs that don't change much if one enterprise stops: insurance, machinery depreciation, fencing, the main vehicle, utilities.
- **Net margin:** gross margins of all enterprises minus fixed costs.

## Ledger template

```
| Date | Enterprise | Item | In / Out | Amount | Quantity | Notes |
```

Tag every purchase and sale to an enterprise as it happens. Shared items (a feed bag split between hens and pigs) get split by a simple rule, written down once.

## Allocating shared costs

For a fairer picture, allocate significant shared costs by a rule you can defend:

- by area used (land rent, fencing);
- by hours of machinery use;
- by number of animals or livestock units;
- by share of sales.

Keep the rule stable from year to year so comparisons hold.

## Your time

Family labour is usually unpaid and invisible. Log hours per enterprise for a few representative weeks, then show **gross margin per hour**. A venture that earns a little per hour may still be worth it for enjoyment or lifestyle, but that should be a choice, not a surprise.

## Quarterly review

1. Gross margin per enterprise, and per hour of labour.
2. Biggest variable cost line in each; is it rising?
3. Prices: are you charging enough to cover costs (see [selling direct](selling-direct.md))?
4. One change to try next quarter.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)

Laying hens for a quarter: egg sales 600, eggs used at home valued at 60, so output 660. Variable costs: feed 310, bedding 40, egg boxes 35, vet visit 50: total 435. Gross margin 225. Hours logged: about 45. Margin per hour: 5. The family decides to raise prices slightly and buy feed in bulk, rather than expand the flock.

## Tax and grants

Records like these help an accountant and support grant or subsidy claims. Tax treatment of farming income, subsidy rules and VAT or sales tax thresholds are **country-specific**; take them to an accountant or the official agency.

## Common mistakes

- Counting only cash sales, missing produce eaten at home or stock changes.
- Putting every cost into one pot, so no venture can be judged.
- Ignoring labour, then wondering why everyone is exhausted.
- Changing the allocation rule every year.
