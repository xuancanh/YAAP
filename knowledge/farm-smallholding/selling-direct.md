# Selling direct: market-day and food rules

Selling direct earns more per item but brings rules. Research the rules for each product before the first sale, then make market day a routine.

## Rules to research first (jurisdiction-specific)

Food selling rules differ by country, state and product. Check with the local food-safety authority or environmental health office. Common questions:

- [ ] **Registration:** do you need to register as a food business? In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, food businesses must register with the local council (free) at least 28 days before trading, and home-based producers can expect an inspection (Food Standards Agency, checked 2026-11).
- [ ] **Product-specific rules:** eggs (grading, labelling, storage), meat (approved slaughter and cutting), dairy (raw milk is tightly restricted in many places), honey labelling, home-baked goods ("cottage food" rules in some US states).
- [ ] **Labelling:** product name, weight, allergens for processed foods, producer details, best-before or use-by dates.
- [ ] **Hygiene and temperature control:** cool boxes, hand-washing, clean surfaces.
- [ ] **Weights and measures:** scales used for trade may need to be approved or certified.
- [ ] **Market's own rules:** insurance (public liability is commonly required), producer-only rules, pitch fees.
- [ ] **Tax:** registering self-employment or a business; sales tax or VAT thresholds. Ask an accountant.

## Pricing method

1. **Know your cost per unit** from the [enterprise margins](enterprise-margins.md) ledger, including packaging and market fees.
2. **Check the market:** what do similar sellers charge for similar quality? Supermarket prices are a floor, not a target, for premium local produce.
3. **Set a price that covers cost plus a margin you choose.** If the market price won't cover cost, change the product, the costs or the channel.
4. **Use round prices** for quick change, and bundles ("3 for") to move volume.
5. **Review each season.** Raise prices when costs rise; regular customers usually accept a short explanation.

## Market-day checklist

**Day before**
- [ ] Harvest list by expected demand; harvest early morning where possible for freshness.
- [ ] Stock count and price list.
- [ ] Float (change), card reader charged, receipts.
- [ ] Gazebo, table, weights, cloth, signs, bags.
- [ ] Cool boxes and ice packs for anything perishable.

**On the day**
- [ ] Display: full, tidy, tallest at the back, prices visible on everything.
- [ ] Hand-washing and sanitiser.
- [ ] Sampling only within hygiene rules.
- [ ] Note what sold out and when.

**After**
- [ ] Sales by product, unsold stock, cash reconciled.
- [ ] Customer requests and comments in the customer file.
- [ ] Clean equipment.

## Building a following

- Keep a customer file (with consent for contact details) and note preferences.
- A simple sign with the holding's story and growing approach.
- Pre-orders or a weekly box for regulars reduces waste.

## Common mistakes

- Selling before registering or checking product rules.
- Prices set by guesswork, below cost.
- No cool chain for eggs, meat or dairy on a hot day.
- Not recording what sold, so harvest plans don't improve.
