# Toxic foods and hazards

If a pet has eaten something on this list, **call the vet or an animal poison helpline now** (US: ASPCA Animal Poison Control, 888-426-4435, fee may apply). Don't wait for symptoms and don't induce vomiting unless told to. See [Emergency signs](emergency-signs.md).

This list covers the common hazards; it isn't complete. If something isn't listed, that doesn't mean it's safe. Check with the vet.

## Foods (ASPCA)

| Food | Why it's dangerous |
|---|---|
| Chocolate, coffee, caffeine | Methylxanthines: vomiting, racing heart, tremors, seizures. Darker chocolate is more dangerous. |
| Xylitol (sweetener in gum, sweets, baked goods, some peanut butters and toothpaste) | Dangerously low blood sugar and liver damage |
| Grapes, raisins, currants, sultanas | Kidney damage in dogs |
| Onions, garlic, chives, leeks | Damage red blood cells (anaemia); cats are more sensitive |
| Alcohol | Poisoning that can be fatal |
| Raw yeast dough | Expands in the stomach; yeast produces alcohol |
| Macadamia nuts | Weakness, tremors, overheating in dogs |
| Other nuts, fatty foods | Vomiting, diarrhoea, pancreatitis |
| Avocado | Dangerous for birds, rabbits, horses and some other animals |
| Salty foods and snacks | Excess salt can cause serious illness |
| Cooked bones | Can splinter and injure or block the gut |
| Raw meat, eggs, bones | Bacteria; bones can injure |
| Milk and dairy | Many pets digest lactose poorly; diarrhoea |

## Plants

- **Lilies and cats:** true lilies (Easter, tiger, Asiatic, stargazer and others) and daylilies are highly toxic to cats. According to the US FDA, as cited by Purdue's veterinary school, every part is poisonous, including the pollen and vase water, and small amounts can cause kidney failure. Treat any exposure as an emergency. The safest choice for cat households is no lilies at all.
- Many other common plants are toxic to some species. The ASPCA keeps a searchable list by species; check before buying plants or when a pet has chewed one.

## Household items (AVMA and ASPCA)

- **Human medicines,** including common painkillers, cold remedies, antidepressants and ADHD medicines. Keep them shut away; never give them to pets.
- **Pet medicines given to the wrong species.** Some dog flea treatments are dangerous for cats.
- **Rodent poisons and slug pellets.**
- **Antifreeze** (sweet-tasting, very dangerous) and other car chemicals.
- **Cleaning products, bleach, detergent pods.**
- **Batteries, coins, small magnets** if swallowed.
- **Essential oils and liquid potpourri,** especially for cats and birds.
- **Cigarettes, nicotine gum, vape liquid.**
- **Cannabis products** and edibles.
- **Strings, ribbons, tinsel** (cats especially): can cause gut blockages.

## Seasonal checklist

- **Holidays:** chocolate, Christmas cake and mince pies (raisins), xylitol in sweets, tinsel and ribbons, lilies in bouquets.
- **Summer:** barbecue bones and corn cobs, heatstroke, blue-green algae in ponds, slug pellets.
- **Autumn/winter:** antifreeze, rodent poison, conkers and acorns.

## Common mistakes

- "Just a little won't hurt." With xylitol, grapes and lilies, small amounts can be serious.
- Waiting for symptoms; some poisons act late.
- Assuming "natural" means safe.
- Giving a pet a treat with xylitol-sweetened peanut butter; check the label.
