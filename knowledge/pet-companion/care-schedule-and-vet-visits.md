# Care schedule and vet visits

Routine care is what slips: the booster that was due in March, the flea treatment nobody remembers giving. The schedule makes it visible. **The vet sets the schedule.** Vaccines, parasite treatments and their timing depend on species, age, lifestyle, health and where the pet lives. Record what the vet says; don't calculate or suggest intervals or products.

## What to track per pet

| Item | Record | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Vaccinations | Name as on the vet record, date given, next due date the vet gave | A few weeks before due |
| Flea, tick and worm treatment | Product name and the schedule the vet set | On the day, plus a "did you give it?" check |
| Heartworm prevention (where relevant) | As prescribed | As prescribed |
| Annual or senior check-up | Date of last, date recommended | A month before |
| Dental checks | As recommended | |
| Weight | Date and value, as weighed | Monthly at home, if the vet asked for it |
| Microchip | That it's registered and the details are current (not the number itself in shared files, if the owner prefers) | After any move or phone change |
| Insurance renewal | Date | A month before |
| Prescriptions | Name, dose as on the label, refill date | Before running out |

Doses are copied exactly from the vet's label. If the owner asks about changing a dose or a product, the answer is "ask the vet".

## Symptom log format

When the pet seems off, facts beat impressions:

```
| Date & time | What you saw | How long / how often | Eating | Drinking | Toilet | Energy | Photo/video |
| 3 Nov 08:00 | Limping on right front leg | Since waking | Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal | video_0301.mp4 |
| 3 Nov 18:00 | Still limping, licks paw | All day | Less | Normal | Normal | Lower | |
```

Useful details: when it started, whether it's getting better or worse, any change in food, routine or environment, anything the pet might have eaten. Short videos of limping, coughing, breathing or odd behaviour help vets a lot.

If anything on the [emergency list](emergency-signs.md) appears, stop logging and call the vet.

## Vet visit prep sheet

```
## <Pet> — vet visit <date, time>
Reason: <one line>
Since when: <date>
Log summary: <key facts from symptom log>
Current medicines and supplements: <from the record, as labelled>
Food: <brand/type, amount, any change>
Questions (most important first):
1.
2.
3.
Bring: vaccination record, medicine packets, a stool or urine sample if the vet asked, videos
```

Helpful questions to suggest:
- What do you think is going on, and what else could it be?
- What signs mean I should bring them back sooner?
- When should it improve?
- What are the options and the costs?

## After the visit

Record what the vet said, in their words: findings, diagnosis if given, treatment and how to give it (as written on the label), follow-up date. Set reminders for medicines and the recheck.

## Getting a nervous pet to the vet

- Leave the carrier out at home with bedding and treats, so it isn't a sign of the vet.
- Cover the carrier in the car; carry it level.
- Ask the clinic about quieter appointment times or a cat-only waiting area.
- Ask the vet about any calming options; don't give anything without their advice.

## Common mistakes

- Guessing when the last booster was.
- Mixing up which pet had which treatment in multi-pet homes.
- Describing symptoms from memory at the vet.
- Changing food or doses on internet advice.
