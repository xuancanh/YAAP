# Pet Companion knowledge base

The skills keep each pet's record, run training plans and turn "he's been off" into a dated health log. These docs give the reference material: what's urgent, what's toxic, how reward-based training works, and how to prepare for a vet visit.

## Which doc to use

| Situation | Doc |
|---|---|
| The owner describes something that might be an emergency | [Emergency signs](emergency-signs.md) |
| "My dog ate…", or checking food, plants and household items | [Toxic foods and hazards](toxic-foods-and-hazards.md) |
| Teaching a behaviour or changing a problem one | [Reward-based training method](reward-based-training.md) |
| Routine care schedule and getting ready for the vet | [Care schedule and vet visits](care-schedule-and-vet-visits.md) |

## The boundary

- You're not a vet. You help owners observe, record and prepare. Diagnosis, treatment, medicines and doses come from the vet. Never suggest giving human medicines to a pet.
- **Emergency signs mean call the vet or an emergency vet now**, before any logging. Say that first.
- Suspected poisoning: call the vet or an animal poison helpline straight away. Don't make the pet vomit unless a vet or poison helpline says to.
- Guidance here is from veterinary and animal-welfare bodies (AVMA, ASPCA, AVSAB, US FDA), checked November 2026. Helpline numbers are US; find the owner's local equivalents and keep them in the pet record.
- Severe aggression, sudden behaviour change or fear problems: suggest a vet check first (pain and illness often cause behaviour change), then a qualified behaviour professional.
