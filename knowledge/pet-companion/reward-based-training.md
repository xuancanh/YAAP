# Reward-based training method

The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior's 2021 position statement says reward-based methods should be used for all dog training, and that aversive methods (pain, force, fear or discomfort) shouldn't be used for training or behaviour problems. The same principles work for cats and many other animals.

## The core idea

Behaviour that gets rewarded happens more often. So:

1. Make the right behaviour easy.
2. Reward it the moment it happens.
3. Make it a bit harder only when the pet succeeds most of the time.
4. Prevent the unwanted behaviour from being rewarded in the meantime.

## The parts that make it work

**Timing.** The reward must come within a second or two, or the pet links it to whatever it did next. A marker (a clicker or a short word like "yes") bridges the gap: mark the instant, then deliver the treat.

**Value.** Use rewards the pet actually wants. Small, soft, tasty treats for hard tasks or busy places; kibble or praise for easy ones at home. Toys and play work for some dogs.

**Small steps.** Break the behaviour down. If the pet fails twice in a row, the step was too big; go back one.

**The three Ds.** Increase **d**uration, **d**istance and **d**istraction one at a time, never two at once.

**Short sessions.** A few minutes, several times a day. Stop while it's going well.

**Management.** While training, prevent the problem from happening (a baby gate, a lead, putting the shoes away). Every time an unwanted behaviour pays off, it gets stronger.

**Teach what to do instead.** "Don't jump" isn't teachable. "Sit to say hello" is.

## Worked example: greeting without jumping (dog)

Goal: the dog sits when a visitor arrives.

| Step | Practice | Move on when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reward sit at home, no visitors | Sits on cue 8 of 10 times |
| 2 | Family member walks in calmly; reward sit before they greet | 8 of 10 |
| 3 | Family member walks in excitedly | 8 of 10 |
| 4 | A friend who's in on it, on a lead | 8 of 10 |
| 5 | Real visitors, still on lead at first | Reliable |

Management meanwhile: dog behind a gate or on lead when real visitors arrive. Visitors greet only when the dog's paws are on the floor.

## Progress log

```
Date | Step | Reps | Successes | Notes
3 Nov | 2 | 10 | 7 | Better after a short break
4 Nov | 2 | 10 | 9 | Ready for step 3
```

Honest numbers show whether it's working. Plateaus are normal; going back a step is not failure.

## When to get help

- Aggression (growling, snapping, biting), especially new or getting worse
- Fear or anxiety that limits the pet's life (can't be left alone, panics at noises)
- Sudden behaviour change in an adult pet
- Any problem that isn't improving after a few weeks of consistent work

Start with a vet check: pain, illness and medical conditions often cause behaviour changes. Then a qualified behaviour professional (a veterinary behaviourist or a certified behaviour consultant). Behaviour credentials aren't regulated everywhere; check qualifications.

## Common mistakes

- Rewarding too late.
- Moving too fast through the steps.
- Training in a busy park before it works in the kitchen.
- Punishing a growl. It removes the warning, not the feeling behind it.
- Inconsistent rules between family members.
