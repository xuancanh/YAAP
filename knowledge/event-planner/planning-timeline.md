# Planning timeline by lead time

Plan backwards from the date. The bigger the event and the busier the season, the earlier the venue and the scarce vendors need booking. Treat the lead times below as starting points; popular venues and peak dates book out far earlier, so check availability before trusting any timeline.

## Pick the tier

| Tier | Example | Typical planning window |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Dinner party, kids' birthday at home | 2–4 weeks |
| Medium | 40-person milestone birthday, baby shower, team offsite | 2–4 months |
| Large | Wedding, big anniversary, multi-day reunion | 6–12+ months |

## Backwards timeline (medium event, scale as needed)

**As early as possible (8–12 weeks out)**
- [ ] Concept: purpose, vibe, format, rough guest count, budget ceiling.
- [ ] Date shortlist; check clashes (holidays, school terms, local events).
- [ ] Venue booked; deposit paid; contract filed.
- [ ] Save-the-date if guests travel or the date is busy.

**6–8 weeks out**
- [ ] Caterer, entertainment, photographer booked if needed.
- [ ] Guest list finalised; addresses and dietary needs collected.
- [ ] Invitations sent with an RSVP deadline.

**3–4 weeks out**
- [ ] Rentals ordered (tables, linens, tent, AV).
- [ ] Decor plan and shopping list.
- [ ] Any permits or licences confirmed if the venue or location requires them.

**2 weeks out**
- [ ] RSVP chase for non-responders.
- [ ] Final numbers to caterer (check the contract's deadline).
- [ ] Run sheet draft; helpers assigned.

**Final week**
- [ ] Reconfirm every vendor: time, address, contact, access.
- [ ] Weather check; activate rain plan if needed.
- [ ] Payments due on the day prepared.
- [ ] Day-of kit packed (see [run sheet](run-sheet-and-contingencies.md)).

**After**
- [ ] Thank-you notes within a couple of weeks.
- [ ] Final payments and deposit returns tracked.
- [ ] Notes for next time: what worked, what didn't, final cost vs. budget.

## Tasks that usually slip

- **RSVPs.** Expect a share of guests not to respond by the deadline. Build a chase step into the plan rather than hoping.
- **Final numbers.** Caterers often need them earlier than people expect.
- **Dietary needs.** Collected late means a scramble. Ask on the invitation.
- **Delivery and pickup times** for rentals, especially when the venue has access windows.
- **Who holds the cash or card** for on-the-day payments and tips.

## Worked example

A 50th birthday for 40 guests on a Saturday in June. Ten weeks out: venue (a restaurant's private room) booked with a deposit; contract says final numbers 7 days before. Eight weeks: invitations with RSVP by three weeks out. Three weeks: 28 yes, 6 no, 6 silent; chase the silent six by phone. Ten days: send numbers, plus one or two for late yeses. Final week: reconfirm cake pickup time and the playlist with the venue's AV contact.

## Weekly sweep questions

1. Which tasks are due in the next 14 days, and who owns each?
2. Which vendor has not confirmed in writing?
3. Is anything waiting on a decision from the host?
4. Is the budget still inside the ceiling?
