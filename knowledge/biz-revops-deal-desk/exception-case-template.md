# Exception case template

An approver should be able to decide an exception in a couple of minutes from your write-up. That means the ask, the evidence, the alternatives and your recommendation, on one page.

## Route to the right approver

The pricing policy names approvers by type and size of exception — for example, by discount band, by term (payment terms, liability) or by deal size. Before writing:

1. Identify every way the deal is outside policy. A deal can need several approvals (a deeper discount and non-standard payment terms).
2. Find the approver for each from the policy's approval matrix.
3. If two approvers are needed, say in the case who approves what.

Never route around an approver or split a deal to stay under a threshold.

## Template

```
Deal: account / opportunity / AE / quote ID
Ask: the exact exception(s) — e.g. "additional 10 points of discount on the platform line (total 30% vs 20% band)"
Approver(s) per policy: name/role for each item

Deal facts:
  Term, products, quantities, ACV/TCV, list and net price
  Effective discount: before and after the ask
  Close date and forecast category

Why the buyer is asking: their stated reason, in their words if possible
Evidence:
  Competitive situation (named competitor, their offer if known, source)
  Buyer's budget constraint (stated by whom)
  Strategic value (logo, reference, market entry) — specific, not generic
Give-get: what we receive in return — e.g. longer term, earlier signature, case study, upfront payment, higher volume

Alternatives considered:
  A. Approve as asked
  B. Partial: <smaller concession>, with <get>
  C. Decline: likely outcome

Precedent: similar exceptions in the log (dates, sizes, outcomes)
Risk: margin, precedent set for this customer at renewal, policy consistency
Recommendation: A / B / C, with one-line reason
Decision needed by: date, and why
```

## The give-get principle

A concession without something in return teaches buyers to ask again. Common gets, ranked by what they're usually worth to the company (check what your policy values):

- Longer term commitment
- Larger volume or more products
- Upfront or annual payment instead of monthly
- Signature by a date that matters to the period
- Reference, case study or logo use (with the customer's genuine agreement)
- Removal of a non-standard term the buyer asked for

Make the get specific and written into the order form where possible.

## Writing the recommendation

- Be clear: "Recommend B: 25% for a three-year term with annual upfront payment."
- Give the reason in terms the approver cares about: margin, win probability, precedent.
- If you recommend declining, say what you expect to happen and how the AE might respond.
- Disclose uncertainty: "Competitor offer is as reported by the champion; not verified."

## After the decision

- Record the decision, approver, date and conditions in the exception log.
- Tell the AE the outcome and any conditions (e.g. "valid if signed by the 30th").
- The AE updates and re-issues the quote; you review it again against the approved terms.
- At renewal, flag that the exception existed so the CSM and AE plan for it.

## Pitfalls

- **"Strategic" without specifics.** Every deal is strategic to the AE. Say what exactly the company gains.
- **Missing precedent.** Approvers need to know if this sets a new low.
- **Late cases.** An exception sent the last day of the quarter pressures the approver; flag likely exceptions as soon as they appear.
- **Unverified competitor claims** stated as fact.
