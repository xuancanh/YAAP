# Travel documents and entry checks

**Everything here is country-specific and changes, sometimes at short notice.** The rules depend on the traveller's nationality (each passport they hold), the destination and every transit country. Use this as a checklist of *what to check*, not as the answer. Always confirm with the official source and record the date you checked.

## Where to check

1. **The destination government's official immigration or embassy site.** This is the binding source.
2. **The traveller's own government travel advice**, such as GOV.UK foreign travel advice for British nationals or Travel.State.Gov for US citizens. These summarise entry rules, safety and health for each country and are usually kept up to date.
3. **The airline.** Airlines can refuse boarding if documents don't meet the destination's rules, so check their requirements too.

Avoid relying on blogs or forums for entry rules.

## The checklist (run at T-60 to T-30, or as soon as the trip is booked)

**Passport**
- [ ] The passport is valid for the whole trip, *plus* any extra period the destination requires. Some countries ask for several months beyond your stay. For example, the EU's Your Europe site says non-EU nationals entering the Schengen area need a passport valid for at least three months after the planned departure, and issued within the last 10 years.
- [ ] Enough blank pages, if the destination specifies a number.
- [ ] Undamaged. Damaged passports can be refused.
- [ ] The name on the booking matches the passport.

**Visa or travel authorisation**
- [ ] Is a visa, eVisa or electronic travel authorisation needed for *this* nationality and purpose (tourism, business, study)?
- [ ] Processing time compared with the departure date. Apply early.
- [ ] Apply only through the official government site. Lookalike sites charge extra fees.
- [ ] Rules on the length of stay, and on single or multiple entry.

**Transit**
- [ ] Does any connection need a transit visa, or require you to clear immigration (for example, changing airports or collecting bags)?

**Health entry rules**
- [ ] Required vaccinations or certificates for the destination, or because of where you've travelled from.
- [ ] Recommended vaccines and health precautions. Check CDC Travelers' Health, the UK's travel-health guidance or the national equivalent, and **refer the traveller to a travel clinic or their doctor**, ideally several weeks before departure. Never advise on specific vaccines or medicines yourself.
- [ ] Rules on bringing medicines. Some medicines that are legal at home are controlled elsewhere. Check the destination embassy's guidance, and suggest the traveller carries a prescription or a doctor's letter.

**Minors and others**
- [ ] Children travelling with one parent or with other adults may need a consent letter or extra documents. Check the destination's rules.
- [ ] Driving: whether an International Driving Permit is required alongside the licence.
- [ ] Pets: the import rules are complex and need long lead times.

**Copies and backups**
- [ ] A photo or scan of the passport ID page, the visa, the insurance policy and key bookings, stored securely and offline.
- [ ] Emergency contacts, plus the address of the traveller's nearest embassy or consulate.
- [ ] Registration with the government's traveller enrolment service, where one is offered.

## Recording the result

Add a short block to research.md or the countdown log:

```
Entry check — <traveller nationality> → <destination> (via <transit>)
Checked: <date>, sources: <URLs>
Passport valid until <date> — meets rule: <yes/no, rule text>
Visa/authorisation: <type, applied date, status>
Health: <requirements; clinic appointment date>
Open items: ...
```

## When to refer on

- Complex visa situations (past refusals, dual nationality questions, work or study, overstays): an immigration adviser or the embassy.
- Medical conditions, pregnancy, vaccines and medicines: a doctor or travel clinic.
- A passport that's lost or expires soon: the traveller's passport office. Check the current processing times.

## Common mistakes

- Checking the rules for the wrong passport when a traveller holds two.
- Forgetting the transit country.
- Assuming the rules are the same as on the last trip.
- Renewing a passport too late. Processing times vary and can spike.
