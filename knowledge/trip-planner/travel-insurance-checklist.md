# Travel insurance checklist

You help the traveller check that a policy fits the trip. You don't recommend insurers or products, and you don't interpret policy wording as advice. If the wording is unclear, the traveller asks the insurer and gets the answer in writing. Insurance rules and public health cover differ by country, so this is general guidance.

## Check that the policy fits the trip

Based on the UK government's guidance on foreign travel insurance, and useful wherever you live:

- [ ] **Covers the whole trip length.** Many policies cap the length of a single trip or the total days abroad per year.
- [ ] **Covers every country you'll visit, including transit stops.** Some policies exclude regions or charge more for them.
- [ ] **Medical treatment** in state or private hospitals, plus **emergency ambulance** transport, which can be charged separately.
- [ ] **Repatriation**: getting you home after treatment if you can't use your original ticket, and repatriation if someone dies abroad.
- [ ] **A companion's costs**, if a family member or friend needs to stay with you or fly out.
- [ ] **24-hour emergency assistance line.** Save the number in your phone and on paper.
- [ ] **Every activity you plan.** Standard policies often exclude adventure sports. GOV.UK gives bungee jumping, jet skiing and quad biking as examples. Check skiing, diving depth limits, trekking altitude and motorbike riding.
- [ ] **Travel company failure**, or whether the booking is protected some other way (in the UK, for example, an ATOL-protected package).
- [ ] **Cruises**, which often need extra cover.

## Pre-existing medical conditions

- **Declare everything the insurer asks about**: existing conditions, pending tests and treatment. GOV.UK warns that failing to declare something may invalidate the policy.
- For serious conditions, a specialist insurer may be needed.
- Don't help the traveller decide what to leave out. Tell them to answer the insurer's questions fully, and to call the insurer if they're unsure.

## Public health cover is not travel insurance

Some countries have reciprocal healthcare arrangements, such as the UK's GHIC/EHIC cards in the EU. GOV.UK states plainly that EHIC and GHIC are **not alternatives to travel insurance**. These cards don't cover repatriation, private treatment or many other costs.

## Other things worth checking

- **Cancellation cover**: what counts as a valid reason, and the maximum amount. Check it covers the non-refundable amounts in bookings.md.
- **Baggage and valuables**: limits for each item, and whether gadgets are covered. Home insurance may already cover some of this.
- **Excess** (deductible): how much you pay per claim.
- **Exclusions**: alcohol-related incidents, travel against official government advice, unattended belongings.
- **Card benefits**: some credit cards include travel cover, but check the actual terms and conditions (often the trip must be paid with that card).

## When to buy

Buy cancellation cover soon after booking non-refundable items, so it protects you from the start. Record the policy number, emergency line and coverage dates in bookings.md.

## Recording it

```
Insurance — <trip>
Policy: <insurer, policy number>   Dates covered: <from–to>
Emergency line: <number>   Countries: <list/regions>
Activities confirmed: <list>   Medical declared: yes (traveller confirmed)
Cancellation limit: <amount> vs non-refundable bookings: <amount>
Checked: <date>
```

## When to refer on

- The traveller is unsure whether a condition counts: they ask the insurer.
- After an incident abroad: they call the insurer's emergency line first, before arranging expensive treatment where possible.
- Claim disputes: the insurer's complaints process, then the national financial ombudsman or regulator.

## Common mistakes

- Buying single-trip cover for a trip that's longer than its maximum length.
- An activity added on impulse (a scooter rental, a dive) that the policy doesn't cover.
- Not saving the emergency number offline.
- Relying on card cover without reading the conditions.
