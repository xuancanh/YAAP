# Packing checklists by trip type

Build packing.md from the **core list**, add one module for each kind of trip involved, then tune it to the person: their medications, contact lenses, kids' gear and camera kit, from what you already know about them.

## Core list (every trip)

**Documents and money**
- [ ] Passport or ID, plus any visa or travel authorisation
- [ ] Copies of the passport, insurance and bookings, stored offline
- [ ] Insurance policy number and emergency line
- [ ] Payment cards (two, kept separately), and a little local cash if needed
- [ ] Driving licence (and an International Driving Permit if the destination requires one)

**Health**
- [ ] Prescription medicines in their original packaging, enough for the trip plus a few spare days, in the carry-on
- [ ] Prescription or doctor's letter if the medicine is controlled at the destination (check before travelling)
- [ ] Small first-aid kit, glasses or contact lenses, sunscreen

**Electronics**
- [ ] Phone, charger, power bank (most airlines want these in the cabin, not in checked bags; check yours)
- [ ] Plug adapter for the destination's socket type
- [ ] Headphones, and offline maps downloaded

**Clothes** (count per day; if there's laundry access on trips over a week, pack for about half the days)
- [ ] Tops, bottoms, underwear and socks for the counted days
- [ ] One warm layer, and a rain layer
- [ ] Comfortable walking shoes, already worn in

**Night before**
- [ ] Charge everything, and check in online
- [ ] Check the forecast against the list
- [ ] Have the day sheet (bookings-daysheet.md) on your phone and on paper

## Carry-on liquids (airport-specific)

Rules depend on the country and the airport, and some airports with newer scanners have changed them. In the US, TSA's rule is that liquids, gels, creams and pastes in carry-on bags must be in containers of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less, all fitting in one quart-sized bag. Medications and infant or child nourishment are exempt, but should be declared at screening. Check the departure airport's current rules, and the rules for the flight home.

## Add-on modules

**City break**
- [ ] Day bag with a closed zip, and a refillable water bottle
- [ ] One smarter outfit for dinners
- [ ] Clothing that covers shoulders and knees, for religious sites that require it

**Beach or hot climate**
- [ ] Swimwear (two), cover-up, sun hat, sunglasses
- [ ] High-SPF sunscreen and after-sun
- [ ] Insect repellent if relevant. Ask a travel clinic about risks at the destination.

**Cold weather**
- [ ] Base layers, mid layer, insulated outer layer
- [ ] Hat, gloves and a scarf or neck gaiter
- [ ] Waterproof boots with grip, and lip balm

**Outdoor or hiking**
- [ ] Boots already worn in, and moisture-wicking socks
- [ ] Map or offline navigation, a headlamp, and a whistle
- [ ] Water capacity and snacks for the planned route
- [ ] Permits printed if required, plus the trail's emergency contact
- [ ] Check that your insurance covers the activity (see [Travel insurance checklist](travel-insurance-checklist.md))

**Business**
- [ ] Outfits for each meeting day, plus one spare shirt or top
- [ ] Laptop, charger, presentation adapter, and the deck stored offline
- [ ] Business cards and the meeting addresses offline

**Family with young children**
- [ ] Travel documents for each child (and a consent letter if one parent is travelling alone; check the destination's rules)
- [ ] Snacks, a change of clothes in the cabin bag, and comfort items
- [ ] Children's medicines, as advised by their doctor or pharmacist
- [ ] Entertainment for the journey, downloaded offline

## Worked example

A 9-day trip to Lisbon in October with a laundry stop:

- Core list, plus the city module.
- Clothes counted for 5 days.
- Rain layer added, because the forecast shows showers.
- Type F plug adapter, after checking the destination's socket types.
- The user's allergy medicine added from their profile, in the carry-on.

## Common mistakes

- New shoes on a walking trip.
- Medicines packed in checked luggage.
- Forgetting the return flight's liquid rules after buying local products.
- Packing for the seasonal average instead of the actual forecast.
