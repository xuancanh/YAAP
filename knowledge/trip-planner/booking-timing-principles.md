# Booking timing principles

There's no reliable magic day for booking cheap travel, and advice that claims one usually comes from averages that won't hold for a particular route. What does hold is a simple rule: **book first whatever is scarce or fixed, keep the rest flexible, and track every deadline.**

## The order to lock things in

1. **The things that make the trip possible.** These are fixed-date events, peak-season lodging in small places, and limited-capacity permits or tickets (popular trails, timed museum entry, festivals). If any of them falls through, the trip changes.
2. **Long-haul transport.** International flights and long-distance trains on busy dates. Seat choice and connection quality get worse as the date approaches, even when the price doesn't.
3. **Lodging in the right area**, as refundable bookings where possible.
4. **Timed-entry sights** that are anchors in the itinerary, once dates are firm.
5. **Local transport and day tours**, often bookable later, or on the day.
6. **Restaurants**: book the special few ahead and leave the rest open.

## Flexible versus locked

| Situation | Lean towards |
|---|---|
| Dates might move | Refundable fares and lodging, even at a premium. Record the cancel-by date. |
| Dates fixed, plans fixed | Non-refundable can make sense if the saving is worth the risk. Say the risk out loud. |
| Peak season or a big event | Book early, with free cancellation, then re-check prices later. |
| Off-season, flexible traveller | Fewer advance bookings. Keep a shortlist and book a few days out. |

## The re-check habit

With free-cancellation bookings you can re-check the price later. If the same room or a better one costs less, book the new one first and then cancel the old one. Note both confirmation numbers in bookings.md until the cancellation is confirmed.

## Deadlines to track for every booking

Each row in bookings.md should carry:

- the **cancel-by date and time, with its time zone**. Some deadlines run on the property's local time;
- the **balance-due date** for deposits;
- **name-change or date-change rules** and their fees;
- the **check-in window**: online check-in opening times, and the latest arrival time at the property.

Bring up any deadline that falls within 14 days every time you touch the file.

## Checks before paying

- [ ] Names exactly as in the passport, for flights and anything checked against ID
- [ ] Dates and times correct. Watch overnight flights that arrive the next day.
- [ ] Total price including taxes, resort or city fees, baggage and seat fees
- [ ] Cancellation terms read, not assumed
- [ ] Connection times realistic (separate tickets on self-made connections usually aren't protected)
- [ ] The booking site is the official provider or a known agent

## Worked example

A couple plans a two-week trip to Japan in cherry-blossom season. Dates are mostly fixed, with a possible shift of one day.

- **Book now:** flights (fixed), and refundable hotels in Kyoto, where peak-season rooms are scarce.
- **Book when tickets open:** any timed-entry sights they care about, after checking each venue's official booking window.
- **Keep flexible:** Tokyo lodging (more supply), day trips and most restaurants.
- **Track:** the Kyoto cancel-by dates in bookings.md, with the next one flagged at T-14.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for a price drop on a scarce item and losing it.
- Booking non-refundable while the dates are still uncertain.
- Forgetting the time zone of a cancellation deadline.
- Booking two separate tickets with a tight connection. If the first leg is late, the second is usually lost.
- Treating a "pay later" booking as free to cancel. Read the actual terms.
