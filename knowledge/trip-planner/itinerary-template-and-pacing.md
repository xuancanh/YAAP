# Itinerary template and pacing

A good itinerary is mostly about what you leave out. Give each day one anchor, cluster it by area, and leave room for the trip to surprise you.

## Template (itinerary.md)

```markdown
# <Destination> — <dates> (<n> days)
Travellers: <who>   Pace: <relaxed / moderate / packed>
Base(s): <hotel and neighbourhood per night range>

## Day 1 — <weekday date> — <area theme>
| Time | Plan | Backup (rain/closed) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15:00 | Arrive, check in | — | Light day after the flight |
| 17:00 | Walk <neighbourhood> | Café / market hall | 10 min from hotel |
| 19:30 | Dinner: <idea near base> | — | Book? no |

Transit: <line/mode, duration>   Opening checks: <verified date>

## Day 2 — ...

## Bookings needed (soonest deadline first)
| Item | Book by | Why | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
```

## Pacing rules of thumb

- **One anchor per day.** This is the thing the day is built around, like a museum, a hike or a food tour. Add one or two flexible extras nearby.
- **Arrival day is light.** After a long flight or a night journey, plan a walk and dinner, nothing timed.
- **Departure day is packable.** Plan something near the base or the airport, and know where you'll leave the luggage.
- **Unplanned block every three days or so.** A free half-day absorbs delays, tiredness and the things you discover along the way.
- **Cluster by area.** Use a map. If two sights on the same day are on opposite sides of the city, swap one with another day.
- **Count transit honestly.** Include walking to the station, waiting and finding the entrance. Getting from door to door usually takes longer than the journey-planner time.
- **Verify opening days for every anchor.** Weekly closing days, holidays and seasonal hours are the classic trap. Note the date you checked.
- **Give every outdoor anchor a rain backup.**
- **Allow for jet lag.** After crossing many time zones, put the demanding anchors on later days.

## Pace presets

| Pace | Anchors per day | Free time | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relaxed (default) | 1 | Afternoons often open | Most trips, families, long trips |
| Moderate | 1–2 | One free half-day every 2–3 days | First visits with a long must-see list |
| Packed | 2+ | A free evening or two | Short trips by people who said they want this |

## Worked example: fixing an overpacked day

The first draft of day 2 in Rome had the Vatican Museums at 09:00, the Colosseum at 13:00, the Borghese Gallery at 16:00 and Trastevere for dinner.

The problems:

- Three timed anchors in three different parts of the city.
- Each one needs hours on its own.
- The Borghese needs a booked time slot, and a late lunch puts it at risk.

The fix:

- **Day 2:** Vatican (anchor), lunch in Prati, an evening walk to Castel Sant'Angelo, dinner across the river in Trastevere.
- **Day 3:** Colosseum and Forum (anchor), with the Monti neighbourhood for the afternoon.
- **Day 4:** Borghese (anchor, time slot booked), then the Villa Borghese park and a free evening.
- **Bookings needed:** Vatican and Borghese time slots go to the top, because they sell out for popular dates.

Before finalising, check the current opening days and booking rules on the official sites for each one.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the guidebook's "top 10" as the day plan.
- No backup for weather-dependent days.
- Forgetting meals. Hungry travellers make bad decisions at 3pm.
- Planning by the clock instead of energy. Put the hard thing in the morning.
- Leaving out the transfer days. Moving between cities usually takes most of a day.
