# Keeping and filing papers

Most papers can go after a short while. A few must be kept for years, and a handful forever. Retention rules are **country-specific**; treat the examples below as what to look up, not as the user's rule.

## Four piles

| Pile | Examples | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| **Forever** (originals, safe place) | Birth, marriage, divorce, death certificates; adoption papers; passports (old ones too, often); wills and powers of attorney; property deeds; citizenship or naturalisation documents | Permanently |
| **While it's live, plus a margin** | Insurance policies, mortgage and loan agreements, tenancy agreements, warranties and receipts for big items, vehicle documents, pension statements | Until replaced or closed, then for as long as a claim or dispute is possible |
| **Tax years** | Payslips, annual tax statements, receipts for deductions, records of investment sales | For the official retention period, see below |
| **Short term** | Utility bills, bank statements once reconciled, routine letters | A few months to a year, unless needed as proof of address or for tax |

## Official examples: tax records

**United States (IRS, reviewed June 2026).** The general rule is 3 years from filing. Longer periods apply in specific cases: 6 years if income was under-reported by more than 25%, 7 years for a loss from worthless securities or bad debt, 4 years for employment tax records, and indefinitely if no return or a fraudulent return was filed.

**United Kingdom (GOV.UK).** Self Assessment filers who file on time should keep records for at least 22 months after the end of the tax year the return is for; if filed late, at least 15 months after sending the return. The self-employed must keep business records for longer.

For other countries, or anything unusual (a business, property sale, inheritance), point the user to their tax authority or an accountant.

## Filing structure

Keep it shallow so things are findable:

```
admin/
  identity/        # what exists and where it's kept, never the numbers
  home/            # tenancy or mortgage, insurance, utilities
  money/           # by year: 2026/, 2025/
  health/          # appointment notes, vaccination records
  vehicle/
  warranties/      # one note per item: bought, from, expires, receipt location
  archive/         # closed accounts, old policies
```

Each filed document gets a one-line record: what it is, date, key facts (expiry, amount, reference *name*, not ID number), where the original is.

## Paper versus scan

- Scan, then shred: most routine statements and bills.
- Keep originals: certificates, deeds, wills, anything with a seal or wet signature that may be needed.
- Shred, don't bin, anything with account details or identity data.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping everything, so nothing is findable.
- Throwing out the receipt for a big purchase still under warranty.
- Storing a will where nobody knows to look. Note its location for the people who'll need it.
- Assuming one country's retention rule applies elsewhere.
