# Appointment prep sheet

People leave appointments having forgotten the one question they came for. A short sheet, made the day before, fixes that.

## Template

```
## <What> — <date, time>
Where:   <address, floor, room>   Getting there: <route, travel time, parking>
Leave by: <time>                  Contact: <phone for running late>
Purpose: <one line: what I want out of this>

Bring:
- [ ] Photo ID / card / reference letter
- [ ] Documents: …
- [ ] List of …

Questions (most important first):
1. …
2. …
3. …

After (fill in within the day):
Outcome: …
Next steps and who does them: …
Follow-up date: …
```

Put the most important question first. If time runs short, it still gets asked.

## Per-type checklists

**Medical (doctor, dentist, specialist)**
- Symptoms written down: what, since when, what makes it better or worse
- Current medicines and supplements with doses as on the label (the person copies these; you don't advise on them)
- Allergies
- Previous test results or letters
- Questions: "What are the options?", "What happens if I wait?", "What should make me come back sooner?"
- Bring someone if the news may be hard or the person struggles to remember detail

**Government office (ID, benefits, licences, registry)**
- The exact appointment confirmation and reference
- The official document checklist from the agency's site, checked fresh: requirements change
- Originals *and* copies
- Photos to spec, fee and accepted payment method
- Proof of address of the age they accept

**Bank or financial adviser**
- What you want: open, close, dispute, borrow, ask
- Recent statements, ID, proof of address
- Questions about fees, notice periods and what happens if you change your mind
- Don't sign anything you haven't had time to read

**Service visit at home (repair, installer)**
- Appointment window and engineer contact
- Fault history: when it started, error codes, photos
- Access: parking, keys, pets shut away, clear path to the appliance
- Warranty or previous job reference

## Worked example

```
## GP: knee pain — Thu 12 Nov, 9:20
Where: Highfield Surgery, room 3   Leave by: 8:55 (bus 12)
Purpose: find out why my knee hurts on stairs and whether I need a scan

Bring: list of current medicines, photo of swelling from Sunday
Questions:
1. What do you think is causing it?
2. Do I need a scan or physio?
3. What should make me come back sooner?
```

## After the appointment

Capture the outcome the same day while it's fresh: what was decided, next steps with owners and dates, and any follow-up appointment. Add follow-ups to the renewals or reminders list. Medical notes are recorded as the clinician said them, not interpreted.

## Common mistakes

- Making the list in the waiting room.
- Using an old document checklist for a government appointment.
- Not writing down the outcome, then forgetting what was agreed.
