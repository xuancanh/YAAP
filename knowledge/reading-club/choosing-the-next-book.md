# Choosing the next book

Good recommendations come from why someone liked a book, not what genre it was. Librarians doing readers' advisory call these "appeal factors"; use them to describe the user's taste.

## Appeal factors

| Factor | Questions to ask of a book the user loved or hated |
|---|---|
| Pacing | Fast and plotty, or slow and immersive? |
| Characters | Few and deep, or a big cast? Likeable or not? |
| Story | Linear, braided timelines, mystery structure? |
| Setting / frame | Vivid world, historical detail, small domestic space? |
| Language / style | Spare, lyrical, funny, dense, conversational? |
| Tone | Warm, bleak, eerie, hopeful, satirical? |
| Length and effort | Short and light, or a long commitment? |

Two thrillers can have nothing in common for a reader: one is fast and funny, the other slow and eerie.

## Building the taste profile

From the finished and abandoned entries (see [reading log formats](reading-log-formats.md)):

1. List the top five books and the bottom five, including abandoned ones.
2. For each, note the appeal factors in the user's words.
3. Look for what the loved books share and what the abandoned ones share. Often it's style or pacing, not subject.
4. Write a three-line profile and show it to the user: "You love voice-driven, slightly strange books under 350 pages. You abandon anything with a big cast and politics. You like a sad ending if it's earned."
5. Revise it every few books.

## Asking about current mood

Taste is stable; appetite isn't. Ask two quick questions:

- "Something like your favourites, or a change?"
- "Light, absorbing or demanding right now?"

Life matters too: a long commute suits audio; a stressful month might call for comfort reads.

## The recommendation

Offer three, not ten:

1. **The safe pick:** closest match to the profile.
2. **The stretch:** shares the key appeal factor but in new territory.
3. **The wild card:** something they'd never find, with an honest reason.

For each: one line on why it fits their history, one honest caveat.

> *The Remains of the Day*: same quiet, unreliable narrator you loved in *Piranesi*, also short. Caveat: almost nothing happens, by design.

## Honesty about hype

If a book everyone is talking about doesn't fit the pattern, say so: "It's popular, but it's long, plot-heavy and has a big cast, which you've abandoned three times this year. Try a sample first." Respect taste without flattering it; it's fine to suggest a stretch, but call it one.

## Checks before recommending

- It isn't already finished, abandoned or on the list (check the log).
- The facts are right: author, rough length, that it's a standalone or which book in the series to start with.
- Content the user has said they avoid (check the profile) is flagged.
- Availability: in print, at the library, on audio, if that matters to them.

## Common mistakes

- Recommending by genre alone.
- Offering bestseller lists as personal picks.
- Ten options that paralyse.
- Forgetting the abandoned-book data.
