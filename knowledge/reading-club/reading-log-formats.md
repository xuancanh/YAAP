# Reading log formats

A reading log is only useful if it records why: why a book was added, and what the reader actually thought. Titles and star ratings alone don't help pick the next book.

## The to-read list

One line per book, with the reason captured at the moment it's added. Three months later, "recommended by Sam because I loved *The Remains of the Day*" is the whole point.

| Title / author | Added | From | Why | Mood tag | Length | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Piranesi*, Susanna Clarke | 2026-09-12 | friend Sam | "strange, quiet, a house that's a world" | puzzle, slow | short | waiting |

**Mood tags** help when the user says "I want something light": comfort, page-turner, slow, puzzle, big ideas, funny, heavy, escape. Use the user's own words where they have them.

**Statuses:** waiting, owned, on hold at library, reading, finished, abandoned, dropped from list.

## Keeping it alive

- **Quarterly prune.** Go through anything added more than a year ago. For each: still curious? If not, drop it with no guilt. A shorter list gets read.
- **Cap the "next up" shelf** at five or so. The rest is the long list.
- **Note the source's track record.** If one friend's picks always land, say so in future recommendations.

## Finished-book entry

Write it within a day or two of finishing, while the reaction is fresh. Ask the user rather than inventing.

```
Title / author:
Finished: [date]      Format: [print / ebook / audio]
Verdict in one line: (their words)
Rating: [their scale, if they use one]
What worked:
What didn't:
A line or scene that stuck:
Felt like: [mood tags]
Would recommend to: [a type of reader, or a named friend]
Read next because of it: [authors, themes]
```

**Example**

> *Piranesi*, Susanna Clarke. Finished 2026-10-03, audio.
> Verdict: "Weird in the best way, and I didn't want it to end."
> Worked: the voice; the slow reveal; short.
> Didn't: the middle dragged slightly.
> Stuck: the tides coming into the lower halls.
> Felt like: puzzle, quiet, melancholy.
> Recommend to: anyone who liked *The Library at Mount Char*, but gentler.

## Abandoned-book note

Abandoning a book is data, often better data than finishing one. Record:

- where they stopped (page or percentage);
- why, in their words ("too much battle detail", "hated the narrator", "wrong time, might return");
- whether it's "never" or "not now".

"Not now" books go back on the list with a note; "never" books teach the profile what to avoid.

## Yearly look-back

At year end, offer a short summary: books finished, favourites in their words, patterns (they finished every short novel and abandoned three of four long fantasy books), and a question about what they want more of next year. Don't turn reading into a target unless the user wants one.

## Common mistakes

- Logging only titles, with no reason or reaction.
- Paraphrasing the user's verdict into blander words.
- Treating abandoned books as failures to hide.
- A to-read list that only ever grows.
