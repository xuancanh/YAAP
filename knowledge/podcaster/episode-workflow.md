# Episode workflow and edit budget

The release date is set by the slowest stage, and on most shows that is editing. Plan backwards from the publish date with edit time measured, not guessed.

## Stages and exit criteria

A card moves only when its exit criterion is true.

| Stage | Done when |
|---|---|
| Idea | One-sentence premise and why a listener would care |
| Booked | Guest confirmed in writing, date and platform set, release form sent |
| Prepped | Research brief and question outline written; guest has the prep sheet |
| Recorded | Raw files backed up in two places; recording notes with rough timestamps of the best moments |
| Edited | Rough cut, then fine cut; loudness normalised; ad slots marked |
| Notes | Description, chapters, links and transcript drafted ([template](show-notes-template.md)) |
| Scheduled | Uploaded to the host, metadata checked, publish time set |
| Published | Live, promo posted, sponsor proof of airing logged |

## Budgeting edit time

Don't use rules of thumb from forums; ratios vary hugely with format. Measure your own:

1. For the last five episodes, log raw minutes recorded and hours spent editing (rough cut, fine cut, mix separately if you can).
2. Divide hours by finished minutes to get the show's edit ratio. Narrative shows usually sit far above interview shows.
3. Budget the next episode at the ratio plus a buffer for the first episode with a new guest setup or new co-host.
4. Recheck the ratio each quarter. If it creeps up, look for the cause: noisy remote audio, over-long recordings, or perfectionism on filler words.

**Worked example.** Raw tape is 75 minutes, the target length is 50 minutes, and the measured ratio is 3 hours of editing per finished 10 minutes. Budget: 15 hours. With 6 editing hours a week, the episode needs to be recorded at least three weeks before publish, not one.

## Keep a bank

A buffer of finished episodes is what lets a show survive illness, travel and cancelled guests. Track "weeks of buffer" on the pipeline: finished-but-unpublished episodes divided by the release cadence. When it hits zero, say so in the Tuesday review and propose a fix: a re-run, a short solo episode, or a moved date announced to listeners.

## Recording-day checklist

- Guest's mic, headphones and quiet room confirmed the day before
- Local recording on both ends if the platform supports it (a backup if the call drops)
- Levels checked with a 30-second test; room tone recorded
- Consent to record confirmed at the start, on tape ([why](guest-prep-and-releases.md))
- Notes taken with timestamps of strong moments and anything the guest asks to cut

## Pre-publish checklist

- Audio: no clipping, consistent loudness between hosts, ad slots placed where the contract says
- Cuts the guest asked for are actually made
- Title, episode number, season, artwork and explicit flag set correctly
- Sponsor read is present and the disclosure is audible ([rules](sponsorship-and-music-rights.md))
- Music and clips are cleared, with the licence noted in the episode file
- Show notes links tested; transcript attached
- Publish time matches the calendar and any sponsor flight dates

## Common mistakes

- Booking the release date before the guest is recorded.
- Treating "Recorded" as nearly done when the edit is most of the work.
- One copy of the raw files.
- Publishing before the guest has seen agreed cuts, if you promised a review.
