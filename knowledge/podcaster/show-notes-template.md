# Show notes template

Show notes do two jobs. The first lines sell the listen to someone deciding in a podcast app. The rest serves the listener who wants the link, the timestamp or the quote.

## What shows where

Podcast apps often cut the description after the first line or two, so the first sentence must stand on its own. Search inside apps and on the web mostly sees the title and the description text, so use the words a listener would type.

## Template

```
Title: [Specific promise or guest + topic]   (avoid "Episode 47")

[One sentence: who, what, and why it matters to the listener.]
[One or two sentences: the most surprising thing they'll hear.]

In this episode:
- [Takeaway 1]
- [Takeaway 2]
- [Takeaway 3]

Chapters:
00:00 Intro
02:15 [Topic]
14:40 [Topic]
...

Links:
- [Guest's site / book / project]
- [Anything mentioned on air]

Sponsor: [Name] — [offer] [link]   (if sponsored; see disclosure rules)

Transcript: [link]
Credits: Hosted by …, edited by …, music: [track] by [artist], [licence]
```

## Chapters and timestamps

- Take them from the final edit, not the raw recording, or every time is wrong after cuts.
- Start at 00:00. Many apps turn correctly formatted timestamps in the description into tappable chapters; check how your host handles chapter markers.
- Name chapters by content ("Why the first prototype failed"), not by question number.

## Transcripts

Transcripts help deaf and hard-of-hearing listeners, people who skim, and search. Machine transcripts are a draft: fix names, jargon and any line that changes meaning. Mark speakers. Remove anything cut from the audio.

## Worked example

> **Title:** Why most home gardens fail in year two, with soil scientist Dana Ruiz
>
> Dana Ruiz has tested soil from hundreds of community plots, and she says the problem is almost never the seeds. Here's what she checks first, and the cheap fix that doubled her own harvest.
>
> In this episode:
> - The one soil test worth paying for
> - Why compost alone can make things worse
> - What to plant when you've got clay
>
> Chapters: 00:00 Intro · 03:10 Year-two collapse · 18:45 Testing on a budget · 31:20 Clay soil
>
> Links: Dana's lab · the extension service guide she mentioned

The title names the problem and the guest. The first line has a hook a listener can't get elsewhere. (The "hundreds" claim must come from the guest, not be invented.)

## Checklist before publishing

- First sentence works alone
- Title is specific and under the length your host recommends
- Timestamps checked against the final file
- Every link opens
- Sponsor line and disclosure match the ad read ([rules](sponsorship-and-music-rights.md))
- Music credits and licences listed
- Guest name and title spelled the way they spell it

## Common mistakes

- A description that recaps the intro chit-chat.
- Keyword stuffing; it reads badly and apps may treat it as spam.
- Claims or numbers in the notes that the guest didn't say.
