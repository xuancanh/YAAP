# Guest prep, consent and releases

Good tape comes from a guest who knows what to expect. The sequence is: a short pitch, a confirmation with logistics, a prep sheet, a reminder, then thanks and the episode link.

## Outreach template

> Subject: Invitation to [Show] — [one-line topic]
>
> Hi [Name], I host [Show], a [format] for [audience]. Your [specific piece of work] is exactly what our listeners keep asking about. I'd love 45 minutes to talk about [angle]. We record remotely; you'd need a headset and a quiet room. Recent episodes: [link]. Would any time in [window] work?

Keep it under 120 words, name something specific they made, and give one clear ask. Follow up once after a week, then let it go.

## Prep sheet (send after they say yes)

- Date, time with time zone, recording link, expected length
- Equipment: wired headphones, a microphone if they have one, laptop plugged in, notifications off
- The angle and three or four topic areas (not a script)
- Anything off-limits they want to name in advance
- How editing works: what you cut, whether they get to review, when it airs
- The release form link and a line on consent to record

## The release form

A guest release is a short written agreement that the show can record, edit and publish the conversation. Have one per guest, stored with the episode. Typically it covers:

- permission to record, edit and distribute the audio (and video, if any) in any podcast format;
- use of the guest's name, likeness and bio in promotion;
- no payment, or the agreed fee;
- whether the guest has any right to review or veto, and until when;
- the guest's confirmation they have the right to share anything they bring (clips, images).

Use a template your lawyer has looked at once; don't draft new terms per guest. Track status on the guest card: sent, signed, filed.

## Recording consent

Laws on recording conversations differ by country and, in the US, by state. Some places allow recording when one party consents; others require everyone on the call to agree. A cross-border remote call can fall under more than one rule. The Reporters Committee's state-by-state guide (see sources) is a starting point for US calls.

The safe practice everywhere: say on tape at the start, "We're recording this for the podcast; is that okay with you?" and keep the guest's yes. Do the same for any third person who joins.

Flag to the host and suggest a lawyer when a guest wants to withdraw after recording, when a recording includes someone who didn't agree, or when a guest discusses identifiable third parties in ways that might be defamatory.

## The relationship file

After each episode, note on the guest card: what went well, their audio quality, whether they shared the episode, and anyone they offered to introduce. Send the published link and a clip they can post. A guest who had a good experience is the best source of the next one.

## Common mistakes

- Asking for "a quick chat" with no topic or length.
- Sending the release after the recording.
- Promising approval rights you don't intend to honour.
- Not recording consent on tape because "they obviously knew".
