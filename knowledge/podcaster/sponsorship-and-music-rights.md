# Sponsorship disclosure and music rights

General information, not legal advice. Rules differ by country; the US points below come from FTC guidance (Endorsement Guides revised in 2023). The UK, EU and others have their own advertising codes. Check the rules where the show and its audience are.

## Disclosure: the principle

Listeners must be able to tell when they're hearing paid content. In the US, the FTC treats a host-read ad as an endorsement, and a material connection (payment, free product, affiliate commission) must be disclosed clearly.

For audio, that means:

- **Say it out loud.** If the endorsement is audible, the disclosure should be audible too, at a normal pace and volume, not a fast tag at the end.
- **Say it where the ad is.** A disclosure at the top of the episode doesn't cover a mid-roll an hour later. Each read carries its own.
- **Repeat it in text.** Where the endorsement appears in show notes or social posts, disclose there too.
- **Plain words.** "This episode is sponsored by…", "[Brand] paid for this segment", "I get a commission if you use this link".
- **Honest claims.** The host can only say they use and like something if it's true. Sponsor-supplied claims ("clinically proven") need the sponsor's substantiation.

Affiliate links and free products count, not just cash.

## Music and clips

The common myth is that a few seconds of a song is fine. It isn't automatically. A recorded song usually has two copyrights: the composition (songwriter or publisher) and the master recording (often a label). Licences that radio stations hold from performing-rights organisations (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and others) generally don't cover on-demand podcast use, and they don't cover the master.

Safer options, in order:

1. **Original music** you commissioned, with a written agreement that covers podcast use.
2. **Production music libraries** with a licence that names podcasts. Keep the licence document.
3. **Creative Commons** tracks, following the licence terms exactly (credit line, no commercial use if it says NC).
4. **Public-domain recordings.** Note that an old composition can be public domain while a newer recording of it is not.

Fair use (US) and fair dealing (UK and others) are defences decided case by case, not permissions. Commentary and criticism of a short clip may qualify; using a song as a theme tune won't. The Copyright Office's Fair Use Index shows how courts have ruled.

Log every track and clip in the episode file: title, source, licence, and the credit line.

## When to send it to a lawyer

- A takedown notice or a claim from a rights holder
- A sponsor contract with exclusivity, approval rights or usage of the host's voice in other ads
- Health, finance or legal products, where advertising rules are stricter
- Any plan to rely on fair use for music
