# Invoice and payment checklist

The biggest lever on getting paid is speed: invoice the day the job or stage finishes. The second is clarity, so the client has no reason to query it.

## Invoice must-haves

- [ ] Your business name, address and contact details
- [ ] The client's name and the site address
- [ ] A unique invoice number and the invoice date
- [ ] The quote reference, with the scope delivered in short lines
- [ ] Each variation itemised with its date and confirmation reference (see [Variations and snagging](variations-and-snagging.md))
- [ ] Deposits and stage payments already received, deducted
- [ ] The total, with any sales tax or VAT shown as your registration requires
- [ ] Payment terms (due date, not just "30 days") and how to pay
- [ ] Certificates referenced or attached where the job has them

**Country-specific:** what an invoice must legally show, especially for tax registration numbers and how tax is shown, depends on where you're registered. Check your tax authority's guidance or ask your accountant. This list is a practical minimum, not the legal one.

## Setting payment terms

| Job size | Typical structure to propose |
|---|---|
| Small, a day or two | Payment on completion |
| Medium | Deposit on booking, balance on completion |
| Large or long | Deposit, stage payments at named milestones, final payment at snag sign-off |

Tips:

- Name milestones you can photograph: "first fix complete", not "halfway".
- Ask for a bigger deposit when materials are expensive or made to order.
- Clients who paid late last time get a larger deposit on their next quote.

## The chase routine

| When | Action | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Due date + 3 days | Short reminder with the invoice attached | Friendly: "flagging in case it slipped" |
| + 14 days | Phone call, then a letter or email: amount, dates, "Is there a problem with the work? Tell me now." | Direct |
| + 30 days | Firm letter: amount, history of reminders, the next step you'll take, and a date | Firm, factual |
| Beyond that, or disputed | Take advice: small-claims guidance, a debt-recovery service or a solicitor | Professional territory |

Log each step in the ledger with its date. A clean record makes any later claim much easier.

## Late-payment rights (country-specific, check before you quote them)

Some countries give businesses statutory rights when other businesses pay late. In the UK, for example, GOV.UK explains that you can claim interest and debt recovery costs if another business is late paying. If no date was agreed, payment counts as late 30 days after the customer gets the invoice or the work is done, whichever is later. Rules for consumer clients (homeowners) are usually different. Never put a legal threat in a letter unless the user has confirmed it applies to them.

## Year-end pack

When the accountant asks, collect:

- every invoice issued, plus the ledger showing paid and unpaid;
- materials invoices and receipts;
- the margin per job (quoted against actual);
- outstanding debts with their age.

Tax treatment, allowable expenses and registration thresholds are for the accountant. Hand over the pack and let them decide.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until Friday night to invoice Monday's job.
- "Payment due on receipt" with no date. It reads as optional.
- Leaving variations as one lump sum with no dates or confirmations.
- Arguing about a dispute in the chase letter. Ask what's wrong, then fix it or escalate.
