# Variations and snagging

Two things leak margin quietly at the end of a job: extra work nobody priced, and a snag list nobody finishes. Both get fixed the same way: write it down as it happens, and get it confirmed.

## Variations (change orders)

A variation is any change to the agreed scope: extra work, less work, different materials or different timing. The client's "while you're here, could you…" is a variation.

### The method

1. **Log it the moment it's asked.** Note the date, who asked and what exactly. A voice note to the assistant is fine. It goes in the job's variations log.
2. **Price it from the price file**, the same way as the original quote: materials, labour, any knock-on delay.
3. **Send the variation note before doing the work.** Most clients accept a same-day text or email.
4. **Get a written yes.** "Yes, go ahead" by text counts as written. File it.
5. **Update the job card**: scope, price, finish date.
6. **Itemise it on the invoice** with its date and confirmation reference.

### Variation note template

```
Variation <job>-V<n>      Date: <date>
Requested by: <name>, <date/time>

Change: <what is added/removed/changed>
Reason: <client request / hidden defect found / material unavailable>
Price change: + <amount>  (or – <amount>)
Time change: + <n> days  (new finish: <date>)

Please reply "confirmed" to go ahead. Work on this item
starts only once confirmed.
```

### Hidden defects

When opening up reveals a problem (rot behind tiles, damaged pipework):

- Stop work on that part and take photos.
- Send the variation note with the photos and the options ("repair now, +X" or "make safe and leave, +Y").
- If it's a safety issue, the qualified person decides what has to happen. The note just records it.

## Snagging

Snags are the small defects and unfinished items found near completion: a dripping trap, a paint skip, a missing cover plate.

### Run it as a list

1. **Walk round with the client** at practical completion, with the scope from the quote in hand.
2. **Log each item**: location, what's wrong, a photo, who fixes it and the target date.
3. **Separate snags from new requests.** "Can you also move that socket?" is a variation, not a snag.
4. **Close items with evidence**: an "after" photo and a date.
5. **Sign off** when the list is empty. The final invoice follows.

### Handover sign-off

```
Handover — <job>, <address>       Date: <date>
Work completed as per quote <n> and variations V1–V<n>.
Snag list: all items closed (attached).
Certificates provided: <list, or "none required">
Guarantees and manuals handed over: <list>
Client signature: ________
```

## Worked example

On day 2 of a kitchen refit, the client asks for an extra double socket. Log V1: the socket, 1.5 h plus parts, from the price file. Send the note at 11:20. The client replies "confirmed" at 11:45, and the electrician adds it to their certification. At handover the snag list has 4 items, all closed by day 6. The final invoice lists the quote total, plus V1 with its date and confirmation, minus the deposit paid.

## Common mistakes

- Doing the "small favour" free, then trying to bill it at the end.
- Verbal-only agreements on price changes.
- Letting the snag list turn into a wish list. Keep variations separate.
- Holding the whole final payment over one tiny snag. Agree a retention amount for that item instead.
