# Quote template and exclusions

A good quote says what's included, what isn't, how long the price holds and how changes get handled. Most disputes start from something the quote never said.

## Pricing build-up (internal, never sent)

Work it out in this order and keep the sheet with the job:

1. **Materials** = quantity × price-file price × (1 + waste allowance). Use the price file's waste figure for each material, not a single blanket figure.
2. **Labour** = hours per stage (from your job-log actuals) × crew rate. Add an awkwardness allowance for occupied homes, poor access, or working at height.
3. **Direct costs**: skip hire, plant hire, parking, disposal fees, permit fees.
4. **Overhead share**: van, insurance, tools, phone and admin time, spread across billable hours or jobs in a way you apply the same every time.
5. **Margin**: a percentage chosen on purpose and written down.
6. **Price** = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5. Round it, and note what the rounding did.

### Worked example (illustrative numbers)

Replace a bathroom basin and tap, occupied flat, third floor, no lift.

| Line | Working | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Materials | basin, tap, fittings from the price file, plus 5% waste on fittings | 212 |
| Labour | 5 h × 45/h, plus 1 h for carrying and access | 270 |
| Direct | disposal of the old basin | 20 |
| Overhead share | 6 h × 8/h | 48 |
| Subtotal | | 550 |
| Margin | 15% of subtotal | 82.50 |
| Price | rounded | 635 |

The check: if a competitor quotes 500, matching them means giving up the whole margin and some overhead. Say that plainly to the user.

## Client quote template

```
QUOTE <number>       Date: <date>      Valid until: <date>
Client: <name, site address>

Scope of work
1. Supply and fit <item>, including <specific inclusions>.
2. ...

Not included (see below if not listed)
- <exclusion>
- ...

Price: <total> <tax line as applicable>
<Itemised to the level the trade prefers>

Payment schedule
- Deposit <x%> on booking
- <Stage> on completion of <milestone>
- Balance on completion and sign-off

Changes: any work outside this scope is quoted in writing and
confirmed by you before it starts.
Materials: prices of <volatile items> hold until <date>; after that
they may be re-quoted.
Start date (provisional): <date>, subject to <access/materials/permit>.
```

## Exclusions bank

Pick the ones that apply and write them for this job:

- Making good beyond patch repair (redecorating, re-tiling outside the work area)
- Moving furniture or contents, and protecting items left in the work area
- Repairs to hidden defects found once work starts (rot, damaged pipes, old wiring). These are quoted separately when found.
- Permits, building-control or inspection fees (or say "included" explicitly)
- Asbestos testing or removal by specialists
- Waste removal beyond the job's own waste
- Work on parts owned by others (shared walls, communal areas) without their consent
- Client-supplied materials: fitting only, no warranty on the item itself
- Out-of-hours or weekend working

## Before it goes out — checklist

- [ ] Scope lines can be ticked off at handover.
- [ ] Exclusions cover this job's likely surprises.
- [ ] Validity date and volatile-material line are present.
- [ ] Payment schedule matches the job size.
- [ ] Any regulated parts name who certifies them (see [Safety and certified-work boundaries](safety-and-certified-work-boundaries.md)).
- [ ] The tax line follows the user's registration status. Confirm it with their accountant; don't assume it.
- [ ] A follow-up is scheduled for day 5.

## Common mistakes

- Quoting labour from memory instead of from job-log actuals.
- One blanket waste percentage for everything.
- "Price includes everything." Nothing ever does.
- No validity date, followed by a price rise three months later.
