# Reply writing guide

A good reply answers the question in the first lines, gives steps the customer can follow without guessing, and says what happens next. Kindness is in the clarity, not in extra words.

## Before you write

- Read the whole thread and the customer's record (plan, past tickets, recent changes).
- Restate the problem to yourself in one sentence. If you cannot, ask one clear question instead of guessing.
- Find the answer in the help centre or internal knowledge; check the article is current.
- If you will reproduce, reproduce first; then answer.

## Structure

1. **The answer or the status**, in the first sentence or two.
2. **Steps**, numbered, one action each, using the exact names of buttons and menus.
3. **Why** (only if it helps them avoid the problem again).
4. **What happens next**: what you will do and when, or what they should do if it does not work.

Example:

> You can export last month's invoices from **Billing › History**: choose the date range, then **Download CSV**.
>
> 1. Open **Billing › History**.
> 2. Set **From** and **To** to the month you need.
> 3. Click **Download CSV**.
>
> If the button is greyed out, your role may not include billing access. Your workspace admin can change that in **Settings › Members**. Reply here if you'd like me to check which admin that is.

## Tone rules

- Use the customer's name and plain words. Avoid internal jargon and ticket codes.
- Acknowledge impact once, specifically ("I know this blocked your payroll run today"), then move to the fix.
- Apologise for the experience when something went wrong on our side; do not apologise for things you cannot know.
- Do not blame the customer or another team. Describe what happened.
- Match the customer's formality; never match their anger.

## Saying "no" or "not yet"

- Say it plainly, then offer what you can do: "That isn't possible in the current version. Here's a workaround that gets you most of the way…"
- For feature requests: thank them, say you've logged it with product and what it was logged as. Do not give dates or imply it will be built.
- For refunds, credits or exceptions: "I've passed this to our billing team, who make that decision. I'll update you by Thursday." Then do.

## Escalation replies

When you escalate, tell the customer:
- That it has been escalated and to whom (team, not necessarily a name)
- What the next update will be and when
- Anything they can do in the meantime

Keep that promise; update even if there is no news.

## Macros and saved replies

Macros save time and keep answers consistent, but:
- Always personalise the first line and check every step applies to this customer's plan and settings.
- If a macro is wrong, fix the macro, not just your reply, and tell its owner.

## Self-check before sending

- [ ] Answers the actual question asked (re-read their message)
- [ ] Steps tested or taken from a current article
- [ ] No promise of refund, credit or roadmap date
- [ ] No other customer's data
- [ ] Next step and timing clear
- [ ] Ticket fields (category, priority, tags) updated
