# Session log and prep template

Write the log for the coach in three months, not for an auditor. Short, factual, and focused on threads: what the client is working on, what they tried, and what keeps coming back.

## Session log template

```
Client: <code>   Session: <n> of <m>   Date:   Format: in person / video
Client's agenda (their words):
Main thread(s): <which goal(s)>
Key moments: <2–4 bullets — insights, shifts, energy changes>
Patterns noticed: <a theme that showed up again; quote if useful>
Experiment agreed: <what, by when>
Coach's note to self: <hunch to test, question to ask next time>
Anything requiring follow-up outside coaching: <yes/no — see referral guide>
```

Keep detail on third parties (colleagues, family) to what's needed. Use roles, not names, where possible.

## Pre-session brief

Built from the log in five minutes before the session:

```
Client <code>, session <n>, <date>
Goals and current read: <one line each>
Last session: <thread, experiment agreed>
Experiment check: <what to ask about it>
Pattern across sessions: <the theme the log shows, with session numbers>
Questions the log suggests: <2–3 open questions>
Logistics: <sessions left, review date, any sponsor check-in due>
```

## Spotting patterns

Read the last three to five logs together and look for:

- **Repeated words or images** the client uses ("firefighting", "I have to").
- **The same obstacle** behind different situations.
- **Avoided topics:** a goal that never gets agenda time.
- **Experiments not done** and the reasons given.
- **Energy shifts:** what lights them up or flattens them.

Offer patterns as observations for the coach to test, not conclusions: "In sessions 3, 5 and 6 she described saying yes to avoid conflict. Worth naming?"

## Good open questions by purpose

- **Clarify:** "What would 'handled well' look like?"
- **Explore:** "What makes this one hard for you in particular?"
- **Challenge:** "What would you tell a peer in this spot?"
- **Commit:** "What's the smallest step you'd actually take this week?"
- **Reflect:** "What did you notice about yourself in that?"

## Worked example

Logs 4–6 each mention a skipped one-to-one with a direct report "because something urgent came up". The brief suggests: "Pattern: one-to-ones dropped under pressure (S4, S5, S6). Question: what would it take for those to be the last thing to go, not the first?"

## Common mistakes

- Transcript-length notes that bury the thread.
- Logging interpretations as facts ("she's insecure").
- Prep that rehearses the last session instead of looking for the pattern.
- Detailed notes on sensitive personal matters that don't serve the coaching.
