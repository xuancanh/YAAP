# Coaching goals and evidence

"Be more strategic" can't be reviewed. "In leadership meetings, spends less time on operational detail and more on next quarter's choices, as noticed by the CEO" can. Good goals describe behaviour other people could see.

## From aim to goal

Ask four questions about each aim the contract names:

1. **What would people see you doing differently?** Push for actions, not traits.
2. **Where and when?** The settings that matter (board meetings, one-to-ones, written updates).
3. **Who would notice?** Name the observers; they become the stakeholders for check-ins.
4. **What's the starting point?** Capture it now, in the client's and observers' words, so progress has something to compare against.

| Vague aim | Observable goal |
|---|---|
| More executive presence | In senior meetings, states her recommendation in the first two minutes and holds it under challenge |
| Better delegation | Hands off two recurring reports fully to direct reports; stops reviewing them line by line |
| Less reactive | Responds to escalations with a question before a decision; team reports fewer reversals |

Keep two or three goals per engagement. More than that dilutes attention.

## Evidence types

Use a mix; none is enough alone.

- **Client self-report:** reflections at each session, a simple 1–10 self-rating per goal tracked over time. Useful for direction, weak as proof.
- **Specific examples:** the meeting where they did it (or didn't). Log date and context.
- **Stakeholder observation:** short check-ins with the named observers at the start, midway and at the end. Ask "What have you noticed?" not "Has she improved?"
- **Artefacts:** delegated work, calendar shifts, written updates. Only with the client's consent.

## The homework ledger

```
| Session | Agreed experiment | By when | What happened | What they learned |
```

Frame homework as experiments. "Didn't do it" is data too: note what got in the way.

## Progress review structure

1. Goals as agreed, with the starting point.
2. Evidence for each, by type, with dates.
3. The client's own assessment.
4. Honest read: moved, partly moved, not moved. Say which, with the reason.
5. What's next: continue, refocus, or complete.

The sponsor report includes only what the contract and the client agreed. A common default is themes and progress against goals, not session content.

## Worked example

Goal: "States her recommendation early in exec meetings." Start: CEO says "We often don't know where she stands until the end." Self-rating 3/10. Midpoint: logs four meetings, two with early recommendations; self-rating 6. End: CEO check-in: "She leads with a view now; I still see her soften under pressure from Finance." Read: largely moved; next focus, holding the view under challenge.

## Common mistakes

- Goals the client can't influence ("get promoted").
- Only self-report, so reviews feel good and prove little.
- Rewriting goals mid-way without recording why.
- Sponsor reports that leak session content.
