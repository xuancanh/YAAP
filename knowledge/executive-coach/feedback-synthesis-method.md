# 360 feedback synthesis method

The aim is a report that is honest, specific and safe for the people who gave feedback. The client should leave the debrief knowing their two or three biggest strengths and development areas, with evidence they can't easily dismiss.

## Before collecting

- Agree with the client who is asked, what anonymity they're promised, and who sees the report (usually the client first, sponsor only as agreed).
- Ask the same core questions of everyone, for example: What does she do that you'd want more of? What gets in her way? If she changed one thing, what would make the biggest difference?
- A minimum group size per rater category protects anonymity. If a category has only one person (often the manager), tell them their input will be identifiable or merge it.

## Synthesis steps

1. **Raw to quotes.** Pull every substantive comment into one sheet: rater group, quote, topic.
2. **Code topics.** Tag each comment with a short theme (decision speed, listening, strategic focus). Let themes come from the data; don't start from a competency list unless the contract requires one.
3. **Count and weigh.** For each theme: how many raters, which groups, how strong the language. A theme raised by several people across groups is a headline; one strong comment is a note.
4. **Find tensions.** Where groups differ (peers vs. direct reports), that's often the most useful insight.
5. **Keep hard messages whole.** Don't soften a theme into nothing. Phrase it plainly and back it with two or three representative quotes.
6. **Strengths first, and real.** Strengths are as specific as development areas.
7. **De-identify quotes.** Remove details that reveal who said it (a project only one person worked on, a distinctive phrase).

## Report shape

```
Purpose and who contributed (groups and counts, not names)
Top strengths (2–3): theme — evidence — representative quotes
Development areas (2–3): theme — evidence — quotes
Where views differ: …
Questions for the client to consider
```

## Debrief preparation

- Expect a reaction to the hardest theme. Prepare the opening line and one question ("What surprises you? What fits?").
- Give the client time with the report before analysis, if the coach's practice allows.
- End with choices: which one or two themes become coaching goals.
- Watch for distress beyond disappointment. If the feedback lands as something larger, see the [boundaries and referral guide](boundaries-and-referral.md).

## Worked example

Twelve raters. Eight mention fast decisions as a strength; five direct reports (but no peers) say decisions arrive without explanation. Synthesis: strength "decisive under pressure"; development "brings the team along: explains the why". Tension: peers see clarity, reports feel shut out.

## Common mistakes

- Averaging away the signal: "generally positive".
- Quoting a comment only one person could have written.
- Letting the coach's own view of the client colour theme selection.
- Sending the full report to the sponsor without the client's agreement.
