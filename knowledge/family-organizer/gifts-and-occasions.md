# Gifts and occasions system

The trick is to capture ideas when they surface (in conversation, in March) and act on them with enough lead time. Three files do it: the occasions list, the ideas file, and the social ledger.

## 1. Occasions list

```
| Person | Relationship | Occasion | Date | Lead time | Budget | Last year's gift | Notes |
```

- **Lead time:** how early to start. Posted gifts or anything personalised need more; a card for a neighbour needs little.
- Include occasions that aren't birthdays: anniversaries, graduations, exam results, new babies, religious and cultural festivals the family marks, teacher thank-yous at year end.
- Set two reminders: one at the lead time ("start thinking"), one a few days before ("card signed and sent?").

## 2. Gift ideas file (private)

```
| Person | Idea | Where heard / why | Price range | Link | Date added | Used? |
```

Capture the reason: "Said her old headphones broke (May, at lunch)." Specific clues make better gifts than lists of categories.

Keep this file private. Never put it in shared/, especially if the recipient is in the household.

## 3. Social ledger

Who hosted you, who sent a gift, who helped out, and whether it's been returned:

```
| Family/person | What they did | Date | Our response | Done? |
```

Not a scorecard; a memory aid. "They had us for dinner twice; we owe them an invitation" is useful to know before it becomes awkward.

## Picking a gift: quick guide

1. Check the ideas file first.
2. Consider the relationship and budget; check last year's gift to avoid repeats.
3. For children's parties, many families set a common budget; follow local norms, or ask the host if unsure.
4. Experiences, consumables and shared time often beat things for people who have everything.
5. When stuck, ask a close family member, or offer a choice with a gift card plus a handwritten note.

## Budget

Set a yearly gift budget and track spending per person. Big seasonal festivals can concentrate spending; spreading purchases through the year helps.

## Thank-yous

Log gifts received at birthdays and festivals with who gave what, so thank-you notes are specific. Younger children can draw a picture; older ones write a few lines.

## Worked example

March: grandmother mentions she'd like to try pottery. Logged with the date. Her birthday is 20 June; lead time three weeks. On 30 May the reminder fires: the assistant suggests a local beginners' class voucher within the agreed budget and a card from the children. On 17 June: "Card posted?" On 25 June the ledger notes her thank-you call.

## Common mistakes

- Ideas held in memory and lost by the occasion.
- Gift list in a shared folder the recipient can see.
- Only tracking birthdays and missing the rest.
- No budget, then an expensive panic purchase.
