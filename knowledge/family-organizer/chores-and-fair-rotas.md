# Chores by age and fair rotas

Rotas break down over fairness, not effort. Make the load visible, match jobs to ability, and agree the rules before the first argument.

## Chores by age (a rough guide)

Children develop at different rates; use this as a starting point and adjust to the child. Supervise anything involving heat, sharp tools, chemicals or heights, and keep cleaning products out of young children's reach.

| Age band | Usually manageable with practice |
|---|---|
| Toddlers and preschool | Putting toys in a box, putting clothes in the laundry basket, wiping a spill, helping set the table with unbreakables |
| Early primary | Making their bed (roughly), feeding a pet with a measure, setting and clearing the table, sorting laundry by colour, watering plants |
| Late primary | Loading and emptying the dishwasher, vacuuming a room, folding laundry, simple food prep, taking out recycling, packing their own school bag |
| Early teens | Cooking a simple meal with supervision, cleaning the bathroom, doing a load of laundry start to finish, mowing with training and supervision |
| Older teens | Planning and cooking a family meal, grocery shopping to a list, most household jobs, looking after younger siblings for short spells where legal and appropriate |

## Building the rota

1. **List every recurring job** and roughly how long it takes and how often. Include invisible jobs: planning meals, booking appointments, buying presents, remembering forms.
2. **Score each job** in minutes per week. This turns "I do everything" into numbers.
3. **Match jobs to people** by age, schedule and preference. Let people pick some jobs; ownership helps.
4. **Balance by time, not count.** One big job can equal three small ones.
5. **Rotate the unpopular jobs** (bins, bathroom) weekly so no one owns them forever.
6. **Write the definition of done.** "Kitchen cleaned" means counters wiped, dishes away, floor swept.
7. **Agree the rules:** swaps allowed if both agree and it's written down; what happens when a job is missed; whether pocket money is linked.

## Rota template

```
Week of <date>
| Job | Done means | Time | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun | Owner |
```

Post it in the shared folder. A visible tick is often enough motivation for younger children.

## Refereeing a fairness argument

1. Pull out the time scores; look at the actual totals.
2. Ask each person what feels unfair. It's often a specific job, not the total.
3. Offer two or three adjustments; the adults decide.
4. Write down the change and review in two weeks.

## Worked example

Family of four: adults each do about five hours a week of visible chores, but Adult A also does about three hours of planning and booking. Making the invisible jobs visible shifts meal planning and present-buying to Adult B. The 10-year-old takes the dishwasher and recycling; the 6-year-old sets the table and feeds the cat.

## Common mistakes

- Counting jobs instead of time.
- Leaving out the mental load.
- Redoing a child's job in front of them, which teaches them not to bother.
- A rota nobody reviews, which quietly dies after a month.
