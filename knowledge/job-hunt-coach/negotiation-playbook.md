# Negotiation playbook

Most employers expect some negotiation, and a polite, specific ask rarely costs an offer. The work is in the research and in asking once, clearly.

## Step 1: research the range

Use at least two kinds of source, and note the date of each:

- **Government wage data.** In the US, the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes wages by occupation and area (OEWS). Many countries' statistics offices publish similar data.
- **Salary ranges in job postings.** Some US states and cities, and some other countries, require ranges in postings; collect them for similar roles.
- **Salary surveys** from professional bodies or recruiters in the field.
- **People.** Peers, mentors, former colleagues, recruiters.

Write down a low, middle and high figure for the role, level and location. Note what pushes towards the top: scarce skills, a competing offer, a bigger scope.

## Step 2: the salary question before an offer

The user doesn't have to give a number first. Some places restrict employers from asking about salary history; check local rules.

> "I'd like to learn more about the role first. Could you share the range budgeted for it?"

If pressed:

> "From my research, roles like this in [location] are paying roughly [range]. I'm flexible depending on the whole package."

Give a range whose bottom is a number the user would be happy with.

## Step 3: when the offer arrives

1. **Thank them and ask for time.** "Thanks, I'm excited about this. Could I have until [day] to look at the details?"
2. **Get it in writing,** with all parts: base, bonus, equity, benefits, start date, title, location or remote terms.
3. **Compare the whole package** (see the offer-evaluation skill).

## Step 4: the ask

One ask, specific, with a reason. Ideally by phone or video, then confirm in writing.

> "I'm really keen to join. Based on [the scope / my experience with X / another offer], I was hoping for a base of [number]. If we can get there, I'm ready to accept."

Then stop talking and let them respond.

If they can't move on base:

> "I understand. Is there flexibility on [signing bonus / start date / extra leave / a review at six months]?"

## Other things that may be negotiable

- signing bonus;
- start date;
- title or level;
- remote or flexible working;
- extra leave;
- training or conference budget;
- an early pay review with agreed criteria;
- relocation support.

## Boundaries

- **Equity, tax and pensions:** what an equity grant is worth, how it's taxed and pension choices depend on the user's situation and country. Flag them for a qualified financial or tax adviser.
- **Contracts:** non-compete, IP or notice clauses that look unusual go to an employment lawyer or, in the UK, advice services such as Acas.
- **Bluffing:** never invent a competing offer. If it's discovered, the offer can go.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting on the spot out of relief.
- Asking for five things at once.
- Negotiating by email only, where tone is easy to misread.
- Comparing base salaries without the rest of the package.
- Apologising for asking.

## Worked example

Offer: base at the middle of the user's researched range. The user has a scarce skill listed as "nice to have" in the posting. Script: thanks, enthusiasm, the specific ask at the upper part of the range with the skill as the reason, and willingness to sign. The employer meets them partway and adds a six-month review. The user confirms everything in writing before resigning from their current job.
