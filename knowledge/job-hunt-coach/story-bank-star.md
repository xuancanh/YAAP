# Story bank (STAR)

Most behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time…") can be answered from six to eight well-prepared stories. Build them once, reuse them every interview.

## The STAR shape

| Part | What to say | Rough share |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | the context in one or two sentences | short |
| **T**ask | what you were responsible for | short |
| **A**ction | what *you* did, step by step ("I", not "we") | the longest part |
| **R**esult | what happened, with numbers or concrete outcomes, and what you learned | short, specific |

Aim for about two minutes spoken. Interviewers ask follow-ups if they want more.

## Story template

```
Title: [memorable label, e.g. "The failed launch"]
Situation:
Task:
Action (3-4 steps):
  1.
  2.
  3.
Result (numbers if real):
What I learned / would do differently:
Covers: [leadership, conflict, failure, ...]
```

## The core set

Try to have at least one story for each:

1. **A success you drove** (the headline achievement).
2. **A failure or mistake** and what you changed afterwards.
3. **Conflict** with a colleague, manager or customer, resolved well.
4. **Leading or influencing** without formal authority.
5. **A tight deadline or competing priorities.**
6. **Learning something new fast.**
7. **Going beyond the brief** (initiative).
8. **A hard decision with incomplete information.**

One good story can cover several themes. Map them in a grid against the role's requirements so each requirement has at least one story.

## Common questions and which story fits

| Question | Story type |
|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | not STAR: 60-90 seconds on now, past, and why this role |
| Greatest achievement | success |
| Time you failed | failure |
| Disagreed with your manager | conflict |
| Handled a difficult customer | conflict or pressure |
| Led a project | leading |
| Too much to do | deadline/priorities |
| Why are you leaving? | not STAR: brief, forward-looking, never bitter |

## Rehearsing

- Say each story out loud two or three times. Don't memorise word for word; memorise the steps.
- Record yourself once and listen for "we" where it should be "I", and for missing results.
- Practise a short and a long version.
- Do a mock interview with a friend or the agent playing the interviewer, with follow-up questions.

## Answering the awkward ones

- **Weakness:** a real one that doesn't cripple the role, plus what you're doing about it.
- **Gap or short stint:** one honest sentence, then move to what you learned or are ready for.
- **Salary expectations:** see [Negotiation playbook](negotiation-playbook.md).

## Common mistakes

- Spending most of the answer on the situation and running out of time for the actions.
- Team stories with no clear individual role.
- No result, or a vague one ("it went well").
- Telling a failure story where the failure was really someone else's.

## Worked example

Question: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager." Story "The pricing page": S, the manager wanted to launch a new pricing page in a week. T, the user owned the copy. A, they pulled support tickets showing customers confused by one plan, proposed a two-day test of a simpler version, and agreed a decision rule in advance. R, the simpler version won the test and launched on schedule; the manager asked them to run the next test too. Learned: bring evidence and a way to decide, not just an objection.
