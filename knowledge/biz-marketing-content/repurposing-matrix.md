# Repurposing matrix

Repurposing gets more value from one approved piece by meeting readers where they already are. The rule that matters most: a derivative may shorten, reorder and reformat, but it may not add, strengthen or change a claim. If you want to say something new, it needs its own review.

## Step 1: extract the atoms

Before writing any derivative, list five to ten "atoms" from the approved piece, each with its source line:

```markdown
| # | Atom (verbatim or close paraphrase) | Type | Source in piece |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Reconciliations cause most close delays in our data" | data point | H2 2, para 1 |
| 2 | Five stages of a close | framework | H2 1 |
| 3 | Customer quote from Jane at Acme (approved) | quote | H2 3 |
```

Types: data point, framework or list, quote, how-to step, contrarian opinion, definition. Derivatives are built only from atoms.

## Step 2: pick derivatives with the matrix

Rows are the source format; columns are common derivatives. Only use channels the company actually runs.

| Source ↓ / Derivative → | Social posts | Email snippet | Sales one-pager | Short video or slide script | Support / help macro |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Guide or how-to** | One post per step or tip | Lead with the problem, link the guide | Rarely | Steps as a 60-second script | If it answers a common ticket |
| **Data or research piece** | One post per finding, chart image | Headline finding + link | Yes: top findings as proof | Chart walkthrough | No |
| **Case study** | Quote card, result post | Customer result as the lead | Yes: always | Customer story summary | No |
| **Opinion or framework** | The core argument, a thread or carousel | The argument in three lines | Only if it frames a sales conversation | Talking-head script | No |
| **Product announcement** | What changed and who it's for | Feature + one-line why | Yes, with Product Marketing | Short demo script | Yes |

## Step 3: adapt per channel

General rules that hold across platforms (check each platform's own current format limits before posting, as they change):

- **Social posts:** one idea per post. Hook in the first line, the atom, then the link or CTA. Write natively for the platform rather than pasting the same text everywhere. Tag only people and companies who agreed.
- **Email snippet:** two to four sentences: the reader's problem, the atom that helps, one link. Subject-line ideas can come from the headline tests.
- **Sales one-pager:** problem, our point of view, two or three proof atoms, the next step for the buyer. Coordinate with the Product Marketer so it matches the enablement kit.
- **Scripts:** write for the ear: short sentences, the point said twice (start and end), on-screen text for numbers.
- **Help macro:** plain answer first, then the link. Hand it to the Support team to adopt; don't publish into support tools yourself.

## Step 4: schedule against the calendar

- Spread derivatives over the weeks after the main piece, not all on launch day.
- Match the campaign calendar's themes; don't double up on a day with a launch.
- Record suggested dates in the derivatives file. Someone with a granted tool, or a human, actually posts.

## Derivatives file template

```markdown
# Derivatives: <slug>
Source: /org/marketing/content/drafts/<slug>.md (approved <date>, by <reviewer>)

## Atoms
<table from step 1>

## Social
- Post 1 (atom 2) — suggested <date> — <text>
## Email snippet
## Sales one-pager
## Notes for reviewer
- Any atom you shortened in a way that might change its meaning
```

## Claim-safety checklist

- [ ] Every derivative maps to a numbered atom.
- [ ] No number is rounded up, no "may" became "will", no "some customers" became "customers".
- [ ] Quotes are exact and still approved for this channel.
- [ ] Disclosure lines are included where the company requires them (sponsored, partner or employee posts).
- [ ] Links point to the canonical URL, with campaign tracking parameters if the team uses them.

## Common mistakes

- Making every derivative a teaser with no value of its own. Each post should be useful even if no one clicks.
- Posting the same text on every platform.
- Repurposing an unapproved draft because the launch is close.
- Losing track of which derivative drove what. Tag links so the [metrics](content-metrics.md) can credit the right piece.
