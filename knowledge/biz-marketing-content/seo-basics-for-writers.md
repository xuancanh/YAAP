# SEO basics for writers

You don't need to be an SEO specialist to write pages that rank. You need to answer the query better than what's already there, and not get in the way of the search engine understanding the page. The SEO Specialist's keyword brief sets the target; this doc covers what you control while writing. Search engine guidance changes, so treat the linked Google pages as the current word.

## 1. Match the search intent

Before writing, search the target query and look at the top results. They tell you what searchers want:

| Results look like… | Intent | Write… |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions, explainers | Learn | A clear answer first, then depth |
| Step-by-step guides | Do | Numbered steps, prerequisites, screenshots |
| Comparisons, "best X" lists | Compare | Honest criteria and a table |
| Product and pricing pages | Buy | Not a blog post; flag to the SEO Specialist |

If your format doesn't match the intent, the piece won't rank however good it is.

## 2. Write people-first content

Google's guidance asks whether content offers original information or analysis, goes beyond the obvious, shows first-hand experience and leaves the reader feeling they've learned enough to reach their goal. It also warns against content made mainly to attract search visits, mass-produced pages, summaries of others with nothing added, and changing dates to look fresh without real updates.

In practice, your angle from the brief is your SEO advantage. Our data, our customer story, our tested method are things the top results can't copy.

Google also asks that it be clear **who** made the content (bylines where readers expect them), **how** (including whether automation or AI was used, where readers would wonder), and **why** (primarily to help people). AI-assisted drafting is not banned, but large volumes of unreviewed generated text are treated as low effort, and scaled content made to manipulate rankings is a spam violation.

## 3. Titles and headings

- **Title element (meta title):** unique, descriptive and concise; no keyword stuffing; site name at the start or end with a separator. Google may rewrite title links that are vague, inaccurate or boilerplate. Google sets no fixed length limit, but long titles get truncated, so put the important words first.
- **One H1** that clearly states the topic, usually close to the title.
- **H2s and H3s** follow the reader's questions. Related questions in the results ("People also ask"-style) are good H2 candidates if they fit the brief.

## 4. Meta description

Not a ranking factor in itself, but often shown as the snippet. Write one or two sentences that say what the reader gets, in plain words, with the main idea of the query. Make each page's description unique.

## 5. Use the language people search

- Use the target query and its natural variations where they fit. Write for the reader; if it sounds forced, it is.
- Explain terms the first time. Searchers often use the plain term; we use the jargon.
- Don't repeat keywords to "add density". Keyword stuffing is a named spam policy.

## 6. Links

- **Internal links:** add the ones in the SEO brief, with descriptive anchor text that says what the target page is about. Link to the related pillar page and from it, if you can ask the site owner.
- **External links:** link to primary sources for claims. It helps readers and reviewers.
- **Never** buy, trade or request links in exchange for anything. Link schemes are a spam policy violation; flag any offer to the Head of Marketing.

## 7. Images and media

- Descriptive file names and alt text that says what the image shows (also an accessibility requirement).
- Compress large images; ask the site owner if pages are slow.
- Original diagrams and screenshots add value that stock images don't.

## 8. Updating instead of rewriting

When the performance review says "refresh", update facts, add what's changed, fix broken links and improve weak sections on the same URL. Change the visible "updated" date only when the content really changed.

## Writer's SEO checklist

- [ ] Format matches the intent of the top results.
- [ ] The answer appears early; the angle adds something new.
- [ ] Unique title and meta description; one clear H1.
- [ ] H2s answer the reader's real questions.
- [ ] Internal links from the brief, descriptive anchors.
- [ ] Claims linked to primary sources; byline present.
- [ ] Alt text on every meaningful image.
- [ ] No stuffing, no thin sections written only to hit a keyword.
