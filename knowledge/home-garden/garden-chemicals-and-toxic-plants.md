# Garden chemicals and toxic plants

Chemicals and plants are where a garden can actually hurt someone. Two rules cover most of it: follow the product label exactly, and check plants against a trusted toxicity list before they come near pets or small children.

## Using pesticides and weedkillers

The US EPA and extension services put it simply: the label is the law. Using a product in a way its label doesn't allow is illegal in the US, and the same principle applies to approved products in most countries.

Before using any product:

1. **Try the non-chemical fix first** (below). Many problems don't need a spray.
2. **Check the product is approved for that use** in the user's country, on that plant, in that place (indoor vs outdoor, edible crops vs ornamentals).
3. **Read the whole label:** dose, timing, protective gear, re-entry time (how long before people and pets can go back on the area), harvest interval for edibles, and disposal.
4. **Mix only what's needed.** Never decant into drink bottles or unlabeled containers.
5. **Apply in calm, dry weather.** Wind carries spray onto neighbors, ponds and bees; rain washes it into drains.
6. **Keep children and pets off** until the label's re-entry time has passed.

**Storage:** in the original container, locked or out of reach, away from food and animal feed, in a cool dry place. Dispose of leftovers through the local hazardous waste service, never down a drain.

## Lower-chemical options

- **Pull or hoe weeds** while small, and mulch bare soil.
- **Pick off pests** by hand (slugs, caterpillars) or use barriers (netting, collars).
- **Encourage predators:** ladybirds, birds and frogs eat many pests.
- **Right plant, right place.** Stressed plants attract pests; check light, water and soil first.
- **Accept some damage.** A few nibbled leaves don't need a spray.

## Toxic plants

Many common garden and house plants are harmful if eaten by pets or children. Don't rely on memory or social media; check the specific plant.

- For pets, the ASPCA keeps a searchable list of plants toxic and non-toxic to dogs, cats and horses.
- Lilies are a well-known danger to cats. True lilies can cause severe kidney injury even in small amounts; keep them out of homes with cats entirely, including cut flowers and their vase water.
- For children, the national poison center or poison control service in the user's country is the authority; many publish lists of common toxic plants.

**Checklist for a new plant in a home with pets or small children:**
- [ ] Botanical (Latin) name confirmed, not just the common name.
- [ ] Checked against the ASPCA list (pets) or national poison center guidance (children).
- [ ] If toxic: out of reach, or a safer alternative chosen.
- [ ] Noted in `home/plants.md` with a "toxic to cats" style flag.

## If something is swallowed

- **A child:** contact the national poison center or emergency services right away. Have the plant or product label with you.
- **A pet:** contact a vet or an animal poison control line straight away.
- Don't make anyone vomit unless a professional tells you to.
- If someone is unconscious, struggling to breathe or having a seizure, call emergency services.

## Common mistakes

- "A bit stronger will work better." Overdosing harms plants, soil life and people.
- Spraying flowering plants when bees are active.
- Storing weedkiller in the garden shed within a child's reach.
- Bringing lilies home for a cat owner.
