# DIY or pro: decision guide

Make the call per task, not per project. A bathroom refresh might be 70% DIY (painting, sealant, accessories) and 30% pro (the plumbing and the shower circuit).

## Five questions per task

1. **Could a mistake hurt someone?** Gas, electrics beyond like-for-like swaps, structure, work at height, asbestos. Yes → pro. No debate.
2. **Is it regulated?** Many countries require permits, building-control sign-off, or certified installers for certain work (electrics, gas, structural changes, windows, drainage). Check the local rules; unpermitted work can cause problems with insurance and when selling.
3. **Is it reversible?** Painting is. Cutting into a joist or tiling over a leak isn't.
4. **Do they have the skill and tools?** Be honest. Renting a tool for a one-off job is fine; learning a trade on your own walls is costly.
5. **Is the saving worth the time?** Price their hours. A pro may finish in a day what takes a beginner three weekends.

## Rough guide

| Usually DIY | Pro, or DIY only with real experience | Always pro |
|---|---|---|
| Painting and decorating | Tiling | Gas appliances and pipework |
| Flat-pack and shelves (into the right wall fixings) | Laying flooring | Consumer unit/panel and new circuits |
| Sealant, grout refresh | Replacing a tap or toilet internals | Structural walls, beams, joists |
| Bleeding radiators | Plastering | Roof work at height |
| Replacing a showerhead or toilet seat | Fitting a new kitchen | Asbestos removal |
| Garden clear-up, planting | Fence panels and posts | Tree felling near buildings or lines |

The middle column depends on skill and on local rules about who may do the work.

## Getting quotes that compare

Send each trade the same brief:

- what's wrong or what's wanted, with photos and measurements;
- what's been tried, and model numbers;
- access and timing constraints;
- the finish level wanted.

Ask every quote to state:

- labour and materials separately;
- what's excluded (making good, waste removal, permits);
- start date and expected duration;
- payment schedule;
- guarantee on workmanship.

Get at least three quotes for anything substantial. The cheapest is often missing something; compare line by line.

## Vetting a tradesperson

- Check registration or licensing where the law requires it (for gas and electrical work in many countries, there's a public register to check).
- Ask for proof of insurance.
- Read recent reviews and ask for a reference from a similar job.
- Be wary of: large cash deposits before work starts, door-to-door offers ("I noticed your roof…"), pressure to decide today, no written quote, no address.
- Agree changes in writing as they come up.

## Common mistakes

- Starting demolition before the replacement items are in hand.
- Choosing on price alone with vague quotes.
- Paying in full before the job is finished and checked.
- DIY electrics or gas "because it's only a small job".

## Worked example

The user wants to replace a kitchen tap and add a socket by the counter. Tap: DIY if they're comfortable isolating the water and the fittings are standard; give the step list with "stop if the isolation valve won't close". Socket: a new socket is electrical work that's regulated in many countries; brief an electrician with photos, the location and the appliance it's for, and ask for a fixed quote including any certification paperwork.
