# Onboarding guide

Good onboarding means a new starter can do useful work in their first week and knows what success looks like by day 30. Most failures happen before day one, because a request went out too late.

## Work backwards from the start date

Set every date as "start date minus N days" when the offer is accepted. Lead times below are starting points; replace them with the real times your IT team, payroll provider and suppliers need.

| When | Task | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Offer accepted | Create the lifecycle file; set all dates | People Ops |
| Offer accepted | Send contract for signature | People Ops / Head of People |
| As soon as signed | Start required pre-employment checks (right to work, references, any background checks the role and law require) | People Ops |
| Early | Request laptop and equipment (delivery takes longest) | IT & Security task |
| Early | Collect payroll, tax and benefits details through the HR system | People Ops |
| ~2 weeks before | Request accounts: email, chat, core tools, role-specific tools | IT & Security task |
| ~2 weeks before | Assign a buddy; brief them | Manager |
| ~1 week before | Draft first-week schedule with the manager | People Ops + manager |
| ~1 week before | Send the welcome note: start time, where to go or how to log in, who to ask for | People Ops |
| Before payroll cut-off | Add starter to payroll inputs | People Ops |

Pre-employment checks are legally specific: what's required, what you may ask, and when you may ask it vary by country. Use the company's documented process; ask the Head of People when hiring in a new location.

## Day one

- [ ] Accounts work (test the login, not just "created")
- [ ] Equipment in hand
- [ ] Welcome from the manager in the first hour
- [ ] Introduction to the buddy and the team
- [ ] Policy acknowledgements sent through the HR system
- [ ] Clear first task for day two

## First week

Aim for a mix: a few short introductions a day, reading with a purpose, and one small real piece of work by the end of the week. A week of nothing but reading makes people anxious; a week of nothing but meetings makes them tired.

Sample shape:
- **Day 1:** setup, welcome, buddy, company overview
- **Day 2:** team and role overview; first small task assigned
- **Day 3–4:** key colleagues in other functions; tools training; working on the task
- **Day 5:** task done or shared; first check-in with the manager on how the week went

## 30/60/90-day goals

Agree these with the manager in week one. One line each, observable:

| By | Goal | How we'll know |
|---|---|---|
| Day 30 | Understands the team's work; has shipped one small piece | Piece reviewed and accepted |
| Day 60 | Owns a regular piece of work | Delivering it without step-by-step help |
| Day 90 | Fully contributing to a key result | Named contribution in the team's update |

Tie the 90-day goal to a real key result so the starter sees why their work matters.

## Buddy brief

The buddy answers the questions people don't want to ask their manager.

- Meet on day one, then a few times in the first two weeks.
- Explain how things really work: where to ask for help, which channels matter, unwritten norms.
- Check in at the end of week one and tell People Ops about any blockers (not personal matters).

## Day 30 check-in

Ask the starter and the manager separately:
- Did you have what you needed on day one? What was missing?
- Is the role what you expected?
- What would have made the first month better?

Record answers in aggregate (themes and counts) for the onboarding quality review. Fix the template when the same gap appears twice.

## Most often missed

- Role-specific tools (only general accounts were requested).
- Payroll cut-off for starters who join just after it.
- Equipment shipping time for remote starters.
- Access to shared calendars and recurring meetings.
- Telling other teams someone new is joining.
