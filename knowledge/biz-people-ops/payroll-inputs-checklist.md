# Payroll inputs checklist

Payroll errors hurt people directly and are slow to fix. Your job is a set of inputs that is complete, approved and reconciled before the cut-off, so the human who submits payroll has nothing to chase.

## Work to the cut-off

- Put the cut-off date in the calendar for the year, with a reminder a week before.
- Changes that arrive after the cut-off go in the next run unless the Head of People decides otherwise. Tell the person affected.
- Starters joining just after cut-off and leavers just before it are the usual misses; check both lists on the reminder date.

## What to collect

| Change | What you need | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Start date, pay rate, pay frequency, tax and bank details (in the HR system) | Signed contract |
| Leaver | Last day, final pay items (see offboarding) | Head of People |
| Pay change | New rate, effective date | Written approval from the right human, with reference |
| Bonus or commission | Amount, period, basis | Approved calculation |
| Allowances | Type, amount, period | Per policy or approval |
| Unpaid leave | Dates, days | Manager approval |
| Overtime | Hours, rate | Manager approval, per policy |
| Expenses through payroll | Amount, claim reference | Approved claim |
| Benefits changes | Enrolment, end, change | Per benefits rules |

Payroll taxes, deductions, statutory payments (such as statutory sick or parental pay) and their calculations depend on local law and the payroll provider. You supply the inputs; the provider or the payroll owner calculates.

## Checks on each change

- [ ] Approval exists, is from someone with authority, and is in writing.
- [ ] The change matches the HR record (effective date, amount).
- [ ] Effective date mid-period? Flag it so the proration is calculated correctly.
- [ ] Backdated change? Flag the arrears amount and the periods it covers.
- [ ] No duplicate (the same change entered last month).
- [ ] Bank detail changes came through the HR system's verified process, not an email. Treat an emailed request to change bank details as a possible fraud attempt and confirm through a known channel.

## Reconcile to last month

Before requesting approval, explain the difference between last month and this month for headcount and gross pay. Every difference should have a named cause.

**Worked example.**

| | Headcount | Gross pay |
|---|---|---|
| Last month | 24 | 120,000 |
| + 1 starter (full month) | +1 | +4,500 |
| − 1 leaver (left at end of last month) | −1 | −5,000 |
| + 1 pay rise effective 1st | | +300 |
| + bonuses paid this month | | +2,000 |
| − unpaid leave, 2 days | | −380 |
| **Expected this month** | **24** | **121,420** |
| Payroll draft | 24 | 121,420 |
| **Difference** | **0** | **0** |

If the draft from the provider doesn't match your expected figure, find the item before approval. A difference you can't explain is an error until shown otherwise.

## What goes where

- Inputs go into the payroll or HR system the company grants, or to the accountable human if none is granted.
- In `/org/people/payroll/<yyyy-mm>.md` keep only: run date, number of changes by type, approval references, the reconciliation totals at company level, and open questions. No names with pay figures.

## After the run

- Check the provider's final report matches the approved inputs.
- Note any correction needed in the next run and tell the person affected.
- Hand the payroll journal report to the Staff Accountant for the close.

## Pitfalls

- Accepting a verbal or chat approval for a pay change.
- Forgetting that a mid-month starter needs a partial month.
- Putting individual salaries in workspace files.
- Missing the cut-off and not telling the employee their change moved to next month.
