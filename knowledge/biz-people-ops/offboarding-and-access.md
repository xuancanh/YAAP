# Offboarding and access removal

A clean exit does three things: the person is paid what they're owed, the company keeps its data and knowledge, and access ends exactly when employment does. Treat every leaver with the same respect, whatever the reason.

## Confirm before you act

- Last working day and employment end date (they can differ, e.g. with garden leave or untaken leave paid out).
- Type of exit, as the Head of People tells you. Keep the reason confidential; it doesn't go in shared files.
- Whether the exit is sensitive (a dismissal, a dispute). If so, timing of access removal and communication is set by the Head of People, not by the checklist.
- Who announces the departure, and when. Don't tell anyone before the agreed time.

## Access removal checklist

Send IT & Security one task with a due time of the end of the last working day (or the time the Head of People sets). List everything:

- [ ] Single sign-on / directory account (removes most access at once)
- [ ] Email: disabled; forwarding or auto-reply as agreed; mailbox kept per retention policy
- [ ] Chat and collaboration tools
- [ ] Tools outside single sign-on (check the tool inventory for local accounts)
- [ ] Admin or privileged accounts; shared or service accounts they knew the password to (rotate those passwords)
- [ ] Multi-factor devices and recovery methods registered to the company
- [ ] API keys, tokens and integrations created under their name
- [ ] Code repositories and cloud consoles
- [ ] Company cards and expense tools
- [ ] Building access, if any
- [ ] Ownership of files, folders, calendars, recurring meetings and workflows transferred to the manager or a named colleague before the account goes

Ask IT & Security to confirm completion, and record the confirmation date.

## Equipment

- Arrange return before or on the last day; for remote leavers, send a prepaid return kit early.
- Note what came back against the asset record.
- IT & Security wipes devices per their process.
- Rules about deducting unreturned equipment from pay are legally restricted in many places; never propose a deduction without the Head of People's approval.

## Knowledge handover

Agree with the manager:
- a list of what the person owns (work in progress, recurring tasks, relationships with customers or vendors);
- who takes each item;
- a handover note per item, written while they're still here;
- introductions to external contacts.

## Final pay inputs

Gather for human review and payroll:

- Days worked in the final pay period
- Accrued but untaken leave (and whether it's paid out, per the contract and local law)
- Notice pay or pay in lieu, if applicable
- Agreed amounts (e.g. under a settlement), with the approval reference
- Outstanding expenses to reimburse
- Any amounts owed back, only as approved
- Benefits end dates

Final pay deadlines, leave payout rules and required documents at exit differ by jurisdiction. The Head of People or the payroll provider confirms them; flag a short deadline the day you learn of the exit.

## Exit interview

Offer it; don't require it. Use the same questions each time so themes can be compared:

1. What made you decide to leave?
2. What did you enjoy most about working here?
3. What would you change about your role, team or the company?
4. Did you have what you needed to do your job well?
5. How would you describe your relationship with your manager?
6. Would you recommend working here to a friend? Why or why not?

Record themes anonymously for the engagement review. If the person raises a serious concern (harassment, discrimination, safety, fraud), stop treating it as feedback: tell them it will go to the Head of People and route it the same day.

## Pitfalls

- Disabling the account before ownership of files and automations is transferred.
- Forgetting tools outside single sign-on.
- Announcing a departure before the person has been told how it will be communicated.
- Putting the reason for leaving in the lifecycle file.
