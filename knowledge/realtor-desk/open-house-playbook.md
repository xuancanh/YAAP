# Open house playbook

An open house has two goals: show the property well and start conversations that turn into clients. Follow-up within a day is where most of the value is.

## A week before

- Seller agrees the date and time, and what's off-limits (rooms, garage).
- Ask the seller to secure valuables, prescriptions, documents, keys, firearms and small electronics.
- Marketing: listing portals, social posts, neighbour invitations (a "neighbours' preview" half-hour before opening works well).
- Prepare a one-page feature sheet: key facts, recent updates, utility costs if the seller has them, disclosures available on request (as the broker directs).
- Check local rules on signs and any HOA restrictions.

## The day before

- Walk-through: lights, temperature, smells, clutter, curb appeal.
- Print feature sheets and comps for curious visitors (optional).
- Sign-in method ready (tablet or sheet). Decide what you'll ask: name, contact, are they working with an agent?

## Safety

- Tell someone where you are, when you start and when you should check in.
- Walk the house first: know the exits, unlock the back door, keep your phone charged and on you.
- Don't lead visitors into basements or small rooms; let them go ahead.
- If possible, work in pairs, especially for vacant homes or evening events.
- Trust instincts. If someone makes you uneasy, stay in the open, call a colleague, or end early.
- Your brokerage and local association may have their own safety protocols; follow them.

## Running it

- Greet everyone at the door; ask whether they're working with an agent (and respect that relationship if they are).
- Short tour or let them roam, depending on the crowd.
- Listen for: timeline, current housing, must-haves, financing status (ask if they've spoken to a lender; don't give financing advice).
- Note feedback on the property for the seller: price reaction, condition comments.
- Fair housing applies here too: answer questions about the property, not about who lives in the area. See [listing language](fair-housing-listing-language.md).

## Visitor privacy

Sign-in details are personal data. Use them only for follow-up about property, store them in the CRM, and honour opt-outs. Some states and countries have rules on marketing calls and texts; follow the brokerage's consent practice.

## Follow-up (within 24 hours)

**Template**

> Hi [name], thanks for stopping by [address] yesterday. You mentioned [something specific: the kitchen, the commute]. [If useful: Here are two other homes nearby with similar layouts: links.] Happy to set up a private showing or send new listings that match what you're after. What's your timeline looking like?

- Visitors with an agent: a short thank-you only, or send the info to their agent.
- Neighbours: thank them; offer a market update for their street.
- Add everyone to the pipeline with a next action and date.

## Report to the seller

Within a day: number of groups, notable feedback in visitors' words, any offers or second showings, and a recommendation (price, condition fix, marketing change). Keep visitor names out unless they've agreed.

## Common mistakes

- No follow-up, or a generic one a week later.
- Leaving the seller's valuables or mail out.
- Giving opinions on financing, schools or the neighbourhood's "type of people".
