# Comparable sales method

A comparative market analysis (CMA) supports the agent's pricing conversation. It is not an appraisal and must never be presented as one; lenders rely on licensed appraisers.

## Selecting comps

Start tight and loosen only as needed. Record why each loosening was made.

1. **Status:** closed sales first. Active and pending listings show the competition and direction, but they're asking prices.
2. **Time:** the most recent sales. Widen the window only in slow markets, and say so.
3. **Location:** same subdivision or neighbourhood; then similar areas on the same side of any big boundary (a highway, a school-district line).
4. **Type and style:** same property type (detached, townhouse, condo); similar age and construction.
5. **Size:** living area within a reasonable band of the subject; similar bed and bath count.
6. **Lot and features:** lot size, garage, pool, view, basement finish.
7. **Condition:** renovated, average or dated, from photos and listing notes.

Aim for three to six good closed comps. Ten weak ones aren't better.

## Exclude or flag

- Non-arm's-length sales (family transfers, foreclosures, short sales), unless the market is full of them.
- Sales with concessions that inflated the price (large seller credits); adjust or note.
- New construction compared with resale, unless the subject is new.

## Adjustments

Adjust the **comp** to look like the subject: if the comp has something the subject lacks, subtract its value; if it lacks something the subject has, add.

Base adjustment amounts on local evidence (paired sales, or the agent's or broker's market knowledge), not a national rule of thumb. Note the basis for each.

Typical adjustment categories: time/market change, location, living area, bedrooms and bathrooms, garage, lot, condition, upgrades, concessions.

Rule of thumb for credibility: if a comp needs large total adjustments, it's not a good comp. Look for a better one.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)

Subject: 3 bed / 2 bath, 1,600 sq ft, two-car garage, average condition.

| | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale price | 410,000 | 425,000 | 398,000 |
| Size | 1,550 (+) | 1,700 (−) | 1,600 |
| Garage | 2-car | 2-car | 1-car (+) |
| Condition | average | renovated kitchen (−) | average |
| Concessions | none | none | 5,000 seller credit (−) |
| Net adjustment | +5,000 | −20,000 | +5,000 |
| Adjusted | 415,000 | 405,000 | 403,000 |

Supported range: roughly 403,000–415,000. Comp B needed the largest adjustment, so weight it least.

## Presenting it

- A range plus a suggested list strategy, not a single "value".
- A map and a one-line reason each comp was chosen.
- Active and pending listings: "your competition".
- Dates and data sources for every figure (MLS, public records), with the pull date.
- What would move the number: condition fixes, staging, timing.

## Common mistakes

- Using active listings as if they were sales.
- Choosing comps to support the price the seller wants.
- Ignoring condition because it's hard to measure.
- Crossing a school-district or neighbourhood boundary without saying so.
- Calling the CMA an appraisal or a "valuation" for lending.
