# Under contract to keys

The contract sets the deadlines. This checklist helps track them; it doesn't interpret them. Steps, forms and who does what vary by state and country (attorney states, escrow states, title practices). The broker or attorney confirms anything about meaning or consequences.

## On acceptance (day 0)

- Fully signed contract and all addenda received by everyone; effective date confirmed
- Build the deadline tracker from the contract (below), dates calculated exactly as the contract defines days
- Earnest money: amount, holder and due date; receipt confirmed
- Contact sheet: buyer, seller, both agents, lender, escrow/title or closing attorney, inspector, appraiser, HOA
- Disclosures delivered and signed as required locally

Review weekly at minimum; daily in the last week. Flag anything due within three working days.

## Documents to collect

- Inspection reports and repair agreements
- Appraisal (or notice that it came in low; the agent and client decide next steps)
- Loan approval / clear-to-close from the lender
- Title commitment, survey if required, HOA documents
- Insurance binder from the buyer
- Closing disclosure or settlement statement (buyer reviews with their lender)
- Utility transfer, keys, codes, garage openers list

## Keeping the client informed

Send a short update after every milestone: what happened, what's next, what they need to do and by when. Clients panic in silence.

> Inspection done. The inspector flagged the water heater age and a loose handrail. Your repair request is due Thursday at 5 p.m. Let's talk tomorrow about what to ask for.

## Wire fraud: say it early and often

Criminals impersonate title companies, attorneys and agents by email and send fake wiring instructions at closing. The FBI advises verifying any payment instruction with the intended recipient through a known phone number, never one in the email, and being wary of urgency or last-minute changes.

Tell clients in writing at contract and again before closing:

> We will never email you new wiring instructions. Before sending any money, call the title company at the number on their website or your contract, not a number in an email.

If money is sent to the wrong account, contact the bank immediately and file a complaint with the FBI's IC3 (US).

## When to escalate

- A deadline will be missed or a party asks to extend it
- An inspection, appraisal or financing problem that could end the deal
- Any question about what a clause means or what happens if it isn't met
- Disputes over earnest money

These go to the broker, and legal questions to an attorney.

## Common mistakes

- Counting calendar days when the contract says business days, or the reverse.
- Assuming the lender or title company is tracking your deadlines.
- Verbal agreements to extend a deadline with nothing in writing.
