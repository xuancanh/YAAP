# Fair-housing-safe listing language

General guidance, not legal advice. US federal law is the floor; many states and cities protect more groups (for example source of income, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status). Outside the US, equality and advertising laws differ. Check the local rules and the brokerage's policy.

## The rule

The US Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to publish any housing ad that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on a protected characteristic (24 CFR 100.75). The federally protected characteristics are **race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability**.

The practical test: **describe the property and its features, never the people who should or shouldn't live there or who lives nearby.**

## Risky phrases and safer swaps

| Avoid | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young family" / "ideal for singles" | Familial status | "Three bedrooms, fenced yard" / "Low-maintenance one-bedroom" |
| "Adults only", "no children" | Familial status (unless a lawful senior-housing exemption applies; broker confirms) | Omit |
| "Walking distance to St. Mary's" | Can signal religious preference | "Walking distance to downtown" or name the street |
| "Exclusive neighborhood", "safe area" | Can read as coded; "safe" also invites claims you can't support | "Quiet cul-de-sac", "near the park" |
| "Not suitable for wheelchairs", "able-bodied" | Disability | Describe the feature: "Second-floor unit, stairs only" |
| "Great for empty nesters" | Familial status, and can imply age preference | "Single-level living" |
| "Bachelor pad", "man cave" | Sex | "Bonus room", "den" |
| "English-speaking neighborhood" | National origin | Omit |
| "Master bedroom" | Not a federal fair-housing violation, but some MLSs and brokerages prefer neutral terms; follow theirs | "Primary bedroom" |

HUD's 1995 guidance notes that describing the property and its amenities (a "family room", "walk-in closets", "jogging trails") is generally fine. The problem is wording aimed at who should buy.

## Neighborhood and school descriptions

- Facts about places are fine: "0.4 miles to the train station", "across from Elm Park".
- Avoid characterising residents ("friendly", "family-oriented community", "up-and-coming").
- Schools: name the assigned schools only from the official source and add "buyer to verify". Avoid "great schools" claims.
- Crime: don't characterise safety. Point buyers to public sources if they ask.

## Photos and targeting

- Photos of people in ads can signal preference; most agents use property-only images.
- Online ad targeting by age, sex, family status or ZIP code proxies can create the same problem as words. Use broad targeting and the platform's housing-ad category.

## Pre-publish check

- Every sentence describes the property, its features or measurable facts about location.
- No words about who the buyer, tenant or neighbours are.
- Accessibility features described as features, not as who can live there.
- The Equal Housing Opportunity statement or logo, if the brokerage uses it.
- Square footage, lot size, school assignments and HOA facts sourced and verified.

## Worked rewrite

Before: "Charming starter home, perfect for a young couple, in a safe, family-friendly neighborhood near St. Luke's and great schools."

After: "Two-bedroom bungalow with a renovated kitchen and fenced backyard, on a tree-lined street two blocks from Maple Park. Assigned schools per the district site; buyer to verify."

## When to escalate

A seller asks you not to show to a certain group; a buyer asks about the racial or religious makeup of an area; a complaint arrives. Stop and send it to the broker. Those are fair-housing issues, not copy edits.
