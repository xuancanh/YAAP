# Backward design and the syllabus

Design the course from the end: what students should be able to do, how you'll know, then what they'll practise. This is Wiggins and McTighe's "Understanding by Design", widely taught by university teaching centres.

## The three stages

1. **Outcomes.** What should a student be able to do by the end that they can't now?
2. **Evidence.** Which assessments would show they can? Each outcome needs at least one.
3. **Learning activities.** What practice, with feedback, gets them there? Only now pick readings and lectures.

The usual failure is starting at stage 3 with a reading list, then writing outcomes to fit it.

## Writing outcomes

An outcome is observable: "Students will be able to [verb] [what] [in what context]."

- Use verbs you can assess: *calculate, compare, design, critique, explain, defend*. Avoid *understand, appreciate, know*.
- Match the verb to the level you want (Bloom's revised taxonomy runs remember → understand → apply → analyse → evaluate → create). An intro course might sit lower; a capstone higher.
- Aim for roughly four to eight course-level outcomes. More than that and nobody can track them.

**Example.** Weak: "Understand regression." Better: "Choose and fit an appropriate regression model to a real dataset and explain its limitations in plain language."

## Alignment check

Make a small table: outcomes as rows, assessments as columns. Every outcome should have an assessment; every assessment should serve an outcome. An empty row means an outcome you never test; an empty column means busywork.

## Syllabus checklist

A syllabus is also a contract. Most grade disputes trace to something it didn't say. Check your institution's required syllabus elements first; then cover:

- **Basics:** course code, term, meeting times, location/online links, instructor contact, office hours, expected reply time for email
- **Outcomes** and how each assessment maps to them
- **Grade breakdown** with weights that add to 100%, and the grading scale
- **Late work:** penalty or grace policy, how to request an extension, who decides
- **Missed exams:** documentation needed, make-up process
- **Regrade requests:** window (e.g. within one week), in writing, with reasons; the whole item may be regraded
- **Academic integrity:** institution policy link; what collaboration is allowed; rules on generative AI tools for each assessment type
- **Attendance and participation:** how measured, if graded
- **Accessibility:** statement directing students to disability services; how approved accommodations are arranged
- **Communication:** where announcements appear; email etiquette
- **Schedule:** week-by-week topics, readings and due dates, with a line saying changes will be announced in [channel]
- **Wellbeing and support:** links to institutional services

## Common mistakes

- Weights that don't add to 100% or that differ between syllabus and the learning platform.
- "Late work will be penalised" with no amount.
- A generative-AI policy that's silent or contradicts the institution's.
- Due dates on public holidays or the exam period, found mid-term.
- Changing a policy mid-term without written notice.
