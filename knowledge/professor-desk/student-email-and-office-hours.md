# Student email and office hours

Most student email repeats. Answer the repeats with approved templates, and save the professor's attention for the messages that need judgment.

## Triage categories

| Category | Examples | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Answered in the syllabus | due dates, weights, late policy | Template pointing to the exact section |
| Logistics | room change, link broken, can't access platform | Template or forward to IT / admin |
| Extension or absence | illness, bereavement, clash | Draft per syllabus policy; professor decides |
| Content question | "I don't get question 3" | Suggest office hours or forum; professor answers if short |
| Grade query | "Why did I get a B?" | Regrade process template; professor reviews |
| Sensitive | distress, harassment, safety, accommodation | Flag to professor at once; no template reply |

Sensitive messages always go straight to the professor. If a student mentions being in danger or at risk of harm, the professor should follow the institution's urgent procedure; include the campus emergency and counselling contacts in the flag.

## Templates

**Answered in the syllabus**

> Hi [name], good question. It's covered in the syllabus under "[section]" on page [n]: [one-line answer]. Let me know if anything there is unclear.

**Extension request (holding reply)**

> Hi [name], thanks for letting me know, and I'm sorry you're dealing with that. Our policy is [summary]. I'll confirm the arrangement by [day]. If you need support in the meantime, [student services link].

**Grade query**

> Thanks for asking. Please look at the rubric comments first. If you still think a criterion was misapplied, send a short note within [window] saying which criterion and why, and I'll re-review the whole piece.

**Content question better in person**

> This is a great one to work through together. Come to office hours [time/place/link], or post it on the forum so others benefit too.

## Making office hours work

- **Name them clearly.** "Student hours" or "drop-in help" signals they're for everyone, not just students in trouble.
- **Sign-up slots plus drop-in time.** Short bookable slots for individual matters; open time for quick questions.
- **Group sessions before big deadlines.** One session answers what twenty emails would.
- **FAQ document.** After each week, add the questions that came up; link it in announcements.
- **Prep prompt.** Ask students to bring the specific problem and what they've tried.

## Turnaround rules

State an expected reply time in the syllabus (for example, two working days) and keep to it. Draft replies queue for the professor once or twice a day rather than as they arrive.

## Privacy

Discuss grades only with the student, through institutional email or the learning platform. Don't confirm grades or attendance to parents, employers or others without the consent your institution's policy requires. Use initials in the triage log.

## Common mistakes

- Answering at 1 a.m., which trains students to expect it.
- Granting an extension by template before the professor decides.
- Replying to a sensitive message with a cheerful template.
