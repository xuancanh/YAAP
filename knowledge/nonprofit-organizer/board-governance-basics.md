# Board governance basics

The board (or trustees) is responsible for the organisation: its purpose, money and conduct. Staff run day-to-day work; the board oversees it. In small orgs the line blurs, so it helps to name it.

This is general information. Legal duties depend on the country and legal form; for specific questions, the regulator's guidance and the org's lawyer are the authority.

## Core duties: two examples

**England and Wales (Charity Commission, CC3).** Trustees must:

1. Ensure the charity is carrying out its purposes for the public benefit
2. Comply with the charity's governing document and the law
3. Act in the charity's best interests
4. Manage the charity's resources responsibly
5. Act with reasonable care and skill
6. Ensure the charity is accountable

**United States.** State nonprofit law generally describes board members' fiduciary duties as:

- **Care:** pay attention, read the papers, ask questions, make informed decisions
- **Loyalty:** put the organisation's interests first; handle conflicts of interest
- **Obedience:** keep the organisation true to its mission and governing documents, and within the law

The IRS also asks about governance practices (such as a conflict-of-interest policy and minutes) on its annual return for many tax-exempt organisations.

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict exists when a board member's personal interest (or a relative's or business's) could affect a decision. The usual practice:

- A written policy, signed annually
- A register of interests
- Declare at the start of every meeting
- The conflicted person leaves the discussion and doesn't vote
- Record it in the minutes

## What the board decides versus what staff decide

| Board | Staff (or volunteers in a staff role) |
|---|---|
| Mission, strategy and big changes | How programs run |
| Annual budget approval; reviewing finances | Spending within the budget |
| Hiring and reviewing the chief executive (if any) | Hiring other staff and volunteers |
| Key policies (safeguarding, finance, conflicts) | Following the policies |
| Major risks | Day-to-day risk management |

## Board meeting template

```
1. Welcome, apologies, declarations of interest
2. Minutes of last meeting; matters arising; action log
3. Decisions needed (with papers circulated a week before)
   - <item>: recommendation, options, risk
4. Finance report (from the treasurer)
5. Program and impact update (headlines and numbers)
6. Risks: new or changed
7. For information only (no discussion unless asked)
8. Any other business; date of next meeting
```

Put decisions near the top while people are fresh. Circulate papers at least a week ahead.

## Minutes that hold up

- Who attended; quorum met
- Declarations of interest
- Decisions made, in clear words, with any vote
- Actions: what, who, by when
- Brief reasons for major decisions

Minutes record decisions, not a transcript.

## Common mistakes

- Board meetings that are all updates and no decisions.
- No conflict-of-interest process until there's a conflict.
- The treasurer as the only person who understands the finances.
- Trustees doing staff work and nobody doing oversight.
