# Compliance calendar

Good standing is mostly about not missing dates. Build the calendar once, check it yearly, and give every item a lead time.

**Jurisdiction-specific and time-sensitive.** The examples below were checked in November 2026. Rules depend on country, state or region, legal form and income. Confirm every item with the regulator's site and the org's accountant.

## Step 1: list the obligations

Research each of these for the org's jurisdiction and legal form:

| Category | Typical obligations |
|---|---|
| Regulator or tax authority | Annual return, annual accounts, changes to trustees or governing documents |
| Company registry (if incorporated) | Annual confirmation, accounts, director changes |
| State or local | Charitable solicitation registration (some US states), local licences for collections, raffles or lotteries |
| Employment | Payroll filings, pension duties, insurance |
| Insurance | Liability, employer's, property, events; renewal dates |
| Funders | Report deadlines from each grant agreement |
| Policies | Annual review of safeguarding, data protection, finance, conflicts of interest |
| Background checks | Renewal for staff and volunteers in roles that require them |

## Step 2: date each one

For each: the deadline, who's responsible, the source page and the date it was checked, and a lead time (often one to two months for anything that needs accounts).

## Worked example: United States (federal)

- Form 990, 990-EZ or 990-PF is due on the **15th day of the 5th month after the end of the accounting year**. For a calendar year, that's **15 May**. Form 8868 gives an automatic six-month extension for those forms.
- Small organisations may be able to file the **990-N (e-Postcard)**; its due date can't be extended.
- An organisation that **fails to file for three consecutive years automatically loses its tax-exempt status**, effective from the due date of the third return. Reinstatement requires a new application.
- State filings (registration, charitable solicitation, annual reports) vary by state; many have their own deadlines.

## Worked example: England and Wales

- Registered charities send their **annual return within 10 months of the end of the financial year**. For a year ending 31 December, that's by 31 October.
- What's required depends on income and legal form; the Charity Commission's pages set the thresholds, which have changed recently. Check the current guidance for the org's financial year.
- Charities in Scotland and Northern Ireland have different regulators and rules.

## Calendar template

```
| Item | Authority | Due | Lead time | Owner | Source + checked |
|------|-----------|-----|-----------|-------|------------------|
| Annual return | <regulator> | <date> | 2 months | Treasurer | <url>, 2026-11-04 |
| Accounts approved by board | Board | <date> | 1 month | Chair | governing doc |
| Insurance renewal | <insurer> | <date> | 6 weeks | Coordinator | policy schedule |
| Funder report: <grant> | <funder> | <date> | 1 month | Coordinator | grant agreement |
| Safeguarding policy review | Board | <month> | — | Safeguarding lead | policy |
```

## Common mistakes

- Treating "small" as "exempt from filing". Even small orgs usually have to file something.
- One person holds all the logins and dates.
- Using last year's rules without re-checking.
- Forgetting state or local registrations when fundraising across borders or online.
