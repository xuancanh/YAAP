# Grant proposal checklist

Most rejections come from things you can check before submitting: wrong fit, missed criteria, unclear budget, missing documents. Run this list on every draft.

## Go or no-go: should we apply?

Answer before drafting:

| Question | If no |
|---|---|
| Are we eligible (type of org, location, size, registration status)? | Stop |
| Does the funder actually fund work like ours? Check past grantees, not just the guidelines | Probably stop |
| Is the typical grant size worth the effort? | Consider skipping or a lighter ask |
| Can we meet the deadline with a good application? | Plan for the next round |
| Can we deliver and report on what they'd fund? | Don't apply for money you can't spend well |
| Is there a contact we can ask a question? | Not a blocker, but a call often helps |

## Content checklist

**Fit**
- [ ] Every section answers the funder's own criteria, in their order, using their headings where possible
- [ ] Word or character limits respected
- [ ] Their priorities named and linked to our work in plain words

**Need**
- [ ] Need stated with local evidence: our own numbers and a reputable local source, dated
- [ ] Why us: what we already do, track record, community links

**What we'll do**
- [ ] Activities described concretely: who, what, where, how often
- [ ] Outcomes measurable (see [Logic model and outcomes](logic-model-and-outcomes.md))
- [ ] How we'll measure and report
- [ ] Timeline with milestones

**Budget**
- [ ] Every line justified in the budget narrative
- [ ] Arithmetic checked: totals, percentages, match funding
- [ ] Budget matches the activities described
- [ ] Overheads or core costs included where the funder allows; small orgs often under-ask for these
- [ ] Other funding sources and in-kind support shown if asked

**Admin**
- [ ] Required attachments: accounts, governing document, policies (safeguarding, equality, data protection), registration proof
- [ ] Signatures and authorisations
- [ ] Contact details correct
- [ ] Portal account set up early; some portals require registration days ahead

**Final read**
- [ ] Read by someone who doesn't know the project: does it make sense?
- [ ] Jargon removed; acronyms spelled out
- [ ] Submitted before the deadline day, with confirmation saved

## After submission

- Log it in the pipeline with the decision date.
- If won: create reporting tasks immediately with dates.
- If lost: thank them, ask for feedback where offered, and note what to change.

## Worked example: a tight-fit check

Funder criterion: "Projects that reduce isolation among older people in the county."

- Weak answer: "Our lunch club is very popular and people enjoy it."
- Stronger: "Our weekly lunch club serves 35 people over 70, most of whom live alone (attendance register, 2026). In our exit survey, most regulars said the club is their main social contact each week. The grant would add a second weekly session in the north of the county, where we have a waiting list of 18."

The stronger version uses the funder's words (isolation, older people, county), gives local evidence, and connects the ask to the need. Numbers in a real application must come from the org's own records.

## Common mistakes

- One generic application sent to many funders.
- Budget that doesn't match the narrative.
- Missing an attachment.
- Promising outcomes you can't measure.
