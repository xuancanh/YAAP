# Emergency and sinking funds

Two different jobs, often confused:

- An **emergency fund** covers the unpredictable: job loss, urgent repairs, sudden travel.
- **Sinking funds** cover the predictable-but-irregular: car insurance, birthdays, a holiday, school uniforms.

Most "emergencies" in a budget are actually sinking-fund items nobody saved for.

## Emergency fund: build it in stages

The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's *Start Small, Save Up* approach encourages starting with a small, automatic amount rather than waiting for a perfect plan. A staged goal keeps it reachable:

1. **Starter cushion:** enough to cover a typical surprise bill (a car repair, a boiler call-out). Look at the user's own past shocks for the size.
2. **One month of essential costs:** rent or mortgage, utilities, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments.
3. **Several months of essential costs.** Common guidance ranges from a few months to more for people with irregular income, single incomes or dependants. This is a personal choice, not a rule.

Use *essential* costs, not full spending; in a real emergency, discretionary spending drops.

## Where to keep it

Somewhere separate from the everyday account (so it isn't spent by accident) and easy to reach within days. Which account or product is the user's decision; don't recommend one. Check that any savings are covered by the country's deposit protection scheme.

## How to build it

- **Automate** a transfer on payday, even a small one.
- **Route windfalls:** part of a tax refund, bonus or gift.
- **Count cancelled subscriptions:** move the saved amount into the fund the same month.
- **Refill after use.** Using the fund is the point; the plan then restarts at stage 1.

## Sinking funds

1. List every irregular cost in the last 12 months (statements reveal most of them).
2. Add known future ones (a wedding, a car service, a holiday).
3. For each: amount ÷ months until due = monthly contribution.
4. Hold them in one savings pot with a tracking list, or separate pots if the bank allows.

## Worked example

| Item | Cost | Due in | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Car insurance | 540 | 9 months | 60 |
| Christmas and birthdays | 600 | 12 months | 50 |
| Summer holiday | 1,200 | 8 months | 150 |
| Car service and tyres | 360 | 12 months | 30 |
| **Total** | | | **290** |

If 290 a month doesn't fit, the honest conversation is which item shrinks or moves later, not hoping it works out.

## Common mistakes

- Using the emergency fund for predictable costs, then having nothing for a real emergency.
- Waiting for a "big enough" amount before starting.
- Keeping the fund in the spending account.
- Sizing the fund off total spending instead of essentials, making the goal feel impossible.
