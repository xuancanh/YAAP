# Debt payoff methods

This doc explains how the two common methods work so the user can choose. It isn't advice on any loan, card or product, and it doesn't cover consolidation loans or balance transfers; those are financial products the user should discuss with a qualified adviser.

## First: is it a payoff plan, or a debt-advice situation?

Point the person to **free, reputable debt advice** if any of these apply:

- Missed or late payments, or can't make minimum payments
- Letters from debt collectors, bailiffs or a court
- Borrowing to pay other debts or essential bills
- Priority debts behind: rent or mortgage, energy, council tax, tax, child support
- Thinking about bankruptcy, insolvency or a formal debt plan

In the US, the CFPB explains how to pick a reputable nonprofit credit counsellor: free information without demanding your finances first, written fee quotes, accredited counsellors, and confirming creditors have accepted any debt management plan before paying. In the UK, MoneyHelper lists free debt advice services. Be wary of firms that charge large upfront fees or promise to make debt disappear.

## The two methods

Both start the same way: pay the **minimum on every debt**, then put every extra amount on one target debt. When that's cleared, roll its payment into the next target.

| | Avalanche | Snowball |
|---|---|---|
| Target order | Highest interest rate first | Smallest balance first |
| Strength | Pays the least interest overall | Quick early wins keep people going |
| Risk | First win can take a long time | Costs more interest if a big high-rate debt waits |

Neither is wrong. The right one is the one the person sticks with.

## Worked example

Extra available each month: 200, on top of minimums.

| Debt | Balance | Rate (APR) | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store card | 600 | 29% | 25 |
| Credit card | 3,000 | 22% | 75 |
| Personal loan | 5,000 | 9% | 150 |

- **Avalanche order:** store card (29%) → credit card (22%) → loan (9%). Here the highest rate is also the smallest balance, so both methods start the same way.
- **Snowball order:** store card (600) → credit card (3,000) → loan (5,000).

Month 1: store card gets 25 + 200 = 225. It's gone in about three months. Then the credit card gets 75 + 225 = 300 a month, and so on.

Where the methods differ: if the credit card were 3,000 at 29% and the store card 600 at 19%, avalanche would target the credit card first and snowball the store card. To compare total interest for real numbers, use an amortisation calculator or spreadsheet, and show the arithmetic; don't estimate.

## Keeping a plan on track

- List every debt: lender (name only, no account numbers), balance, rate, minimum, due date.
- Put minimums on automatic payment so nothing is missed.
- Review monthly with the spending review; update balances from statements.
- Don't add new debt to the paid-off card. Many people close the gap and then refill it.
- Keep a small emergency cushion even while paying down debt, so a surprise doesn't go on a card (see [Emergency and sinking funds](emergency-and-sinking-funds.md)).

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring priority debts (rent, energy, tax) to pay credit cards.
- Paying extra across all debts equally; focus beats spreading.
- Guessing at interest savings.
- Signing up with a fee-charging "debt relief" company without checking it.
