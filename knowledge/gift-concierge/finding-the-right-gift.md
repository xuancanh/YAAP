# Finding the right gift

Good gifts come from evidence about the person, not from "gifts for dad" lists. This doc helps when the person's file is thin.

## Signals worth capturing

Write these into the person's file the moment they come up:

- **Wants spoken aloud:** "I'd love to try…", "I keep meaning to buy…"
- **Complaints:** a broken umbrella, a phone that won't hold charge, "my knives are useless."
- **Running out:** the good olive oil, the notebook brand, the tea they always drink.
- **New or deepening hobbies:** a first pottery class, training for a race, a new garden.
- **Life changes:** new home, new job, a baby, retirement, a move abroad.
- **Admiration:** they lingered over something in a shop or praised a friend's thing.
- **No-gos:** allergies, diet, clutter-averse, already owns it, ethical lines (no leather, no fast fashion).

## Questions that fill an empty profile

Ask the user, not the recipient (it keeps the surprise). Pick three or four.

1. What do they do on a free Saturday?
2. What have they complained about lately?
3. What do they spend their own money on without guilt?
4. What's something they'd never buy themselves but would love?
5. Do they prefer things, experiences, or consumables?
6. What was the best gift you've seen them react to? The worst?
7. Do they have space, or are they decluttering?
8. Who else might know? (A partner or close friend often has the answer.)

## Idea generators

Work through these for the person's main interest:

- **Upgrade the everyday.** The best version of something they use daily and own a cheap one of: a good chef's knife, a quality umbrella, proper headphones.
- **Consumables of the hobby.** Climbers need chalk, painters need paper, coffee lovers need beans. Low risk, always used.
- **Experiences.** A class, tickets, a tasting, a day out. Book a date or include the plan; vague vouchers often go unused.
- **Time and help.** Babysitting, a cooked meal, fixing the thing they've been putting off. Often the most valued gift for new parents, carers and the busy.
- **Memory and meaning.** A printed photo book, a framed map of a place that matters, a recipe written out by grandma.
- **Subscriptions,** used with care. Only if they'll keep enjoying it; check for auto-renewal they'd have to cancel.

## Present three options

Safe bet, thoughtful reach, wildcard. For each: what, price, where, arrives by, and one line of evidence from the file ("you noted in March she wanted to get back into film photography").

## Common mistakes

- Buying for the giver's taste instead of the recipient's.
- "Hobby gifts" that assume the wrong level: beginner kits for experts, pro gear for someone who just started.
- Gifts that create a chore: plants for the frequent traveler, pets, anything that needs assembly or upkeep they didn't ask for.
- Repeating a category (another scarf, another candle). Check the given-history first.
- Novelty items that become clutter by February.

## Worked example

File for "Sam, brother": loves cooking, complained in June his pans are warped, lives in a small flat, budget about 60. Options: a good carbon-steel or stainless frying pan in budget (safe bet, fixes a stated complaint), a booked knife-skills class (reach), a spice blend set from a local shop he once mentioned (wildcard, consumable, no storage problem). Each checked for delivery before his birthday.
