# Gift budget and group gifts

A yearly gift budget turns December from a shock into a plan. Group gifts let people give something bigger, if someone runs them cleanly.

## Building the yearly budget

1. **List every recurring occasion** from the occasion file: birthdays, anniversaries, the year-end season, plus a line for the unexpected (weddings, babies, leaving dos).
2. **Use the per-relationship norms** already in the person files ("siblings ~50, colleagues ~15").
3. **Add it up by month.** The total divided by 12 is what to set aside each month if the user likes a sinking fund (a savings pot for a known future cost).
4. **Mark the heavy months.** December and any birthday clusters. These are where the plan pays off.
5. **Review once a year,** after the season, against what was actually spent.

## Keeping within budget

- Decide the number before browsing. Browsing first sets the number for you.
- Count postage, wrapping and cards; they add up.
- For big families, suggest a draw (each person buys for one other) or "kids only" gifts. Someone usually welcomes the suggestion.
- Price isn't the measure. A well-chosen inexpensive gift beats an expensive generic one.

## Running a group gift

1. **One organiser, one place.** A shared note or message thread with the idea, the target amount and the deadline.
2. **Suggested amount, not a demand.** Let people give less without explaining. Keep who paid what private.
3. **Collect before buying,** or buy only what's already covered.
4. **Show the total spent** and keep the receipt in case of returns.
5. **Card signed by everyone,** including anyone who couldn't contribute.
6. **Leftover money:** add to the gift, give it as a voucher, or return it; say which up front.

## Cash and gift cards

Cash and vouchers are welcome in many families and cultures, especially for teenagers, weddings and people saving for something.

- Gift cards can expire or have fees depending on the card and the country's rules. Check the card's terms and say so to the recipient.
- Buy from the retailer or a trusted shop, and keep the receipt.
- **Scam warning.** The FTC warns that anyone demanding payment by gift card is a scammer. No real business, government agency or relative in trouble needs to be paid in gift cards. If a request to buy gift cards "for" someone arrives by message or phone, the user should check directly with that person through a number they already know.

## Common mistakes

- Planning only for December and being caught out by three summer birthdays.
- Group gifts with no deadline, so the organiser ends up paying the gap.
- Giving gift cards for shops the recipient doesn't use.
- Not tracking the given-history, so spending drifts upwards unnoticed.
