# Lead times and the December plan

Most gift stress is a timing problem. Work backwards from the day it must be in hand.

## Act-by date

```
act-by = occasion date
       − delivery time (as quoted by the seller, plus a buffer)
       − decision time (research + the user choosing)
       − prep time (wrapping, card, personalisation, travel to hand over)
```

Typical planning windows (adjust to the user's habits and their sellers' quoted times):

| Gift type | Start looking |
|---|---|
| Card only | 1-2 weeks before |
| Off-the-shelf gift, local delivery | about 3 weeks before |
| Personalised or made-to-order | 4-8 weeks before, or as the maker quotes |
| Handmade by the user | 6+ weeks, depending on the craft |
| International parcel | check the carrier's published last-posting dates for that country; customs can add days |
| Experience booking | as soon as the date is known; popular slots sell out |

Always use the seller's or carrier's current quoted dates, not these defaults. Postal services and couriers publish holiday last-posting dates every year.

## Sending internationally

- Check whether the item is allowed: many countries restrict food, plants, seeds, alcohol, batteries and aerosols.
- Gifts may still owe import duty or tax above a threshold, and the recipient may be asked to pay it. Thresholds vary by country and change; check the destination's customs site or the carrier's guidance.
- Often easier: order from a retailer in the recipient's country and ship locally.

## The December plan

From mid-October (or about eight weeks before the user's main gift date), run the season as one project.

**1. The list.** Everyone getting something, with a budget each and a running total.

```
| Person | Budget | Idea | Status | Order by | Arrived | Wrapped |
|--------|--------|------|--------|----------|---------|---------|
```

Statuses: idea → chosen → ordered → arrived → wrapped → given.

**2. Batch the research.** One sitting for all the easy ones, so the hard ones get real attention.

**3. Order early for anything shipped.** Delays build up near holidays, and stock runs out.

**4. Track arrivals weekly.** Chase anything late while there's still time to switch.

**5. Keep a backup shelf.** Two or three good generic gifts (a nice candle, chocolates, a book token) for the forgotten guest or surprise visitor.

**6. Afterwards.** Log what was given and how it landed in each person's file, and note thank-you notes to send.

## The last-minute save

If something slips, offer without comment:

- an experience booked today, with a printed or emailed confirmation;
- a local shop, florist or bakery pickup;
- a digital gift (an e-book, a subscription, a donation in their name to a cause they care about);
- a card that says the real gift is coming, with the date.

## Common mistakes

- Using "standard delivery" estimates in peak season.
- Forgetting time to wrap, write cards or travel to hand the gift over.
- Ordering personalised items at the last minute; returns usually aren't possible.

## Worked example

Mum's birthday is 14 December; the chosen gift is an engraved bracelet the maker quotes at 10-14 days plus 3 days' post. Add a 4-day peak-season buffer and 2 days to wrap and post on: the order must go in by about 22 November. The radar files the decision deadline as 18 November so there's time to choose.
