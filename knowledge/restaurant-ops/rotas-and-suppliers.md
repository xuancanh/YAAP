# Rotas and supplier routines

## Building the rota from demand

Staff to the rush, not to habit.

1. **Covers by daypart.** From the till export, average covers per half-hour or hour for each day of the week over the last four to eight weeks. Note outliers (holidays, events).
2. **Covers per staff member.** With the owner, set how many covers one server, one cook and one runner can handle at your standard. These are your own numbers.
3. **Build the curve.** Staff needed per hour = expected covers ÷ covers per person, rounded up, with minimum cover for opening and closing duties.
4. **Shift shapes.** Fit shifts to the curve: openers, a peak shift that starts before the rush, closers. Short peak shifts often beat long all-day ones.
5. **Check labour cost** against sales forecast for each day.
6. **Publish** with as much notice as local law or your policy requires.

## Fairness checklist

- Weekend and closing shifts rotate, not always the same people
- Requested days off honoured where possible, first-come or by a published rule
- No "clopens" (closing then opening the next morning) unless the person agreed and the rest time meets local law
- Hours roughly match contracts and what people asked for
- Under-18s scheduled within youth employment limits

Employment law on breaks, rest periods, overtime, minimum wage, tips and predictable scheduling varies by country, state and city. Check the official government guidance or a professional; don't guess.

## Delivery check (at the door)

- Compare items and quantities to the order and the delivery note
- Check chilled and frozen items with a probe (see [food safety records](food-safety-records.md))
- Check use-by dates, packaging damage, signs of pests
- Reject or note shortfalls on the delivery note **before** signing
- Put chilled and frozen items away first
- Log the check

## Invoice reconciliation (weekly)

- Match each invoice to its delivery note; chase credits for rejected items
- Flag price changes over last invoice for each key item
- Update plate costs when a key price moves (see [food cost math](food-cost-math.md))
- Every quarter, get a comparison quote for your top ten items by spend

## Replying to reviews

Reply to most reviews, briefly. Future guests read the replies.

**Positive**

> Thanks so much, [name]. The team will love hearing you enjoyed the [dish they mentioned]. Hope to see you again soon.

**Negative, fair**

> Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry the [specific issue] wasn't up to standard. That's not what we aim for. I've [what changed]. I'd like to make it right; please email me at [address].

**Negative, unfair or inaccurate**

> Thanks for your feedback. We don't recognise [specific claim] from our records of that evening, but we'd like to understand what happened. Please contact me at [address].

Rules: never argue, never reveal private details of the guest's visit or booking, and reply within a few days. Thank the specific staff member when someone is praised. Health complaints such as suspected food poisoning or allergic reactions get a private reply asking for contact details, and follow the local reporting procedure.

## Common mistakes

- Copying last week's rota without checking the forecast.
- Signing for a delivery before checking it.
- Copy-paste review replies that ignore what the guest said.
