# Food safety and allergen records

**This is a reference, not the rulebook.** The local health department or environmental health office sets and enforces the rules. In the US, the FDA Food Code is a model that each state or locality adopts, sometimes an older edition and sometimes with changes. In the UK, England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland each have their own regulations and guidance. Always check the local version; when in doubt, call the inspector's office.

## Reference temperatures

**US: FDA Food Code (model)**

| Control | Model value |
|---|---|
| Cold holding of time/temperature-controlled foods | 41°F (5°C) or below |
| Hot holding | 135°F (57°C) or above |
| Cooling cooked food | 135°F to 70°F (21°C) within 2 hours, then to 41°F within a further 4 hours (6 hours total) |
| Reheating for hot holding | 165°F (74°C) for 15 seconds, within 2 hours |
| Cooking: poultry | 165°F (74°C), instantaneous |
| Cooking: ground meat | 155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds |
| Cooking: whole cuts of beef/pork, fish | 145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds |
| Receiving refrigerated foods | 41°F or below (with listed exceptions) |

**UK (England)**

| Control | Requirement / guidance |
|---|---|
| Chilled holding | Legal maximum 8°C for foods that need it (FSA recommends keeping fridges at 5°C or below) |
| Hot holding | At or above 63°C |
| Cooling | Cool as quickly as possible; follow the SFBB "Chilling down hot food" sheet and the local officer's advice |
| Cooking and reheating | Follow the SFBB safe-method sheets, which use probe checks |

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have parallel rules; check theirs.

## Regulated allergens

- **US (nine major food allergens):** milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame. Sesame became the ninth on 1 January 2023 (FASTER Act). Labelling rules apply to packaged foods; restaurants follow state and local rules, and many now require allergen notices.
- **UK (14 allergens):** celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide and sulphites (above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre). Businesses must give allergen information for non-prepacked food; food prepacked for direct sale (PPDS) needs a full ingredients label with allergens emphasised.

Other countries have their own lists. Customers can be allergic to foods outside these lists, so staff should always check rather than say "it's fine".

## Allergen matrix (keep current)

| Dish | Celery | Gluten | Crustaceans | Eggs | … | Updated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rebuild the row whenever a recipe or a supplier's product changes. Keep supplier spec sheets on file. Brief staff on every change.

## Records to keep

- Fridge and freezer temperature log (at least daily, as local rules require)
- Hot-holding and cooling checks for batches
- Probe thermometer calibration
- Delivery checks: temperature, condition, use-by dates, rejected items
- Cleaning schedule sign-off
- Allergen matrix and staff briefing record
- Staff food-safety training certificates and expiry dates
- Pest-control visits
- The food safety management plan required locally (e.g. a HACCP-based plan, or SFBB in England and Wales)

## Renewal reminders

Track with a lead time: business licence, food business registration, health permit, staff certificates, fire safety checks, pest contract, inspection follow-ups. Note the issuing authority and the date.

## When to call the authority or a professional

- A suspected food-poisoning complaint (follow the local reporting procedure)
- A failed temperature check on a large batch: if unsure, throw it out
- An allergic reaction on premises: emergency services first
- Building or equipment changes that may need approval
