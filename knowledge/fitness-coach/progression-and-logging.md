# Progression and logging

Progress is measured in numbers, not vibes. Log the few things that let next week's targets be set, and read the trend over weeks, not single sessions.

## What to log

```
| Date | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load | Effort (RIR or 0–10) | Notes (sleep, soreness, pain?) |
```

For cardio: date, type, duration, distance if relevant, average effort, how it felt.

Add a weekly check: sessions planned vs. done, average sleep, energy (1–5), any pain.

**RIR (reps in reserve):** how many more reps they could have done with good form. "2 RIR" means two left in the tank. It's a simple way to keep effort right without testing maximums.

## Progression rules (double progression)

A simple rule that works for most people:

1. Pick a rep range, for example 8–12.
2. Use the same load until they can do the top of the range for all sets at the target effort.
3. Then increase the load by the smallest available step and drop back to the bottom of the range.
4. If they can't reach the bottom of the range, the step was too big; go back.

For cardio, increase one thing at a time: duration first, then frequency, then intensity. Many coaches increase weekly running volume gradually rather than in big jumps.

## Reading the weekly trend

- **Going up:** reps or load rising, effort steady. Carry on.
- **Flat for two to three weeks:** check sleep, stress and attendance first. If those are fine, change one variable (rep range, exercise variation, an extra set).
- **Going down:** effort rising for the same work, poor sleep, low energy, or persistent soreness. Time to hold or back off.

## When to back off (deload)

Signs: performance dropping over several sessions, unusually sore or tired, low motivation, life stress spiking, illness.

A simple deload week: keep the exercises, cut sets roughly in half and keep effort easy. Many plans build one in every few weeks for people training hard; beginners rarely need them on a schedule.

**Pain is different.** Pain that is sharp, in a joint, or getting worse is a reason to stop that exercise and see a professional, not to deload and push on.

## Missed sessions

- One missed session: carry on with the plan; don't double up.
- A missed week: repeat the last completed week.
- Two weeks or more: use the [returning after a break](returning-after-a-break.md) ramp.

## Weekly review output

```
Week <n>: 3/3 sessions. Squat 3×10 @ 16 kg, 2 RIR (up from 3×8). Row stalled at 3×12 @ 12 kg.
Sleep avg 6.5 h; energy 3/5.
Next week: squat 3×11–12 @ 16 kg; row moves to 14 kg at 3×8; keep the rest.
Note: knee niggle mentioned Tuesday — if it's sharp or persists, check with a physio.
```

## Common mistakes

- Increasing load and reps at the same time.
- Judging progress from one bad session.
- Logging so much detail that logging stops.
- Ignoring sleep and stress, then blaming the program.
