# Returning after a break

Most comeback problems come from restarting where you left off. Fitness fades more slowly than motivation suggests, but tissues need a few weeks to re-adapt. Start lower, build steadily, and make the first week feel almost too easy.

## Before restarting: check the reason for the break

| Reason | Before restarting |
|---|---|
| Holiday, busy period | Nothing special; use the ramp |
| Minor illness (cold) | Feeling well again for a day or two; start very easy |
| Illness with fever, chest symptoms, or a significant infection | Fully recovered; for anything more than a mild illness, or chest symptoms, check with a doctor first |
| Injury | Cleared by a doctor or physio, ideally with their guidance on what to avoid |
| Surgery, pregnancy, new health condition | Cleared by their clinician, following any return plan they gave |

The assistant never clears someone to train; it asks whether they've been cleared.

## The ramp (general guide)

Scale by the length of the break. Longer breaks get longer ramps.

**Week 1: re-entry**
- About half the usual sets, at lighter loads; effort easy (4 or more reps left in reserve).
- Cardio shorter and gentler than before.
- Focus on technique and how the body feels.

**Week 2: build**
- Most of the usual sets, still lighter loads; effort moderate.
- Cardio duration back towards normal at an easy pace.

**Week 3: approach**
- Usual sets, loads stepping back towards previous levels; effort normal.
- Intensity in cardio reintroduced gradually.

**Week 4 onward: resume progression**
- Back to the usual progression rules from where performance actually is, not where it was.

After a long break (months), repeat weeks 1–2 before moving on.

## Daily check-in questions

1. How did yesterday's session feel, 0–10?
2. Any soreness that's more than normal, or any pain?
3. Sleep and energy?
4. For an injury return: any symptoms in the injured area during or after?

If symptoms in an injured area return or worsen, stop that exercise and go back to the physio or doctor.

## Motivation side

- Shrink the goal: "three sessions this week", not "back to my best".
- Schedule sessions as appointments.
- Celebrate showing up; numbers will follow.
- Expect a few flat days. They're normal and pass.

## Worked example

User trained three times a week, squatting 3×8 at 60 kg, then had three weeks off with flu. Cleared by feeling well for several days and no chest symptoms (they'd have seen a doctor otherwise). Week 1: two sessions, squat 2×8 at 40 kg, walking instead of running. Week 2: three sessions, 3×8 at 45–50 kg, easy runs. Week 3: 3×8 at 55 kg. Week 4: 60 kg if it feels right, then normal progression.

## Common mistakes

- Picking up at the old weights on day one.
- Doubling sessions to "catch up".
- Mistaking returning symptoms of an injury for normal soreness.
- Restarting hard while still recovering from illness.
