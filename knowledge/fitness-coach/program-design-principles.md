# Program design principles and templates

The best plan is the one the person will actually do. Start from their real schedule and equipment, then apply a few principles.

## Principles

**Specificity.** You get better at what you practise. A 10k runner needs running; someone wanting to carry groceries more easily needs loaded carries and leg strength.

**Progressive overload.** Gradually ask for a bit more: more reps, more weight, more sets, more distance, or harder variations. Change one variable at a time.

**Recovery.** Fitness improves between sessions, not during them. Hard sessions for the same muscles or system usually need a day or more in between. Sleep matters.

**Consistency over intensity.** Three moderate sessions a week for months beats two weeks of daily hard training and a burnout.

**Minimum effective dose.** Begin with the least that will work, and add. It leaves room to progress.

## Choosing exercises

Cover the main movement patterns across the week:

- squat (squat, sit-to-stand, leg press)
- hinge (deadlift variations, hip bridge)
- push (push-up, press)
- pull (row, pulldown, band pull)
- carry (farmer's carry)
- core (plank variations, dead bug)
- plus aerobic work the person enjoys

Pick variations that fit the equipment and that the person can do with good form today.

## Reps and effort (general guide)

- **Beginners:** moderate weights and moderate reps; finish each set with a few reps "in the tank". Technique first.
- **Strength focus:** heavier loads and fewer reps, longer rests.
- **Muscle endurance:** lighter loads, more reps, shorter rests.

Use effort (reps in reserve, or a 0–10 scale) rather than fixed numbers, so the plan adapts to the person.

## Weekly templates

**Two days a week (full body each time)**
- Day A: squat, push, pull, core; 15–20 minutes of moderate cardio.
- Day B: hinge, push variation, pull variation, carry; 15–20 minutes of cardio.
- Add brisk walks on other days to reach the aerobic target.

**Three days a week (full body)**
- Mon A, Wed B, Fri A; next week B, A, B. Cardio on two other days or after sessions.

**Four days a week (upper / lower)**
- Mon lower, Tue upper, Thu lower, Fri upper; cardio on one or two other days.

**Runner adding strength**
- Two short strength sessions on easy-run days or rest days, not before key runs.

## Session shape

1. Warm-up (5–10 min): easy cardio plus lighter sets of the first exercise.
2. Main lifts or intervals.
3. Accessory work.
4. Cool-down: easy movement.

## Worked example

User: 40s, desk job, three 45-minute slots a week, home dumbbells and a bench, goal "feel stronger and fitter". Plan: three full-body sessions (goblet squat, dumbbell row, push-up from an incline, hip bridge, farmer's carry, plank), two sets to start, finishing each set with two or three reps left. Brisk walks at lunch on two other days. Progress by adding a rep each week until the top of the range, then a heavier dumbbell.

## Common mistakes

- Copying an advanced athlete's program.
- Too many exercises, too little progression.
- No rest days, or hard sessions back to back for the same muscles.
- Changing the plan every week, so nothing can progress.
