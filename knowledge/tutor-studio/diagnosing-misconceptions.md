# Diagnosing misconceptions

A misconception is a consistent wrong model, not a careless slip. The sign is a *pattern*: the same type of wrong answer, given confidently. Re-teaching the correct method rarely fixes it, because the student is still applying the old model. You have to surface it, show where it breaks, and replace it.

## Slip, gap or misconception?

| Type | Looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Slip | Random, the student self-corrects when asked to check | Teach a checking habit |
| Gap | Doesn't know where to start, says "we never did this" | Teach it |
| Misconception | The same wrong method every time, with confidence | Diagnose and confront it (below) |

## The method

1. **Ask them to think aloud.** "Talk me through it." The method they describe is the data.
2. **Probe with a bait item.** This is a question where the misconception gives a specific, predictable wrong answer.
3. **Name it precisely** in the file: "adds numerators and denominators", not "weak on fractions".
4. **Create a conflict.** Find a case where their model gives an answer they can see is wrong. "You said 1/2 + 1/2 = 2/4. Is half a pizza plus half a pizza really half a pizza?"
5. **Rebuild.** Teach the correct model with a representation that makes it obvious: a diagram, objects, a number line.
6. **Check later** with a fresh bait item in a later session. Old models come back under pressure.

## Starter bank of common misconceptions

**Maths**

| Misconception | Bait item | Their typical answer |
|---|---|---|
| Adds tops and bottoms of fractions | 1/3 + 1/4 | 2/7 |
| A longer decimal is bigger | Which is bigger, 0.5 or 0.45? | 0.45 |
| Multiplying always makes bigger | Is 8 × 0.5 more or less than 8? | More |
| The equals sign means "the answer comes next" | 7 + __ = 10 + 2 | 3 |
| Variables stand for objects ("a" means apples) | "a = number of apples": what is 3a? | "three apples" |
| Squaring a binomial distributes | (x + 3)² | x² + 9 |
| Percentage increase then decrease cancels out | Increase 100 by 10%, then decrease by 10% | 100 |

**Science**

| Misconception | Probe |
|---|---|
| Heavier objects fall faster (ignoring air resistance) | "Drop a ball and a heavier ball of the same size. Which lands first?" |
| Plants get their mass from the soil | "Where does a tree's wood come from?" |
| A force is needed to keep something moving | "A puck slides on ice. What keeps it going?" |
| Seasons happen because the Earth is closer to the Sun | "Why is it summer in Australia when it's winter in the UK?" |

**Reading and writing**

| Misconception | Probe |
|---|---|
| Reading means decoding words, not making meaning | "Tell me what just happened in your own words." |
| A paragraph is just a block of a certain length | "Why did the writer start a new paragraph here?" |
| Long words make writing better | Compare two versions of a sentence |

These are starting points. Add your own students' patterns to the file over time.

## Recording it

In students/<name>.md:

```

Conflict used: pizza halves; fraction wall
Status: rebuilt 10-21; retest 11-04 → ✔ (1/3 + 1/6 correct, explained)
```

## Worked example

Leo, 12, says 0.45 > 0.5 "because 45 is bigger than 5". This is the longer-decimal-is-bigger model.

- **Conflict:** "Would you rather have 0.5 of a pizza or 0.45?" Shade both on a 100-square grid: 50 squares against 45.
- **Rebuild:** place value with columns, writing 0.5 as 0.50.
- **Retest two sessions later** with a fresh set: 0.3 vs 0.29, and 0.7 vs 0.65. All correct, with the reason given.

## Common mistakes

- Marking the answer wrong and showing the right method, without finding out the model behind it.
- Diagnosing from one error. You need a pattern.
- Telling the parent "weak on fractions". Name the misconception and the plan.
- Assuming it's fixed after one good session. Retest later.
