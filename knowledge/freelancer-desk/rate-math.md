# Rate math and the raise conversation

The floor rate is the lowest rate at which the business works. Below it, every hour loses money. Calculate it from the freelancer's own numbers, not from what others charge.

## Floor rate calculation

```
Target annual take-home pay (before personal tax)    A
+ Business costs per year (software, equipment, insurance,
  accountant, workspace, training, marketing)          B
+ Benefits you now pay yourself (pension, health cover,
  sick and holiday buffer)                              C
= Revenue needed                                        R = A + B + C

Working weeks per year (52 − holidays − public holidays − sick allowance)   W
Billable hours per week (not total hours worked)                           H
Billable hours per year = W × H

Floor hourly rate = R ÷ (W × H)
```

Tax treatment differs by country; the accountant can help estimate the personal tax that will come out of A.

## Billable hours: be honest

Not every working hour is billable. Admin, sales, invoicing, learning and emails take real time. Ask the freelancer to track a few typical weeks to find the real billable share; don't assume a full working week is billable.

## Utilisation

**Utilisation = billable hours ÷ available working hours.**

- Consistently low: not enough work, or too much unpaid admin. Look at pipeline and processes before cutting price.
- Consistently very high, with a waiting list: a strong signal to raise rates.
- Very high with no slack for sales: next quarter's pipeline may dry up.

## Market signals

- Win rate on proposals: winning nearly everything often means the price is low.
- What comparable freelancers publish (where they do).
- Clients mentioning price unprompted, or never.
- Demand for your specialism rising or falling.

## Raising rates

**New clients:** new rate from today.

**Existing clients:** give notice and a reason.

> "From 1 February my day rate for new work will be 650, up from 580. I'm giving you plenty of notice so you can plan. Work already agreed stays at the current rate. Thanks for working with me; I've really enjoyed the projects this year."

Tips:

- Give a clear date and enough notice (a month or more is common courtesy).
- Don't over-justify; one line of context is enough.
- Accept that a few low-paying clients may leave; that's often the point.
- For long-standing clients, a smaller rise or a later date can be a deliberate choice.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)

Target take-home 48,000; costs 6,000; pension and buffer 6,000: R = 60,000. Working weeks 44; tracked billable hours 22 a week: 968 hours. Floor rate ≈ 62 an hour. The freelancer charges 55, so every hour is below floor. Plan: new clients at 75 from now; existing clients to 70 from the next quarter, with notice.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year.
- Forgetting the costs an employer used to pay.
- Discounting to fill quiet months, then anchoring there.
- Raising rates with no notice mid-project.
