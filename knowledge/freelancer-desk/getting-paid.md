# Getting paid: terms and the chase ladder

Most late payment is prevented at the start: clear terms, a deposit, and invoices that are easy to approve.

## Terms that help

- **Deposit up front** for new clients or fixed-price projects (commonly a third to a half).
- **Milestone payments** for longer projects, so you're never far out of pocket.
- **Clear due date** on every invoice ("Due 14 November 2026"), not just "30 days".
- **Late-payment terms** in the agreement, where the law allows.
- **Stop-work clause:** work pauses if an invoice is overdue by a set time.

## Invoice checklist

- [ ] Your name or business name, address, contact.
- [ ] Client's legal name and address; their PO or reference number if they use one.
- [ ] Unique invoice number and date.
- [ ] What was delivered, matching the proposal's wording.
- [ ] Amount, any tax shown as required locally, total.
- [ ] Due date and how to pay (bank details, link).
- [ ] Sent to the right person (often accounts payable, not your contact).

## The chase ladder

Adjust the timing to the client and the size of the invoice.

1. **A few days before due:** friendly reminder. "Just a heads-up that invoice 042 is due Friday. Thanks!"
2. **Due date + a few days:** polite nudge. "Invoice 042 was due on the 14th. Could you check it's in the payment run?"
3. **A week or two overdue:** specific ask. "Invoice 042 is now 10 days overdue. Can you confirm when it'll be paid? If there's an issue with it, let me know and I'll sort it."
4. **Several weeks overdue:** firm. Call your contact; email accounts with the contact copied. Mention the agreement's late-payment terms and that work is paused.
5. **Final notice:** a formal letter stating the amount, the date, and the next step (interest, a collection service, a small-claims process).

Keep a log: date, channel, who, response.

## Your rights (country-specific)

Many countries give businesses statutory rights on late payment. Examples, checked 2026-11:

- **UK:** for business-to-business transactions, if no payment date is agreed, payment is late 30 days after the customer receives the invoice or the work is done (whichever is later). You can usually claim statutory interest of 8% plus the Bank of England base rate, plus fixed debt-recovery compensation, unless the contract sets a different arrangement (GOV.UK).
- **Elsewhere:** rules vary (the EU has a late-payment directive implemented nationally; US rules vary by state). Look up the official source for the client's and freelancer's jurisdictions.

Before formal steps, suggest the freelancer checks the contract and, for larger sums, speaks to a lawyer, a business-advice service or a small-claims adviser.

## Worked example

Invoice 042 for 2,400, due 14 Nov. 11 Nov: heads-up. 18 Nov: nudge; the contact says it's "with finance". 26 Nov: email to accounts with PO number, contact copied; finance says the PO was wrong. Corrected invoice sent same day; paid 3 Dec. Client file note: "Always get the PO before invoicing."

## Common mistakes

- Waiting weeks before the first chase.
- Invoicing your contact instead of accounts.
- Continuing to work for a client with several overdue invoices.
- Apologetic chasers that read as optional.
