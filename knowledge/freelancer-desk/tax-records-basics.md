# Tax records basics (country-specific)

This is general and educational, not tax advice. Tax rules, rates, deadlines and allowable expenses differ by country and change often. The freelancer should confirm everything with an accountant or the tax authority.

## Records to keep (most countries)

- [ ] Every invoice issued and every payment received.
- [ ] Every business expense, with the receipt or invoice.
- [ ] Bank statements for the account(s) used for business.
- [ ] Mileage or travel logs, if claimed.
- [ ] Equipment purchases (may be treated differently from day-to-day costs).
- [ ] Contracts and change orders.
- [ ] Tax payments made and correspondence with the tax authority.

Keep them for as long as the tax authority requires; the period varies by country. A separate business bank account makes everything easier.

## Monthly routine

1. Match payments received against invoices.
2. Match expenses to receipts; photograph paper receipts.
3. Tag each expense by category.
4. Move a share of each payment into a tax savings account.
5. Note anything unusual for the accountant (a big purchase, a foreign client, working from home).

## Setting money aside

Freelancers usually don't have tax deducted at source, so a bill arrives later. A common habit is to move a fixed percentage of every payment received into a separate account. The right percentage depends on income, country and circumstances; ask an accountant to suggest one, and review it yearly.

## Examples of how it works (checked 2026-11)

- **United States:** the IRS says self-employed people generally must file an annual return and pay estimated tax quarterly, covering income tax and self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare). See the IRS Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center for current forms and deadlines. State taxes may also apply.
- **United Kingdom:** sole traders must keep records of business income and expenses for their Self Assessment tax return (GOV.UK). Payment-on-account arrangements and VAT registration thresholds apply in some cases; check GOV.UK for current figures.

Other countries have their own systems (registration, advance payments, sales taxes such as VAT or GST on services). Look up the official tax authority's guidance for the freelancer's country of residence, and note that working for foreign clients can raise extra questions.

## When to see an accountant

- At the start of freelancing, to set up correctly.
- Income rising quickly, or crossing a sales-tax or VAT threshold.
- Clients abroad, or moving country.
- Thinking about forming a company.
- Any letter from the tax authority that isn't routine.
- Before the first annual return.

## Preparing for the accountant

Send: income summary by client, expenses by category, bank statements, list of equipment bought, mileage log, tax already paid, and questions in writing.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing personal and business spending in one account.
- Spending the tax money.
- Missing advance or quarterly payment dates.
- Claiming expenses without receipts or a business reason.
