# Change orders and scope wording

Scope creep is rarely bad faith. Usually the client learned something and wants more. The fix is to make every change a visible decision with a price and a date.

## Is it in scope?

Check the request against the agreement:

1. Is it in the deliverables list? In scope.
2. Is it in "not included"? Out of scope.
3. Is it a revision within the agreed rounds? In scope, but count it.
4. Neither listed nor excluded? Grey zone. Decide based on size and the relationship, and say which way you chose.

Signals of creep: "while you're at it", "quick tweak", new people joining with new opinions, revisions that change direction rather than refine it.

## Three responses

**1. The diplomatic no.** For things that don't fit the project or your skills.

> "Good idea. It's outside what we set up for this project, and I don't think I'm the best person for it. Happy to suggest someone."

**2. The paid yes.** For things you're glad to do.

> "Happy to add that. It's outside the current scope, so I'll send a short change order: about 6 hours, so 540, and it moves delivery by two days. Shall I go ahead?"

**3. The swap.** When budget is fixed.

> "We can fit that in if we drop the second banner set. Same price and date. Want to swap?"

**Small favours.** Occasionally doing a tiny extra for free can build goodwill. Name it so it doesn't become the norm: "That one's on me; anything more I'll quote."

## Change-order template

```
Change order #<n> — <Project>          Date:
Requested by:
Change: <what's being added, removed or changed>
Reason:
Effect on price: <+ amount, or none>
Effect on timeline: <new dates>
Effect on other deliverables: <if any>
Approval: "Reply 'approved' to this email to confirm."
```

Keep a running log of change orders in the client file. It's useful at renewal and in any dispute.

## Revision rounds

Define a round in the agreement: "One round means one consolidated set of feedback from the client." Count them in each message: "Here's round 2 of 2."

## When it's become a different project

If changes add up to a new direction, pause and re-scope: "We've moved a long way from the original brief. Can we take 20 minutes to reset the scope and price so we're both clear?"

## Common mistakes

- Doing the extra work first, then trying to bill for it.
- Vague replies ("I'll see what I can do") that the client hears as yes.
- Change orders by phone with no written record.
- Absorbing every small extra until the margin is gone.
