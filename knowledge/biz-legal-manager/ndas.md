# NDAs

NDAs are the highest-volume, lowest-value legal request in most companies. The goal is to make them fast. A standard NDA on the company's own paper should be self-serve, and a counterparty's NDA should take minutes to check against a short list. This isn't legal advice. Anything outside the list below goes to General Counsel, and anything unusual goes to a qualified lawyer.

## Mutual or one-way?

- **Mutual:** both sides will share confidential information. Typical for partnerships, joint evaluations and M&A talks. Both sides are bound by the same obligations.
- **One-way (unilateral):** only one side discloses. If we're disclosing (for example, to a contractor or prospective vendor), a one-way NDA protecting us is fine. If we're only receiving, we're the one bound, so check the obligations carefully.

If you're not sure who will share what, use mutual. It's usually quicker to agree because it reads as balanced.

## What to check

- [ ] **Parties:** exact legal entities. Are affiliates included, and is that needed?
- [ ] **Purpose:** information can be used only for a defined purpose (for example "evaluating a potential partnership"). A purpose that's too broad weakens protection, and one that's too narrow can block the real work.
- [ ] **Definition of confidential information:** marked-only, or anything reasonably understood to be confidential? Is oral disclosure covered?
- [ ] **Standard exclusions:** information that is public, already known to the recipient, independently developed, or lawfully received from a third party.
- [ ] **Compelled disclosure:** allowed when the law or a court requires it, with notice to the discloser where lawful.
- [ ] **Who may receive it:** employees, advisers and contractors on a need-to-know basis, bound by similar duties.
- [ ] **Term:** how long the NDA lasts and how long confidentiality survives. Trade secrets may need protection for as long as they remain secret. The playbook sets our positions.
- [ ] **Return or destruction** on request or at the end, with a carve-out for routine backups and legal retention.
- [ ] **No licence and no obligation:** sharing information grants no IP rights and doesn't commit anyone to a deal.
- [ ] **Remedies:** injunctive relief is normal. Watch for penalties, liquidated damages or uncapped indemnities.
- [ ] **Governing law and courts:** acceptable per the playbook.

## Red flags (send to General Counsel)

- Non-solicitation or non-compete clauses hidden in the NDA.
- Residuals clauses (the recipient may use what its people remember). These can effectively hand over know-how.
- IP assignment, licences or "feedback" ownership.
- Exclusivity or standstill provisions (common in M&A, and always counsel's call).
- Indemnities, penalties or liquidated damages.
- Very long or perpetual terms for all information.
- An NDA covering personal data at scale. That usually needs data processing terms as well (see [Privacy basics](./privacy-basics.md)).

## Self-serve template note

Keep one mutual and one one-way template, approved by counsel, in the knowledge base or template library, with a one-paragraph guide: when to use which, what can be filled in (parties, purpose, date), and what can't be changed without legal. Track the self-serve rate as a key result: NDAs completed on our template without legal edits ÷ all NDAs × 100.

## Worked example (made-up)

Marketing wants to share the product roadmap with an agency. Only we disclose, so the one-way template fits. The agency sends back its own mutual NDA instead. It has a residuals clause and a two-year term. The review keeps their paper but strikes the residuals clause and extends confidentiality for roadmap information per the playbook. The issues list has two lines, and the Contracts Specialist approves at fallback 1. Signing is by a human with signing authority.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating every NDA as bespoke.** Use templates and the checklist, and keep the queue for real contracts.
- **Signing the counterparty's NDA without reading it** because "it's just an NDA". The red flags above are where the surprises are.
- **No record.** Log every signed NDA in the matter log with its term, so confidentiality obligations can be found later.
- **Over-relying on the NDA.** It doesn't replace limiting what is shared to what the purpose needs.
