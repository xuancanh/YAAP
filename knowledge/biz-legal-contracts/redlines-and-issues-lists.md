# Redlines and issues lists

A redline is a negotiation document; the issues list is a decision document. Write the redline for the other side's lawyer and the issues list for our deal owner.

## Triage first

Before editing, sort every playbook topic into:

1. **Blockers.** Must change or escalate before we can sign (e.g. uncapped liability, governing law outside the playbook).
2. **Important.** Should change; there's a fallback we can accept.
3. **Nice to have.** Preferences. Consider leaving them.

Fewer changes get accepted faster. A redline that touches every paragraph signals that we haven't prioritised and invites a slow, line-by-line fight. Spend your changes on blockers and important points.

## Redline rules

- **Use track changes on their document.** Don't retype or reformat it; that hides changes and breaks trust.
- **Change the minimum words.** Insert "mutual" rather than redrafting the clause. Small edits are easier to accept.
- **Use the playbook's preferred language**, then its fallback if the preferred wording was already rejected.
- **Keep defined terms consistent.** If you introduce a term, define it once.
- **Check cross-references** after any deletion or renumbering.
- **No silent changes.** Every edit should be visible.

## Margin comments

Comments explain why, in neutral terms, so the other side can agree without losing face.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Unacceptable." | "We need the cap to apply to both parties; this keeps the risk balanced." |
| "Delete." | "Removed as the indemnity in 9.1 already covers third-party IP claims." |
| "Our policy doesn't allow this." | "We can offer 30 days' notice of material changes to the security measures instead." |
| "Please explain." | "Could you share the concern behind this clause? We may be able to address it another way." |

Don't put internal reasoning, risk ratings or our fallback positions in comments the other side will see. Those go in the issues list.

## The issues list

For the deal owner and anyone who must approve a concession. Plain words, no clause numbers without explanation, most important first.

| # | Issue | What it means for us | Our proposed position | If they refuse | Who approves a concession |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | |

Add at the top: counterparty, document, value, term, governing law, the deal owner's target date, and a one-line overall view ("Signable once issues 1 and 2 are resolved; the rest are minor").

## Worked example

Vendor's services agreement for a 12-month data-enrichment service.

> **Overall:** Two blockers, one important point. Expect one round.
>
> 1. **Their liability is capped at one month's fees; ours is uncapped.** If their service causes a data incident, we'd recover very little while being fully exposed to them. *Position:* mutual cap at 12 months' fees, with data protection breaches at a higher cap per the playbook. *If refused:* escalate to the General Counsel; don't sign.
> 2. **They can use our data to "improve their services".** That could include training on our customers' data. *Position:* use only to provide the service to us. *If refused:* escalate; also needs the Privacy & Compliance Officer.
> 3. **Auto-renews for 12 months unless we give 90 days' notice.** Easy to miss. *Position:* 30 days' notice. *If refused:* accept 90 days and add to the obligations register (deal owner can approve).
> 4. Minor: invoice terms 15 days; we asked for 30. Fine to concede.

## Rounds

- Track what was agreed each round in the issues list ("agreed in round 2"), so nothing reopens unnoticed.
- Compare each new version they send against our last version, not theirs. Counterparties sometimes change other clauses between rounds; always run a comparison.
- When stuck after two rounds on the same point, suggest a call. Explaining the concern often finds a third option.

## Before you hand off for signature

- [ ] Clean version matches the last agreed redline exactly.
- [ ] Every blocker resolved or approved by the right person, with the approval recorded.
- [ ] Order form or commercial terms match what the deal owner agreed.
- [ ] Turnaround and time spent recorded for the metrics.
