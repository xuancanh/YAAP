# Clause review guide

For each topic: what it does, what to check, and the drafting that quietly shifts risk. Your positions come from the playbook; this guide helps you spot when a clause departs from it.

## Before the clauses

- **Parties.** Correct legal entities on both sides; the one that signs is the one that's bound.
- **Order of precedence.** Which document wins when the master agreement, order form, policies and statements of work disagree.
- **Definitions.** Read them first. A narrow "Confidential Information" or a broad "Losses" changes every clause that uses the word.
- **Incorporated documents.** Online terms, policies or "as updated from time to time" links can change after signature. Note each one.

## Limitation of liability

**Does:** caps how much each side can recover and excludes some kinds of loss (often indirect or consequential loss, lost profits).
**Check:** the cap amount and what it's based on (fees paid, fees payable, a fixed sum, a period); whether it's mutual; what's excluded from the cap ("carve-outs").
**Watch for:** carve-outs so broad the cap means little (e.g. "any breach of confidentiality or data protection" uncapped); a cap that applies only to one side; lost profits excluded in a deal where lost revenue is our main risk. Some liabilities often can't be limited by law; those vary by jurisdiction.

## Indemnities

**Does:** one side promises to cover the other's losses from specified third-party claims (classically IP infringement claims).
**Check:** who indemnifies whom, for what triggers, and whether it sits inside or outside the liability cap; the procedure (notice, control of defence, cooperation).
**Watch for:** indemnities for "any breach of this agreement" (turns every breach into uncapped exposure); indemnities for the other side's own negligence; no duty to mitigate.

## Warranties

**Does:** promises about the product or service (works as described, performed with reasonable skill, no malicious code).
**Check:** that warranties match what we can actually deliver; the remedy (fix, re-perform, refund) and whether it's exclusive; disclaimers.
**Watch for:** warranties of "fitness for the customer's purpose" or compliance with "all laws" without limits.

## Intellectual property

**Check:** who owns what's created; licences granted, their scope and duration; our pre-existing IP stays ours; feedback and usage data rights.
**Watch for:** assignment of "all work product" that captures our platform or tools; licence to use customer data for broad purposes beyond the service.

## Confidentiality

**Check:** definition, exclusions (public, already known, independently developed, received from a third party), permitted disclosures (advisers, legally required), duration, return or destruction.
**Watch for:** one-way terms in a two-way exchange; no exclusion for independently developed information; perpetual obligations on routine information.

## Data protection

**Check:** whether personal data is processed and in which role (controller, processor); a compliant data processing agreement; security commitments that match reality; sub-processor terms; breach notice times we can meet; international transfers. Involve the Privacy & Compliance Officer.

## Term, renewal and termination

**Check:** start and end; automatic renewal and the notice period to stop it; termination for convenience (who has it, with what notice); termination for breach (cure period); what survives; refunds on termination.
**Watch for:** short windows to give notice of non-renewal; termination for convenience for the other side only; long notice combined with long renewal terms. These feed the [obligations register](obligations-extraction.md).

## Payment

**Check:** fees, invoicing frequency, payment terms, currency, late payment provisions, price increases on renewal, taxes, set-off rights.

## Assignment and change of control

**Check:** whether either side can transfer the contract, and whether a change of ownership counts as assignment. Overly tight terms can block a future sale or reorganisation.

## Governing law and disputes

**Check:** whose law applies and where disputes go (courts or arbitration). Use only what the playbook allows; anything else is an escalation.

## Answering "what does our contract say?"

1. Find the executed version (not the draft) and any amendments; check which document takes precedence.
2. Quote the clause with its number.
3. Explain the effect in one or two plain sentences for the question asked.
4. Say what you can't answer: interpretation, disputes, or law outside the contract go to the General Counsel.

> "Can we cancel the analytics tool early?" Section 12.2: either party may terminate for convenience on 90 days' written notice. So yes; notice must be in writing to the address in Section 18. Fees already paid aren't refunded (Section 12.5).
