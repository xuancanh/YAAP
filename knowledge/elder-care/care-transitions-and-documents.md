# Care transitions and documents

Moves between care settings are where things go wrong: medicines get changed, information gets lost, and families decide under pressure. Researching early and keeping the documents ready turns a crisis into a choice.

## The options, roughly in order of support

| Option | Typical fit | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Family care at home, with aids and adaptations | Needs are moderate and the carer can sustain it | Is the carer's health holding up? What's the backup? |
| Paid home care visits | Help with washing, dressing, meals, medicines prompts | Hours available? Same carers each visit? Missed-visit policy? |
| Adult day programme | Social contact, carer gets daytime respite | Transport? Dementia-capable? |
| Live-in care | Needs round-the-clock presence but not nursing | Cost, the carer's breaks, cover arrangements |
| Assisted / supported living | Mostly independent with on-site help | What happens when needs increase? |
| Care home / nursing home | Needs continuous care or nursing | Staffing, inspection reports, visiting, fees and increases |

Names, regulation and funding differ by country and region. Look up the official inspection body's reports (for example, a national care regulator) and cite the date you checked.

## Decision guide

1. What does the person want? Record it in their words.
2. What are their needs now, and likely in a year? Ask their clinicians.
3. What can the current carers sustain without breaking?
4. What does each option cost, and who pays? Funding rules are **country-specific and often means-tested**; get an assessment from the local authority or insurer and advice from a benefits adviser or elder-law professional.
5. Visit at least two options, at different times of day.

## Hospital discharge checklist

- [ ] Ask early: "What's the expected discharge date and what support will be needed?"
- [ ] Get the discharge summary and the **new medication list**; have the pharmacist reconcile it with the old one.
- [ ] Confirm equipment (bed, commode, frame) is delivered before arrival home.
- [ ] Confirm who visits first, when, and the number to call if things go wrong.
- [ ] Follow-up appointments booked and in the calendar.
- [ ] Update the one-page summary (see [appointment prep](appointment-prep-and-summary.md)).

## The document folder

Gather copies (originals stored safely) and note where each is kept:

- ID, health and insurance cards;
- the one-page summary and medication list;
- any legal authority documents for health and for finances (power of attorney or local equivalent);
- advance directive, living will or care preferences, and any do-not-resuscitate form recognised locally;
- will location and solicitor/lawyer contact;
- bank, pension, benefits and bills list;
- property and tenancy documents;
- passwords handled through a secure method, not a plain list.

Legal instruments must be made with a qualified lawyer or under the local official process, while the person still has capacity to make them. The assistant organises; it never drafts these documents or advises on them.

## Common mistakes

- Starting research after the hospital says "discharge tomorrow".
- Choosing on brochures without reading inspection reports.
- Leaving legal authority until capacity is already in doubt.
