# Appointment prep and the one-page summary

Elder care means many providers who do not share notes. A current one-page summary, handed over at every visit, saves time and catches errors. Keep it to one side of paper.

## One-page care summary template

```
Name / date of birth:                     Updated: <date>
Main contact (caregiver) + phone:
Who holds health decision authority (if any), and where the document is kept:

Conditions (as diagnosed, in the clinician's words):
Allergies and reactions:
Current medicines (name, strength, how often, prescriber) — copy from the medication list, do not retype from memory:
Recent changes (last 3 months): new/stopped medicines, hospital stays, falls:
Providers: GP/primary care, specialists, pharmacy, home care — with phone numbers:
Daily function: mobility aids, hearing/vision, memory, continence, eating:
What matters most to them (their words):
Advance care wishes on file? Yes/No, where:
```

Ask the person (where they can) what they want shared. Their privacy and dignity count even when a family member is coordinating.

## Before the appointment

- [ ] Confirm the time, place, and whether it is in person or remote.
- [ ] Arrange transport and check accessibility (parking, lifts, wheelchair).
- [ ] Print the summary; bring the medicine list and, if asked, the actual medicine boxes.
- [ ] Write the **top three questions**, most important first. Doctors may run out of time.
- [ ] Note symptoms with dates and specifics: what, when, how often, what makes it better or worse.
- [ ] Bring glasses, hearing aids, ID and insurance or health cards.
- [ ] Agree with the person who speaks first and what they want help with.

## During

- Let the person speak for themselves where they can; fill gaps, do not take over.
- Ask the clinician to repeat back any change: "So the new plan is…?"
- Ask: What is the next step? When should we expect results? Who do we call if it gets worse?
- Write down any medicine change exactly as said, then confirm it with the pharmacist.

## After (same day)

1. Debrief: decisions, changes, tests ordered, follow-up date.
2. Update the summary and the medication list.
3. Put the follow-up and any test dates in the calendar.
4. Send the family update (see [family updates](family-updates-and-caregiver-load.md)).
5. If something was unclear, call the practice now rather than later.

## Worked example: top three questions

A daughter preparing for her father's cardiology visit lists: "1. He's been more breathless climbing stairs since the new tablet in August; is that related? 2. Should he still take it on days he feels dizzy? (Ask the doctor; we don't decide that.) 3. When is the next echo?" Specific dates and observations get specific answers.

## Common mistakes

- Arriving with a bag of medicines and no list.
- Asking the most important question last, as the clinician stands up.
- Leaving without knowing who to call if things get worse.
- Updating the summary "later" and never doing it.
