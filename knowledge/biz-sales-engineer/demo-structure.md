# Demo structure

A demo proves that the product solves the buyer's stated problems. It is not a tour. Show the fewest screens that prove each use case, in the buyer's language, with data that looks like theirs.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Discovery notes read: pains, use cases, decision criteria, who attends
- [ ] Use cases ranked by what matters most to the decision-maker in the room
- [ ] For each use case: the "before" (how they do it today), the "after" (in our product), the proof point (the metric or moment that shows it works)
- [ ] Demo environment set with realistic, non-customer data; tested end to end the day before
- [ ] Integrations and logins checked; a backup (recording or screenshots) ready
- [ ] Agenda agreed with the AE: who opens, who handles commercial questions
- [ ] Questions you expect, with honest answers, including gaps

## The structure

1. **Confirm the agenda and goals** (a few minutes). "From our last call, you need A, B and C. Is that still right? Anything to add?" Adjust on the spot.
2. **Show the outcome first.** Start each use case with the end result (the report, the finished workflow), then show how you get there. People remember the first thing they see.
3. **One use case at a time**: tell (what you'll show and why it matters to them) → show (only the steps needed) → tell (what they just saw, tied to their metric).
4. **Check in** after each use case: "How does that compare with how you do it today?" Their answer is discovery.
5. **Close**: recap which needs were proven, list open questions with owners, agree the next step (deeper session, POC, proposal).

## Tailoring

- Use the buyer's terms (their names for teams, records, processes).
- Speak to the role: executives want outcomes and risk; admins want setup and control; users want speed and fewer steps.
- If a key attendee joined late, spend a minute on their priorities, not on repeating the start.

## Handling gaps live

- If asked for something the product doesn't do: say so plainly, ask what problem it solves, and show the closest supported approach if one exists. Write it down as a gap.
- Never improvise a workaround you haven't tested.
- For roadmap questions, give only what product has confirmed, or say you'll check and follow up.

## When it breaks

- Stay calm, say what happened, switch to the backup and move on.
- Don't debug in front of the buyer for more than a moment.
- Follow up with a short recording of the part that failed.

## After the demo (same day)

- Write up: needs proven, needs not proven, gaps, questions, next step.
- Update the technical win criteria in the deal record.
- Send follow-up materials through the AE.

## Pitfalls

- **Feature dumping.** Every extra feature is a chance to confuse or raise an objection.
- **Generic data.** "Acme Corp" sample data makes it harder to picture their own work.
- **Talking over silence.** Pauses are where buyers think and ask; let them.
- **Ignoring the decision-maker.** If the economic buyer cares about one thing, show it first, even if it's the least exciting.
