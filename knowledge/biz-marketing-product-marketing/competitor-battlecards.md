# Competitor battlecards

A battlecard helps a rep in a live deal answer three things: why customers pick them, why customers pick us, and what to say when the competitor comes up. It is not a list of everything the competitor does. Keep the brief (deep, quarterly) in `/org/marketing/competitors/<name>.md` and the battlecard (one screen) at the top of it.

## Where intelligence comes from

**Allowed and useful:**

- The competitor's public website, pricing page, docs, changelog, release notes, status page and job postings.
- Public reviews on review sites (read many; single reviews are anecdotes).
- Their public filings, press releases, webinars and conference talks.
- Our win/loss interviews and sales call notes where the prospect mentioned them.
- Public community forums and social posts.

**Not allowed:**

- Posing as a prospect or customer to get demos, trials or pricing under false pretences.
- Asking new hires or customers to share a competitor's confidential information.
- Accessing non-public areas, leaked documents or anything obtained by breaking terms of service.
- Guessing their roadmap and presenting it as fact.

If you're unsure whether a source is acceptable, ask Legal before using it.

## Battlecard template

```markdown
# Battlecard: <competitor> — verified <date>

## In one line
<who they are and who they're best for, fairly stated>

## When we win
- <situation> — because <our strength> (source: win/loss refs)
## When we lose
- <situation> — because <their strength> (source)

## Quick dismissals (claims they make and our honest response)
| They say | Our response | Proof |

## Landmines (questions to ask early that highlight our strengths)
- "How do you handle <scenario where we are better>?"

## Traps to avoid
- Don't compete on <area where they're genuinely stronger>; reframe to <value>.

## Pricing and packaging (public only)
<as listed on their site on <date>, with link>

## Recent changes
- <date> — <what changed> — <link> — impact for us

## Customer proof against them
- <approved story of a customer who switched or chose us>
```

## Fairness rules for comparisons

Comparative claims carry legal risk and, more often, credibility risk. Rules differ by country, so check with Legal before publishing any external comparison.

- **Verifiable:** every fact about the competitor links to a public source and has an "as of" date.
- **Current:** re-verify before any external use; their product changes too.
- **Like for like:** compare equivalent plans and features; don't compare our top plan to their entry plan.
- **No disparagement:** state differences, not insults. "They don't support X natively (docs, date)" not "their X is broken".
- **Acknowledge their strengths internally.** Reps lose trust in a card that pretends the competitor is bad at everything.
- **Internal vs external:** battlecards are internal. Anything customer-facing needs a separate, reviewed comparison.

## Quarterly brief refresh

1. Check their changelog, pricing and docs for changes since the last verified date.
2. Pull deals where they were listed as a competitor: wins, losses, reasons.
3. Update win/loss patterns and the win rate against them (see [metrics](win-loss-and-metrics.md)).
4. Update the battlecard; mark changed lines with the date.
5. Tell sales what changed in two or three lines.

Off-cycle triggers: a pricing change, a notable release, an acquisition, or a sudden shift in win rate.

## Common mistakes

- Feature matrices with dozens of rows that nobody reads in a call.
- Stale pricing that a prospect corrects in front of the rep.
- Treating every vendor as a competitor; focus on those that appear in our deals.
- Ignoring the "do nothing" or "build it ourselves" alternative, which is often the real competitor.
