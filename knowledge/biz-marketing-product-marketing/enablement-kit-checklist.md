# Enablement kit checklist

An enablement kit succeeds when a rep or support agent who missed the session can still explain, demo and defend the product correctly. Write for them: short, scannable, and true today. Save it as `/org/marketing/enablement/<topic>.md`.

## Kit contents

### 1. One-page summary (read first)

- What it is, in one sentence a customer would understand.
- Who it's for (best-fit customer) and who it's *not* for.
- The problem it solves and the outcome it delivers.
- What's new vs before, and which plans include it.
- Availability: date, regions, limits.

### 2. Talk track

A short script, not a speech:

```markdown
**Opening question:** "How do you handle <problem> today?"
**If they describe <pain>:** "A lot of teams like yours <relatable situation>.
<Product> lets you <outcome> because <differentiator>."
**Proof:** "<Customer type> used it to <result>." (approved story only)
**Next step:** "Want me to show you how that looks with your data?"
```

Write it in spoken language. Read it aloud; if you stumble, rewrite.

### 3. Discovery questions

Five to eight questions that uncover whether the prospect has the problem and how much it costs them. Pair each with what a good answer sounds like.

### 4. Demo script

- Setup: the demo account, data and flags needed.
- The story: three to five steps that show the value pillars in order, each with "say this" and "show this".
- What not to show (unfinished areas, known issues).

### 5. Objection handling

Use the same pattern for every objection: acknowledge, clarify, respond with proof, confirm.

```markdown
**Objection:** "We already use <alternative>."
- Acknowledge: "Makes sense, it's widely used."
- Clarify: "What made you look at other options?"
- Respond: "<Differentiator> matters when <situation>. <Proof>."
- Confirm: "Does that match what you're seeing?"
```

Collect real objections from sales call notes and win/loss, not imagined ones. Cover pricing, switching cost, security, missing features and "not now".

### 6. Competitive notes

Link the relevant [battlecard](competitor-battlecards.md). Don't copy it in. Copies go stale.

### 7. Support section

- FAQ with the exact answers support should give.
- Known limitations and workarounds.
- How to escalate a bug or a feature request.

### 8. Assets and links

Customer-facing one-pager, deck slides, help articles, recording of the session. Note which are approved for external use.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every claim matches what shipped and what the positioning doc says.
- [ ] Every proof point is approved for customer-facing use, or marked internal.
- [ ] Limitations are stated plainly. Reps who over-promise create churn.
- [ ] A new rep could run the demo from the script alone.
- [ ] Objections came from real calls.
- [ ] Support has reviewed the FAQ.
- [ ] Kit has an owner and a review date.

## Running the session

- Keep it short; most of the time on demo and objections, not slides.
- Ask a rep to pitch it back at the end. Where they stumble is where the kit needs work.
- Record it and link the recording in the kit.

## Checking it's used

Coverage counts who has the kit; use counts who uses it. Both matter (formulas in [win/loss and metrics](win-loss-and-metrics.md)).

- Ask sales leads which parts they use, and read a sample of call notes for the new messaging.
- Track questions arriving in support or sales channels that the kit should have answered; each one is a kit fix.
- Update the kit within days when the product, price or competitor changes.

## Common mistakes

- A 40-slide deck instead of a kit.
- Features without the customer problem.
- Hiding limitations.
- No owner, so it rots after launch.
