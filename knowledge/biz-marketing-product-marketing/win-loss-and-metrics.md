# Win/loss and PMM metrics

Your KPIs are launch adoption, win rate against competitors and enablement coverage. Win/loss analysis feeds all three: it tells you why deals go the way they do, which shapes positioning, enablement and battlecards.

## Win/loss interviews

CRM loss reasons are a starting point, not the answer. Reps pick a reason quickly and "price" often hides something else. Talk to the buyer.

**Who to interview:** recent decisions (within a few weeks, while memory is fresh), both wins and losses, across segments and competitors. Ask the account owner first and respect a "no".

**How:**

- Someone other than the rep asks; buyers are more candid.
- 20–30 minutes, open questions, no selling, no arguing.
- Ask for consent to take notes and say how they'll be used. Follow the company's privacy rules for storing interview data.

**Question guide:**

1. What was happening that made you look for a solution?
2. Which options did you consider, including doing nothing?
3. How did you evaluate them? What mattered most?
4. Where did each option stand out, good or bad?
5. What almost changed your decision?
6. How did the sales process feel?
7. What would you tell a peer considering the same choice?

**Synthesis:** tag each interview by segment, competitor, outcome and the decisive factors. Look for patterns across several interviews before changing positioning. Quote customers exactly and cite the interview file.

## Metric definitions

### Launch adoption

```
Adoption rate (feature, day N) = eligible accounts (or users) that used the feature
                                 at least once within N days of launch
                                 / eligible accounts (or users) active in that period
Retained use (day M)           = accounts that used it in the latest period
                                 / accounts that ever adopted it
```

- **Eligible** means they have access (right plan, region, flag on). Don't divide by all customers if only some can use it.
- "Used" needs a defined event that shows real use, not just opening the page. Agree it with product before launch.
- Report at fixed checkpoints (for example two and six weeks) so launches are comparable.
- For new-customer impact, add pipeline or deals where the feature was cited, from CRM fields or call notes, labelled as such.

### Win rate vs competitors

```
Win rate vs X = closed-won deals where X was a competitor
                / (closed-won + closed-lost deals where X was a competitor)
```

- Use closed deals only, within a stated period. Open deals aren't in the denominator.
- Decide whether "no decision" losses count. Usually report them separately.
- Depends on reps recording competitors. Report the share of closed deals with the competitor field filled in; if it's low, the number is weak.
- Small samples swing; show the count of deals next to the rate, and prefer rolling two-quarter views.

### Enablement coverage

```
Coverage (kit)  = people in scope who completed the enablement (session attended,
                  recording watched or quiz passed) / people in scope
Asset coverage  = required kit sections complete and current / required sections
Freshness       = kits reviewed within their review date / total live kits
```

- "In scope" is the sales and support people who'll meet the topic.
- Coverage isn't use. Pair it with a usage signal: kit views, messaging appearing in call notes, or fewer repeat questions in support and sales channels.

## Reporting

- One table per KPI with the period, the source and the sample size.
- Name the definition changes since the last report.
- Close with the decision each number suggests: refresh positioning, update a battlecard, re-run enablement.

## Worked example

Q3: 40 closed deals listed Competitor A; 22 won, 18 lost. Win rate vs A = 22 / 40 = 55%. Previous quarter, 31 deals at 61%. The difference is a few deals and within normal noise, so flag it and watch, and read the loss interviews before changing anything. Illustrative numbers only.
