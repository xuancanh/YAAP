# SEO brief template

Your brief feeds the Content Marketer's own content brief. Give them what only search research can tell them: the query, the intent, what the current results do well and badly, and the on-page requirements. Leave voice and angle to them, but point out where an angle would win. Save as `/org/marketing/content/briefs/<slug>-seo.md`.

## The template

```markdown
# SEO brief: <slug>

**Cluster:** <from keyword map>   **Priority:** <score>
**Target URL:** <new: proposed path | existing: url to update>
**Main query:** <query>
**Secondary queries (same page):** <list>
**Intent:** <informational / commercial / transactional>  **Funnel stage:** <…>

## What the results page shows (checked <date>)
- Formats ranking: <guides, lists, tools, videos>
- Common subtopics covered by most top results: <list>
- Gaps: what none of them do well <outdated data, no examples, no template>
- Results-page features: <answer box, questions, video> — implication: <…>

## Recommended format and angle opportunity
<e.g. step-by-step guide with downloadable checklist; our task data could
answer "which steps slip most", which no result covers>

## Suggested outline
- H1: <topic>
- H2: <question/section> — must cover: <points>
- H2: …
- FAQ candidates: <questions from the results page that fit>

## On-page requirements
- Title idea: <draft, important words first, unique>
- Meta description idea: <one or two sentences>
- URL: <short, lowercase, hyphenated>
- Internal links OUT: <url — suggested anchor text>
- Internal links IN (ask site owner to add): <from url — anchor>
- Images: <diagrams or screenshots that would add value>
- Structured data: <type if relevant, e.g. Article, FAQ only if eligible; for site owner>

## Avoid
- Competing with <our existing url> — differentiate by <…>
- <claims or topics that need legal or product review>

## Success measure
- Leading: impressions and average position for main query by <date>
- Outcome: organic clicks and <conversion event> from this URL
```

## How to do the results-page analysis

1. Search the main query in a private window, signed out, in the target region and language if you can.
2. Open the top results and note format, depth, freshness, author expertise and what the reader gets.
3. Ask: what would a reader still need after reading all of them? That's the gap.
4. Note who ranks. Forums and community answers ranking often means searchers want first-hand experience; big brands ranking means authority matters and we need a real angle.

Don't tell the writer to copy the structure of the top result. Cover what readers need, and add what's missing.

## Length

Don't set a target word count from competitors. Give a range only if the format implies one (a definition is short; a complete guide is long), and say "as long as needed to fully answer, no padding".

## Checks before handing over

- [ ] The target URL doesn't compete with another of our pages for the same query.
- [ ] Intent and format match what ranks.
- [ ] At least one clear gap or angle the writer can exploit.
- [ ] Internal links point to live, canonical URLs.
- [ ] Success measure is readable in Search Console or analytics.

## After publishing

- Ask the site owner to add the incoming internal links.
- Check the page is indexed (Search Console URL inspection) within a couple of weeks.
- Add the URL to the monthly report watchlist.
