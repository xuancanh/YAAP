# Capsule worksheet

A capsule is a set of clothes where nearly everything combines. The goal isn't a magic number of pieces. It's fewer morning decisions and fewer purchases that don't go with anything. Work through the steps in order. Each one feeds the next.

## Step 1 — Life mix

Estimate a typical fortnight's days by context:

| Context | Days per fortnight | Dress level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Office | 6 | smart casual | Presenting on Tuesdays |
| Remote | 2 | casual | Video calls, so the top half matters |
| Weekend | 4 | casual | Park, kids, errands |
| Evenings out | 2 | dressy casual | |

The capsule's proportions should follow this. Weekend clothes for 4 days in 14 shouldn't make up half the wardrobe.

## Step 2 — Palette

From the pieces they love and wear most (inventory, worn-lately column):

- **Base neutrals (2–3):** used for most bottoms, outerwear and shoes, such as navy, grey and cream.
- **Accents (2–3):** colours they feel good in, used in tops and accessories, such as rust and olive.
- **Odd ones out:** pieces whose colour matches nothing else. Name them. They're often why getting dressed feels hard.

The quick check: lay out every bottom and every top. Can most tops go with most bottoms? If not, the palette is the problem, not the number of clothes.

## Step 3 — Shop the closet

Fill each category from what's already owned and fits:

| Category | Pieces (from inventory) | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Bottoms | navy trousers, grey trousers, dark jeans, black skirt | 4 |
| Tops | white shirt, blue shirt, 2 knits, 3 tees | 7 |
| Layers | navy blazer, grey cardigan | 2 |
| Outerwear | camel coat, rain jacket | 2 |
| Shoes | loafers, white trainers, ankle boots | 3 |

## Step 4 — Outfit formulas

Write repeatable patterns, not individual outfits:

- **Office default:** trousers + shirt + loafers (+ blazer to present).
- **Office relaxed:** dark jeans + knit + ankle boots.
- **Remote:** jeans + tee + cardigan.
- **Weekend:** jeans + tee + trainers + rain jacket.
- **Evening:** black skirt + knit + boots.

Aim for one formula for each context in Step 1, and two for the most common context.

## Step 5 — Combination maths

Count for morale, roughly. Multiply the compatible options within a formula:

- Office default: 2 trousers × 2 shirts × 1 shoe = 4 outfits (8 with or without the blazer).
- Office relaxed: 1 jeans × 2 knits × 2 shoes (boots or loafers) = 4.
- Weekend: 1 jeans × 3 tees × 2 shoes = 6.

Across these 18 pieces that's more than twenty distinct outfits. Show the user three or four examples, not the whole list.

## Step 6 — Gap ranking

For each gap, ask how many formulas or outfits it unlocks:

| Gap | Unlocks | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Second pair of smart trousers (a neutral, not navy or grey) | +4 office outfits, and helps on laundry days | High |
| Dressier shoe for evenings | Evening formula, plus office presenting | Medium |
| Statement jacket | Mostly a joy buy | Low (allowed, and named as one) |

Hand the top gaps to gap shopping, with the formulas each one has to fit.

## Worked example: the "nothing to wear" closet

A user has 60 tops and still has "nothing to wear". The inventory shows 8 bottoms, but 5 of them are light-coloured and need specific tops. Most of the tops are patterned in colours that clash with those bottoms. The fix isn't more tops. One pair of dark neutral trousers and one pair of dark jeans make about 30 of the existing tops wearable. Those two pieces are the whole shopping list.

## Common mistakes

- Starting from a "40-piece capsule" list online instead of the user's own life and closet.
- Building around trend colours they never wear.
- Ignoring shoes. They're often the piece that doesn't go.
- Counting pieces that don't fit today. Use the fit-today column honestly.
- Seasonal climates: build a capsule for each season with shared core pieces, not one for the whole year.
