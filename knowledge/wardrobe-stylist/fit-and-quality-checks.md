# Fit and quality checks

Fit matters more than price or label. A well-fitting inexpensive piece usually looks better than a poorly fitting expensive one. Talk about fit as something the garment does ("the sleeve ends past the wrist bone"), never as something wrong with the body.

## Fit checkpoints

**Tops, shirts and jackets**
- [ ] **Shoulder seam** sits at the edge of the shoulder bone (unless it's a deliberately dropped-shoulder style).
- [ ] **No pulling** across the chest or bust. Buttons lie flat with no gaps.
- [ ] **Sleeves** end around the wrist bone with arms relaxed. A shirt cuff can show a little under a jacket.
- [ ] **Moving test:** reach forward and raise both arms. Can you move without strain, and does it stay in place?
- [ ] **Jacket collar** sits against the neck without a gap at the back.

**Trousers and skirts**
- [ ] **Waist** stays put without a belt doing all the work, and sits flat without digging in.
- [ ] **Seat and thighs:** no horizontal pulling lines, and no fabric pooling.
- [ ] **Rise** is comfortable when sitting.
- [ ] **Length** suits the shoes they'll mostly be worn with. Try them on with those shoes.
- [ ] **Sitting test:** sit, crouch and climb a step.

**Shoes**
- [ ] Try them on later in the day if possible, wearing the socks or hosiery you'll actually use.
- [ ] Room for the toes, and no heel slip when walking.
- [ ] Walk on a hard floor, not only on carpet.

## What a tailor can and can't easily fix

| Usually easy and affordable | Possible but costly | Usually not worth it |
|---|---|---|
| Hemming trousers, skirts and sleeves (unless there are vents or buttons) | Taking in the waist or seat | Shoulders that are too wide or too narrow |
| Taking in a shirt's sides | Shortening jacket sleeves with working buttons | Making something much larger |
| Replacing buttons | Reshaping a jacket's body | Adding length where there's no seam allowance |

Rule: if it fits in the shoulders (tops) or the seat (trousers), alterations can usually handle the rest. Get a real price from a local tailor before deciding between keeping and releasing, and compare it with the cost of a replacement.

## Quality signals by garment type

These are signals, not guarantees. Check several together.

**Knitwear**
- Dense knit that springs back after a gentle stretch.
- Seams joined neatly rather than cut and overlocked, on better pieces.
- Fibre content: check the label. Natural fibres and blends behave differently. Some pill more than others, regardless of price.

**Shirts and woven tops**
- Pattern matching at seams and pockets, on striped or checked fabrics.
- Small, even stitches with no loose threads.
- Buttonholes stitched cleanly, and buttons sewn on firmly.

**Trousers and jeans**
- Generous seam allowances (useful for alterations).
- Reinforced stress points: pocket corners and the crotch seam.
- A zip that runs smoothly and lies flat.

**Outerwear**
- A full lining where expected, sewn in with some ease so it doesn't pull.
- A sturdy zip and firm buttons. Check whether a spare button is supplied.
- For rainwear, check whether it's labelled waterproof or only water-resistant, and look for taped seams.

**Shoes**
- Even stitching, and soles attached cleanly with no glue showing.
- Check whether they can be resoled, if that matters to the user.

## The keep, alter or release decision

1. Does it fit today, or can it be fixed easily (see the table)?
2. Do they like wearing it, honestly?
3. Does it go with at least three things in the capsule?
4. If it needs work, is the repair cost worth it compared with replacing it?

Two or more "no" answers: put it on the release list (sell, donate or recycle) without guilt.

## Worked example

A user loves a wool coat, but it hasn't been worn in two winters because "the sleeves are long". The shoulders fit, and the sleeves have no working buttons. That's a straightforward hem. The tailor's quote is a fraction of the cost of a replacement coat. Put it on the alteration list and fold it back into the capsule.

## Common mistakes

- Buying the size on the label instead of the fit. Sizing varies between brands.
- Trying on trousers without the shoes they'll be worn with.
- Treating price as proof of quality.
- Keeping "one day" pieces that need alterations they'll never book.
