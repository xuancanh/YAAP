# Dress codes decoded

Dress codes are conventions, not laws, and they shift with country, region, venue and the host's culture. Use the table as a starting point. The best source is always the host, the invitation's wording, or photos from the same event in earlier years. Respect religious and cultural dress requirements and any personal or gender-expression preferences. Offer options, not rules.

## Common codes, from most to least formal

| Code | Typical meaning (Western conventions) |
|---|---|
| White tie | The most formal. Tailcoat, white waistcoat and bow tie, or a full-length formal gown. Rare: state events and some balls. |
| Black tie | Dinner suit or tuxedo with a bow tie, or a floor-length or elegant cocktail dress, or formal separates. Evening events. |
| Black tie optional / creative black tie | Black tie welcome, but a dark suit and tie, or a dressy cocktail outfit, also works. "Creative" invites some personality: colour, texture, statement pieces. |
| Cocktail / semi-formal | A suit (a tie is usual), or a knee-to-midi dress or smart dressy separates. |
| Business formal | A dark suit and tie, or a suit or tailored dress. |
| Business / smart casual | No suit needed. Tailored trousers or a skirt, a collared shirt or a smart knit, a blazer optional, and clean, smart shoes. Meaning varies widely between industries. |
| Dressy casual | A step up from everyday: nice trousers or dark jeans (if the setting allows), a smart top or a casual dress. |
| Casual | Everyday clothes, clean and in good condition. Check the activity and the venue. |
| Themed | Follow the theme as far as you're comfortable. A small nod is fine. |

## Decoding an ambiguous code

Ask these in order:

1. **What does the host say?** Re-read the invitation, the event site or the FAQ. Ask the host or a fellow guest. Asking is normal.
2. **When and where is it?** An evening event in a hotel ballroom is dressier than a lunchtime one in a garden. A beach venue changes the shoes.
3. **What's the culture of the group?** "Smart casual" at a startup and at a law firm are different things. Look at photos from earlier events if you can.
4. **Are there religious or cultural norms?** Covered shoulders or heads at some places of worship. Colours to avoid at certain weddings or funerals in some cultures (for example, white at many Western weddings). Research the specific tradition, or ask.
5. **Weather and practicality:** grass (heels sink), stairs, a long day on your feet, outdoor temperature after dark.

When unsure, go slightly more formal. A jacket can come off, but you can't add one you didn't bring.

## Weddings: extra points

- Don't upstage the couple. The usual Western convention is to avoid white, ivory or anything bridal-looking unless the couple asks for it. Other traditions have their own colour norms (for example, red is traditional for brides in several cultures). Check.
- Ceremony and reception may have different codes. Plan a layer for the change.
- Order alterations early. Book them at least two weeks out, and more for complex work.

## High-stakes outfit checklist

- [ ] Code decoded, with the source noted (the invitation's wording or the host's reply)
- [ ] Outfit chosen from the capsule first, with gaps flagged early
- [ ] Full try-on at least the night before, including shoes, underlayers and accessories
- [ ] Movement test: sit, walk and (if relevant) dance
- [ ] Weather plan: a coat or wrap, and an umbrella
- [ ] Backup outfit identified
- [ ] Kit: plasters (band-aids), a stain pen, safety pins
- [ ] Notes in style-notes.md afterwards: "felt great" or "never again", and why

## Worked example

An invitation reads "Garden party, 3pm, smart casual — lawn" in June.

- **Decoded:** daytime, outdoors, on grass, and smart rather than formal.
- **Outfit:** light trousers or a midi dress, a breathable shirt or top, a light blazer or cardigan for later, and block heels, loafers or smart flats (no stilettos on grass).
- **Backup:** a packable rain layer.

Note the decision and the source in week.md.

## Common mistakes

- Reading "casual" on a wedding invitation as "jeans". Check with the couple.
- Trying the outfit on for the first time on the day.
- Forgetting the shoes and the venue's ground.
- Assuming one culture's norms apply to another culture's ceremony.
