# Care labels and garment care

The care label tells you the gentlest safe method for the whole garment. In the US, the FTC's Care Labeling Rule requires most clothing to carry at least one safe care instruction (washing or dry cleaning), plus warnings about things that would damage it. In Europe and many other countries, the symbols follow the GINETEX/ISO system. When the symbols and the words disagree, follow the words. When in doubt, choose the gentler option.

## The five symbol families

| Symbol | Means | Key variations |
|---|---|---|
| Wash tub | Washing | A number is the maximum water temperature in °C. Dots are used on some labels (more dots mean hotter). A hand in the tub means hand wash. A cross means do not wash. One bar underneath means a gentler cycle, and two bars a very gentle (wool or delicates) cycle. |
| Triangle | Bleaching | Empty: any bleach. Two diagonal lines: only oxygen or non-chlorine bleach. Crossed out: do not bleach. |
| Square | Drying | A circle inside means tumble dry: one dot for low heat, two for normal, crossed out for do not tumble dry. Lines inside mean air-drying methods (line dry, dry flat, drip dry). |
| Iron | Ironing | One dot for low heat, two for medium, three for high. Crossed out means do not iron. |
| Circle | Professional cleaning | Letters tell the cleaner which process to use. A W in a circle means professional wet cleaning. Crossed out means do not dry clean. |

## Care habits that make clothes last

- **Wash less often** where hygiene allows. Air out knitwear and outer layers between wears.
- **Wash colder** when the label allows it and the clothes aren't heavily soiled.
- **Turn inside out** for prints, dark colours and knits.
- **Close zips and empty pockets.** Use mesh bags for delicates.
- **Don't overload the machine.** Clothes need room to move to get clean.
- **Dry flat for knitwear.** Hanging it wet stretches it.
- **Use a shaver or comb for pilling** instead of pulling the bobbles off.
- **Use shoe trees and rotate shoes**, giving each pair time to dry out between wears.
- **Mend early.** A loose button or a small seam split is a five-minute fix now and a bigger job later.

## Seasonal storage

1. **Clean before storing.** Food and body residue attract moths and set stains.
2. **Store dry, cool and dark.** Breathable cotton bags or boxes. Avoid sealed plastic for natural fibres over long periods.
3. **Fold knitwear**, and hang structured coats and jackets on shaped hangers.
4. **Deter moths:** clean clothes, regular checks and good airflow are the basics. Cedar is a common deterrent, but not a guarantee.
5. **Keep a storage note** in closet.md: what's stored, where, and any care note ("coat — needs button resewn before November").

## The care tax (for cost-per-wear)

Running costs are part of what a piece costs. Note them when shopping:

| Care need | Lifestyle cost |
|---|---|
| Machine wash, tumble dry | Lowest effort |
| Machine wash, dry flat or hang | Space and time to dry |
| Hand wash only | Time, every wash |
| Dry clean only | A recurring cost and trips to the cleaner |
| Iron every wear | Time, every wear |

Someone who never irons should hesitate over a shirt that needs ironing, however good it looks. Say so kindly at the checkout stage.

## Common mistakes

- Washing everything on one cycle and temperature.
- Tumble-drying knitwear or anything labelled "dry flat".
- Storing clothes that haven't been washed.
- Ignoring "wash separately" warnings on new dark denim or bright colours.
