# Renewal timeline

Renewals are won in the year before them and lost in the last month. Work backwards from the renewal date and the contract's notice period, whichever comes first.

## Find the real deadline

Before anything else, read the contract (or ask the deal desk) for:

- **Renewal date** and term length
- **Notice period** for non-renewal or changes, if any
- **Auto-renewal** clause, if any, and its conditions
- **Price uplift** terms, if any
- **Who signs** on the customer's side and their procurement steps

The working deadline is the earliest of these. A contract with a notice period effectively renews earlier than its end date.

## Timeline

**120 days out — check the ground**
- Health score current and explained
- Success plan goals reviewed: are they being met?
- Stakeholder map updated: sponsor, users, economic buyer, procurement
- Flag any red or amber signal to your manager now

**90 days out — build the case**
- Value story written with the customer's own metrics (see [business review structure](business-review-structure.md))
- Risks listed with owners
- Commercial path agreed with your manager: flat, uplift, expansion or right-size
- Expansion opportunities handed to sales if that is the company's process

**60 days out — have the conversation**
- Renewal discussion held with the sponsor; objections written down word for word
- Procurement and legal steps known, with their lead times
- Proposal or order form requested from the deal desk

**30 days out — close**
- Paperwork with the customer
- Daily check on open items
- If not signed by the working deadline, escalate with a written status

## Renewal file template

```
Account / ARR / renewal date / working deadline
Health: band, score, one-line reason
Value delivered: 2–3 outcomes with metrics and sources
Stakeholders: name, role, stance (supporter / neutral / blocker)
Risks: risk, evidence, owner, action, date
Commercial path: proposed, approved by, date
Next step: what, who, when
```

## Forecasting renewals

Give every renewal a category your manager can roll up:

- **Commit**: verbal yes from the economic buyer, paperwork in motion
- **Likely**: healthy, no objection raised, conversation not yet held
- **At risk**: open save plan or unresolved objection
- **Expected churn**: notice given or decision communicated

Move a renewal between categories only with a written reason.

## Traps

- **Treating auto-renewal as safe.** Customers can still give notice, and an unhappy auto-renewal becomes next year's churn.
- **Starting with price.** Start with value; price is a later conversation.
- **One contact.** If your only contact leaves at 45 days, you have no renewal. Multi-thread from the 120-day mark.
- **Promising terms.** Credits, discounts and price caps go to your manager first, every time.
