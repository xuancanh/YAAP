# Business review structure

A business review is a meeting about the customer's goals, not a product demo or a ticket report. If the customer's sponsor would not forward the deck to their own boss, it is not done.

## QBR vs EBR

- **QBR** (quarterly business review): working-level owners. Progress against the success plan, adoption, issues, next quarter's milestones.
- **EBR** (executive business review): executive sponsor and their boss. Business outcomes, strategic direction, the next year. Shorter, fewer numbers, more decisions.

## Outline template

1. **Their goals, in their words** (1 slide). Copy the success plan goals. Confirm they still hold.
2. **Results against those goals** (1–2 slides). The metric, the baseline, where it is now, the source.
3. **What made the difference** (1 slide). The features, practices or teams that drove the result. Credit their people.
4. **What is in the way** (1 slide). Adoption gaps, open issues and what each side is doing about them. Be candid; they already know.
5. **Recommendations** (1 slide). Two or three specific actions with an expected effect.
6. **Next period's plan** (1 slide). Milestones, owners on both sides, dates.
7. **Asks** (1 slide). What you need from them (a sponsor, access, a reference) and what they need from you.

Keep usage charts in an appendix; bring them out if someone asks.

## The value story method

Turn activity into outcomes with a simple chain:

> **Because** [capability they adopted] **they** [changed behaviour] **which** [moved their metric] **from X to Y** [source, dates].

Example: "Because the support team moved to shared queues, tickets are answered by whoever is free, which cut first response time from about a day to a few hours (their helpdesk export, June–September)."

If you cannot complete the chain, you have found the adoption gap for slide 4.

## Prep checklist (two weeks out)

- [ ] Success plan re-read; goals and owners still current
- [ ] Metrics pulled with source and date; customer-owned metrics requested from them
- [ ] Health score and recent changes explained in one line
- [ ] Open tickets and their status from support
- [ ] Commercial context from the account file (renewal date, seats, any open asks) — for your eyes, not the deck
- [ ] Attendees confirmed, including a decision-maker for EBRs
- [ ] Pre-read sent, so the meeting is for discussion

## Running it

- Open by asking what has changed on their side since the last review.
- Spend most of the time on slides 4–6.
- End with the agreed actions read back, with owners and dates.
- Send the summary the same day; update the success plan and the account file.

## Pitfalls

- **Feature parade.** New features belong only if they solve a goal on slide 1.
- **Our metrics, not theirs.** Logins mean nothing to a CFO; hours saved or revenue protected do.
- **Surprises.** Never reveal a price change or a renewal ask in the room. Those need their own conversation, planned with your manager.
- **No asks.** A review with no actions is a status meeting. Leave with at least one commitment from each side.
