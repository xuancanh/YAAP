# Churn risk playbook

Most churn has a cause you could have seen months earlier. Your job is to name it early, pick the action that fits the cause and escalate before the customer has decided.

## Early warning signals

Treat any of these as a reason for an unscheduled health check:

- Executive sponsor or champion leaves, changes role or goes quiet
- Usage of the core workflow falls for several weeks in a row
- The customer is acquired, merges or announces cost cuts
- A competitor's name comes up in calls or tickets
- Repeated escalations on the same issue
- Procurement asks for contract terms, notice periods or data export
- Business reviews get cancelled or delegated downwards

## Root cause categories

Write the save plan against the root cause, not the symptom. Most cases fall in one of these:

| Root cause | What it looks like | Typical actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Never reached value** | low adoption since onboarding, success plan vague | re-run a short onboarding for the key workflow; reset goals with a new owner |
| **Lost sponsor** | champion left, no one owns the tool | map new stakeholders; get an intro through remaining users; executive outreach |
| **Product gap** | a missing capability blocks a key use | confirm with product what exists or is planned; workaround; honest answer if not |
| **Quality problems** | outages, bugs, slow support | incident review with the customer; named escalation path; fix dates from engineering only |
| **Budget or business change** | cuts, merger, strategy shift | right-size options prepared with your manager; show cost of switching |
| **Competitive displacement** | evaluating alternatives | value story with their own data; involve sales leadership |

Often there are two causes. Name the primary one and treat it first.

## The five whys, briefly

Ask "why" until you reach something the company can act on. "Usage dropped" → "the team stopped using reports" → "the reports don't match their new process" → "their process changed after the restructure and no one reconfigured it". The last answer is the root cause; the fix is a configuration session, not a discount.

## Save plan essentials

A save plan is short and dated:

1. **Account, ARR, renewal date, current band**
2. **Root cause** with the evidence
3. **Goal**: what "saved" means (renewed at current ARR, renewed with reduction, or a managed exit)
4. **Actions** with owner and date, both sides
5. **Help needed** from your manager, sales, product or engineering
6. **Review date**: weekly until resolved

## Escalation rules

- Escalate the same week the account turns red. Late escalation is the most common reason saves fail.
- Bring the evidence and a recommendation, not just the alarm.
- Commercial concessions are your manager's decision. Model the options; do not mention them to the customer.

## When a save is not possible

Sometimes the right outcome is a clean exit. Make it graceful: help with data export, record the honest reason in the churn log and keep the relationship. Churn reasons feed product and the health score calibration, so be specific ("missing SSO for their new identity provider", not "product fit").
