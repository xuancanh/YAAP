# Car seat checks

A car seat is one of the few baby items that's a genuine safety device. The guidance below is from the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), checked November 2026. **Car seat laws and approval standards are country-specific** (for example, the UK and EU use their own approval standards and labels). Check the family's national rules, and always follow the seat's and the car's manuals.

## Core guidance (NHTSA)

- **Rear-facing as long as possible,** up to the top height or weight limit set by the seat's manufacturer. Children under 1 should always ride rear-facing.
- **Back seat.** Never put a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag.
- **Register the seat** with the manufacturer (card in the box or online) so you're told about recalls.
- **Replace after a moderate or severe crash.** NHTSA says a seat doesn't automatically need replacing after a *minor* crash, which it defines as: the car could be driven away, the door nearest the seat wasn't damaged, nobody was hurt, airbags didn't deploy, and the seat has no visible damage. If in doubt, follow the manufacturer's instructions.

## Buying checklist

- [ ] Fits the baby's weight and height from birth (infant-only or convertible)
- [ ] Fits the car: check the car's manual and the seat maker's vehicle fit list; try it in the car before buying if possible
- [ ] Fits the family's routine: is it carried to and from the car, or stays installed?
- [ ] Meets the family's national safety standard
- [ ] Expiry date: seats have one; note it in the gear file

## Used or hand-me-down seat (NHTSA checklist)

- [ ] Never in a moderate or severe crash. Only accept one if you know its full history.
- [ ] Label with model number and date of manufacture is readable (needed for recall and expiry checks)
- [ ] Instruction manual included (or downloadable)
- [ ] Not past its expiry date
- [ ] No recalls on the model
- [ ] No cracks, missing parts or frayed straps

If any box is unticked, don't use it.

## Installation

Installation mistakes are easy to make and hard to spot yourself. Help is available:

- Follow both manuals: the seat's and the car's.
- In the US, certified Child Passenger Safety technicians check installations, often free; NHTSA lists inspection stations.
- In other countries, retailers and road-safety organisations often offer fitting checks.
- Practise the install before the due date.

## Quick checks every trip

- Harness straps snug: you can't pinch a fold of strap at the shoulder.
- Chest clip (where used) at armpit level.
- No bulky coats under the harness; use a blanket over the straps instead.
- The seat doesn't move more than about an inch at the belt path (NHTSA's install tip); follow the seat's own guide.

## Common mistakes

- Turning forward-facing early because legs look cramped.
- Buying a second-hand seat online with no history.
- Not registering the seat, so recall notices never arrive.
- Leaving a sleeping baby in the car seat after the journey.
