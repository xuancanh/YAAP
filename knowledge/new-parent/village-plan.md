# Village plan

Most people want to help and don't know how. Clear asks get better help than "let us know if you need anything".

## Visitor ground rules

Agree these before the birth, then send one message to family and friends.

```
We're so happy to share the news. For the first few weeks:
- Please message before visiting; we may say "not today".
- Visits are short: about an hour.
- Please don't come if you're unwell, even mildly.
- Wash hands when you arrive.
- The best gift is food or a chore. We'll share a list.
Thank you for understanding — we can't wait for you to meet them.
```

Add any rules from the family's midwife or paediatrician (some advise specific vaccinations for close contacts; that's their call, not the agent's).

## Meal train

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Dates | Start from the first week home; two or three weeks is common |
| Drop-off window | Pick a time that suits naps, e.g. 17:00–18:00 |
| Doorstep or visit | Doorstep by default |
| Dietary needs | Allergies, vegetarian, foods the breastfeeding parent has been advised to avoid (as told by their clinician) |
| Preferences | Freezable meals in disposable or labelled containers; easy one-handed food |
| Contact | One coordinator who isn't the parents |

Send the volunteer a short brief: date, address, drop-off time, dietary needs, "label with what it is and the date".

## Help rota (practical tasks)

People like a specific job. Offer a menu:

- Walk the dog
- School run for older siblings
- An hour of laundry
- A grocery shop from the list
- Sitting with the baby so the parents can sleep or shower
- Cleaning the bathroom and kitchen
- Lifts to appointments

Keep a shared list: task, who, when, done.

## Scripts

**Asking:** "Could you bring dinner on Thursday? We'd love it if you leave it at the door — we're not up for visitors yet."

**Declining a visit:** "We're taking it slow this week. Could we do a video call instead, and plan a visit when we're more rested?"

**Redirecting advice:** "Thanks — our health visitor is guiding us on that."

**When a relative is staying:** agree jobs up front (cooking, laundry, older children), not just baby-holding.

## Return to work and childcare

Start research well before the return date; good childcare often has waiting lists.

- Options: nursery or daycare, childminder, nanny, family, a mix.
- Questions for each: hours, cost (including what's charged for sick days and holidays), ratios, settling-in period, notice period, any government childcare support the family may qualify for. Support schemes are **country-specific**; check the official site.
- Plan a settling-in period before the first full day back.

## Common mistakes

- No coordinator, so the parents end up managing the help.
- Saying yes to visits out of guilt.
- Meal trains with no dietary info.
- Starting childcare research a month before going back.
