# Safe sleep reference

Use this when the family is setting up the nursery or researching sleep products. It's quoted from health agencies, checked November 2026; follow the family's own health visitor or paediatrician, who may give advice specific to their baby (for example, a premature baby). Questions about a specific baby's sleep go to them.

## The core guidance (NHS and AAP agree)

- **On the back for every sleep,** day and night. If the baby rolls, the NHS advises moving them back onto their back until they can roll both ways on their own.
- **Firm, flat, non-inclined surface** in a cot, crib, Moses basket or bassinet. The AAP's 2022 update says inclined sleep surfaces are not safe for infant sleep.
- **Clear sleep space:** nothing that could cover the face or head. No pillows, quilts, duvets, bumpers or soft toys. The AAP also lists weighted blankets and weighted sleepers as unsafe.
- **Same room as the parents, separate sleep surface.** The NHS advises this for every sleep for at least the first 6 months; the AAP also recommends room-sharing, ideally for at least 6 months.
- **Feet to foot** (NHS): baby's feet at the end of the cot so they can't wriggle under covers.
- **Don't overheat.** The NHS suggests a room temperature of 16–20°C, and no hot water bottles, electric blankets or heaters near the baby.
- **Smoke-free** in pregnancy and around the baby.
- **Never sleep with the baby on a sofa or armchair.**

## When bed-sharing is particularly risky (NHS)

The NHS says don't share a bed if the baby was born before 37 weeks or weighed under 2.5 kg (5.5 lb), or if either parent smokes, has drunk alcohol, or has taken drugs or medicine that makes them drowsy. The AAP doesn't recommend bed-sharing at all. Agencies differ in how they frame this; give the family both and point them to their own health visitor or paediatrician.

## Other points

- **Car seats are for travel,** not sleep. The NHS advises not leaving babies in car seats longer than needed once you arrive.
- Premature babies: the NHS advises following safe sleep advice for a year from the due date.
- The AAP says home heart and breathing monitors shouldn't be relied on to reduce sudden infant death risk.

## Gear-check checklist (before buying)

- [ ] Does the product put the baby on a flat, firm surface? Avoid anything that inclines or props.
- [ ] Is the mattress the right size for the cot, with no gaps at the edges?
- [ ] Any padding, bumpers or soft sides? Leave them out.
- [ ] Second-hand cot: complete, no missing parts, no recalls. Check the national product-safety recall list. A new mattress is commonly recommended.
- [ ] Sleeping bags: the right size and the right warmth (tog) for the room; no hood.
- [ ] Ignore marketing claims that a product "prevents SIDS". Ask the health visitor or paediatrician instead.

## Common mistakes

- Buying a cot bumper or nest because it looks cosy.
- Letting the baby sleep in a swing, bouncer or lounger.
- Putting a hat on a baby for sleep indoors.
- Treating "everyone does it" as safety advice.
