# Recalls and the history file

## Safety recalls

A safety recall is a manufacturer's fix for a defect that affects safety. In many countries the repair is free at an authorized dealer. Recalls go unrepaired surprisingly often, especially on second-hand cars, because notices go to the registered owner at the time.

**How to check (country-specific; checked 2026-11-04):**

- **United States:** NHTSA's lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls takes the 17-character VIN and shows unrepaired safety recalls. Recall repairs are free at authorized dealers. NHTSA also offers recall alerts by email.
- **United Kingdom:** the GOV.UK recall checker takes the registration number (or make, model and year) and shows unfixed safety recalls; it also covers parts and accessories such as child car seats and tyres. GOV.UK says you will not usually have to pay for recall repairs.
- **Elsewhere:** search for the national vehicle safety regulator's recall database, and check the manufacturer's owner site with the VIN.

**Where to find the VIN:** usually on the registration document, at the base of the windscreen on the driver's side, and on the driver's door pillar.

**When to check:** at setup, when buying a used car, once a year, and when a recall makes the news for the model.

**Booking the repair:** call a franchised dealer for the brand with the VIN and recall number. Ask how long it takes and whether parts are in stock. Afterwards, file the dealer's paperwork and re-run the check.

A lookup that shows nothing is good news but not a guarantee. Coverage of older recalls varies, and the manufacturer's own system is the second check.

## The history file

A documented history shows a buyer the car was looked after and helps a mechanic diagnose problems faster. Keep one file per vehicle.

**Identity block**

```
Make / model / year / engine / transmission:
VIN:            Registration:
Bought: date, price, mileage, from whom
Insurance renewal:      Registration/tax renewal:      Inspection due:
Tyres: size, brand, fitted date and mileage per axle
Timing: belt or chain; last change date and mileage (with receipt)
Known quirks: [issue, diagnosis, date]
```

**Service log** (one line per event)

| Date | Mileage | Work done | Parts/fluids | Cost | Who | Receipt filed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-12 | 41,200 | Annual service, brake fluid | OEM filter, DOT 4 | 280 | Main St Garage | yes |

Also log: recalls done, inspections passed or failed (with advisories), tyres, batteries, accident repairs, and warranty claims.

## Gaps and honesty

A car that arrives with missing history gets a file anyway. Write down what is known, what is missing, and what was assumed (for example, "timing belt history unknown, treated as due"). At sale time, honest gaps are better than claims you can't back up.

## Common mistakes

- Receipts in a glovebox instead of a file; photos of them are fine.
- Logging work without mileage.
- Skipping the recall check on a used purchase.
- Forgetting advisories, so "pads at 30%" becomes a surprise.
