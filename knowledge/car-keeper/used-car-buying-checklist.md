# Used car buying checklist

Buy on total cost and condition, not sticker price. Paperwork rules and history-check services differ by country; research the local ones and verify before money changes hands.

Insurance quotes vary a lot between models; get them for the shortlist before viewing.

## 2. Before viewing

- [ ] Price range researched from several sources for this year, mileage and trim (note dates).
- [ ] Known problem years and common faults for the model.
- [ ] Recall check by VIN or registration (see [recalls and the history file](recalls-and-history-file.md)).
- [ ] Official history or title check where your country has one (for outstanding finance, write-offs, theft, mileage records).
- [ ] Questions sent: service history? timing belt done? accidents? number of owners? why selling?

## 3. Viewing walk-around

- Panel gaps and paint colour consistent; signs of repair.
- Tyres: matching brands, even wear (uneven wear can hint at alignment or suspension issues), age.
- Under the bonnet (engine cold): leaks, oil level and condition, coolant level.
- Inside: wear matches mileage (pedals, wheel, seat), all electrics, air-con, windows, locks.
- Warning lights come on at ignition and go out after start.
- VIN on the car matches the documents.

## 4. Test drive

- Start from cold (ask the seller not to warm it up). Listen for rattles or knocks.
- Town driving, a fast road, and full-lock turns both ways in a car park.
- Brakes: straight stop, no pull, no grinding.
- Gears: smooth changes, no slipping; clutch bite not at the very top.
- Steering: no wander, no clunks over bumps.
- Afterwards: look for new leaks and warning lights.

For anything expensive, pay for an independent pre-purchase inspection. It costs little compared with a hidden fault.

## 5. Paperwork

- [ ] Seller's identity matches the registration document.
- [ ] Service records match the mileage history.
- [ ] Outstanding finance cleared (or settled as part of sale).
- [ ] Transfer process for your jurisdiction understood (who notifies the registry, by when).

## 6. Payment safety

- Don't pay a deposit for a car you haven't seen, and be wary of sellers who won't meet at their address.
- Use a traceable payment method. For sellers: keys go only after funds have fully cleared, not when a payment "shows as pending".
- Get a dated receipt with both names, the VIN, price and "sold as seen" terms if used.

## Selling: quick list

- Compile the history file and receipts into a sale pack.
- Value from comparable sold or listed cars, adjusted honestly for condition.
- Listing: facts, service highlights, known faults, clear photos (all sides, interior, odometer, tyres).
- Test drives: check the driver's licence and insurance position under local rules, and go with them.
- Payment cleared before keys; complete the official transfer promptly.
