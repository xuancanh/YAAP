# Reading a service schedule

The manufacturer's schedule for the exact model, engine and year is the source of truth. It's in the owner's handbook or service booklet, and often on the manufacturer's owner website. Generic "every 3,000 miles" rules and garage window stickers are not.

## Two clocks

Most items are due at a mileage **or** a time, "whichever comes first". Both matter:

- A low-mileage car still needs time-based items. Oil ages, brake fluid absorbs moisture, coolant and rubber parts degrade.
- A high-mileage car hits mileage items early in the year.

To date an item: estimate monthly mileage from the file (two odometer readings a few months apart), then work out when the mileage limit falls and compare with the time limit. Use the earlier date.

## Normal versus severe service

Many handbooks have two schedules. The "severe" or "special operating conditions" one usually applies to things like lots of short trips, stop-start city driving, towing, dusty roads or extreme temperatures. Many everyday cars fit the severe definition. Read the handbook's definition and pick honestly.

## Items worth flagging

| Item | Why it matters | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Timing belt (cambelt) | On many engines, a snapped belt can wreck the engine | Belt or chain? If belt, the interval in miles and years, and when it was last done (receipt). Unknown history on a used car: treat as due and ask a mechanic |
| Oil and filter | Engine wear | Interval, oil specification (grade and approval code) |
| Brake fluid | Absorbs water over time, which affects braking | Usually time-based |
| Coolant | Corrosion and overheating | Often long intervals; type matters |
| Spark plugs, air and cabin filters | Running and air quality | Mileage-based |
| Auxiliary belt, hoses | Failure strands you | Inspection at services |
| Tyres | Grip and braking | Pressure monthly; tread and age per handbook and local law |

Timing chains are often designed to last much longer than belts, but the handbook still says what to watch for.

## Spotting upsells

Ask: "Where is this in the manufacturer's schedule, and at what mileage?" Common extras to question:

- Fluid "flushes" not in the schedule or well ahead of the interval.
- Fuel system or engine cleaning treatments.
- Replacing parts "while we're in there" without a measurement (pad thickness, tread depth) or a photo.

Items found on inspection are different: a worn part is worn whatever the schedule says. Ask for the measurement, a photo, or to see the old part, and an itemized quote before work starts.

## Garage visit brief

```
Car: [make model year engine] · Mileage: [ ] · VIN on file
Due per schedule: [items, with handbook reference]
Not yet due (please don't do): [items]
Known quirks: [e.g. rattle diagnosed harmless, date]
Please: call before any work outside this list; itemized quote; keep old parts
```

## After the visit

- Log date, mileage, work done, parts, cost and who did it.
- Turn advisories ("rear pads at 4 mm", "tyre wear on inside edge") into dated re-check reminders.
- Recompute the next due dates.

## Warranty note

New-car warranties may require servicing to the manufacturer's schedule with specified parts and fluids, and records to prove it. Rules differ by country; check the warranty booklet before using an independent garage, and keep every receipt.
