# Symptom triage

Severity first, causes second. You're helping the owner decide how urgently to get help and what to tell the garage. You're not diagnosing or guiding repairs.

## Stop driving: pull over when safe, switch off, get recovery

- Brakes feel soft, sink to the floor, pull hard to one side, or a red brake warning comes on while driving.
- Steering suddenly heavy, loose, or the car wanders.
- Red oil-pressure or red temperature warning while driving.
- Smell of fuel, visible smoke, or burning smell with smoke.
- A wheel feels loose, heavy vibration starts suddenly, or a tyre is visibly flat or damaged.
- Airbag or seatbelt warning after a crash.
- Any warning the handbook says means stop.

Get people out and away from traffic if stopped on a fast road; follow local roadside safety advice. Call roadside assistance or emergency services as appropriate. Don't "nurse it home".

## Warning light colours

Many manufacturers follow a traffic-light convention, but the handbook for the exact model is the authority:

- **Red:** serious or safety-related; stop safely and check the handbook.
- **Amber or yellow:** something needs attention soon; drive gently and book a check.
- **Green or blue:** a system is on (lights, cruise control).

A **flashing** engine warning often signals a more serious fault than a steady one; many handbooks advise reducing speed and getting it checked promptly. Look it up for the model.

## Narrowing questions (for everything else)

- When does it happen: cold start, warm, braking, turning, accelerating, over bumps, at a certain speed?
- What does it sound or feel like: squeal, grind, knock, hum, clunk, vibration through wheel or seat?
- Where does it seem to come from: front, back, left, right, under the bonnet?
- What changed recently: service, tyres, fuel, a pothole, weather?
- Is it getting worse?

## Owner-safe checks

Only on level ground, engine off and cool, parking brake on:

- **Oil level:** dipstick, wipe, re-dip, read between the marks.
- **Coolant:** look at the expansion tank level only. Never open a hot cooling system.
- **Washer fluid**, wiper blades, bulbs.
- **Tyre pressures** against the placard (door jamb or fuel flap), cold. **Tread** with a gauge or the tread-wear indicators; the legal minimum depends on where you live.
- **Leaks:** colour and location of drips on a sheet of card overnight.

Stop at anything that needs the wheels off, tools inside systems, or work on brakes, steering, airbags or fuel lines.

## Garage brief template

```
Car: [make model year engine] · Mileage: [ ]
Symptom: [in the owner's words]
Started: [date] · Getting worse? [ ]
When it happens: [conditions]
Warning lights: [which, colour, steady/flashing; photo if possible]
Checked so far: [e.g. oil at max mark, tyre pressures correct]
Recent work: [ ]
Context found: [recalls or known issues for this model, as background only]
Please call with diagnosis and quote before repairs.
```

## Worked example

"Squeal when braking, only in the morning, goes after a few stops, no warning light." This pattern is often surface moisture or rust on discs, but it could be wear indicators. Category: garage soon, not urgent, unless it turns to grinding or braking feels different, which moves it to stop driving. Brief written, check booked, outcome logged to the history file.

## Common mistakes

- Topping up and ignoring why a fluid dropped.
- Clearing a warning code and calling it fixed.
- Treating forum posts as a diagnosis. They are context for the mechanic.
