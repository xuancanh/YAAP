# Consumer and platform rules checklist

General background, checked on 2026-11-04. Laws differ by country and state and change over time, and marketplace policies change often. Before acting, check the current official source and the platform's seller rules. For a specific business question (tax registration, a dispute, product compliance), ask an accountant or lawyer.

## Shipping promises (United States)

The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule:

- Ship within the time you state. If you state no time, ship within 30 days of receiving a properly completed order.
- If you can't ship on time, tell the buyer before the deadline, give a revised date, and explain their right to cancel for a full, prompt refund.
- Have a reasonable basis for any shipping time you advertise.

Practical: keep processing times on listings realistic, update them before peaks, and use a delay message template.

## Reviews (United States, and platform rules everywhere)

The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials took effect on 21 October 2024. Among other things it prohibits:

- Fake reviews and testimonials, including ones from people who didn't use the product.
- Buying positive or negative reviews, or offering incentives conditioned on the review being positive.
- Certain review suppression, such as using threats to stop negative reviews.
- Buying or selling fake social media indicators (followers, views).

Most marketplaces go further and ban any incentive for reviews, and "review gating" (asking only happy customers to review). Check the platform's policy before sending any review request or insert.

Safe defaults: ask every buyer the same neutral way, offer nothing in return, never ask a buyer to change or remove a review in exchange for a refund.

## Returns and cancellations (EU, and UK similar)

For online sales to consumers in the EU:

- Buyers generally have 14 days from delivery to withdraw from the purchase without giving a reason.
- Exceptions include made-to-order or clearly personalised goods, rapidly perishable goods, unsealed audio, video or software, and some digital content.
- If you don't tell buyers about this right, the withdrawal period can extend by up to 12 months.
- Refunds are generally due within 14 days of the buyer telling you they're withdrawing (you may usually wait until the goods are back or proof of return is provided).
- Buyers usually pay return postage, unless you offered to pay or didn't tell them they'd have to.

The UK has a similar 14-day cancellation right under its own consumer contract rules. Faulty goods are a separate right in both places. Other countries have their own rules.

## Sales tax (United States)

- Since *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (June 2018), states can require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax based on economic activity in the state, not just physical presence.
- Each state sets its own thresholds and rules.
- Many states require marketplaces to collect and remit tax on sales made through them, but sellers may still have obligations for sales on their own site.

Ask an accountant which states you must register in. VAT in the EU and UK has its own rules for distance selling and marketplaces.

## Product safety and labelling

Toys, children's products, cosmetics, food, candles, electrical items and textiles often have specific safety, labelling or testing rules. Check the rules for each country you ship to before listing.

## When to get professional help

- Registering for sales tax or VAT, or a tax notice arrives.
- A buyer threatens legal action, or a chargeback dispute is large.
- Product compliance for a regulated category.
- Account suspension appeals involving policy interpretation.
