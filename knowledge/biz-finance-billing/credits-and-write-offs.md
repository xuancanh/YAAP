# Credit notes and write-offs

Each kind of credit has a different cause, different evidence and a different accounting effect. Picking the right one keeps the ledger honest and makes the approval quick.

## Decision guide

1. **Did we bill something wrong?** (price, quantity, period, tax, entity) → **Billing-error credit**, usually followed by a corrected invoice (credit and rebill).
2. **Did we fail to meet a contract commitment that carries a credit?** (an uptime or response-time service level) → **Service credit**, as calculated by the contract.
3. **Was the bill right, but we want to keep the relationship?** → **Goodwill credit**. A commercial decision; needs the account owner's support and manager approval.
4. **Was the bill right, the customer won't or can't pay, and collection has failed?** → **Bad-debt write-off**. A finance decision after the ladder is exhausted.

If none fits, it's probably a dispute that isn't resolved yet. Don't propose a credit to make a dispute go away without knowing who is right.

## Evidence each needs

| Type | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Billing error | The invoice, the contract clause or order form, the usage record, and what the right amount is |
| Service credit | The service level clause, the measured performance (incident reports, monitoring data), the contract's credit formula |
| Goodwill | The customer's complaint in their words, the account owner's recommendation, account value and renewal date |
| Write-off | Invoice history, every collection message and reply, the contract, any insolvency notice, the Head of Finance's decision |

## Effect on the numbers

- **Billing-error credit:** reduces revenue (or deferred revenue if the period isn't earned yet) and AR. If the original invoice crossed into a closed month, tell the Staff Accountant so the period effect is handled correctly.
- **Service credit:** typically reduces revenue. How service credits are treated (as a price reduction or otherwise) is a revenue recognition judgment; the Head of Finance and the company's accountants decide the policy.
- **Goodwill credit:** reduces revenue and AR. Track separately so the cost of goodwill is visible.
- **Bad-debt write-off:** doesn't reverse revenue. It moves the debt from AR to bad-debt expense (or uses an existing provision for doubtful debts, if the company keeps one).
- **Tax:** a credit note normally needs to reverse the tax charged on the original invoice. Rules on credit-note content and on reclaiming tax on bad debts differ by jurisdiction. Check with the Head of Finance.

## Writing the request

Keep it to one page:

- **Customer and invoice(s):** number, date, amount, status.
- **What happened:** the facts, including the customer's words if they complained.
- **Type and reason:** one of the four above, and why.
- **Amount and calculation:** show the maths.
- **Effect:** on revenue, AR and tax for this period.
- **Recommendation:** yours, marked as yours; alternatives considered.
- **Prevention:** what caused it and the fix, so it doesn't recur.

## Worked example

A customer was invoiced 1,200 for 40 seats in October. Their order form says 32 seats from 1 October at 30 each. Correct amount: 960.

- Type: billing error (wrong quantity; seat reduction wasn't entered).
- Request: credit note for 240 against invoice INV-1043; no rebill needed if the credit is applied to the same invoice.
- Effect: revenue and AR down 240 in October; tax reversed on 240 per the tax set-up.
- Prevention: seat changes from signed order forms go into the billing system the day they're signed; added to the accuracy-check sample for three months.

## Pitfalls

- Issuing a credit before approval because the customer is upset. Approval is quick when the evidence is there.
- Writing off without proving the ladder was followed.
- Treating a repeat billing error as a one-off. Each credit should come with a cause.
- Applying credits to the wrong invoice, so the ageing looks better on paper than it is.
