# Choosing a mover

The cheapest quote is often the most expensive move. A good choice comes from comparable quotes based on a proper survey, a checked licence or registration, and everything in writing.

## Get quotes you can compare

1. **At least three companies.** Ask local people and check independent reviews.
2. **A survey,** in person or by video. A quote over the phone with no look at your stuff is a warning sign.
3. **The same scope for all:** dates, addresses, access (stairs, lifts, parking distance), packing service or not, specialist items (piano, artwork), storage.
4. **In writing,** with what's included and what costs extra.

## Questions to ask

- Is this a fixed price, or an estimate that can change? On what basis?
- What's included: packing materials, disassembly, parking permits, fuel?
- What insurance or liability cover do you provide, and what's the limit per item? (Basic cover is often far below replacement value.)
- How do claims for damage work, and how long do I have to make one?
- Is the crew your staff or subcontracted?
- What deposit, and how is payment taken? When is the balance due?
- Cancellation and date-change terms.

## Red flags

- No physical address, no registration or licence number where one is required.
- Very low quote without a survey.
- Large cash deposit up front.
- No written estimate or contract, or blank spaces on the paperwork.
- The company name on the truck differs from the one you booked.
- Demands for more money after your belongings are loaded ("hostage load").

## Worked example: US interstate moves

For a move across state lines in the US, the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) regulates household-goods movers. Its *Protect Your Move* guidance covers:

- **Check the USDOT number** on FMCSA's search tools. A mover without proper authority may not have valid insurance.
- **Binding versus non-binding estimates.** A binding estimate fixes the price for the described goods and services. Under a non-binding estimate, FMCSA's "110 percent rule" limits what the mover can demand at delivery to 110% of the estimate; the rest is billed later.
- **Read the paperwork:** the estimate, the order for service, and the bill of lading (your contract and receipt). Don't sign anything blank.
- **Report problems** to FMCSA.

Moves within a state, and moves in other countries, follow different rules. Look up the local regulator or trade association.

## Doing it yourself instead

Worth comparing for small moves: van hire plus friends, or a "you pack, we drive" container service. Count fuel, tolls, time off work and the cost of a strained back.

## Common mistakes

- Booking late in peak season, when good movers are full. Many moves cluster at month ends and in summer.
- Not declaring access issues, then paying extra on the day.
- Assuming the mover's basic cover pays replacement value.
- Paying the full price in advance.
