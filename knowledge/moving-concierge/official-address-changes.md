# Official address changes

Mail forwarding is the safety net, not the plan. Government bodies often don't share address changes with each other, and some mail isn't forwarded at all. Each needs its own update.

**Country-specific and time-sensitive.** Details below were checked in November 2026. Fees and processes change; re-check before advising.

## United States (examples)

**USPS mail forwarding**
- Online at the official USPS site (moversguide.usps.com) with a small identity-verification fee charged to a card ($1.25 when checked), or free in person at a Post Office using PS Form 3575.
- First-Class Mail is forwarded for 12 months as standard, extendable for a fee. Periodicals are forwarded for 60 days.
- Allow at least 7–10 business days to take effect. File before the move.
- Beware look-alike sites charging large fees for the same service. Use usps.com.

**IRS**
- A USPS change *may* update IRS records, but the IRS doesn't guarantee it. Use Form 8822 (individuals) or put the new address on your next return. Not all post offices forward government cheques.

**Also update:** state DMV (licence and vehicle registration; deadlines are set by each state, often short), voter registration, Social Security Administration if receiving benefits, state tax agency if moving states.

## United Kingdom (examples)

- **Royal Mail redirection:** paid service; book in advance.
- **HMRC:** update online for income tax, National Insurance, Child Benefit, State Pension and tax credits. Business taxes are updated separately.
- **DVLA:** driving licence and vehicle log book (V5C). Updating a licence address is free. Failing to update can lead to a fine; check the current rules.
- **Electoral register:** register again at the new address (GOV.UK "Register to vote").
- **Council tax:** tell the old council you're leaving and register with the new one; they're separate.
- **TV Licensing, DWP benefits, Student Loans Company** if they apply.

## Researching any other country

For each item, find the official page and record:

| Question | Note |
|---|---|
| Who must be told? | Tax, ID registry, vehicle authority, electoral roll, local municipality, health system |
| Is there a legal deadline? | Some countries require registering a new address within days |
| How: online, in person, by post? | In-person ones need an appointment early |
| What documents? | Tenancy agreement, landlord confirmation, ID |
| Does one update cascade? | Some countries have a single registration that updates many agencies; most don't |

Record the date you checked each page.

## The gaps forwarding leaves

- Government mail and cheques in some countries
- Parcels and courier deliveries
- Mail addressed to former residents
- Anything after the forwarding period ends

That's why the who-to-tell list still matters. Use redirected mail as a detector: every forwarded item is a straggler to update.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on forwarding for tax and licence mail.
- Using a third-party site that charges more for the same official form.
- Forgetting voter registration until an election.
- Missing a short legal deadline for registering a new address or vehicle.
