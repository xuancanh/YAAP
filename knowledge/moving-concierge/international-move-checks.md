# International move checks

International moves add a layer of rules that domestic moves don't have. Most of them are set by governments, change often and depend on the person's nationality and circumstances. This doc lists what to find out, not the answers.

**Official sources only.** For visas, residency, work rights, tax residency and customs, find the destination government's official site (and the home country's where exit rules apply). For anything complex (family members, previous refusals, self-employment, dual nationality), suggest a regulated immigration adviser or lawyer. Never tell the user they're eligible.

## Before committing to dates

- [ ] **Right to live and work** in the destination: which visa or permit, processing time, whether you can travel before it's granted.
- [ ] **Family:** do spouse, partner and children need their own permits?
- [ ] **Passports** valid long enough for the destination's rules, for everyone.
- [ ] **Documents** that may need certified or legalised copies (apostille, translation): birth and marriage certificates, degrees, police checks, driving record.
- [ ] **Driving:** can the licence be exchanged, and for how long can it be used before it must be?

## Money and tax

- [ ] Banking at both ends: can the old account stay open; how to open a new one before you have a local address.
- [ ] Tax residency and whether you need to notify the home tax authority. This is technical: point to the tax authority's guidance and a tax adviser.
- [ ] Pension and benefits: what happens to them abroad.
- [ ] Moving money: fees and exchange rates vary widely; compare before a large transfer.

## Health

- [ ] Healthcare access on arrival: is there a waiting period, is insurance required for the visa?
- [ ] Prescriptions: enough supply for the transition, and whether the medicine is legal and available in the destination. Some common medicines are controlled elsewhere. Check with the destination's official health or customs authority and the prescriber.
- [ ] Vaccination records for everyone; any required for entry.
- [ ] Copies of medical and dental records.

## Belongings and customs

- [ ] What can be imported duty-free as household goods, and the time limit after arrival.
- [ ] Prohibited and restricted items (food, plants, weapons, some medicines, alcohol and tobacco limits).
- [ ] A detailed inventory for customs; the mover's international team usually needs one.
- [ ] Whether it's worth shipping furniture and appliances at all (voltage, plug types, space, shipping time of weeks to months).

## Pets

Pet import rules can take months: microchip, rabies vaccination, blood tests, waiting periods, approved routes and airlines. Start with the destination's official pet import page and the vet. Some breeds are banned in some countries.

## Children

- [ ] School places and application windows; records and reports from the current school.
- [ ] Consent letters if travelling with one parent.

## Admin at the home end

- [ ] Notify tax, electoral, vehicle and benefits authorities that you're leaving.
- [ ] Decide on the home phone number (keep it for two-factor codes?).
- [ ] A forwarding address or mail-scanning service for stragglers.

## Common mistakes

- Booking the shipment before the visa is granted.
- Assuming the home driving licence works indefinitely.
- Forgetting document legalisation, which can take weeks.
- Treating forum advice as the rule. Check the official page and its date.
