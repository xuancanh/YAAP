# Packing and labelling system

Good packing is mostly order and labels. The goal: nothing broken, and the kettle findable on night one.

## Packing order

Work from least-used to most-used:

1. **Declutter first.** Every item you sell, donate or bin is one you don't pack, move and unpack.
2. **Storage spaces:** loft, garage, spare room, off-season clothes.
3. **Books, decorations, hobby gear, spare linens.**
4. **Most of the kitchen** (keep a minimal set), most of the wardrobe.
5. **Last days:** bathroom, daily kitchen, bedding, kids' favourite things.
6. **Moving day:** the open-first box and the carry-yourself bag.

Rule of thumb for effort: double what you planned. Don't say so to discourage; say it so the plan has slack.

## Box rules

- **Heavy in small boxes, light in big ones.** Books in small boxes; duvets in big ones.
- **Fill boxes fully** so they don't crush when stacked; pad gaps with towels or paper.
- **Plates on edge**, wrapped individually; glasses with paper inside and out.
- **Tape the bottom twice.**
- **No box over what one person can lift safely.**

## Labelling scheme

Each box gets:

- **Number** (for the inventory: box 12 = what's inside)
- **Destination room** at the new place, not the old one
- **Priority:** 1 = open first day, 2 = first week, 3 = whenever
- **"Fragile"** and **"This way up"** where they apply

Label on two sides and the top, so it's visible when stacked. Colour-coding by room (tape or stickers) helps the crew put boxes in the right place. Put a matching colour sign on each room door at the new home.

## Open-first box (priority 1)

- Kettle, mugs, tea or coffee, a few plates and cutlery
- Toilet paper, soap, towels, basic toiletries
- Bedding for every bed, a change of clothes per person
- Phone chargers, extension lead, torch
- Basic tools: screwdriver, scissors, box cutter, picture hooks
- Cleaning kit: cloths, spray, bin bags
- Light bulbs, a few snacks, kids' comfort items, pet food and bowls

## Carry-yourself bag (never in the van)

- ID documents, passports, birth certificates
- Medicines and prescriptions
- Keys (old and new), moving paperwork, contracts
- Laptops, phones, hard drives
- Jewellery, cash, small valuables
- Anything irreplaceable (photos, keepsakes)

## Items movers usually won't carry

Check the mover's list, but commonly refused: flammables (paint thinner, petrol, gas canisters, fireworks), corrosives and pool chemicals, aerosols in bulk, ammunition, perishable food, and plants on long or cross-border moves. Dispose of hazardous items through the local council or waste service, not the bin.

## Worked example

Inventory row:

```
Box 12 | Kitchen | Priority 2 | Fragile
Contents: wine glasses (8), serving bowls (3), cake tin
Packed: 14 Nov | Notes: top shelf at new place
```

## Common mistakes

- Labelling by the old room ("Spare room") when the new house has no spare room.
- Packing the kettle and the bedding on day one.
- Overloaded large boxes that split.
- Important documents in a box among fifty identical ones.
