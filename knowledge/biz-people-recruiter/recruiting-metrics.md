# Recruiting metrics

Recruiting metrics are only comparable when the start and end points are defined the same way every time. Write the definitions below into the hiring folder and use them for every requisition.

## Time to hire and time to fill

Two different numbers that are often confused:

> **Time to hire** = date the candidate accepted the offer − date the candidate entered the pipeline (applied or was sourced)

> **Time to fill** = date the offer was accepted − date the requisition was approved

- Time to hire measures how fast the process moves a candidate through; time to fill includes the time before anyone applied (sourcing, approvals, a slow job ad). Your KPI is time to hire; report time to fill as context.
- Use calendar days and say so.
- Report the **median** across hires and the range. One hard-to-fill role distorts an average.
- Break it down by **stage age**: days candidates spend in each stage. That shows where to act (often the gap between final interview and decision).

## Candidate response time

> **Response time** = time from a candidate action (application, interview completed, question asked) to our next message to them

- Report the **share within target** (the target set by the Head of People) and the median.
- Measure separately for applications (time to first decision), post-interview (time to outcome) and candidate questions.
- An acknowledgement doesn't count as the decision for "time to first decision". It does count for "time to first response" if you report that too.

## Stage conversion

> **Stage conversion** = candidates who moved to the next stage ÷ candidates who completed this stage

Example funnel for one role:

| Stage | Entered | Moved on | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application review | 120 | 24 | 20% |
| Screening call | 24 | 10 | 42% |
| Interviews | 10 | 3 | 30% |
| Final | 3 | 1 | 33% |
| Offer | 1 | 1 | 100% |

(Illustrative numbers.) How to read it:
- Very low application-to-screen conversion: the ad is attracting the wrong people, or the must-haves are too strict.
- Very low screen-to-interview: the screen isn't filtering, or the hiring manager's bar differs from the scorecard. Recalibrate together.
- High late-stage drop-outs (candidates withdrawing): the process is too slow, or the offer isn't competitive.

Count **withdrawals** separately from rejections; they mean different things.

**By source**: the same funnel split by sourcing channel shows which channels bring candidates who get hired, not just applications.

**Fairness check**: where the company collects voluntary demographic data in line with local law, the Head of People may review conversion by group in aggregate to spot stages where some groups drop out. Never use that data in individual decisions, and don't handle it yourself unless granted.

## Offer acceptance

> **Offer acceptance rate** = offers accepted ÷ offers extended (in the period)

- Count each formal offer once. A revised offer to the same candidate is the same offer.
- Record the reason for each decline in categories (pay, another offer, role scope, location or working pattern, timing, process experience, personal). With small numbers, the reasons tell you more than the rate.

## Supporting measures

- **Hiring manager satisfaction** with the shortlist (a short question after each hire).
- **New-hire outcome**: at 90 days, did the hire meet their early goals? Ask People Ops; this checks whether the scorecard predicts success.
