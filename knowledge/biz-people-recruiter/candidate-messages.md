# Candidate messages

Candidates judge the company by how it treats them while they wait. Short, specific and on time beats warm and late. All messages are drafts for human review unless the company has granted you a sending channel.

## Timing rules

- Every application is acknowledged.
- Every stage ends with a message: next step or decision. Silence is never a decision.
- If a decision will take longer than promised, send an update before the promised date, not after.
- Declines after an interview are sent within the response target, and ideally offered by a person for late-stage candidates.

## Acknowledgement

> Hi {first name},
>
> Thanks for applying for {role}. We review every application against the requirements in the job ad, and you'll hear from us by {date} either way.
>
> If you need any adjustments during the process, just reply to this email.

## Invitation to interview

> Hi {first name},
>
> Thanks for your time so far. We'd like to invite you to {stage} for {role}.
>
> - **Who:** {names and roles}
> - **What:** {what it covers, e.g. "your experience with queue management and coaching"; any task and how long it should take}
> - **How long:** {duration}
> - **Format:** {video link / location}
>
> Please pick a time that suits you here: {link or options}. Let us know if you need any adjustments.

## Update (decision delayed)

> Hi {first name},
>
> A quick update: we're taking a little longer than planned to decide on {stage}. You'll hear from us by {new date}. Thanks for your patience.

## Declines

**After application review**
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thank you for applying for {role}. We've reviewed your application and won't be moving forward this time. We received many applications, and this decision is about the specific requirements of this role.
>
> {Optional: We'd be glad to see your application for future roles.}

**After a screening call**
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thank you for speaking with us about {role}. We've decided not to move forward. {One honest, general sentence, e.g. "We're looking for more direct experience running a support queue to a service target."} Thanks again for your time.

**After interviews**
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thank you for the time you put into interviewing for {role}. After careful consideration, we've decided to offer the role to another candidate.
>
> {If policy allows feedback: You did particularly well on {competency}. The main gap for us was {competency, stated as evidence, not as a personal judgement}.}
>
> {Optional: We'd like to stay in touch about future roles, if you're happy for us to keep your details.}

Feedback rules: only give feedback tied to the scorecard, never compare with other candidates, never mention anything relating to a protected characteristic, and follow the company's policy on written feedback. If unsure, offer to talk instead.

## Offer stage

**Verbal offer follow-up** (after the hiring manager or Head of People has made the offer):
> Hi {first name},
>
> Following your conversation with {name}, the written offer is attached. The key points: {title}, {start date}, {conditions such as references or right-to-work check}. The offer is open until {expiry date}.
>
> If you have questions, {name} is happy to talk them through.

Never state pay, terms or conditions in a message unless they match the approved offer exactly. Negotiation is for the deciding human.

**Offer declined**
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thanks for letting us know, and for considering us. We enjoyed getting to know you and wish you every success. {Optional: We'd be glad to stay in touch.}

Ask, gently and optionally, what drove the decision, and record the reason category for the offer acceptance metric.

## Tone rules

- Use their name and the role; never a blank "Dear candidate".
- Say what happens next and when.
- No false hope ("we'll keep your CV on file") unless it's true and the candidate has agreed.
- Keep personal data out of shared files; messages live in the applicant tracking system.
