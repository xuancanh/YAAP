# Job ads and screening

The job ad decides who applies; screening decides who gets a chance. Both should be built from the scorecard, and both should be the same for every candidate.

## Job ad structure

1. **Title.** The standard market title someone would search for. Internal or playful titles reduce applications from people who don't recognise them.
2. **The opening.** Two or three sentences: what this person will do and why it matters.
3. **What you'll do.** The outcomes from intake, in plain words, as four to six bullets.
4. **What you'll need.** Must-haves only, at most five or six, written as capabilities, not credentials.
5. **Nice to have.** Clearly labelled as optional.
6. **About the team and company.** Short. What it's actually like.
7. **Pay, working pattern, location, benefits.** State the range where policy or law requires it, and ideally anyway.
8. **The process.** Stages, rough timeline, and when they'll hear back.
9. **Accommodations.** How to ask for adjustments in the process.

## Inclusive language checklist

- [ ] Must-haves are the real minimum. Long lists discourage capable people who don't tick every box from applying.
- [ ] No degree requirement unless the role legally or genuinely needs it; describe the capability instead.
- [ ] No "X+ years" unless the scorecard truly needs it; describe what they should be able to do.
- [ ] No gender-coded or exclusionary words: "rockstar", "ninja", "aggressive", "dominant", "digital native", "young and dynamic".
- [ ] No unnecessary physical requirements ("must be able to lift…") unless genuinely part of the job.
- [ ] Jargon and acronyms explained or removed.
- [ ] "You" and "we", short sentences, active voice.
- [ ] Pay range and working pattern included.
- [ ] Reads well on a phone.

Rules on what job ads must or must not contain (pay transparency, prohibited preferences, required statements) vary by jurisdiction and are changing in many places. Check the company's policy for each location before posting.

## Screening method

Screen against the must-haves only. Nice-to-haves are for ranking a shortlist, not for rejecting.

For each applicant, log per must-have:

| Must-have | Met / Not met / Unclear | Evidence (quote or line from CV or answers) |
|---|---|---|

- **Met**: clear evidence.
- **Not met**: clear evidence it's missing, or no plausible way the CV supports it.
- **Unclear**: possible but not shown. Don't reject on "unclear" alone; a short screening question or call can resolve it. Apply the same rule to everyone.

## Ignore these proxies

Don't use them, even as tie-breakers:
- name, photo, age or graduation year, address;
- school or employer prestige;
- gaps in employment (ask neutrally if relevant; don't assume);
- writing style for roles where writing isn't a must-have;
- anything about family, health, religion, nationality or other protected characteristics.

Where possible, review applications with names and photos hidden.

## Screening calls

Use the same questions for every candidate, written in advance from the must-haves, plus logistics (pay expectations within the range, start date, working pattern). Note answers against the scorecard during the call. Avoid questions that touch protected characteristics, directly or indirectly ("Do you have children?", "Where are you from originally?", "When did you graduate?").

## Shortlist and declines

- Shortlist everyone who meets all must-haves. If that's too many, rank by nice-to-haves and strength of evidence, the same way for all.
- If very few meet the must-haves, check with the hiring manager whether one is really a must-have before lowering the bar for some candidates only.
- Draft declines promptly (see [candidate messages](candidate-messages.md)). Long silence is a poor experience and hurts future applications.

## If you use automated tools

Automated screening and AI-assisted ranking are regulated in some jurisdictions and can encode bias. Use them only as the company has approved, keep a human reviewing every rejection, and keep a record of the criteria. Ask the Head of People before introducing any new tool.
