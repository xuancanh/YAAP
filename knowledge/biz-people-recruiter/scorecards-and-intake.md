# Scorecards and intake

Most failed searches fail at intake: the role was vague, the must-haves were a wish list, or the panel disagreed about what good looks like. Spend the time here and the rest goes faster.

## Start from outcomes, not a list of skills

Ask the hiring manager: "A year from now, this hire has been a clear success. What did they do?" Push for two to four outcomes that are observable.

- Weak: "Strong communicator who drives results."
- Strong: "Within 6 months, runs the monthly customer webinar on their own and gets it to the attendance target."

Then derive the competencies from the outcomes: what must someone be able to do to achieve them?

## Intake questions that prevent surprises

Beyond the basics in your playbook, ask:

- What would make you reject an otherwise strong candidate? (Surfaces hidden must-haves now, not after five interviews.)
- Is there someone internal who could do this? Has that been considered?
- Who else must approve the hire? Are they on the panel?
- Where have past hires for this kind of role come from? Which worked?
- How flexible is the band, the location and the start date, and who can change them?
- What can the panel commit to in interview time each week?

Write down the answers in the brief and get the hiring manager's confirmation. If any of these change mid-search, record the change and its effect on the timeline.

## The scorecard

Four to six competencies. Fewer is better: each one must be tested, and each interviewer can only assess a couple well.

For each competency:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Competency | Name and one-line definition tied to an outcome |
| Must-have or nice-to-have | Must-haves are the true minimum to succeed |
| Evidence of strong | What a strong answer or work sample shows |
| Evidence of weak | What a concerning answer shows |
| Tested at | Which stage and which interviewer |

Use a rating scale with defined levels (see [structured interviews](structured-interviews.md)) so "3" means the same to every interviewer.

## Worked example: customer support lead

**Outcomes**
1. Within 3 months, first-response times meet the service target on the team's queue.
2. Within 6 months, a documented escalation process the team follows.
3. Coaches two new support agents to work independently.

**Scorecard (extract)**

| Competency | Type | Strong evidence | Weak evidence | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Queue management: prioritises and staffs a queue to a target | Must | Describes a specific queue, the target, the changes they made and the measured effect | Talks about "working hard"; no numbers or method | Hiring manager interview |
| Process design: turns repeated problems into a process people follow | Must | Gives an example of a process they wrote, how they got adoption, what they changed after feedback | Process existed only on paper; no adoption | Work sample |
| Coaching | Must | Specific example of developing someone; what they did differently for that person | Generic "I lead by example" | Peer interview |
| Customer judgment in escalations | Must | Weighs customer impact, policy and cost; knows when to escalate | Rigid policy-following, or promises outside authority | Situational questions |
| Familiarity with our helpdesk tool | Nice | Has used it | Hasn't, but learned similar tools quickly | Screen |

Note the tool familiarity is nice-to-have: it can be learned, and requiring it would shrink the pool for little gain.

## The hiring plan

From the brief, write: sourcing channels and why; stages with owners and target days per stage; the interview panel and their availability; and checkpoints (e.g. "review the funnel with the hiring manager after the first week of screening"). An expected funnel is useful for planning, but base it on the company's own history when it exists and label it as an estimate.

## Pitfalls

- Copying the last job description as the scorecard.
- Years of experience as a competency. It's a proxy; describe the capability instead.
- "Culture fit" as a competency. It invites bias. If there are values to assess, name the specific behaviour.
- Changing the scorecard after meeting a candidate you like. If the role really changed, re-run intake and re-assess everyone on the new scorecard.
