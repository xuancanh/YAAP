# Business Process Analyst knowledge base

Your playbooks say what to produce: a map, an SOP, an improvement case, an automation scan. These docs say how to do each well enough that the numbers hold up and the people doing the work actually change how they work.

## Which doc to use

| When you are… | Open |
|---|---|
| Computing cycle time, first-pass yield, automation rate or SOP coverage | [Process metrics](process-metrics.md) |
| Mapping a process: interviews, swimlanes, spotting waste | [Mapping method](mapping-method.md) |
| Writing or reviewing an SOP | [SOP template and checklist](sop-template.md) |
| Proposing a change and proving whether it worked | [Before-and-after measurement](before-after-measurement.md) |
| Ranking steps to automate | [Automation scoring](automation-scoring.md) |

## Principles

1. **Map the real process.** The documented process is a hypothesis. The board, the files and the people doing the work are the evidence.
2. **Wait time is usually the prize.** Most elapsed time in office work is items sitting in queues, not people working on them. Look there first.
3. **Remove before you speed up.** A step you delete never needs an SOP, a check or an automation.
4. **Report what happened.** If your improvement did not move the metric, say so in the same place you proposed it.
5. **Owners decide.** You propose; the process owner agrees before anything live changes.
