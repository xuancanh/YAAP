# Manager training content

Each module below is a short method, an example, and a practice exercise. Use them as the core of a session or as a standalone guide. Adapt examples to the company's own work.

## Module 1: running one-to-ones

**Method.** The one-to-one is the report's meeting, not a status update.
- Regular and protected: same slot, rarely cancelled.
- Report sets most of the agenda; manager adds one or two items.
- A simple structure: how are things going; what's blocking you; one development topic; agreed actions.
- Status updates go in writing elsewhere.
- Write down actions and check them next time.

**Practice.** Draft the agenda for your next one-to-one with each report. Then swap with a partner: is it about the person or about the work status?

## Module 2: giving specific feedback

**Method: Situation, Behaviour, Impact (SBI).** The Center for Creative Leadership's model: describe the *situation* (when and where), the *behaviour* you observed (what they did or said, not your interpretation), and its *impact* (on you, the team, the customer, the result). CCL extends it by asking about the person's *intent*, which turns it into a conversation.

> **Vague:** "You need to be more of a team player."
> **SBI:** "In Tuesday's planning meeting (situation), you committed the team to the Friday deadline before checking with Sam and Lee (behaviour). They've had to drop the onboarding work to make it (impact). What were you aiming for there? (intent)"

Works for positive feedback too: "In the customer call this morning, you summarised their three concerns back to them before answering. They said afterwards it was the first time they felt heard."

Tips: give it soon; in private if it's developmental; one point at a time; then listen.

**Practice.** Rewrite three vague comments ("great job", "be more proactive", "communication needs work") in SBI form. Model answers in the facilitator notes should name a specific situation and observable behaviour.

## Module 3: setting goals tied to key results

**Method.**
- Start from the team's key results; each person's goals should show how their work contributes.
- Two to four goals, each with a measure or a clear "done" description.
- Mix outcome goals (what changes) with a development goal (what they'll get better at).
- Agree, don't assign: the person drafts, the manager adjusts.
- Review monthly in one-to-ones, not just at review time.

**Practice.** Turn "improve customer satisfaction" into a goal for a support specialist that has a measure, a date and a link to a key result.

## Module 4: writing evidence-based reviews

**Method.**
- Collect evidence through the period: notes from one-to-ones, completed work, feedback from colleagues.
- For each goal or competency: what they did (examples), the result, the rating against the defined scale.
- Separate the person's contribution from luck and from the team's.
- Development points: specific behaviour and what good looks like, not personality.
- No surprises: anything serious in a review should already have been discussed.

> **Poor:** "Sam is a strong performer who sometimes lacks attention to detail."
> **Better:** "Sam delivered all four launch reports on time and the March pricing analysis changed the plan. Two reports went out with incorrect totals (April, June); using the review checklist before sending would catch this."

**Practice.** Rewrite a sample review paragraph so that every claim has an example.

## Module 5: checking for bias

Common patterns in reviews and decisions:
- **Recency:** the last few weeks outweigh the whole period. Counter: notes through the period.
- **Halo/horns:** one trait colours every rating. Counter: rate each area separately with evidence.
- **Similarity:** rating people like you more highly. Counter: compare evidence, not impressions.
- **Different standards:** the same behaviour described differently for different people (e.g. "assertive" vs "abrasive"). Counter: swap the name and reread.
- **Central tendency or leniency:** everyone rated the same. Counter: anchor to the scale's definitions.

**Practice.** Review two anonymised sample reviews written about similar work; find the differences in language and evidence.

## After the session

Ask each manager to use one method within two weeks (an SBI conversation, a rewritten goal) and bring an example to a short follow-up. Track completion and collect examples as the applied-skill signal.
