# L&D metrics

Completion shows reach; applied-skill signals show whether learning worked. Report both, and never let a high completion rate stand in for impact.

## Training completion

> **Completion rate** = people who completed the programme ÷ people enrolled (or assigned)

- Define "completed" per programme before launch: attended all sessions, submitted the practice task, passed the check.
- Use enrolled as the denominator, not "people who started"; dropping out is part of the picture.
- Report by team and in total, with counts.
- Track **drop-off points**: the session or module where people stop. That's where the design needs work.

## Mandatory training on time

> **Mandatory on-time rate** = required completions finished by their due date ÷ required completions due in the period

- A "required completion" is one person × one required course × one due date. Someone who needs three courses counts three times.
- Due dates come from the requirement: a deadline after joining, a renewal period, or a fixed date (an audit, a regulatory date). Store each requirement's source.
- Starters: count them from their individual due date, not the company deadline, so new joiners don't make the rate look worse unfairly.
- Report **overdue** (count by team and course) and **due within 30 days**, the list managers can act on.

**Chase timing** (adjust to the company's policy):
- Reminder to the person well ahead of the due date.
- Second reminder nearer the date, with the manager told.
- Overdue: manager and Head of People told.
- Deadlines tied to an audit or regulatory date get raised as a risk early.

Names go only in the HR or learning system the company grants. Workspace files and channels carry counts by team.

## Applied-skill signals

> **Applied-skill signal** = the chosen work measure for the trained group after the programme, compared with before (and with an untrained group where possible)

Each programme defines its own signal at design time (see [evaluating learning](evaluating-learning.md)). Common forms:

| Signal type | Formula |
|---|---|
| First-time acceptance | Tasks accepted on first review ÷ tasks submitted, before vs after |
| Rework | Review rounds per task, before vs after |
| Independence | Share of a standard task done without help, before vs after |
| Manager-observed | Managers reporting a concrete example of the behaviour ÷ managers asked |

Rules:
- Pick the signal before the programme starts. Picking afterwards invites cherry-picking.
- Use the same measurement window length before and after.
- Note other changes in the period (new tool, new process, new hires) that could explain the movement.
- With small groups, report the counts and treat the result as indicative.

**Learning gain** (Level 2): average after-score − average before-score on the same rubric.

## Reporting format

Monthly to the Head of People:

| Metric | This month | Last month | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandatory on-time rate (by course) | | | |
| Overdue / due in 30 days (by team) | | | |
| Programme completion (by programme) | | | drop-off point |
| Applied-skill signal (by programme) | | | before → after |
| Deadlines at risk | | | |

Quarterly, add the gap analysis follow-up: for each prioritised gap, what was done and whether its signal moved.

## Pitfalls

- Counting enrolments as completions.
- Treating a mandatory course as complete when the certificate has expired.
- Reporting a single "applied skills improved" line without the measure, window and counts.
- Sharing individual completion lists in channels.
