# Maintenance response tiers

Most places put a legal duty on landlords to keep homes safe and in repair, and some set response deadlines. The targets below are good practice for a small landlord; check the property's jurisdiction for legal timescales and use whichever is stricter.

## Triage table

| Tier | Examples | Target response | First action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency** | gas smell; CO alarm; major leak or flooding; no heat in freezing weather; electrical burning or sparking; break-in or door that won't lock; sewage backing up | same day, often within hours | safety instructions to the tenant at once; emergency trade |
| **Urgent** | boiler failing intermittently; no hot water; leaking appliance; broken window; fridge failure; pest infestation | 24-48 hours to attend | book the trade; interim fix |
| **Routine** | dripping tap; sticking door; loose handle; small cracks | within a few weeks, batched | schedule with tenant |

When unsure, tier up. Damp and mould deserve extra care: gov.uk guidance for rented housing stresses they can harm health, especially for children, older people and people with health conditions, and should be dealt with quickly and at the cause, not just painted over.

## Reply templates

**Emergency (gas smell)**
> Thanks for telling me. Please leave the property now, don't use switches or flames, and call the gas emergency line ([number for the country]) from outside. I'm arranging an engineer and will call you in [time].

**Emergency (leak)**
> Please turn off the water at the stop valve ([location from property file]) and keep electrics away from the water. A plumber is on the way, aiming to arrive by [time].

**Urgent**
> Thanks for reporting the [issue]. I've booked [trade] for [day/time window]. Could you send a photo and the model number on the [appliance]? In the meantime, [interim step, e.g. a portable heater is being dropped off].

**Routine**
> Thanks, noted. I'll bundle this with [other job] and come back to you with dates by [day]. Shout if it gets worse.

## Access and entry

- Give the notice the law and tenancy require, in writing, with a reason and a time window. Emergencies may allow shorter notice; check local rules.
- Propose times; don't just announce them.
- Confirm who's attending and whether the tenant needs to be home.

## The record (every report)

```
| Date reported | How | Issue | Tier | Reply sent | Trade booked | Attended | Fixed | Cost | Notes |
```

Log photos before and after. The record shows responsiveness and protects both sides if there's a dispute about condition or deposits.

## Repeat failures

The third call about the same boiler or the same damp patch is a pattern. Draft the repair-versus-replace comparison: age of the item, repair costs to date, likely remaining life, the cost of a replacement, and the disruption to the tenant.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for a quote before telling an emergency caller what to do.
- Treating mould as a "lifestyle" issue without checking for leaks, ventilation or insulation problems.
- Entering without notice.
- Fixing the symptom (repainting) without finding the cause.

## When to get help

- Structural concerns, gas, or electrics: qualified trades only.
- A tenant reports health problems linked to the property: act fast, document, and take advice on the duty.
- A dispute about whether the landlord is responsible: landlord association advice line or a solicitor/attorney.
