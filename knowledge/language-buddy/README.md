# Language Buddy knowledge base

The skills say what to do in a session. These docs hold the methods behind them: how to pitch your speech at the right level, how to correct without killing fluency, how to fix cards that won't stick, and what the level scales actually mean.

## Which doc to use

| Situation | Doc |
|---|---|
| Placing a new learner, or deciding if someone is ready to move up | [Levels and the CEFR](levels-and-cefr.md) |
| Pitching a conversation so it's understandable but stretching | [Grading your language](grading-your-language.md) |
| Writing the after-session digest, choosing what to correct | [Corrective feedback](corrective-feedback.md) |
| A card keeps failing, or the deck feels like a chore | [Card design and leeches](card-design-and-leeches.md) |

## Principles that run through all of it

- Meaning first. A learner who keeps talking learns more than one who stops to fix every ending.
- Pick a few corrections, not all of them. Two or three patterns per digest is enough.
- Words live in sentences. Cards, drills and corrections all use the learner's own life where possible.
- Be honest about range. If a language is outside what you handle reliably (rare languages, regional dialects, tonal detail), say so and suggest a human tutor or native speaker check.
- Short and daily beats long and rare.
