# Grading your language

The aim is input the learner understands mostly from context, with a few new pieces they can work out. If they understand everything, they're not stretching. If they're lost, they've stopped learning and started surviving.

## The rule of thumb

Aim for the learner understanding nearly everything you say, with one or two new words or structures per turn that they can guess from context. If you introduce more than that, slow down.

## Techniques that make speech easier to follow

- **Short sentences, one idea each.** Join them later as level rises.
- **High-frequency words.** Prefer "big" to "enormous" at A2. Save rarer words for when they serve the topic.
- **Repeat key words.** Reuse a new word two or three times in the next few turns. That repetition is what gets it remembered.
- **Talk about the learner.** Their job, their weekend, their plans. Familiar content frees attention for the language.
- **Signal topic shifts.** "Now, about your trip…" helps lower levels keep up.
- **Give the answer shape.** "Do you prefer coffee or tea?" is easier than "What do you drink?" Offer a choice at A1–A2, open questions from B1.
- **Paraphrase, don't translate.** If a word doesn't land, say it another way in the target language first. Fall back to the first language only if two tries fail.

## Stretching at each stage

| Level | Stretch with |
|---|---|
| A1–A2 | Past tense of common verbs, question words, numbers and times in context |
| B1 | Reasons and connectors ("because", "although"), telling a story in order |
| B2 | Hypotheticals, opinions with counterpoints, idioms from the learner's interests |
| C1+ | Register shifts (formal vs casual), humour, implied meaning, specialist vocabulary |

## Signs you've pitched it wrong

Too hard: one-word answers, long pauses, the learner switches language, answers that don't match the question.

Too easy: fast fluent replies with no errors, boredom, the learner asking for "something harder".

Adjust within the same session. Don't wait for the next one.

## Worked example

Learner at A2 Spanish, topic: their weekend.

- Too hard: "¿Qué tal te fue el finde, hiciste algo que valiera la pena?"
- Graded: "¿Qué hiciste el sábado? ¿Fuiste a algún sitio?" Then, after their answer: "Ah, fuiste al cine. ¿Qué película viste? ¿Te gustó la película?"

The graded version uses one past-tense verb, repeats it, and builds on what they said.

## Common mistakes

- Simplifying so much the language becomes unnatural. Keep it real, just shorter.
- Correcting mid-flow. Save it for the digest (see [Corrective feedback](corrective-feedback.md)).
- Asking a stack of questions in one turn. One question per turn at lower levels.
- Dropping the stretch entirely when a learner seems tired. Lower the load, keep one new thing.
