# Card design and leeches

Spaced repetition works because reviewing just before you'd forget strengthens memory more than cramming. But a badly written card fails no matter how well it's scheduled.

## Rules for a good card

- **One thing per card.** One word or one chunk. "To take a decision / prendre une décision" is a fine chunk; a list of five synonyms is not.
- **Context sentence on the front.** Ideally one the learner said or heard. Blank the target word: "Hier, j'ai ___ une décision." (pris)
- **Language-specific data on the back.** Gender, plural, measure word, verb class, irregular forms: whatever the language needs to use the word correctly.
- **Separate recognition and production.** Target → meaning is recognition. Meaning → target is production, and it's harder. Track them as two cards.
- **Pictures beat translations** for concrete nouns where possible.
- **Personal beats generic.** "My sister lives in ___" outlasts a textbook sentence.

## Leitner schedule reference

A simple five-box version:

| Box | Review every | On correct | On wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | session | → box 2 | stays in 1 |
| 2 | 2–3 days | → box 3 | → box 1 |
| 3 | about a week | → box 4 | → box 1 |
| 4 | about 2 weeks | → box 5 | → box 1 |
| 5 | about a month | retire after a pass | → box 1 |

Intervals are a starting point; tune to the learner. If almost everything passes, stretch them. If box 2 is overflowing, add fewer new cards.

## New cards per day

Each new card creates reviews for weeks. A learner doing a ten-minute daily session usually copes with a handful of new cards a day. If due reviews pile up past one session's worth, pause new cards until the backlog clears.

## Rescuing a leech

A leech is a card that keeps failing (five misses is a good trigger). Don't just reset it. Diagnose:

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Confused with a similar word | Make a contrast card: both words, one sentence each |
| Context too vague | New sentence where only this word fits |
| No hook | Draft a mnemonic: a sound-alike image in the learner's first language |
| Not useful to them | Retire it. Not every word earns a place. |
| Too many forms at once | Split: base form first, irregular forms later |

## Worked example

Leech: German *die Gabel* (fork). Learner keeps saying *der*.

Rebuilt card: "Ich esse mit ___ Gabel." (der → dative of *die*), plus a colour cue (feminine nouns highlighted one colour throughout the deck) and a mnemonic: "the fork is a *Gabel*: she's always at the table."

## Common mistakes

- Adding every new word from a session. Pick the ones they'll use.
- Translation-only cards with no context.
- Reviewing only recognition, then wondering why they can't speak.
- Letting the review pile grow until the habit breaks. Smaller decks done daily win.
