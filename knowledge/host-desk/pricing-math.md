# Pricing math

The skill sets the calendar; this doc does the sums. Use the host's own numbers. The figures below are illustrations, not market data.

## Step 1: the floor price

The floor is the lowest nightly rate at which a stay is worth the host's effort.

```
cost per stay  = cleaning + laundry + consumables + platform fee share + wear allowance
cost per night = fixed monthly costs ÷ expected booked nights
floor (for a stay of N nights) = (cost per stay ÷ N) + cost per night + the host's minimum profit per night
```

Worked example (illustrative):
- Cleaning and laundry 45, consumables 10, wear allowance 5 → 60 per stay.
- Fixed costs (utilities, insurance, internet, licence) 400 a month, about 20 booked nights → 20 per night.
- Host wants at least 25 a night for their time.
- 1-night stay floor: 60 + 20 + 25 = **105**.
- 3-night stay floor: 20 + 20 + 25 = **65** a night.

Short stays cost more per night because the turnover cost is spread over fewer nights. That's the real case for minimum stays or a cleaning fee.

## Step 2: rate vs occupancy

Full isn't the same as profitable. Compare revenue *after* per-stay costs.

| Option | Rate | Nights booked (of 30) | Stays | Revenue | Per-stay costs | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: low price | 80 | 27 | 9 | 2,160 | 540 | 1,620 |
| B: higher price | 110 | 20 | 7 | 2,200 | 420 | 1,780 |

Option B earns more with fewer turnovers, and leaves gap nights for maintenance. Run this with the host's own costs; the answer depends on cleaning costs and how sensitive local demand is to price.

Platform fees: check how each platform's fee affects what the guest sees and what the host receives, since that changes the real rate.

## Step 3: gaps

**Orphan nights** (one night between bookings):
- Allow a 1-night stay just for that gap, if the platform supports it.
- Or offer the neighbouring guests a discounted extra night.
- Check the 1-night floor first; a single night at a discount may lose money.

**Unbooked near dates (within about a week):**
- A last-minute discount can fill them, but only above the floor.
- Note the trade-off: regular discounting can teach guests to wait. Keep it modest and occasional.

**Far dates:**
- Compare bookings on the books with the same point last year. Running behind means check price and listing; running ahead means room to raise prices.

## Step 4: events and seasons

- List the local events that move demand (festivals, conferences, sports, school holidays) with dates, from official calendars.
- Set those dates early, before platforms' pricing tools or competitors react.
- Use a minimum stay on peak weekends to avoid a single night blocking a longer, more valuable booking.

## Yearly review

For each month: nights booked, average rate, revenue, costs, net. Mark which events were caught and missed. Next year's calendar starts from this file.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing from what feels fair rather than the floor.
- Chasing 100% occupancy and burning out the cleaner and the host.
- Copying a neighbor's rate without comparing size, amenities and reviews.
- Forgetting tax and fees in the margin. Ask the accountant what to set aside.
