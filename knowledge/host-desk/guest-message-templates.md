# Guest message templates

Adapt to the host's voice, fill the brackets, and keep them short. Guests skim on phones; the key fact goes in the first line.

## The standing sequence

**1. Booking confirmed (same day)**
> Hi [name], thanks for booking [place] for [dates]! I'll send directions and check-in details three days before you arrive. If you have questions in the meantime, just message here.

**2. Pre-arrival (3 days before)**
> Hi [name], looking forward to having you on [day].
> **Check-in:** from [time], [method: key box code sent on the day / smart lock].
> **Getting here:** [from the station: route + time] / [parking: exactly where, any cost].
> **Wifi:** [network] / [password].
> **First things:** heating is [how], coffee is [where].
> House manual: [link]. Any arrival-time changes, let me know.

**3. Check-in day (morning)**
> Hi [name], your code is [code], valid from [time]. The door is [description, photo link]. Message if anything isn't right; I'm reachable until [time].

**4. Settled in (evening of day 1, short stays: skip)**
> Hope you found everything okay! Anything missing or not working?

The day-1 check catches problems while they're fixable, instead of in the review.

**5. Before checkout (evening before)**
> Hi [name], checkout is by [time] tomorrow. Before you go: [the 2-3 actual asks, e.g. "bins in the outside bin, keys back in the box"]. No need to strip beds. Safe travels!

**6. After checkout**
> Thanks for staying, [name]. I've left you a review; I'd really appreciate one back when you have a moment. Hope to host you again.

Check each platform's own timing and wording rules for review requests.

## The difficult ones

**Early check-in you can't do**
> I'd love to, but the cleaners need until [time] to get it ready. You're welcome to drop bags at [option] from [time], and [café nearby] is a good place to wait.

**Noise complaint from a neighbor**
> Hi [name], a neighbor has mentioned noise this evening. The house rules ask for quiet after [time] because [the building is residential]. Could you keep it down from now? Thanks for understanding.

If it continues, follow the platform's process. Keep the tone factual, quote the house rules, and log times.

**Damage found**
> Hi [name], during the clean we found [item] was [damage]; photos attached. Could you let me know what happened? I'll need to [repair/replace] it, which costs about [amount, with quote].

Then follow the platform's damage-claim process and its deadlines, with photos, dates and receipts. Don't accuse; describe.

**Something broke during the stay (host's fault)**
> I'm really sorry about the [issue]. [Fix and when]. In the meantime, [workaround]. I'll [refund/credit amount] for the trouble.

Fix fast, apologise once, and offer something concrete. Guests often review the recovery more than the problem.

## Responding to a critical review

1. Thank them for staying.
2. Acknowledge the specific point.
3. Say what's been fixed.
4. No arguing or private details. Future guests read the reply, not the guest who left it.

> Thanks for staying, and for flagging the shower pressure. We've had a plumber fit a new mixer since. Glad you enjoyed the garden.

## Common mistakes

- Burying the check-in code in a long paragraph.
- Sending the same generic message to every guest and getting their name or dates wrong.
- Arguing in public review replies.
- Not logging the difficult conversations; platform disputes need the record.
