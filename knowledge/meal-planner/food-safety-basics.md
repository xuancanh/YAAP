# Food safety basics

These are the rules that make a meal plan with leftovers and batch cooking safe. Figures are from US (USDA FSIS, FoodSafety.gov) and UK (Food Standards Agency) guidance as checked in November 2026. Other countries' agencies may differ slightly; if the user is elsewhere, look up their national food-safety agency. When in doubt, throw it out: you can't see, smell or taste most food-poisoning bacteria.

## Cooling and refrigerating

| Rule | US (FSIS) | UK (FSA) |
|---|---|---|
| Get cooked food into the fridge | Within 2 hours; within 1 hour if the air is above 90°F (32°C) | Cool quickly, ideally within 1–2 hours, then fridge or freeze |
| Fridge temperature | 40°F (4°C) or below | 5°C or below |
| Freezer temperature | 0°F (−18°C) | −18°C |

**Cool it fast:** divide large batches into shallow containers. A deep pot of stew cools slowly in the middle. Don't leave food out overnight to cool.

## How long leftovers keep

| | US (FSIS) | UK (FSA) |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked leftovers in the fridge | 3–4 days | Within 2 days (48 hours), or freeze |
| Cooked rice | Treat as a leftover | No more than 1 day in the fridge before reheating |
| Leftovers in the freezer | Safe indefinitely when frozen; best quality within about 3–4 months | Follow labels; freezing stops bacteria growing but doesn't kill them |

Plan around the stricter number if you're unsure which applies. Write the date on the container.

## Reheating

- **US:** reheat leftovers to **165°F (74°C)**, checked with a food thermometer.
- **UK:** reheat until **steaming hot all the way through**, and **only reheat once**.
- Stir microwaved food partway; it heats unevenly.

## Thawing

- **Fridge:** safest. Plan ahead: big items take a day or more.
- **Cold water:** in a sealed bag, water changed every 30 minutes; cook straight away (FSIS).
- **Microwave:** cook immediately after.
- **Never** on the counter at room temperature.
- Food thawed in the fridge can be refrozen (quality may drop). Food thawed by cold water or microwave should be cooked before refreezing.

## Safe minimum cooking temperatures (US, FoodSafety.gov)

| Food | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Poultry (whole, pieces, ground) | 165°F (74°C) |
| Ground beef, pork, lamb | 160°F (71°C) |
| Beef, pork, lamb, veal steaks, roasts, chops | 145°F (63°C), then rest 3 minutes |
| Fish and shellfish | 145°F (63°C) |
| Egg dishes | 160°F (71°C) |
| Leftovers and casseroles | 165°F (74°C) |

The UK FSA gives "cooked until steaming hot throughout, juices run clear, no pink" for poultry, pork, burgers, sausages and rolled joints, rather than a number. A thermometer is still the reliable check.

## Planning implications

- Batch-cook on the day before the meals you need, not a week out, unless you freeze.
- With UK 2-day guidance, a Sunday cook covers Monday and Tuesday; freeze portions for later in the week.
- Cook rice fresh, or cool it fast and use it the next day.
- Defrost in the fridge: move Wednesday's portion from freezer to fridge on Tuesday night.

## Common mistakes

- Cooling a whole pot on the hob overnight.
- Reheating the same portion more than once.
- Thawing meat on the counter.
- Judging by smell.
