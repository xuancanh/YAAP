# Allergen checks

A food allergy can be life-threatening. The plan has to be right every time, not most of the time. This doc is for planning; it doesn't replace the advice of the person's allergist or doctor. If someone has a reaction with breathing difficulty, swelling of the throat or tongue, or collapse, that's an emergency: use their prescribed adrenaline if they have it and call emergency services.

## The official lists

**United States: nine major allergens (FDA).** Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame. Sesame was added by the FASTER Act (2021) with labelling required from 1 January 2023.

**United Kingdom: 14 allergens (FSA).** Celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide and sulphites. The EU uses the same 14.

Other countries have their own lists. A person can be allergic to something not on any list; their own list is what counts.

## Where allergens hide

| Allergen | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Milk | Butter, ghee, whey, casein, some breads and processed meats, "non-dairy" creamers that contain casein |
| Egg | Mayonnaise, fresh pasta, glazes on pastry, meringue, some meat substitutes |
| Wheat / gluten | Soy sauce, stock cubes, gravy, battered foods, beer, some spice mixes |
| Peanut / tree nuts | Pesto, satay, some curries, praline, nut oils, baked goods, marzipan |
| Soy | Soy lecithin, many processed foods, tofu, edamame, some vegetarian products |
| Sesame | Tahini, hummus, some breads and crackers, some oils and dressings |
| Fish / shellfish | Worcestershire sauce (anchovy), fish sauce, some Asian pastes, Caesar dressing |
| Mustard, celery (UK) | Sauces, stock, spice mixes, soups |

## Checklist for every plan

- [ ] Every recipe's ingredients checked against the household allergy list, including sauces and stocks
- [ ] Substitutions checked too: a swap can introduce an allergen (nut milk for dairy, for example)
- [ ] Shopping list notes "check label" for processed items. Recipes change; reformulated products can add allergens
- [ ] "May contain" warnings respected if the person's allergist has told them to avoid them
- [ ] Eating out or takeaway: tell the person to ask the restaurant directly; menus are not enough

## Cross-contact at home

Cross-contact is when an allergen gets into a safe food through shared surfaces, oil, utensils or hands.

- Cook the allergen-free dish first, then cover it.
- Clean surfaces and utensils with soap and water; wiping isn't enough.
- Separate toaster, butter and spreads if needed (crumbs carry).
- Don't reuse frying oil from an allergen dish.
- Store allergen-free food on a higher shelf, labelled.

## Allergy versus intolerance versus preference

Record which it is, because the planning rules differ:

| Type | Planning rule |
|---|---|
| Allergy | Absolute. Includes cross-contact. |
| Intolerance (e.g. lactose) | Avoid as the person has specified; amounts may matter to them |
| Coeliac disease | Medical. Strict gluten avoidance including cross-contact; follow their clinician |
| Preference or dislike | Flexible; ask before working around it |

## Common mistakes

- Checking the main ingredients and forgetting the sauce.
- Assuming last month's product label still applies.
- "Just a little" or "picking it out".
- A guest's allergy noted once and then forgotten on the next visit. Keep it in their profile.
