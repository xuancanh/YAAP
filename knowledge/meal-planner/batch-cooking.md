# Batch cooking method

Batch cooking turns one long session into several easy nights. It works best with a few dishes that freeze and reheat well, cooked in the right order.

## Two patterns

- **Cook once, eat twice.** Make double of tonight's dinner; the second half becomes a meal later in the week or goes in the freezer. Low effort, no special session.
- **The prep session.** Two to three hours, usually at the weekend: two or three main dishes, plus prepped components (grains, chopped vegetables, sauces).

Start with the first pattern. Most households get most of the benefit from it.

## What freezes well, and what doesn't

| Freezes well | Freezes poorly |
|---|---|
| Stews, curries, chilli, soups, bolognese and other sauces | Salad leaves, cucumber, raw tomatoes |
| Casseroles, lasagne, pies (cooked or uncooked) | Cream- or yoghurt-based sauces (can split) |
| Cooked beans and lentils | Boiled potatoes (go grainy); mashed is fine |
| Bread, flatbreads, muffins | Fried, crispy foods (go soggy) |
| Grated cheese, butter | Hard-boiled eggs |
| Raw marinated meat for later cooking | Mayonnaise-based dishes |

Pasta and rice can be frozen but are better cooked fresh. Cooked rice has stricter fridge rules; see [Food safety basics](food-safety-basics.md).

## Session order

1. **Start the long cooks first:** stews, braises, beans, stock.
2. **While they simmer:** chop for everything else; make sauces.
3. **Oven items** go in as the hob frees up.
4. **Components last:** grains, roasted vegetables, washed greens.
5. **Cool fast** in shallow containers, then fridge or freeze promptly. Don't leave pots out to cool for hours.
6. **Clean as you go** so the session doesn't end in a disaster kitchen.

## Portioning and labelling

- Portion into meal sizes for the household, not one giant tub.
- Freeze flat in bags for quick thawing and easy stacking.
- Label every container: **dish, date cooked, portions, allergens if relevant, reheat note.** "Chilli, 8 Nov, serves 4, reheat from defrosted."
- Keep a freezer list in the pantry file so meals don't get lost at the back.
- First in, first out.

## Worked example: a two-hour session

Goal: three later dinners plus lunches for two adults.

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Start chilli (double) and a pot of lentil soup |
| 0:20 | Chop vegetables for a tray bake; start rice |
| 0:45 | Tray bake into the oven |
| 1:15 | Portion soup into lunch tubs; cool chilli in shallow trays |
| 1:30 | Cool rice quickly, fridge for tomorrow |
| 1:45 | Label and freeze half the chilli; fridge the rest for Monday |
| 2:00 | Done: Monday chilli, Tuesday tray-bake leftovers, freezer chilli for next week, four soup lunches |

## Common mistakes

- Cooking five new recipes in one session. Stick to two or three you know.
- Making food nobody wants to eat three times. Vary the toppings and sides instead.
- Forgetting labels, so the freezer becomes a mystery.
- Freezing food that was left out too long first. Freezing doesn't make unsafe food safe.
