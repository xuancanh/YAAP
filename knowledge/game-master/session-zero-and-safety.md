# Session zero and table safety

A session zero is the meeting before the first real session where the table agrees what game they're playing. Most campaign blow-ups (the player who wanted comedy in a grimdark game, the scene that hit someone's real trauma) trace back to skipping it.

## Session-zero agenda

Run it in about an hour, before or instead of the first adventure.

1. **The pitch.** Setting, genre and tone in three sentences. "Low-magic political intrigue, more talking than fighting, deaths are possible."
2. **Expectations.** How often you play, how long, what happens when someone can't come (play anyway? character fades into background?).
3. **Content limits.** Run lines and veils (below). Record them in the bible's table-notes file.
4. **Safety tool.** Pick one in-play tool and demo it once so nobody is shy to use it.
5. **Characters.** Build together or at least share concepts. Ask for one tie between each PC and another PC or the world; it gives you instant hooks.
6. **House rules.** Anything that changes the book: death rules, rolling in the open, phone use.
7. **Lethality and agency.** Say how deadly the game is and whether players can refuse a scene's consequences.

Write the outcome as a one-page "table charter" and file it.

## The common tools

- **Lines** are content that won't appear at all. **Veils** are content that can happen but off-screen ("fade to black"). Collect them privately (a form or DM) as well as aloud, since some people won't say things in front of the group.
- **X-card.** Anyone can tap a card or type "X" in chat to remove the last bit of content, no explanation needed. The GM just edits and moves on.
- **Open door.** Anyone can leave the session, at any time, for any reason, without it being a big deal.
- **Check-ins.** A short "how's everyone doing with this?" after an intense scene, and a quick "stars and wishes" (what you loved, what you'd like more of) at session end.

The TTRPG Safety Toolkit collects these and many others with how-to guides. Point the GM there rather than inventing new tools.

## Common mistakes

- Treating safety tools as only for horror games. Spiders, phobias and family themes come up anywhere.
- Asking "any lines?" once, aloud, and never again. Revisit when a new player joins or the campaign's direction shifts.
- Arguing when a tool is used. The point is that no justification is needed.
- Letting the loudest player set the tone. Session zero exists to hear the quiet ones.

## Worked example

A GM pitches "pirate adventure". At session zero one player marks drowning as a veil (real-life fear) and another marks harm to children as a line. The GM notes both in the bible, keeps shipwrecks but cuts away from drowning scenes, and swaps a planned "orphan cabin boy in danger" hook for a kidnapped navigator. Nobody had to explain anything.

## When it's beyond the table

Safety tools are for game content, not for real conflict between players or for someone in distress. If a player discloses a real crisis, step out of game mode and suggest they talk to someone they trust or a local support service.
