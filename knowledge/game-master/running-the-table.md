# Running the table

Prep gets you to the table; these habits keep the session moving once you're there.

## Pacing

- **Start in motion.** Open with the recap, then drop straight into the strong start. No shopping, no "you're at the tavern."
- **End scenes early.** Leave a scene when its question is answered, not when everyone runs out of things to say. "Anything else before we cut?" works.
- **Use a soft timer.** Glance at the clock at the halfway mark. If you're behind, collapse two planned scenes into one, or move the cliffhanger earlier.
- **End on a hook.** Stop on a question, a reveal or a door opening. It feeds the next recap.

## Spotlight

Keep a quick tally of who's had a moment this session.

- Quiet players often need a direct invitation: "Rin, the scout sees something on the ridge. What is it?"
- Give each PC's personal thread a beat every couple of sessions. The bible's threads file tells you whose is overdue.
- When one player dominates, don't scold. Cut to another character: "Meanwhile, across the market…"

## Improvising

- **Say yes, or "yes, but."** Let player ideas work, with a cost or complication attached. A flat "no" is for things that break the setting.
- **Ask questions back.** "What does your character know about this guild?" Players will build the world for you.
- **Steal from your larder.** The improv list (names, places, complications) from the prep sheet is for exactly this moment.
- **Write down what you invent.** Any name or fact you make up goes on a scrap list for the recap, so the bible stays consistent.

## Rules calls

Make a fast ruling, note it, and look it up after the session. Stopping play for ten minutes to find a rule costs more than a slightly wrong call. Tell the table the ruling stands for tonight and you'll confirm next time.

## Common GM mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Railroading: the plot only works if players choose X | Prep situations, not sequences; sketch "what if they don't" |
| Hiding the one clue that unlocks the adventure | Plant three clues for every conclusion players must reach |
| NPCs who talk too long | Two sentences, then ask the players what they do |
| Punishing creative plans with bad luck | Reward clever plans with an edge or reduced risk |
| Ending sessions mid-shopping | Move admin into downtime between sessions by message |

The "three clues" rule is widely taught for mystery scenarios: if the party must work something out, plant at least three separate ways to discover it, because they'll miss some.

## Worked example

Midway through a heist session, the party spends 40 minutes planning. The GM checks the time, cuts in with "Okay, you've got a plan. Let's go in," and resolves the remaining prep with flashbacks during the heist. The session ends on the vault door opening, which becomes the next strong start.
