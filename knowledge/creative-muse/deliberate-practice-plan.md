# Deliberate practice plan

Deliberate practice means working on a specific weakness at the edge of your ability, with feedback, rather than repeating what you can already do. The idea comes from Anders Ericsson's research on expertise. In everyday terms: isolate the hard bit, slow it down, get feedback, repeat.

## 1. Baseline

```
Craft: ___
What I can do now: ___ (a recording, photo set or sketch dated today)
What frustrates me most: ___ (specific: "fast runs in bar 12", "hands look stiff")
Real time per day: ___ minutes (the honest number on a busy week)
Goal in 3 months: ___ (observable: "play the piece at tempo", "draw a convincing hand in 5 minutes")
```

A short daily session beats an ambitious weekly one because skills consolidate between sessions.

## 2. Session structure

For a 20-30 minute session:

| Part | Share of time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | ~10-15% | Easy, familiar material to get loose |
| Isolation | ~60-70% | The hard thing on its own: slowed, simplified, repeated with attention |
| Integration | ~15-25% | Use the skill in context: play the whole piece, take a photo walk |
| Log | 1 minute | What I worked on, one-line self-rating |

Isolation techniques by craft:

- **Music:** loop two bars; slow the tempo with a metronome and raise it in small steps; practise rhythms separately from notes.
- **Drawing:** studies of one thing (hands, ellipses, folds); timed gestures; copy a master drawing to see choices.
- **Photography:** one setting at a time (manual exposure only today); one subject from ten angles.
- **Writing:** rewrite one paragraph five ways; dialogue-only scenes; imitate a sentence you admire.
- **Voice:** single phrases on a recorder; breath exercises; one vowel across a range.

## 3. Standard progressions

Most crafts have established progressions: graded exam pieces for instruments, drawing curricula that build from simple forms, classic photography exercises. Look these up for the person's level rather than inventing a sequence.

## 4. Feedback

- Record or photograph work regularly. Hearing or seeing yourself from outside is the cheapest feedback.
- A teacher, a critique group or a trusted peer catches what you can't.
- Compare with a reference: the original recording, a photo you admire.

## 5. Monthly re-record

Once a month, record or redo the baseline piece under the same conditions. Put it next to last month's. Progress that's invisible day to day often shows clearly month to month, and that keeps motivation up.

## Practice log

```
Date | Minutes | Focus (isolation) | Integration | Self-rating 1-5 | Note
```

## Plateaus

Check the log before adding more time.

| What the log shows | Likely issue | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly integration, little isolation | Playing through, not practising | Shift the ratio toward isolation |
| Same exercise for weeks | Too easy now | Raise difficulty: tempo, time limit, complexity |
| Lots of isolation, little joy | Burnout risk | Add more integration; pick a fun piece |
| Gaps of days | Time or motivation | Shorter sessions, fixed time of day |
| Self-ratings all 5 | Not at the edge | Find the next frustrating thing |

## Common mistakes

- Practising only the parts that already sound good.
- Speeding up before it's clean at a slow tempo.
- No recordings, so progress is a feeling instead of evidence.
- Plans that need an hour a day from someone who has twenty minutes.

If practice causes pain (wrists, back, voice strain), stop and get advice from a teacher or a health professional before continuing.
