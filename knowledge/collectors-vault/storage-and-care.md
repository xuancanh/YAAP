# Storage and care

Most damage to home collections comes from the environment and handling, not accidents. Stability matters as much as the exact numbers: big swings in temperature and humidity stress materials. Specific targets differ by material; check the hobby's own guidance or a conservation body such as the Library of Congress before setting up storage for valuable pieces.

## Principles that apply to almost everything

**Environment**

- Cool, dry and stable. Living spaces are usually better than attics, basements and garages, which swing in temperature and humidity and are where leaks happen.
- Keep items away from exterior walls, radiators, heating vents, windows and under pipes.
- Store off the floor so a leak doesn't reach them.
- A cheap thermo-hygrometer in the storage area tells you what's actually happening. Log readings occasionally.

**Light**

- Light fades paper, ink, dyes and photographs, and the damage adds up and can't be reversed.
- Keep stored items in the dark. Display away from direct sun, and rotate displayed items.

**Materials**

- Use archival-quality enclosures: acid-free, lignin-free paper and boards, and inert plastics (such as polyester, polypropylene or polyethylene sleeves) sold for archival use.
- Avoid PVC sleeves for long-term storage, rubber bands, paper clips, sticky tape and ordinary cardboard boxes in contact with items.

**Handling**

- Clean, dry hands; handle by edges.
- Support items fully: carry books and folders flat; don't lift coins or cards by the face.
- One item out at a time on a clean, clear surface.

## Material notes (general)

| Material | Main risks | Basic care |
|---|---|---|
| Paper (comics, stamps, prints) | Light, humidity, acid, pests | Archival sleeves and boards, flat or upright with support, dark storage |
| Vinyl | Heat (warping), dust, scratches | Store upright, not packed too tight, inner sleeves, away from heat |
| Coins and metals | Fingerprints, humidity, reactive materials | Inert holders, handle by the rim, don't clean |
| Photographs | Light, humidity, handling | Archival enclosures, handle by edges, cool and dark |
| Textiles | Light, pests, folding stress | Pad folds, breathable storage, regular checks for pests |
| Watches | Moisture, magnetism, lack of service | Dry storage, service per maker's advice |

## Never-do list

- Don't clean coins. Cleaning usually lowers value.
- Don't tape tears, glue loose parts, or iron creases on valuable items.
- Don't laminate documents or cards.
- Don't store in plastic bins sealed with damp items inside.
- Don't use household cleaners or polishes on collectibles.
- Don't attempt restoration on valuable pieces; get a conservator's opinion first.

## Disaster readiness

- Know where the most valuable items are and how to move them quickly.
- Keep the catalog and photos backed up off-site or in the cloud.
- After water damage, act quickly and get professional advice; mould can start soon.

## When to call a professional

A professional conservator should look at active mould, pest damage, water damage, flaking or powdering surfaces, and any repair or cleaning on items of real value.
