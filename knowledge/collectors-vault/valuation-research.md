# Valuation research

A value is only meaningful with a date, a source and a condition match. Asking prices are wishes; sold prices are evidence.

## The method

1. **Pin the exact item.** Same variant, edition or pressing. A near-match isn't a comparable.
2. **Collect sold prices.** Completed auction results, sold marketplace listings and dealer sales where published. Aim for several recent sales. Record each with date, venue, condition and price (and whether fees were included).
3. **Match condition.** Use comparables at the same grade if possible. If not, adjust and explain the adjustment in one line.
4. **Give a range.** Low to high from the condition-matched comparables. Avoid a single precise figure; it implies more certainty than the data supports.
5. **Add a liquidity note.** How often does this item sell? A rare item may be valuable but take months to find a buyer.
6. **Date it.** Write the research date on every value. Re-check notable pieces yearly or when the market moves.

## Valuation log entry

```
Item: [ID, short name]       Research date: 2026-11-04
Comparables:
- 2026-09-14 · [auction house] · grade VF · sold 420 (incl. fees)
- 2026-07-02 · [marketplace] · grade F · sold 310
- 2026-05-20 · [dealer list] · grade VF · sold 450
Asking (not evidence): two listings at 600-650
Adjustment: our copy has a small edge tear → toward lower end of VF band
Range: 350-430 · Liquidity: about one sale every two months
Type: market research, not an appraisal
```

## Which value?

Different purposes use different values:

- **Replacement value:** what it would cost to buy a similar item, often used for insurance.
- **Fair market value:** what a willing buyer and willing seller would agree, often used for estates and some tax matters.
- **Sale proceeds:** what you'd actually receive after fees, commission and shipping.

Tell the user which one the research approximates. Insurers, estates and tax authorities may require a specific definition and a qualified appraiser.

## When to get a formal appraisal

- The insurer requires one for scheduled items above a threshold.
- An estate, divorce, donation or tax matter needs a documented value.
- A high-value sale where you need to know the reserve.
- Authenticity is uncertain.

Look for an appraiser with recognized credentials in the specific hobby, who charges a flat or hourly fee rather than a percentage of value, and who doesn't offer to buy the item.

## Worked example

User: "What's my first-pressing record worth?"

1. Check runout etchings to confirm the pressing; it matches the first press.
2. Six sold results in the last year: two at Near Mint, four at Very Good Plus.
3. User's copy self-graded VG+ record, VG sleeve (a seam split).
4. VG+ sales cluster in a narrow band; the sleeve split pulls toward the low end.
5. Reply: range with date, "sells a few times a year", and "if you're insuring it separately, your insurer may want a formal appraisal."

## Common mistakes

- Using the highest asking price on the internet.
- Mixing variants or grades.
- Undated values in the inventory.
- Forgetting fees: the hammer price isn't what the seller receives.
- Treating a collection's total as a price someone would pay for it all at once.
